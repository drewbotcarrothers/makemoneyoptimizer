User testing sites pay you to try a website, an app, or a product while you talk through what you see, or to join a screened interview. In Canada the three names that keep coming up are UserTesting, User Interviews, and Respondent. Each one has an official page that includes Canada. They pay more per session than a short survey, and they also turn away more applicants. They suit someone with a working microphone, a stable connection, and patience for screeners. They do not suit someone who wants to click tasks for an hour and cash out the same day.

## What does each site say about Canada?

**UserTesting.** The article written for customers lists standard contributor countries available to all customers: the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Canada, India, and Australia. It also says UserTesting will not recruit contributors from countries where PayPal does not do business, because contributors are paid through PayPal. PayPal does operate in Canada, which is consistent with Canada being on that list. A separate participant article says the company does not share a fixed list because availability depends on supply and demand, and that the signup page is the check. Both can be true: Canada is a standard market, and a given week can still be thin. The application page says the practice test and signup take about 15–20 minutes, and that a decision comes within a few days.

**User Interviews.** The participant country article, updated October 5, 2026, lists Canada under the Americas, with Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Mexico, Puerto Rico, and the United States. If your country is not on the list, you cannot join yet. The company says it limits the list so people who join have a real chance of studies. Its research-panel page publishes a Canada panel size (294,493 on the page checked for this guide). That is the company's own snapshot of people in the panel, not a count of studies you will be invited to.

**Respondent.** Researcher help lists 21 primary markets, and Canada is one of them, alongside the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, and others. The company also lets researchers choose "worldwide." A Canada panel marketing page exists. Incentives are listed and paid in USD only ([setting the incentive](https://help.respondent.io/en/articles/5489350-setting-your-incentive)).

## How does the money actually arrive?

**UserTesting** sends every test payment to a verified PayPal account in your name. You do not share that PayPal account with another contributor. The payments FAQ says amounts vary by test type, duration, and demand, and that the dashboard shows the amount for each test. It does not publish a standard dollar figure, so this guide will not invent a "$10 a test" rate. Payment usually goes out 14 days after you complete the test. The company does not withhold taxes and says you are responsible for tax obligations. Customers can cancel pay for incomplete tests, skipped tasks, or a recording that does not meet the brief. The FAQ says customers generally expect about a 15-minute recording.

**User Interviews** splits payment in two. If the study says "choice of dozens of digital gift cards," User Interviews pays through Tremendous after the researcher marks attendance. The redeem article says cash options such as PayPal, Venmo, bank transfer, and a physical prepaid Visa are not offered on that path. A prepaid digital Visa is the closest cash-like option, and you must pick the right currency because a wrong-currency redemption is not reissued. If the study lists something else, the researcher pays it, and the type is on the study page before you apply. Researchers have up to 10 business days after the session.

**Respondent** pays the advertised incentive minus a fulfillment fee of 5%, with a US$1 minimum. Its own examples: a US$20 incentive pays US$19 (the US$1 minimum, because 5% of US$20 is US$1). A US$95 incentive pays US$90.25. Incentives run from US$5 to US$1,000 in US$5 steps. You redeem a Tremendous gift card. The help article says PayPal through Tremendous may exist for some projects under US$20. There is no balance to withdraw. Payment typically takes 7–10 business days from confirmation. Off-platform payment is prohibited ([why the incentive is lower](https://help.respondent.io/en/articles/5465041-why-is-my-participation-incentive-lower-than-expected)).

### Illustrative example

Assumed session, using Respondent's published fee. A study advertises US$60. The fee is 5% × 60 = US$3, so you receive US$57. At the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4254 CAD per US dollar on October 5, 2026, US$57 is about $81.25 CAD before the gift card's own conversion. If you are screened out, you receive nothing for that application. The US$60 is an assumption inside Respondent's allowed range. It is not a typical study.

## How do you get accepted more often?

### Step 1: Apply to the real site

Use usertesting.com, userinterviews.com, and respondent.io. A Telegram or email account that asks you to pay a "panel fee" is not these companies. That pattern belongs in [side hustle scams](/side-hustles/side-hustle-scams-canada/).

### Step 2: Finish one profile honestly

UserTesting says a more complete profile qualifies you for more tests. User Interviews and Respondent use screeners. Answer with the devices, job, and city you actually have. Contradictions get you removed, and they waste the studies that did match you.

### Step 3: Expect most applications to end at the screener

These platforms exist so researchers can reject non-matches. A low hit rate is the ordinary experience, not a sign you filled the form in wrong. Apply when the pay and the time are worth it. Do not apply to every listing in the hope that volume replaces fit.

### Step 4: Do the session the instructions describe

UserTesting can cancel pay if tasks are skipped or the recording is thin. Respondent will not pay an incentive the researcher never confirms. Read the length before you book a call you cannot attend.

### Step 5: Add a study platform that does not need a camera

[Prolific](/side-hustles/prolific-review-canada/) pays fixed rewards for online studies and lists Canada. It will not replace a well-paid interview, and it also will not leave you with an empty week if every screener says no.

## What mistakes do Canadians make on user testing sites?

- **Quoting a US blog's per-test rate.** UserTesting's payments FAQ says the dashboard shows the amount. Use that number.
- **Missing the Respondent fee.** The headline incentive is not the amount you redeem.
- **Redeeming a Tremendous card in the wrong currency.** User Interviews says it will not reissue it.
- **A shared PayPal login.** UserTesting forbids it.
- **Booking interviews you cannot attend.** Unpaid time plus a poor participant record is a bad trade for a US$20 incentive.

## Which tax rules apply to user testing pay in Canada?

These payments are income. Report them in Canadian dollars. Business income goes on Form T2125. UserTesting says it does not withhold tax. Respondent notes that tax documents, if any, come from PayPal or Tremendous, and it tells participants to talk to their own tax advisor. Gift cards are still value you received.
