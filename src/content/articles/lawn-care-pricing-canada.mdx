Charging for lawn care in Canada usually means a per-cut price set by lot size and the minutes it takes, or a seasonal contract that bundles weekly cuts from spring to fall, with edging, aeration, and leaf cleanup sold as add-ons. Job Bank lists employed lawn care workers at $17.00 (low), $20.75 (median), and $28.00 (high) an hour nationally. A self-employed cutter prices above that because fuel, mower wear, travel between lawns, and both halves of CPP come out of the same rate. This guide suits someone who will time a real lawn before quoting the street.

## What do lawn care workers earn per hour in Canada?

Employed lawn care workers in Canada earn a median of $20.75 an hour, according to [Job Bank's lawn care worker wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/24694/ca) (NOC 85121, landscaping and grounds maintenance labourers, reference period 2023–2024, updated November 19, 2025). Those are employee wages. A self-employed price per cut should work out above them once travel and equipment are counted.

## Which pricing models work for lawn care in Canada?

Lawn care in Canada is usually sold per cut by lot size, as a seasonal contract with a set number of visits, or as one-off cleanups. Add-ons cover work that is not mowing.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per cut | Price by lot size and obstacles | New clients, irregular schedules | Clients who call only when it is knee-high |
| Seasonal contract | Set number of weekly or biweekly cuts, paid in instalments | Predictable routes | Drought weeks and rainy weeks; write a skip policy |
| Spring / fall cleanup | One-off price from timed hours | Leaves, thatch, debris | Bag and disposal costs |
| Add-ons | Edging, hedge trim, aeration, overseeding | Raising the ticket on the same visit | Doing them for free |

Write what happens when grass does not grow in August, and when rain cancels a visit. A contract without those two sentences creates arguments.

## How do you calculate a lawn care rate in Canada?

Calculate a lawn care rate in Canada from your target hourly pay, the share of the day lost to driving and loading, the timed minutes per lawn, and fuel and wear per cut. Then round and check against local listings.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

Read your province's Job Bank row. Your take-home per working hour should not land below the employee low.

### Step 2: Measure the route

Lawns on one block cost less time between stops than lawns across town. Clustered routes let you hold prices and still earn more per hour.

### Step 3: Time each lawn

Mowing, trimming, blowing off the walk, and bagging if the client wants clippings removed.

### Step 4: Add fuel, wear, and disposal

Fuel, blades, trimmer line, and yard-waste bags. Equipment purchases are startup costs; recover them over the season.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $24 an hour, between Job Bank's national median of $20.75 and high of $28.00. Suppose 75% of your time is billable. The billable rate is $24 ÷ 0.75 = $32 an hour.

Suppose a standard suburban front and back lawn takes 40 minutes including trimming. Labour is (40 ÷ 60) × $32 = $21.33. Add an assumed $3 of fuel and wear: $24.33, which rounds to $25 per cut before tax.

Suppose a seasonal contract covers 22 weekly cuts. That count is an assumption, not a climate normal; use your own season. At $25 a cut, the contract is 22 × $25 = $550, which you might bill as five monthly instalments of $110. If the client skips three drought weeks and the contract says skipped weeks are not refunded, your hourly result rises; if it says they are credited, the contract is 19 × $25 = $475. Choose one and write it down.

## When should you raise lawn care prices in Canada?

Raise lawn care prices in Canada before the season starts, when you send renewal contracts, rather than mid-season. Fuel cost changes, a full route, or timings that run long are good reasons.

A full route with a waiting list is the clearest signal. Lawns far from the cluster are the first to reprice or let go.

## What lawn care pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common lawn care pricing mistakes in Canada are pricing by guess instead of timed minutes, selling seasonal contracts without a skip policy, and giving edging away. Each one lowers your hourly result all season.

For the pesticide licence, safety, and insurance mistakes lawn workers make most, and how to fix them, see the [lawn care mistakes guide](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada/#common-lawn-care-mistakes).

Charge extra for overgrown first cuts. Write what happens on rain days. Price bag disposal. Do not cut prices for a far-off lawn that eats 20 minutes of driving. Keep a log of minutes per lawn for the first month.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect lawn care prices in Canada?

Lawn care income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Add GST/HST only once registered; registration is required after you exceed the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, and snow removal, cleaning, and other work count toward the same total.

When registered, use the rate for the province where you cut from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers the details.
