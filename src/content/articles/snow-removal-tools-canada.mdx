## What snow removal tools does a first route in Canada need?

A first snow removal route in Canada needs a snow shovel, a smaller shovel for steps, ice melt, a headlamp with spare batteries, and footwear you trust on ice. The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety says shovelling snow is strenuous and that, where possible, it is more practical to shovel early and often, because fresh dry snow is lighter than wet or packed snow.

## When should a snow blower replace the shovel on a Canadian route?

A snow blower replaces the shovel on a Canadian snow removal route when the timed morning is too long to finish safely on foot, and not before clients exist. [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/landscaping/snow_throwers.html) says a snow blower does not make the work effortless or risk-free, that it remains physical work in the cold, and that it cannot remove ice built up under the snow.

Follow the owner manual. The fact sheet was last revised on 24 December 2021. Read the live page before you rely on a step.

### Step 1: Clear the area and the people

CCOHS says to keep people away, pick up debris, and remember that thrown objects can injure someone or damage the machine. Do not aim the chute at a person, a window, or a car.

### Step 2: Start gas engines outside

CCOHS says to start a gas engine outside, not in a shed or garage, because carbon monoxide can accumulate indoors. Let the machine adjust to the outdoor temperature. Disengage the clutches before you start.

### Step 3: Set the runners for the surface

On gravel, CCOHS says to use extreme caution and to adjust the runners so the scraper blade does not contact gravel. Stones go through windows. A single-stage machine that must touch the pavement is a poor match for a loose driveway. Say so in the quote. Pricing that difference is in [how to price snow removal in Canada](/side-hustles/snow-removal-pricing-canada/).

### Step 4: Stop the machine before you clear a clog

CCOHS says abnormal vibration and a clog are a warning. Before cleaning or inspecting: turn the machine off, disengage all clutches, remove the key, wait until moving parts stop and hot parts cool, remove the spark-plug wire, and unplug an electric machine. Never put hands or feet down the chute or near the blades. Do not use your hands to clear a clog. Use a tool. Fingers have been amputated when a blade turned unexpectedly.

### Step 5: Fuel it the way the page describes

CCOHS says fuel is highly flammable, to use an approved container, to refuel outdoors, and to wait until the engine has cooled. Do not fill a tank indoors or while the engine is running or hot. Store the can the way the can and your municipality describe, not on a balcony.

Shear pins are meant to break when the auger hits a hard object. Carry spares. The pin is not a reason to bypass the clog steps above.

## How do single-stage, two-stage, and three-stage snow blowers differ?

Single-stage, two-stage, and three-stage snow blowers differ by how many mechanisms move the snow, and the right one for snow removal in Canada depends on pavement, gravel, and depth. Clearing width and intake height are specifications on that model. They are not a Canadian standard.

[Ariens](https://www.ariens.eu/ie/expert-tips/choosing-the-right-snow-blower-for-your-winter-needs/) describes a single-stage blower as one auger, typically rubber paddles, that touches the ground and scoops and throws in one action. The same page says that contact can leave a cleaner path and that this style suits smaller areas and lighter, fresher snow, and that the auger on a two-stage machine does not touch the ground, so that style suits uneven or gravel surfaces. A two-stage machine uses a metal auger to gather snow and an impeller to throw it. Ariens' [Canadian S18 page](https://www.ariens.com/en-ca/power-equipment/snow-products/snow-blowers/s18-single-stage) describes that compact single-stage model as a paddle machine that clears to the pavement and as suited to smaller jobs. Do not copy one model's snowfall figure onto every single-stage blower. Read the sheet for the machine in front of you, and see a used machine start in the cold.

[Cub Cadet](https://www.cubcadet.com/en_US/snow-blowers/three-stage-snow-blowers) describes its three-stage machines as an auger that breaks up and draws snow in, an accelerator that pulls material through, and an impeller that throws it. Any speed comparison on that maker's site is a comparison with its own two-stage models. It is not an independent test of every brand. Treat "three-stage" as a design to confirm on the model page, not as a reason to finance a heavy machine before the route exists.

## When is a plough the right snow removal tool in Canada?

A plough is the right snow removal tool in Canada for long private driveways and lots you are allowed to clear, after insurance describes the blade. A public street is a municipal job. A side hustle that dumps snow into the road is the wrong machine.

## How should you choose ice melt for snow removal in Canada?

Choose ice melt for snow removal in Canada by scraping first, reading the bag, and using less than a panicked handful. Environment and Climate Change Canada's [Code of Practice for road salts](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/pollutants/road-salts/code-practice-environmental-management.html) is written for road organizations, including those that use large tonnages or have salt-vulnerable areas, and it is not a filing duty for a 20 kg bag.

The practical point for a driveway is the same idea the code states for roads: salt has an environmental cost, so application should be deliberate. Store the bag dry. Tell the client what you use. "Pet-safer" on a label is a prompt to read the warnings. It is not a promise the product is candy.

## Which tax rules apply to snow removal tools in Canada?

Snow removal income in Canada is self-employment income, including a seasonal lump sum, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. Salt, fuel, and repairs are current expenses. A blower that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion, not necessarily the full price in year one. GST/HST for snow removal uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the receipts. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add lawn, cleaning, and handyman revenue.
