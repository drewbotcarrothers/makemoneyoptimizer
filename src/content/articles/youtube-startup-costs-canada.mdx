Starting a YouTube side hustle in Canada, meaning a series of videos on one topic you can keep publishing, can cost about $0–$40 when a phone and a window are enough, about $60–$180 for a typical start with a microphone and a small light, or about $250–$800 when a better mic, a stable tripod, invoicing for a sponsor you actually have, and an insurance quote are in the same plan. Those YouTube figures are mostly typical Canadian retail ranges to verify locally. Joining the Partner Program is not a fee you pay YouTube for the thresholds below. The budget suits people who can edit a rough cut, and it does not assume ad revenue in month one.

How to pick the topic and read the eligibility rules is the [YouTube side hustle guide](/side-hustles/youtube-side-hustle-canada). This page is the cash. A camera body is not the start.

## How much does a YouTube channel cost to start in Canada?

A YouTube channel in Canada starts at about $0–$40 when you film on a phone you own, about $60–$180 when an external microphone and a light or a tripod are the first purchases, and about $250–$800 when you add a better microphone, headphones for editing, a paid design tool you have priced, and you have asked about insurance. The ranges are not quotes. This guide does not publish a dollar figure per thousand views.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What you can publish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0–$40 | The phone, a window or a lamp you own, and a free editor on a computer you have. A stack of books can be the tripod. |
| Typical | $60–$180 | An external microphone in the retail range below, a small light if evenings are your only time, and inexpensive headphones so you can hear the hum. |
| Doing it properly | $250–$800 | The typical kit, a microphone you chose after the first videos showed the problem, FreshBooks only if a sponsor or a client is paying an invoice, a domain if you want a site, a course only at that course's checkout, and a broker's quote. A cinema camera is still the wrong first purchase. |

Eligibility is a set of rules, not a line in the cash table. YouTube's [Partner Program overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en) says you follow the channel monetization policies, live where the program is available, have no active Community Guidelines strikes, turn on 2-Step Verification, have advanced features access, and link an AdSense for YouTube account. The same page says a channel can become eligible with 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. Qualified watch hours from Shorts in the Shorts feed do not count toward the 4,000 watch-hour threshold. Meeting the threshold is not automatic acceptance. YouTube reviews the channel. The page says Partner Program updates are effective 1 February 2027, and tells creators to review terms in YouTube Studio. Read that notice.

The [expanded Partner Program](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en) page lists Canada among the countries where earlier fan-funding and Shopping access can apply, at lower thresholds than ad revenue. Confirm the live page before you treat any number as the one that pays you. Ads can appear on videos when you are not receiving a share. The eligibility page says you are not eligible to receive a share until you qualify.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for YouTube in Canada?

Ongoing YouTube costs in Canada are gear you already bought, software you kept, and hours of filming and editing. YouTube break-even in the sketch is a method for the gear, not a revenue forecast. This guide does not state a rate per thousand views, because that rate is not a rule you can copy from the help page onto your channel.

**Review.** Meeting a subscriber number does not turn monetization on. YouTube reviews the channel.

**Inactivity.** The eligibility page says YouTube may turn off monetization on channels that have not uploaded a video or posted to the Posts tab for 6 months or more.

**Other income.** A digital product or a service you sell from the description is a separate offer, with its own costs. It is not ad revenue.

### Step 1: Add the microphone only if the test failed

A quiet room and the phone can be the whole budget.

### Step 2: Add light and stability second

A window is $0. A lamp is the small end of the range.

### Step 3: Keep editing on one free or already-owned tool

A second editor is a later argument with yourself.

### Step 4: Leave ad math out of month one

Use the gear total as the number to earn back later, from whatever income actually arrives. Do not invent a view rate to make the camera look affordable.

### Step 5: Invoice only money that is not AdSense

A sponsor fee is your invoice. A platform payout is a report you keep.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a per-view rate.**

Suppose the phone and the computer are yours. Suppose the first purchases are a microphone at $50 and a light at $25, inside the ranges above. Gear = $75. Suppose you publish four videos and you are not in the Partner Program, so ad share in this sketch is $0. Suppose a local business later pays an assumed $150 to mention a real offer in one video, and that $150 is an assumption, not a market rate. Suppose FreshBooks is not on yet.

Remainder after gear = $150 − $75 = $75. Suppose the four videos plus the sponsored one took 20 hours. This sketch is $75 ÷ 20 = $3.75 an hour before tax set-aside. The hour figure is low because the sketch refuses to invent ad income. That is the point. A $1,200 camera on the same $150 would not clear. Do not copy $150. If you are registered and the sponsor's payment is your taxable supply, tax on the invoice is not extra profit.

## Where can a YouTube startup budget in Canada stay smaller?

A YouTube startup budget in Canada stays smaller when the phone is the camera, the editor is free, and the microphone waits for a failed audio test. YouTube's own creator pages, linked from the eligibility article, are the course that costs $0. A title you can say out loud beats a paid thumbnail tool. One format for the first month means you learn editing once.

Skip a domain, a scheduler, and invoicing software until a human is paying you outside the platform. Skip a yearly design subscription in week one. Skip any budget that only works if ads pay a number you found on a forum.

## Where should YouTube spending not be cut in Canada?

YouTube spending in Canada should not skip intelligible audio, rights to the footage and music, or the monetization policies if you hope to be reviewed later. A cheap mic that removes a roar is worth more than a camera that films the roar clearly. Clips you do not have rights to, and titles that promise income, are how a channel fails a later review. The eligibility page says channels lose monetization when they violate the monetization policies, regardless of subscriber count.

Do not film people in a way you cannot explain. Do not buy a camera on credit against views. Insurance is a quote if you are on someone else's premises or using gear that could hurt someone. This is not insurance advice.

Keep any payout, when it exists, separate from rent. A quiet year is still a year you should be able to explain from records, including a note that the amount was small.

## Which tax rules apply to a YouTube budget in Canada?

YouTube income in Canada, when it arrives, is income to record on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. A microphone and a light are generally current expenses. A camera or computer that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion, not the full price in month one. GST/HST on your own taxable supplies uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Platform payouts can also raise questions on the CRA's [digital-economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) pages. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep AdSense or sponsor records if you receive them. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over four consecutive quarters. A channel is not ride-sharing. Add other side hustles to the same pot.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use. You generally need to be a registrant and you need receipts. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST on your own invoices and do not claim the credit. How a platform's payout is taxed is a question for the CRA or a tax professional before you add tax on top of a payout that already had a platform's own treatment.

Once you charge tax yourself, use the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set it aside.
