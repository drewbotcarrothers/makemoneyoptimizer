Group fitness instruction in Canada is paid work leading a class: the warm-up, the main block, and the cool-down, for several people at once, on a schedule a facility or a park can repeat. It suits people who can cue movement out loud, show up early, and get the certificate the gym actually asks for. ## Who does group fitness instruction in Canada suit?

Group fitness instruction in Canada suits people who like a room, a playlist, and a start time that does not move. Early mornings and evenings are when employed adults can attend. It is a poor fit if you dislike cueing in public, if you cannot maintain CPR, or if you hoped to skip a practical exam because you already work out.

A gym's membership perk, a free pass or a staff class rate, is a benefit on top of the class fee only if the facility puts it in the offer. It is not a wage. Ask what is paid per class, what is employee hourly, and what is a discount. Those are three different deals.

## Which certification bodies register group fitness instructors in Canada?

Certification for group fitness in Canada is not one federal licence. Facilities, insurers, and provincial registries name the credentials they accept. Read the current page, because packages and fees change. This guide does not restate course prices.

**canfitpro, Fitness Instructor Specialist.** The [FIS page](https://www.canfitpro.com/fitness-instructor-specialist/) describes a course for people who want to lead group classes. It says you need time for 20 hours of self-study, then a live course in person or virtual with a PRO TRAINER. You pass a theory exam and a live practical exam observed by a PRO TRAINER. An 80% grade is required. You submit a valid CPR certification. The minimum stated on that page is CPR Level A. The page lists a standard package and a premium package. The premium package includes CPR Level A and AED; the standard package's checklist marks CPR as not included. Confirm the live package before you pay. canfitpro also offers a Personal Training Specialist credential. That path is the one-to-one certificate covered in the [personal training guide](/side-hustles/personal-training-side-hustle-canada/). FIS is the group-class credential on canfitpro's own page.

**Fitness Alberta.** The [group exercise page](https://www.fitnessalberta.com/get-certified-group-exercise) starts with Foundations in Physical Activity and Exercise, then a Foundations exam with a minimum of 80%. You choose a specialty. The page names six: older adult exercise, aquatic exercise, group conditioning, group cycle, mind-body, and group choreography. A Fitness Alberta educator observes a practical exam. CPR must be current, issued within the last 24 months, from an approved provider. Online CPR is not accepted. The page says certification fees include professional insurance and that certification is valid for 2 years. You have one year from the Foundations course to finish.

**Ontario Fitness Council.** The [scope of practice](https://ontariofitnesscouncil.com/scope-of-practice) page says an OFC group exercise instructor has met exercise-theory prerequisites and a specialty course with theory and practical exams. Specialties listed there include portable equipment and conditioning, mind and body, indoor cycling, choreography, and step hi-lo. The page says step hi-lo and other aerobic leaders are recognized nationally within the National Fitness Leadership Alliance, and that you contact the other province about a transfer. It also says instructors screen with a current PAR-Q or medical clearance, and that nutrition advice stays within Canada's Food Guide unless you refer out. Read the scope before you advertise rehab, nutrition plans, or special populations you are not registered for.

**BCRPA in British Columbia.** Many BC facilities expect a BCRPA registered fitness leader, not only a national course certificate. BCRPA's [transfer page](https://www.bcrpa.bc.ca/manage-fitness/transfer-to-bcrpa-fitness/) says registration includes liability insurance. It also says a direct transfer is available when your current body is a Fitness Leadership Canada member, and it names Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, New Brunswick, and Nova Scotia among those members. A certificate from a body outside that list follows a longer path on the same page, including a theory exam and an instructor competency evaluation. Confirm whether your gym wants BCRPA registration before you assume a canfitpro card is enough in BC.

**CSEP.** The [CSEP-CPT](https://csep.ca/csep-certification/csep-cpt/) is a personal-trainer credential. Applicants need the equivalent of at least two years (60 credits) of related post-secondary study. Registration requires in-person CPR Level C and emergency first aid. It is the academic path for one-to-one work described in the personal training guide. It is not, by itself, the group-fitness specialty a studio listed on the Fitness Alberta or OFC pages.

Ask the facility which card it accepts. Then buy that path. A second certificate can wait until a second employer asks.

## How do group fitness instructors get paid in Canada?

Group fitness instructors in Canada are paid either as employees of a facility or as contractors who invoice a class. [Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/24578) reports wages for program leaders and instructors in recreation, sport and fitness (NOC 54100), updated 19 November 2025, reference period 2023–2024. The occupation includes personal trainers and recreation leaders, so the row is wider than "group fitness class."

| Area | Low | Median | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada | $15.44 | $19.00 | $31.00 |
| Ontario | $17.95 | $18.50 | $31.00 |
| British Columbia | $18.25 | $23.74 | $35.00 |
| Alberta | $15.50 | $20.00 | $35.00 |
| Quebec | $16.60 | $18.75 | $29.00 |

Those are employee hourly wages. A per-class contract can be higher or lower once you count unpaid setup, music licensing the facility may already hold, and a commute. Do not turn the median into a quote. Read the facility's rate. If you are an employee, the wage and source deductions are on the pay stub. If you invoice, you are in the tax section below.

Membership perks, a staff membership, or a guest pass are compensation only when they are written down. Ask whether the perk is taxable. This guide does not calculate that benefit.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote or a survey. Suppose a rec centre pays an assumed $32 a class, and the class is 55 minutes plus 15 minutes of setup you are not separately paid. Suppose you teach three classes in a week. Gross is 3 × $32 = $96. Suppose the bus costs an assumed $12. Amount left is $84 before tax. The three classes plus setup are about 3.5 hours, so this sketch is $84 ÷ 3.5 = $24 an hour before a tax set-aside. Job Bank's national median is $19.00 for employees in the wider occupation. This sketch is not that median, and it is not a reason to demand $32. If the centre instead puts you on payroll at $19 an hour for only the class time, the comparison changes. Use the offer in front of you.

## Where can you teach a group class in Canada?

You can teach where a facility hires you, or where you have permission to run a paid class. City-owned studios are not automatically available. Toronto's [online booking conditions](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/venues-facilities-bookings/booking-park-recreation-facilities/online-booking-information-conditions/) say bookable fitness and dance studios must not be used for business purposes or for-profit services, including personal training or fitness instruction. Read your own city's parks and recreation booking rules before you sell a class in a public room. A condo gym usually bans commercial classes. A park may need a permit. Outdoor spring classes still need that permission, a first-aid plan, and a certificate the participants' insurer or the city recognizes.

Insurance is part of several registries: Fitness Alberta says its fees include professional insurance, and BCRPA's transfer page says registration includes liability insurance. A gym may still ask for a certificate of insurance that names the facility. Compare that request with what the registry actually provides. ## How do you start group fitness instruction in Canada?

### Step 1: Ask two facilities which credential they hire

Write down the exact name. Do not buy a US certificate and hope.

### Step 2: Complete that body's course, exams, and CPR

Use the CPR level the page names. Fitness Alberta rejects online CPR. canfitpro's FIS page states CPR Level A as the minimum to submit.

### Step 3: Register, if the province uses a registry

OFC registration in Ontario, BCRPA in British Columbia, Fitness Alberta certification in Alberta. Other provinces have their own Fitness Leadership Canada members. BCRPA's transfer page is one place those names are listed. Confirm the body in your province.

### Step 4: Sub for a class before you build a weekly grid

One class shows whether the cueing works. A weekly grid of six classes before the first sub is how people buy a certificate they do not use.

### Step 5: Put the pay in writing

Employee or contractor. Per class or hourly. What the membership perk is. Who carries the insurance.

## What mistakes do new group fitness instructors make in Canada?

Common mistakes are teaching for exposure, using a personal-training certificate for a group class the gym did not accept, and running a paid class in a city studio the booking page forbids. Another is letting CPR lapse and discovering the registry suspended the card. Another is quoting nutrition plans beyond Canada's Food Guide when the OFC scope says to refer out.

A further mistake is treating Job Bank's $19.00 median as the class rate. The occupation mixes programs. Your offer is the rate.

## Which tax rules apply to group fitness instruction in Canada?

It depends who pays you. An employee wage is employment income, with source deductions, and it is not a taxable supply you add GST/HST to. A contract you invoice is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both shares of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net.

GST/HST on those invoices follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). Other taxable side hustles share the total. When registered, use the provincial rate on the CRA's [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) page. A T4 job and a Saturday contract can exist in the same year. Keep them in separate records. The [tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/) and [T2125 vs T776](/side-hustles/t2125-vs-t776-canada/) cover the business side. Employee wages do not go on T2125.
