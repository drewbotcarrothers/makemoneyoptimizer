Pricing an ebook in Canada comes down to three numbers: what readers pay, what each store keeps, and how many copies you need to cover editing and a cover. BookNet Canada's 2025 survey puts the average price Canadian ebook buyers paid at $11.85. Amazon.ca and Kobo both pay 70% royalties from C$2.99, and Amazon.ca's 70% band now runs to C$12.99. Practical non-fiction sold directly as a PDF can sit higher: registered dietitians selling in Canadian dollars list recipe and meal-planning ebooks at $14.99 to $29.99 CAD.

## What do Canadians pay for ebooks in 2026?

Canadian ebook buyers paid an average of $11.85 per ebook in 2025, according to BookNet Canada's [Scrolling stories: Ebook use in Canada 2025](https://booknetcanada.ca/research/scrolling-stories-ebook-use-in-canada-2025/), published July 14, 2026. The study draws on 1,979 Canadians surveyed in July and December 2025, 327 of them ebook buyers, with a margin of error of ±3 percentage points.

### Average price paid by format (BookNet Canada, 2025)

| Format | Average price paid | Change since 2020 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardcover | $25.41 | +11% |
| Paperback | $18.16 | +11% |
| Ebook | $11.93 | +3% |
| Audiobook | $13.72 | −5% |

Source: [BookNet Canada, Canadians and rising book prices 2025](https://www.booknetcanada.ca/blog/2026/5/25/canadians-and-rising-book-prices-2025) (May 25, 2026), from its Canadian Book Consumer Study 2025. The $11.93 figure is for all buyers of new books; the $11.85 figure is for ebook buyers only. Both include traditionally published books, so they sit above typical self-published prices.

### How Canadian ebook buyers paid (BookNet Canada)

| How the ebook was bought | 2024 | 2025 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paid full price | 47% | 38% |
| Discounted, in a sale or money off | 27% | 33% |
| Other special price or offer | 11% | 9% |
| Part of a subscription | 6% | 9% |
| Coupon or voucher | 7% | 7% |
| Multi-buy deal | 3% | 5% |

BookNet also found 41% of ebook buyers chose where to buy because of a good price, offer or promotion, against 29% of all book buyers, and 77% agreed or sometimes agreed that they prefer to pay for a bundle rather than a single item. Fiction made up 71% of ebook purchases; romance led at 19%, and self-help was the top non-fiction subject at 21%. Kindle was the most-used ebook app (31%), followed by Google Books (22%), Kobo (16%) and Apple Books (14%).

## What do Amazon.ca, Kobo and direct-sale platforms keep?

Amazon.ca and Kobo both pay 70% from C$2.99; Amazon.ca subtracts a delivery charge and caps the 70% band at C$12.99, while Kobo has no cap or delivery charge. All figures were on each platform's page on October 9, 2026.

| Platform | Price rule | You receive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Amazon KDP, Amazon.ca](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560) 70% option | C$2.99 to C$12.99 | 70% × (list price − C$0.15 per MB delivery) |
| Amazon KDP, Amazon.ca 35% option | From C$0.99 (under 3 MB), C$1.99 (3 to 10 MB) or C$2.99 (10 MB+), up to C$200 | 35% of list price, no delivery charge |
| [Kobo Writing Life](https://kobowritinglife.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360058976032-What-will-my-earnings-be) | C$2.99 or more | 70% of list price, excluding tax |
| Kobo Writing Life | Below C$2.99 | 45% of list price |
| Kobo via OverDrive (libraries) | Your library price | 50% per library purchase |
| [Gumroad](https://gumroad.com/pricing) | Your price | Price − 10% − $0.50 (direct links); 30% fee through Discover |
| [Payhip](https://payhip.com/pricing) | Your price | Free plan 5% fee; Plus (US$29/month) 2%; Pro (US$99/month) 0%; card fees on top |
| [Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing) (card fees on your own site or Payhip) | — | 2.9% + C$0.30 per Canadian card payment |
| [Draft2Digital](https://draft2digital.com/faq/) (wide distribution) | Store rules apply | Commission per sale; US$20 one-time activation, US$12 a year if sales are under $100 |

KDP's [pricing page](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634500) rounds file sizes up to the nearest kilobyte and says average delivery costs are $0.06 per unit. Kobo pays 45 days after month end once you pass C$50. KDP widened the 70% band on July 7, 2026: on Amazon.com the top moved from US$9.99 to US$12.99, with equivalent changes in other marketplaces, so Amazon.ca's band now ends at C$12.99.

## What does it cost to produce an ebook?

Editing is usually the largest cost. Editors Canada's [2022 membership survey](https://reviseurs.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2025/01/Editors-Canada-2022-Membership-Survey-Report_final-edits-with-Alt-text_08OCT2023.pdf) found most respondents charged $40 to $59 an hour for most types of work; 46% charged $60 or more for developmental editing and 44% for structural editing. For proofreading, 31% charged under $40, 45% charged $40 to $59, 19% charged $60 to $79 and 5% charged $80 or more.

Editors Canada's [rates page](https://editors.ca/hire-an-editor/rates/) gives the pace that turns an hourly rate into a project fee: copy editing a standard text runs 4 to 7 pages an hour, proofreading 6 to 9 pages an hour, and substantive editing 2 to 3 pages an hour.

**Illustrative.** A 60,000-word manuscript is 240 pages at 250 words a page. Copy editing at 4 to 7 pages an hour is 34 to 60 hours; at $50 an hour that is about $1,700 to $3,000. Cover, formatting and ad budgets are in [ebook publishing startup costs](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-startup-costs-canada/).

## How do you calculate an ebook price in Canada?

Work out what you keep per copy at three or four candidate prices, divide your production cost by each, and pick the price whose break-even you can reach inside your category's range.

```text
Store royalty   = rate × (list price − delivery cost)
                  KDP Amazon.ca 70%: delivery = MB × C$0.15
                  Kobo 70% (≥ C$2.99) or 45% (< C$2.99): no delivery
Direct net      = price − platform % − card fee (2.9% + C$0.30 on Stripe)
Blended royalty = Σ (royalty per store × share of sales)
Break-even      = production cost ÷ blended royalty   (round up)
Sanity check    = price within your category's range on Amazon.ca and Kobo
```

Look up 10 comparable books in your exact category on Amazon.ca and Kobo, note price, page count and publication date, and price inside that band. BookNet's $11.85 average is the ceiling reference for traditionally published books, not a target for a debut.

## What do worked ebook pricing examples look like?

**Illustrative.** Prices, file sizes, production costs and the sales split are my assumptions; royalty rates and fees are from the platform pages above. No tax is included in the list prices.

### Example 1: debut romance novel, Amazon.ca and Kobo at C$4.99

- Amazon.ca, 2 MB file: delivery 2 × C$0.15 = C$0.30. Royalty 0.70 × (C$4.99 − C$0.30) = **C$3.28**.
- Kobo: 0.70 × C$4.99 = **C$3.49**.
- Assumed split 60% Amazon, 40% Kobo: 0.60 × C$3.28 + 0.40 × C$3.49 = C$3.36 a copy.
- Production cost C$2,000 (editing plus cover): C$2,000 ÷ C$3.36 = 595.2, so **596 copies** to break even.

### Example 2: practical guide at C$12.99 vs C$14.99

- At C$12.99 on Amazon.ca, 3 MB file: 0.70 × (C$12.99 − C$0.45) = **C$8.78**.
- At C$14.99, above the band, the 35% option applies: 0.35 × C$14.99 = **C$5.25**. Raising the price C$2 costs C$3.53 a copy on Amazon.ca.
- On Kobo, C$14.99 still earns 70%: **C$10.49**. If you want a price above C$12.99, Kobo and direct sales are where it pays.

### Example 3: recipe PDF sold directly at C$24.99

- Payhip free plan: 5% × C$24.99 = C$1.25. Stripe card fee 2.9% × C$24.99 + C$0.30 = C$1.02. Net **C$22.72** a sale.
- If you're registered for GST/HST and the buyer is in Ontario, you add 13% HST: C$24.99 + C$3.25 = C$28.24 charged, and the C$3.25 is remitted to the CRA, not income. (Stripe's percentage fee then applies to the larger total.)
- On Gumroad priced at US$17: fee US$1.70 + US$0.50 = US$2.20, net US$14.80, about **C$21.08** at the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4240 for October 8, 2026. Gumroad collects sales tax as merchant of record.
- A C$24.99 price sits inside the $22 to $29.99 band of the CAD-priced creator ebooks listed above.

### Example 4: series ladder with a first book at C$0.99 vs C$2.99

| Price point (2 MB file) | Amazon.ca royalty | Kobo royalty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C$0.99 (35% / 45%) | 0.35 × C$0.99 = C$0.35 | 0.45 × C$0.99 = C$0.45 |
| C$2.99 (70% / 70%) | 0.70 × (C$2.99 − C$0.30) = C$1.88 | 0.70 × C$2.99 = C$2.09 |
| Box set of 3, C$9.99, 4 MB | 0.70 × (C$9.99 − C$0.60) = C$6.57 | 0.70 × C$9.99 = C$6.99 |

At C$0.99 the first book earns about a fifth of what it earns at C$2.99, so a C$0.99 entry price only works when it reliably sells books 2 and 3 at full price. Use it as a dated promotion rather than a standing price.

## How do you raise ebook prices?

- **End launch prices on a date** you announced, and record royalty per week before and after.
- **Add material, then raise.** A workbook, checklist or bonus chapter justifies a new price on the direct-sale version.
- **Bundle.** BookNet's finding that 77% of Canadian ebook buyers at least sometimes prefer bundles supports a box set or guide-plus-workbook tier.
- **Use the wider Amazon.ca band.** Since July 7, 2026, a long non-fiction title can move from C$9.99 to C$11.99 or C$12.99 and stay on 70%.
- **Run sales instead of permanent cuts.** With 33% of Canadian ebook purchases made on sale or discount in 2025, a dated promotion reaches price-sensitive buyers without lowering your list price.
- **Raise new editions,** not the copy someone just bought. The scripts are in [how to raise your side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/).

## What ebook pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common ebook pricing mistakes in Canada are pricing at C$2.49 instead of C$2.99, pricing above C$12.99 on Amazon.ca without noticing the drop to 35%, leaving launch prices running indefinitely, and ignoring delivery charges on image-heavy files.

The delivery charge matters for recipe and illustrated books: a 20 MB file costs 20 × C$0.15 = C$3.00 a copy on Amazon.ca's 70% option, which is why picture-heavy guides often sell better as direct PDFs (Krista Williamson's is a 59 MB PDF) or on Kobo. Compare Gumroad and Amazon prices only after converting currency and subtracting fees.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect ebook pricing in Canada?

Ebook royalties and direct sales in Canada are self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both [CPP shares](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net income. GST/HST registration follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all your side hustles.

Who collects tax on a sale depends on the platform. Gumroad says that since January 1, 2025 it acts as merchant of record and handles sales tax collection. Payhip collects EU VAT and UK VAT automatically and lets you set up other taxes yourself. The CRA explains platform rules in its [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html), and the [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) covers rates for sales you make yourself. The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers registration, and [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) covers the holdback.

If you sell directly, <AffiliateLink program="gumroad" label="Gumroad" /> is one platform to compare. Royalties and expenses are easier to report with software such as <AffiliateLink program="wealthsimple-tax" label="Wealthsimple Tax" />.

This is not tax, legal, or accounting advice.
