## How much does graphic design freelancing cost to start in Canada?

Graphic design freelancing in Canada costs $0 with a computer you own and free software, $84.72 with a pen tablet and a headset, $276.80 adding Canva Pro and cloud backup, $626.80 with a starting liability policy, or $1,708.05 with a second monitor, a portable drive, Adobe Illustrator and invoicing software. Goods and software include 13% Ontario HST. Insurance is added without HST. Use your province's rate.

## Who should use a graphic design startup budget in Canada?

A graphic design startup budget in Canada fits people who can take a messy brief and return a file a client can use, in English or in French if that is the market. The $0 column fits one flyer for one kind of local business, made on a computer you already own. The lean kit fits someone who draws lettering or icons and has briefs on calls. The standard kit fits two or three regular clients who expect brand kits and a backup. The pro kit fits someone whose clients send Illustrator files and expect them back.

Graphic design freelancing is a poor fit if you need Friday's pay from a sample with no client, if feedback feels like an insult, or if the budget assumes a laptop payment and a stack of apps before anyone has agreed a date.

## What does the Canva licence mean for client work?

Canva's [Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/) says you may transfer a Canva design that contains Free or Pro content to a client for that client's own use if you have a written agreement that the client's use stays within those terms, you remain responsible for the client's compliance, and the design goes to a single client. The same section says this does not let you hand the client Canva content on its own, outside a design. Pro content may not be copied or distributed as a standalone item. Branded content, education content, editorial-use content, and Disney content have tighter limits. Read the current agreement before you export. It can change.

A logo is the hard case. A client usually wants to own and register their mark, so build it from your own shapes and a font whose licence allows logo use, not from a template element.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for graphic design in Canada?

There's no licence to work as a graphic designer in Canada. Two things matter more.

**Insurance.** Zensurance's [freelancer insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/freelancer-insurance) lists graphic designers and illustrators among the freelancers it covers, names a graphic designer as an example of why professional liability matters, and says freelancer insurance generally starts from $350 to $500 a year for professional liability with a $1 million limit. That's an estimate, not a quote, read on October 9, 2026. Ask whether a home policy covers paid client work and equipment. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance or legal advice.

**Business licence.** Search your city for a home-based business licence if you present yourself as a local business. This guide does not state a licence fee.

### A first-client sketch

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of Canadian design rates.** Suppose a café pays an assumed $150 for one menu page with one revision, on a date you wrote down. You already own the computer, design it in Affinity for $0, and buy the H390 headset for the briefing call: $35.00 × 1.13 = $39.55. Suppose the work takes four hours, including the revision.

Fees collected = $150. Cash out = $39.55. Remainder = $150 − $39.55 = $110.45, before a tax set-aside. Hour sketch = $110.45 ÷ 4 = about $27.61. A second menu in a later month does not subtract the headset again: $150 ÷ 4 = $37.50 an hour. If you are registered for GST/HST, tax sits on top of the $150. That tax is not wages. Do not copy $150. If the licence does not let you transfer the elements you used, the $150 is not a completed sale.

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Software you kept:** Canva Pro $142 a year, Illustrator $29.99 a month, FreshBooks from $26 a month, Google One $27.99 a year. Cancel what the last quarter's jobs didn't use.
- **Insurance,** from about $350 a year once you carry it.
- **Stock and fonts.** A file you cannot hand to the client is not a finished job. Read the licence before you price the piece.
- **Unpaid scope.** A second revision round, a logo hunt the client never sent, and "can you also do the website" are costs. Write the cap before you export. Casa Media House publishes $150 per extra refinement round. That's a reference, not a rule.
- **Quiet months.** A subscription billed in a month with no client is still a subscription. The $0 column exists so that month can stay near $0.

## Where can graphic design startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Use Affinity and Canva Free** until a client's file format or brand kits make a paid plan save real time.
- **Use a mouse** for layout work. Buy the tablet when you draw.
- **Skip the second monitor** until several paid pieces exist.
- **Skip Creative Cloud Pro.** One app at $29.99 a month covers most client .ai requests. Twenty apps is a different job.
- **Skip paid brand courses** until you can name what one teaches that the first client didn't.

## Where should graphic design spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't skip the written note on who may use the file, or the licence for every element in it. A pretty flyer you cannot legally hand over is not a cheap start. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office explains [copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright) in plain language. Do not paste someone else's logo and change the colour.

Do not advertise a client you do not have. Label the sample. A fake testimonial is a misrepresentation, not a portfolio. Keep a second copy of a paid file once the work is real. One laptop is a single point of failure, which is why backup sits in the standard kit, not the pro one.

If you email businesses, commercial messages have their own rules. The first-client guide covers that posture. A purchased list is not a budget saving.

## Which tax rules apply to a graphic design budget in Canada?

Graphic design income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. Software subscriptions and a headset used for the work are generally current expenses. A monitor or laptop that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion, not automatically the full receipt. GST/HST for graphic design uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement, the export you were paid for, and the e-transfer. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing, social media, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases, such as the tablet or the software, can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, if you have the documents. If you are not registered, you can't claim it. Confirm the current rules with the CRA or a tax professional.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set any tax you must collect aside.
