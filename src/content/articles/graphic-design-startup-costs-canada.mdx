## How much does graphic design freelancing cost to start in Canada?

Graphic design freelancing in Canada starts at about $0–$40 when the computer and internet already exist, about $25–$120 when a headset for a booked call is the purchase, and about $80–$280 when you add a backup drive and you have asked for a liability quote. The gear ranges are typical Canadian retail prices to verify locally. They are not quotes. A financed laptop is outside all three columns. Software list prices belong on the vendor's checkout, read the day you subscribe.

| How you start | One-time cash, about | What you can deliver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0–$40 | The computer you have, one sample flyer labelled as a sample, and a one-page agreement. A headset only if a call is already booked and you do not own one. |
| Typical | $25–$120 | The headset, and Canva on whatever plan the content you used actually requires. Confirm that price. Still no new computer. |
| Doing it properly | $80–$280 | The typical gear, an external drive so the client's file is not only on one laptop, and a broker's quote for liability if a contract asks for it. A paid brand course waits until you can name what it teaches. |

Canva's [Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/) says you may transfer a Canva design that contains Free or Pro content to a client for that client's own use if you have a written agreement that the client's use stays within those terms, you remain responsible for the client's compliance, and the design goes to a single client. The same section says this does not let you hand the client Canva content on its own, outside a design. Pro content may not be copied or distributed as a standalone item. Branded content, education content, editorial-use content, and Disney content have tighter limits. Read the current agreement before you export. It can change.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for graphic design freelancing in Canada?

Ongoing graphic design costs in Canada are the subscriptions you kept, stock or fonts you were allowed to use, and revision rounds you forgot to cap. Graphic design break-even in the sketch is a method, not a project rate and not a survey of Canadian fees. This guide does not state Canva's price. Put the live checkout in your own arithmetic.

**Subscriptions.** Cancel the ones the sample did not need. A free plan can carry a first flyer if the licence allows the elements you used.

**Stock and Canva elements.** A file you cannot hand to the client is not a finished job. Read the licence before you price the piece. A violation is not a rounding error.

**Unpaid scope.** A second revision round, a logo hunt the client never sent, and "can you also do the website" are costs. Write the cap before you export.

**Quiet months.** A subscription billed in a month with no client is still a subscription. The lean column exists so that month can stay near $0.

### Step 1: Add the one-time gear

One-time gear means the headset, if you bought it, and a backup drive only after a paid file exists.

### Step 2: Add the tools you will still pay for next month

Tools mean the list price on the vendor's page, not a trial. Canva and invoicing software are separate lines.

### Step 3: List the hours, including the rounds you included

Hours are the variable cost. A one-page flyer and a full identity are different jobs. This guide does not publish a national design rate.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of Canadian design rates.**

Suppose the computer is already yours. Suppose a headset at the low end of the $25–$80 line costs $30, and you skip a backup drive. Suppose you do not put a Canva dollar in the sketch, because that figure has to come from Canva's page the day you subscribe. Suppose a café pays an assumed $150 for one menu page, one revision, on a date you wrote down. That $150 is an assumption for the arithmetic, not a market rate. Suppose the work takes four hours, including the revision.

Fees collected = $150. Cash out for gear in this sketch = $30. Remainder = $150 − $30 = $120, before any Canva charge you confirm, before a tax set-aside, and before a marketplace fee this sketch does not invent. Hour sketch = $120 ÷ 4 = $30.

A second menu in a later month does not subtract the headset again. If you are registered for GST/HST, tax may sit on top of the $150. That tax is not wages. Do not copy $150. If the licence does not let you transfer the elements you used, the $150 is not a completed sale.

## Where should graphic design spending not be cut in Canada?

Graphic design spending in Canada should not skip the written note on who may use the file, or the licence for every element in it. A pretty flyer you cannot legally hand over is not a cheap start. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office explains [copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright) in plain language. Do not paste someone else's logo and change the colour.

Do not advertise a client you do not have. Label the sample. A fake testimonial is a misrepresentation, not a portfolio. Keep a second copy of a paid file once the work is real. One laptop is a single point of failure.

## Which tax rules apply to a graphic design budget in Canada?

Graphic design income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. A headset is gear. A laptop that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion, not automatically the full receipt. GST/HST for graphic design uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement, the export you were paid for, and the e-transfer. Report design on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing, social media, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Graphic design is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. This guide does not explain input tax credits. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set any tax you must collect aside.

Copyright and the Canva licence sit beside the invoice. Search your city for a business licence if you present yourself as a local business. This guide does not state a licence fee.
