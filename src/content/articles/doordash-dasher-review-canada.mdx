## What do you need to become a DoorDash Dasher in Canada?

You need to be 18 or older, have a smartphone and a way to get around, consent to a background check, and give DoorDash two pieces of government ID plus a valid Social Insurance Number or Business Number ([DoorDash Canada](https://dasher.doordash.com/en-ca)). Drivers also need a full-class licence, and DoorDash's insurance page says Dashers must keep their own primary auto insurance.

The tax number is not optional. DoorDash's [TIN FAQ](https://help.doordash.com/en-ca/dashers/article/canada-tin-collection-faqs) says Canada's digital platform reporting rules require it to report each Dasher's name, date of birth, address and SIN or Business Number, accounts without a valid TIN can be paused, and the CRA may impose a $500 penalty on a Dasher who doesn't provide one. You can only use a SIN once to sign up.

### Step 1: Check your zone before you sign up

DoorDash runs in many Canadian cities, but what matters is how many restaurants and stores send orders in the zone you will actually work. Open the customer app at the dinner rush in your area and count the restaurants that are open and delivering. A thin zone means long waits between offers, which no pay model fixes.

### Step 2: Choose your vehicle honestly

DoorDash accepts a car, scooter or motorcycle, and a bicycle in select cities. Your cost per kilometre is the biggest number you control (see the take-home model below). A bike or e-bike changes the maths completely; read [bike and e-bike delivery in Canada](/side-hustles/bike-courier-side-hustle-canada/) before you assume you need a car.

### Step 3: Sign up and pass the background check

The [sign-up process](https://help.doordash.com/en-ca/dashers/article/dasher-signup-process) runs through the app: personal details, documents, background check consent, and your payout choice. You can choose "Instantly" (paid after every dash through DoorDash Crimson), "Weekly" direct deposit to your bank, or decide later.

### Step 4: Call your auto insurer

Do this before your first dash, not after a fender-bender. The insurance section below explains why.

### Step 5: Start a mileage log on day one

The CRA wants the date, destination, purpose and kilometres for each business trip, plus your odometer at the start and end of the year ([motor vehicle records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/motor-vehicle-records.html)). The [CRA mileage log guide](/side-hustles/mileage-log-cra-side-hustle-canada/) has a template.

## How does DoorDash pay Dashers in Canada?

DoorDash pays three things: base pay, 100% of customer tips, and promotions ([Dasher pay](https://dasher.doordash.com/en-ca/about/pay)). The amount on the acceptance screen is the minimum guaranteed for that offer, and the final total can be higher if a customer tips after delivery ([How Dasher Pay Works](https://help.doordash.com/en-ca/dashers/article/how-is-dasher-pay-calculated)).

| Pay part | How DoorDash describes it (November 2026) |
| --- | --- |
| Base pay (Earn per Offer) | $2 to $10+, set by estimated time, distance and "desirability" (how other Dashers have treated similar offers, order size, address type) |
| Base pay (Earn by Time) | A minimum guaranteed hourly rate for time spent actively on a delivery; tips may be less frequent in this mode |
| Tips | 100% to the Dasher; DoorDash gives the example of $6 base pay plus a $5 tip = $11 |
| Peak Pay | Extra per offer in busy times, added to the total shown on the offer |
| Challenges | Extra pay for a set number of deliveries in a set time |
| Delivery Streaks | Extra pay for accepting and completing offers in a row while the promotion runs |
| Boosts | Extra pay added to some offers, shown in the offer total |

Two details matter for Canadians. First, "desirability" means unpopular offers (long, slow, apartment drop-offs) carry more base pay; that is DoorDash's own description, so a low base on a short hop is by design. Second, some orders come through a merchant's own website, where the customer is never given a chance to tip, so a zero tip does not always mean the customer chose not to.

### What do provincial pay floors add?

In Ontario, the [Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers) entitles delivery platform workers to at least the general minimum wage for each work assignment or pay period, counted on engaged time and excluding tips. In British Columbia, online platform delivery workers get at least $21.89 per engaged hour plus $0.35 per kilometre from June 1, 2026 ([B.C.](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage)). Waiting between offers is not engaged time under either rule, and the other provinces had no equivalent page when this was written. Our [Uber Eats vs DoorDash vs Skip comparison](/side-hustles/uber-eats-vs-doordash-vs-skip-canada/) has the side-by-side.

## How do DoorDash payouts and Fast Pay work in Canada?

Weekly direct deposit is free: it covers Monday to Sunday, takes two to three days to process, and usually lands by Wednesday night ([How Dasher Pay Works](https://help.doordash.com/en-ca/dashers/article/how-is-dasher-pay-calculated)). Fast Pay lets you cash out once a day for $1.99.

Fast Pay in Canada needs a debit card (not prepaid) from Scotiabank, CIBC, RBC or TD, at least 25 lifetime deliveries, 14 days on the platform, and a 7-day wait after adding or changing your card ([Canada Fast Pay](https://help.doordash.com/en-ca/dashers/article/canada-fast-pay-dasher-support)). Crimson, DoorDash's banking account, offers instant pay after every dash.

**Illustrative example.** If you dash three times a week for 50 weeks and cash out with Fast Pay after each dash: 150 × $1.99 = $298.50 a year in fees. Weekly deposit costs $0. Fast Pay is a cash-flow tool, not a default.

## How much of DoorDash pay do you actually keep?

In this illustrative model, about $13.49 an hour, not the $22.50 an hour the dash appears to pay. DoorDash does not publish average Canadian earnings, so every input below is an assumption you should replace with your own Earnings tab and odometer.

**Assumed inputs (not DoorDash figures):** a 4-hour evening dash earns an assumed $90 in base pay, tips and promotions combined, and you drive an assumed 80 km including the drive to and from your zone. Your vehicle cost (fuel, maintenance, depreciation, insurance top-up) is an assumed $0.25/km. You dash three times a week for 50 weeks.

| Line (illustrative model, not DoorDash figures) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| 150 dashes × $90 | $13,500.00 gross |
| Vehicle cost: 12,000 km × $0.25 | −$3,000.00 |
| Net self-employment income | $10,500.00 |
| CPP: 11.9% × ($10,500 − $3,500 basic exemption) | −$833.00 |
| Tax set-aside: an assumed 15% of net | −$1,575.00 |
| **Take-home** | **$8,092.00** |
| **Per dash hour (600 hours)** | **about $13.49** |
| Screen rate ($13,500 ÷ 600 hours) | $22.50 |

Self-employed Canadians pay both halves of CPP: 11.9% on net self-employment earnings between the $3,500 basic exemption and the 2026 maximum of $74,600 ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/payroll-deductions-contributions/canada-pension-plan-cpp/cpp-contribution-rates-maximums-exemptions.html)). If you also have a job, your T4 contributions count toward the same ceiling; [CPP on side hustle income](/side-hustles/cpp-on-side-hustle-income-canada/) shows how that changes the bill. The 15% set-aside is a placeholder; size yours with [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### What moves the DoorDash number most?

Change one assumption at a time (same method, all illustrative):

| Change | Take-home per dash hour |
| --- | --- |
| Base model above | about $13.49 |
| 50 km per dash instead of 80 (tight zone close to home) | about $14.86 |
| $110 per dash instead of $90 (better tips or Peak Pay) | about $17.14 |
| $0.40/km instead of $0.25 (thirstier SUV or truck) | about $11.29 |

Kilometres and cost per kilometre matter about as much as the offer itself. That is why a minimum-dollars-per-kilometre rule (decided before you look at offers) beats accepting whatever pops up. The same ticket for other gigs is on our [Take-Home Ticket](/side-hustles/take-home-ticket/) page, and [how to work out your real hourly rate](/side-hustles/gig-app-real-hourly-rate-canada/) shows the method in full.

### A simple offer rule you can copy

```text
My DoorDash offer rule (fill in your own numbers)
- My vehicle cost per km: $____ (fuel + maintenance + depreciation ÷ km)
- Minimum I keep per km after vehicle cost: $____
- Accept if: offer total ≥ (km to restaurant + km to customer + km back to zone) × (vehicle cost + minimum)
- Decline: offers that leave my zone at the end of a shift unless they pay the drive home
- Log every dash: start/end odometer, hours online, hours active, earnings
```

## What insurance do DoorDash Dashers need in Canada?

Keep your own primary auto insurance and tell your insurer you deliver. For Canada, DoorDash says it provides **excess** third-party auto liability insurance during the "Delivery Service" period (from accepting an offer until it is delivered, unassigned or cancelled), and only if you are liable and your own auto insurer has already denied the claim in writing ([DoorDash insurance](https://help.doordash.com/en-ca/dashers/article/understanding-auto-insurance-maintained-by-doordash)). Damage to your own vehicle is your responsibility.

In practice that means a personal policy that excludes delivery leaves a real gap. Insurance rules and delivery endorsements vary by province; our [side hustle insurance guide](/side-hustles/side-hustle-insurance-canada/) covers the questions to ask.

## What are the pros and cons of DoorDash for Canadian Dashers?

**Pros**
- Low entry bar: 18+, and bikes and scooters accepted in some cities.
- Tips go 100% to you and never reduce base pay.
- The offer total shown is the guaranteed minimum.
- Free weekly deposit, with Fast Pay and Crimson for faster cash.

**Cons**
- No published hourly or weekly earnings for Canada; base pay can be as low as $2 per offer.
- Vehicle costs, Fast Pay fees and waiting time are on you.
- Canadian auto cover is excess only, after your insurer says no.
- Pay floors exist only in Ontario and B.C., and only for engaged time.

## Who is DoorDash best for in Canada?

DoorDash suits Canadians with a cheap-to-run vehicle (or a bike in a dense city), a busy zone close to home, and the discipline to decline low offers. It is a weaker fit if your car costs $0.40 a kilometre or more to run, if you live far from the restaurant cluster, or if you need guaranteed hours. If you want scheduled blocks instead of on-demand offers, compare [Amazon Flex](/side-hustles/amazon-flex-review-canada/); for grocery orders, see the [Instacart shopper review](/side-hustles/instacart-shopper-review-canada/).

## What mistakes do new DoorDash Dashers make in Canada?

- **Judging offers by the dollar amount alone.** Count the drive back to your zone.
- **Skipping the insurance call.** DoorDash's Canadian cover only kicks in after your insurer denies the claim.
- **Using Fast Pay by habit.** At $1.99 a cash-out, daily use adds up.
- **No mileage log.** Without one, you can't support a vehicle deduction.
- **Not saving for tax and CPP.** DoorDash withholds nothing; set aside a share of every deposit.
- **Ignoring the TIN prompt.** No SIN or Business Number on file can pause your account.

## How is DoorDash income taxed in Canada?

Dashers are self-employed, so DoorDash earnings (base pay, tips and promotions) go on Form T2125, and you pay both halves of CPP on Schedule 8. The business share of vehicle costs is deductible if you keep a [CRA-style logbook](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/motor-vehicle-records.html). See [vehicle expenses for delivery and rideshare drivers](/side-hustles/vehicle-expenses-delivery-rideshare-canada/).

Under the platform reporting rules, DoorDash reports your identity and the amounts paid to you to the CRA, and you should get a copy of what was reported by January 31 each year ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2024/using-app-website-earn-income.html)). Match it against your own records; see [platform slips and reporting](/side-hustles/t4a-t5018-platform-slips-side-hustle-canada/).

Food delivery follows the usual $30,000 GST/HST small-supplier test over four consecutive calendar quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)); ride-sharing has a different rule. If you track receipts on your phone, a free spreadsheet works; paid tools like <AffiliateLink program="quickbooks" label="QuickBooks" /> are optional.
