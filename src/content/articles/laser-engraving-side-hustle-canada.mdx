## How do you run a laser engraver safely at home in Canada?

Run a laser engraver in Canada inside the enclosure the manual requires, with the exhaust running, and with you close enough to stop a fire. The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety page on [lasers](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/lasers.html) is written for health-care settings. It is still the clearest Canadian public explanation of the hazards a beam creates.

CCOHS says there are beam hazards and non-beam hazards. Beam hazards include eye and skin burns. It says the eye is the most vulnerable, and that eye protection is needed when using Class 3B or Class 4 lasers to limit harm from reflected and scattered beams. It says a fire can start when the beam or a reflection strikes combustible material such as rubber, plastic, or paper products. Non-beam hazards include fumes emitted from materials exposed to the beam. It also says to follow the manufacturer's operating procedures for protective equipment.

Use that list at home this way:

- Keep the lid and interlocks working. Do not defeat a switch to "just watch" an open beam.
- Exhaust to the outside, or through the filter the manual specifies, and change that filter on the manual's schedule. A window cracked behind an idle fan is not a substitute the fact sheet describes.
- Read the banned-materials list. Some plastics produce dangerous fumes when a laser hits them. If the manual forbids a plastic, do not test it.
- Stay nearby. Paper and wood burn. A job you leave for an errand is a fire risk CCOHS already names.
- Wear the eye protection the manual specifies for your laser's class and wavelength. CCOHS says alignment glasses for a low-power visible beam are not the protection for a high-power or invisible beam.
- Check with your province or municipality if a home business, a fire rule, or a zoning rule applies. CCOHS says to check your local jurisdiction about which laser standard a workplace must follow. A home shop is not automatically the health-care laser-safety-officer setup in CSA Z386, and it is also not a reason to skip the manual.

## What are you allowed to engrave and sell in Canada?

Engrave words and artwork you have the right to use: a customer's own name, a phone number they give you, or a design you drew. Do not engrave a team logo, a cartoon, or a company mark because a buyer asked.

[CIPO](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/learn-copyright-canadian-intellectual-property-office) describes copyright as the sole right to reproduce a work or a substantial part of it. Section 3 of the [Copyright Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-3.html) states that right in any material form. Tracing a poster onto a sign is a reproduction.

A registered trademark's owner has the exclusive right to use it in Canada for the registered goods, under section 19 of the [Trademarks Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/T-13/section-19.html). Section 20 deems that right infringed by a person who is not entitled to use the mark and who sells or advertises goods in association with a confusing trademark, or who makes labels or packaging bearing that mark for goods that are not the owner's. A sheet of tags with a brand you do not own is that problem. CIPO's [trademark overview](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/learn-trademarks-canadian-intellectual-property-office) is the place to start a search. A customer's request is not a licence. This is not legal advice.

## How do you get the first engraving orders in Canada?

Get engraving orders in Canada from businesses that already buy gifts and labels, and deliver a small batch they can hold.

### Step 1: Choose blanks the manual allows

Wood, slate, and coated metal are common, and only the ones your machine's manual allows. A <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="pack of unfinished wood tags" search="unfinished wood gift tags engraving" /> is a test quantity, not a closet of blanks. Pet tags and asset tags use less material than a large sign and are easier to reorder.

### Step 2: Make five samples with your own lettering

Your name, a fake café name you invented, and a phone number you made up. No sports logo. Photograph them in daylight.

### Step 3: Visit businesses that lose tags and gifts

Property managers, clinics, dog groomers, and shops that give a small gift at a contract signing. Offer a sample and a price for ten, not a speech about lasers. Leave a card.

### Step 4: Quote the batch, the blank, and your time

A one-off marketplace sale has to carry the machine. A batch spreads it. Write the blank cost, the minutes at the machine, and the packing. The sketch below shows why the batch matters.

### Step 5: Stay with the machine, ship or deliver, and ask for the next date

Note when they will need the next set of new-hire badges or seasonal gifts. That date is the business. Holiday markets are an extra channel, with the rules in the [holiday shop guide](/side-hustles/holiday-etsy-craft-market-shop-canada/).

## What does a batch of engraved tags earn against the machine?

A batch of engraved tags in Canada is the order minus the blanks and the packing. The machine is a separate pile of cash you recover over many orders.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical engraving job.**

Suppose a local office orders 10 name plates at $28 each, so $280. Blanks are an assumed $4 each, $40. Packing is an assumed $1 each, $10. Remainder: $280 − $40 − $10 = $230 before your time and before tax.

Suppose the engraver cost $1,800, an assumption you must replace with the price you are actually offered. $1,800 ÷ $230 is about 8 orders of this size before the machine is covered, before your time.

A single marketplace sign at an assumed $35, with a $6 blank, leaves $29 before packing and fees. $1,800 ÷ $29 is about 62 of those sales to cover the same machine. The batch is the shorter path in this sketch. Do not copy $28 or $1,800.

## What mistakes are common with laser engraving in Canada?

Common mistakes are an open beam, a banned plastic, a trademark you do not own, and a machine purchase justified by one hypothetical marketplace hit.

- **Defeating the lid switch.** Reflected beams are the eye hazard CCOHS describes.
- **Engraving a plastic the manual forbids.** Fumes are a listed non-beam hazard.
- **Leaving the room.** Paper and wood are on the fire list.
- **A team logo because the client is a fan.** Get a licence or decline.
- **Buying blanks for a shop you have not visited.** Five samples are enough to start the conversations.

## Which tax rules apply to laser engraving in Canada?

Engraving income in Canada is generally business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income. Blanks and packaging are current costs. An engraver that lasts for years may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Once registered, charge the rate for your province. Questions people mix up are in the [side hustle tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/). Product sales stay on T2125, not the rental form in [T2125 versus T776](/side-hustles/t2125-vs-t776-canada/).
