Making a first reselling sale in Canada means listing an item you already own, with the flaw in the first lines, at a price that still works after the platform fee you looked up. Reselling suits people who can describe wear honestly, answer a message, and meet in public or ship in the same week.

The first sale should come from a closet or a shelf you already have. A pallet, a wholesale lot, and a buying trip are later decisions. The wider start is in [how to start reselling on Canadian marketplaces](/side-hustles/reselling-side-hustle-canada/).

| Fact | Detail for a first resale in Canada |
| --- | --- |
| First inventory | Items you already own, in one category you can judge |
| Local pickup | Facebook Marketplace or Kijiji for heavy or awkward items. This guide does not state a household listing fee |
| Shipped clothing | Poshmark Canada publishes a fee and a prepaid label. [Getting started](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/get_started_on_poshmark) |
| Wider search | eBay.ca publishes insertion and final value fees. [Selling fees](https://www.ebay.ca/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822) |
| Claims | Condition and inclusions must not mislead. [Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) |
| GST/HST | Small-supplier threshold of $30,000. [When to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) |

A checkout you own is a [Shopify store](/side-hustles/shopify-store-side-hustle-canada/). A file with no mailer is a [Notion template](/side-hustles/notion-templates-side-hustle-canada/).

## What should you list for a first resale in Canada?

The first resale in Canada should be one category you can inspect, using items you already own, with measurements and defects written before the story. Clothes in sizes you understand, kids' gear you have used, or tools you can test are enough. Untested electronics can wait until you can explain the flaw.

Pick pickup or shipping for each item before you write the price. Heavy items suit a local meetup. Easy-to-pack clothing can suit Poshmark. A widely searchable item can suit eBay.ca after you read the fee page. Take the other ads down when it sells so you do not owe two buyers the same thing.

Search [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before you list car seats, cribs, helmets, and similar children's products. If you are unsure, do not list the item.

## Where do Canadians find a first resale buyer?

Canadians find a first resale buyer on local pickup listings and, for items that pack easily, on shipped marketplaces whose fees are published. The buyer is a person who wants that object. You do not need a personal brand to make the first sale.

Facebook Marketplace is the usual local board. Pickup is arranged between you and the buyer. Meta's help page on [getting paid for selling with shipping](https://www.facebook.com/help/449101635835192) says some shipping features are not available to all sellers. This guide does not state a Canada-wide Marketplace commission. Open the help page from your own account before you promise shipping.

Kijiji is the other local classifieds path. Read the current rule for your category inside the product. This guide does not state a household-goods listing fee.

Poshmark Canada's [getting started](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/get_started_on_poshmark) guide says that for sales under C$20 the fee is a flat C$3.95, and for sales of C$20 or more the fee is 20% of the listing price. That fee includes payment handling and a prepaid, pre-addressed label. The [shipping guide](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/how_to_ship) and the [label upgrade](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/how_to_get_overweight_shipping_label) page are where weight rules live. Confirm them before you price a heavy coat.

eBay's [selling fees](https://www.ebay.ca/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822) page says most categories get up to 250 zero-insertion listings a month, then $0.30 per listing, and a final value fee of 13.6% on the total amount of the sale up to $7,500, calculated per item, plus a per-order fee of $0.30 on orders of $10.00 or less and $0.40 on orders over $10.00. The total includes the item price, handling, shipping collected from the buyer, and sales tax, with exceptions on that page. Other categories differ. Confirm the live page.

Cold email is the wrong tool for a used jacket. If you do email people about a sale, [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies to commercial electronic messages. ISED's [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page covers consent, your identity in the message, and an unsubscribe path acted on within 10 business days. A listing on a marketplace the buyer chose to browse is a different posture from a purchased address list. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first resale listing say?

A first resale listing in Canada should lead with what the item is, the size or measurement you took, what is included, and the flaw. The story of why you are selling can come after those facts. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) is the standard for those claims.

**Example listing.** This is a generic example. It is not a listing that sold.

> Black rain jacket, women's size measured flat as [chest width]. Zip works. One small mark on the left cuff, shown in photo 3. No hood. Local pickup near [intersection], weekday evenings. I will not hold it without a time we both confirm.

Answer messages with the same facts. Meet in a public place in daylight. Show the flaw again in person.

## How do you prove an item's condition without fake reviews?

You prove a resale item's condition in Canada with daylight photos of the real object, including the wear, and a first line that states the defect. A stock photo, a filtered image that hides a stain, or a review you wrote yourself is not proof.

Photograph the label, the measurement, and the damage. If a piece is missing, say so before the buyer asks. Seller "ratings" you invent, or comments from friends posted as strangers, are misleading.

Your record of the sale is the proof you need later: date, platform, price, fee, shipping, and what you paid. That log is what you will use for [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html).

## How should you price the first resale in Canada?

Price the first resale in Canada from the fee backward: your cost if you bought it, the fee on the official page, the mailer if you ship, and the minimum you will accept. If the likely price cannot clear that sum, do not buy a second one to "make it back."

| Path | Buyer experience | What changes the money you keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local pickup | Meetup in public | Your time and the drive. This guide does not state that Marketplace or Kijiji charged $0 |
| Poshmark, C$20 or more | Prepaid label on that platform | 20% of the listing price, on the getting-started guide, plus any label upgrade |

**Illustrative example.** Suppose you list a jacket you already own on Poshmark at C$40. The getting-started guide says the fee at C$20 or more is 20% of the listing price, so 0.20 × $40 = $8, and you keep $32 before a label upgrade. Suppose you paid nothing because you already owned it, and you buy one mailer only if you are not using Poshmark's label. The sketch of money in is $32 before your time and before any tax set-aside. Suppose the same $40 item sells on eBay.ca and you simplify the fee base to the item price only: 0.136 × $40 = $5.44, plus the $0.40 per-order fee, which is $5.84. eBay's page includes shipping collected and sales tax in the fee base, so a real fee can be higher. These totals are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first resale look like?

A 14-day plan for a first resale in Canada should list items you already own, answer messages with the same facts as the ad, and complete one pickup or one shipment if a buyer appears. A sale is not promised by day 14. Buying inventory in this window is how beginners fill a closet twice.

### Days 1 and 2: Choose ten items

Pick one category. Pull ten things you own. Search recalls if any item is for a child. Decide pickup or ship for each.

### Days 3 to 5: Photograph and read the fee

Photograph in daylight. Measure. Write the flaw in the first lines. Read the eBay.ca fee page and the Poshmark fee section if those are the channels. Do not copy a fee from a video.

### Days 6 to 9: Publish a small set

List three local-pickup items and, if one packs easily, one shipped listing. Share a listing only with people who asked to see it. Do not email a scraped neighbourhood list.

### Days 10 to 14: Complete the handoff or adjust the ad

Answer with the facts already in the listing. Meet in public, or ship at the weight the platform's guide describes. Log the payout. If nothing sells, change the photo or the price on the same items. Do not take a buying trip to escape a quiet ad.

## What mistakes block a first resale in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first resale in Canada are buying stock first, hiding wear, and pricing as if the platform fee were optional. Your own items are the school.

For the product safety, fee, and record mistakes resellers make most, and how to fix them, see the [reselling mistakes guide](/side-hustles/reselling-mistakes-canada/).

Listing the same object in four places and forgetting one ad creates a buyer you cannot supply. Meeting at a private doorway at night is an avoidable risk. A "brand new" line on a used item is the kind of claim the Competition Bureau page is about.

A one-off clear-out of personal belongings is not the same activity as an ongoing shop. Keep the log either way.

## Which tax rules apply to a first resale in Canada?

Reselling income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for reselling follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase notes, payout reports, and supply receipts. A single clear-out is a different picture from ongoing buying and selling. Ask a tax adviser if you are unsure which one you are doing. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Reselling is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.

Platforms that sell to people in Canada can make GST/HST more complicated than a cash pickup. The CRA's [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) describes rules, in effect since 1 July 2021, for certain non-resident suppliers and distribution platform operators. A line on a payout is not your whole registration answer.
