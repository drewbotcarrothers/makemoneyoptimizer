## How do you choose a holiday market in Canada?

Choose by who shops there and what the booth costs, not by size alone. Ask each organiser the same questions so you can compare.

| Question to ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| What is the booth fee, and is there a commission on sales? | Sets your break-even |
| How many days, and what are the hours? | A multi-day market multiplies staffing and stock |
| Indoor or outdoor, with power? | Outdoor tables in Canadian winter limit products and card readers |
| Is the market juried, and how many vendors sell similar items? | Too much overlap splits sales |
| What does the organiser require (insurance, permits, food rules)? | Some markets require proof of liability insurance or food permits |
| Can you see a past vendor list or map? | Shows the mix and foot traffic flow |

## What does it cost to sell at a holiday market?

Your costs are the booth fee, any commission, stock, display, and payment fees. Booth fees vary by market, so the example below uses a placeholder.

- **Card fees:** Square lists 2.5% for in-person card payments, 0.75% plus 7 cents for in-person debit, and 1.5% for instant transfers to your bank on the free plan ([Square pricing](https://squareup.com/ca/en/pricing)). Shopify lists 2.6% for in-person card payments and 15 cents for Interac on its plans ([Shopify pricing](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing)).
- **Display:** a table cover, risers, signage, a price list, and bags. A lockable cash box or float if you accept cash.
- **Weather:** for outdoor markets, plan for cold-weather battery life on phones and card readers.

### Worked example

Assume a one-day market with a $120 booth fee, products selling at $30 with $10 in materials each, and card payments on every sale at Square's listed 2.5%. The booth fee, price and materials are assumptions to replace with your own.

- **Contribution per sale:** $30 − $10 − (2.5% × $30 = $0.75) = $19.25.
- **Break-even:** $120 ÷ $19.25 = 6.2, so 7 sales to cover the booth fee.
- **At 25 sales:** 25 × $19.25 = $481.25, minus $120 = $361.25 before display costs, travel, your time, and tax.

## Which product rules apply at a Canadian holiday market?

The rules depend on what you sell. The common ones for holiday tables are textile labels, light and decoration safety, and food rules.

- **Textiles:** consumer textile articles such as clothing and many household textiles need labels showing fibre content and dealer identity under the [Textile Labelling and Advertising Regulations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/guide-textile-labelling-and-advertising-regulations). The Competition Bureau's guide includes alternatives to commercially printed labels for home-crafted articles.
- **Lights and decorations:** Health Canada advises buying lights with a Canadian certification mark such as CSA, cUL, or cETL, and choosing trimmings made of plastic or non-leaded metals ([holiday safety](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/tips-holiday-safety.html)). If you sell light-up decor, source certified components.
- **Soap, bath bombs, balms and other cosmetics:** Health Canada's [notification page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/cosmetics/notification-cosmetics.html) says every manufacturer must notify it within 10 days after first selling a cosmetic in Canada, and a cosmetic cannot be sold after that 10-day window if the notification is incomplete. The [Cosmetic Notification Form](https://health.canada.ca/apps/radar/CPS-SPC-0007.08.html) names "handmade" cosmetics sold through home-based businesses or craft sales, and each product in a gift basket is notified separately. File before the first market, not after.
- **Candles:** the federal [Candles Regulations](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2016-165/FullText.html) say a candle must not reignite spontaneously after it is extinguished. Health Canada's [candle safety page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/healthy-living/your-health/products/candle-safety.html) says relight candles have been prohibited since 1977, warns against lead-core wicks, and advises trimming wicks to 5–7 mm before lighting, which belongs on your label card.
- **Food:** baked goods and preserves fall under provincial and local rules; see [holiday baking and cottage food rules by province](/side-hustles/holiday-baking-cottage-food-rules-by-province-canada/).

## How do you run the table on market day?

Plan the day like a short retail shift: stock, prices, payments, and a record of each sale.

1. **Price everything visibly.** Round prices make change and card totals faster.
2. **Charge your reader and phone,** and bring a backup battery.
3. **Record sales as they happen,** including cash, so your totals match payouts.
4. **Collect follow-up contacts with consent** if you plan to email buyers later.
5. **Restock by category** so the display looks full near closing.

## What mistakes do holiday market vendors make?

The common mistakes are committing to a booth without a break-even and treating cash sales as off the books.

- **Booking every market.** Multiple weekends of booth fees can outrun sales.
- **No card reader.** Many shoppers expect to tap.
- **Unlabelled scarves or toques.** Textile labels apply to handmade items too.
- **Uncertified light-up products.** Use certified light strings.
- **Not recording cash.** All sales are income.

## Which tax rules apply to holiday market sales in Canada?

Market profits are generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and booth fees, materials, card fees, and display costs are generally deductible. You generally must register for GST/HST once your taxable sales pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters ([when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)); registrants charge the rate for the province where the sale is made ([which rate](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)).
