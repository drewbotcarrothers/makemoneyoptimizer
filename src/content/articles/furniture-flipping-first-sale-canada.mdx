A first furniture flip sale in Canada usually comes from one solid-wood piece bought cheaply, cleaned and repaired rather than rebuilt, photographed in daylight, listed locally with measurements, and handed over at a safe meetup. Keep the first project small enough that a mistake costs a weekend, not a month.

Two safety checks come before paint: old finishes may contain lead, and some furniture is regulated or recalled. ## What should you flip for a first furniture sale in Canada?

Flip a small, sturdy, solid-wood piece for a first furniture sale in Canada: a side table, a nightstand, or a small dresser that fits in a hatchback. Small pieces are easier to carry, store, photograph, and deliver than a sectional or a wardrobe.

Two documented Canadian first flips show the scale: Kaylee Brouwer of Ramara, Ont. painted a $10 coffee table and sold it for $80 ([OrilliaMatters](https://www.orilliamatters.com/local-news/ramara-entrepreneur-turns-old-furniture-into-stylish-home-pieces-11023649), August 4, 2025), and Marcus Faccenda told [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/life/style/article-diy-home-projects/) that his first piece, a roadside dresser, sold for $500 after refinishing. Both started with one piece, not a garage full.

Check joints, drawers, and the back panel before you buy. A wobble you can fix with glue and clamps is fine. Veneer lifting across a whole top, water damage, or pests are reasons to walk away on a first project.

Skip cribs, bassinets, and other children's sleep furniture. Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says cribs and bassinets are regulated and that recalled items that have not been corrected should not be sold. A side table carries far less risk.

## How do you check paint and recalls before a first flip in Canada?

Check paint and recalls before a first flip in Canada by assuming an old painted finish could contain lead, choosing methods that do not create dust or fumes, and searching the item on the government recall site. Both checks happen before you spend money on supplies.

Health Canada's [lead-based paint page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/lead-based-paint.html) says not to use sanders, heat guns, or blowlamps on lead paint. If a piece has an old painted finish you cannot identify, the simplest first-project choice is a piece with a clear finish or bare wood instead.

Search the brand and model on [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en). The second-hand guide says dealers should check recalls and not sell recalled items that have not been corrected. Search even ordinary furniture such as a dresser, and leave any piece whose model matches an uncorrected recall.

## Where do Canadians find a first furniture buyer?

Canadians find a first furniture buyer mainly through local marketplace listings, community buy-and-sell groups that allow sales, and people they know who need a piece. Local pickup avoids freight, which on a large item can cost more than the profit.

List with dimensions in centimetres and inches, the wood if known, and the condition in plain words. Say who loads the piece and whether delivery is offered.

## What should a first furniture listing say?

A first furniture listing in Canada should say the piece, its size, what you repaired or refinished, what flaws remain, and how pickup works. Buyers who see the flaws in the listing do not argue about them at the door.

**Example listing text to copy and adapt:**

> Solid wood nightstand, 45 cm wide × 40 cm deep × 60 cm tall. Cleaned, drawer runners waxed, loose leg re-glued, top oiled. Small dent on the left side, shown in photo 4. Pickup from a public parking lot near [area]; buyer brings help for loading. Cash or e-transfer received before loading.

## How do you show condition without fake reviews?

You show condition on a first furniture flip with daylight photos from every side, close-ups of flaws, a photo of the drawers open, and a before photo if you have one. New sellers have no reviews, so the listing has to carry the trust.

Do not stage a photo that hides a crack or call a veneer piece solid wood. A buyer who feels misled at pickup will walk away, and you have spent the time anyway.

## How should you price the first furniture flip in Canada?

Price the first furniture flip in Canada by checking what similar pieces in similar condition are listed for nearby, then subtracting purchase price and supplies to see whether the work is worth it. A useful yardstick for your hours is Job Bank's [furniture refinisher wage](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/12732/ca): $17.00 low, $22.79 median and $30.00 high per hour in Canada. If a flip pays less than about $17 an hour after supplies, it is practice, not profit.

| Path | What you do | What you watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean and repair | Wash, tighten, re-glue, oil | Lowest supply cost, quickest turnaround |
| Refinish clear-coat piece | Strip or scuff a known clear finish and recoat | Fumes, ventilation, and drying time |
| Paint | Prime and paint a piece with a known safe finish | Old finishes that might contain lead |

### Illustrative example

Suppose you buy a solid-wood nightstand for $40 and spend $25 on cleaner, glue, sandpaper, and oil, part of which carries over to the next piece. Suppose it sells for $150 at local pickup. What is left is $150 − $40 − $25 = $85 before fuel, your time, and tax. If it sells at $110 after a week, the sketch is $110 − $40 − $25 = $45.

In the illustrative example, 4 hours of work on the $85 margin is $21.25 an hour, near the Job Bank median; 6 hours drops it to about $14.

## How do you hand over a first furniture sale safely?

Hand over a first furniture sale safely by meeting in a public, well-lit place when the piece is small enough, telling someone where you are, and confirming payment before loading. Facebook's [meeting-in-person tips](https://www.facebook.com/help/2329750133711372?locale=en_GB) say to meet in a public, well-lit place, share your plan with someone, keep your home address private, and carry minimal cash.

Facebook's [scams page](https://www.facebook.com/help/721562085854101/) says not to trust payment screenshots and to watch for overpayment scams. Check that an e-transfer has landed in your bank app before the piece leaves. If you are targeted by a scam, the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/index-eng.htm) takes reports.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first furniture flip look like?

A 14-day plan for a first furniture flip in Canada should produce one piece bought, checked, repaired, photographed, and listed. Drying and curing times on paint and oil set the pace, so buy on day 3 at the latest.

### Days 1 and 2: Read the safety pages

Read Health Canada's lead paint page and the second-hand products guide. Decide which pieces you will not buy.

### Days 3 to 5: Buy one piece

Look at five listings. Buy one small solid-wood piece. Search its maker on the recall site.

### Days 6 to 9: Clean and repair

Clean, tighten, re-glue, and oil. Avoid methods that make dust from an unknown old finish.

### Days 10 to 14: Photograph, list, and meet

Photograph in daylight, list with measurements and flaws, and meet buyers in public with payment confirmed first.

## What mistakes block a first furniture flip sale in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first furniture flip sale in Canada are buying a piece too large to move, sanding an unknown old finish, and hiding flaws until pickup. Each one costs time or health and can end the sale.

Overpaying because a piece "just needs paint" erases margin. Selling a crib or a recalled dresser shifts a safety problem onto a buyer. Releasing a piece on a payment screenshot invites a scam.

## Which tax rules apply to a first furniture flip sale in Canada?

Furniture flipping income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase receipts or listing screenshots, supply receipts, and sale records. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Flipping furniture is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
