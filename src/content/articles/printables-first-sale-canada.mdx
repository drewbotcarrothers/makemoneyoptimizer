The first sale is one file and one buyer who is not you. A catalogue of thin pages is a later problem. ## What printable should you sell first in Canada?

The first printable to sell in Canada is one page you made, for one job, that you have printed and checked. A CAD budget sheet, a checklist, or a planner page that tells the buyer to confirm their province's school calendar is a product. A famous character with the colours changed is not.

The Canadian Intellectual Property Office explains [copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright). Your own writing and drawings are the safe material. If you used Canva, read the current content licence before you upload. Selling to the public is a different permission from handing one design to one client. This is not legal advice.

Provincial school calendars are not identical. Do not label a file as a Canadian school planner and then copy another country's dates.

## Where do Canadians find a first printable buyer?

Canadians find a first printable buyer among people who already do the job the page serves, and through one shop whose fee they have read. A stranger's scraped inbox is a poor shelf.

Show the page to people who asked for that kind of sheet. Ask them to print it and say where the margin failed. That is a test, not a review you can invent later.

List on one checkout. [Etsy fees](https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/) and [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing) are the pages to read. This guide does not restate a percentage. Those pages change. A second shop can wait until the first payout matches the page you read.

If you email about the file, ISED's [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page says you need consent before commercial emails or texts, and it describes identification and an unsubscribe path acted on within 10 business days. Do not scrape a community that forbids promotion. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first printable listing say?

A first printable listing in Canada should say what the page does, what paper it was tested on, what it does not include, and that results are not guaranteed if the page is about money or school dates.

**Example listing.** This is a generic example. It is not a listing that sold.

> One-page CAD budget sheet. Print on letter paper. I tested the margins on a home printer. You fill in your own numbers. This file does not include tax advice, and it does not promise that you will save money. Sample figures on the page are examples. School dates are not on this file. Refunds follow the sentence in this listing: a digital file that has been downloaded is hard to return, and a file that will not open is replaced.

Write the refund sentence before the first buyer, not after a dispute. Look at the file on a phone and on paper. A layout that looks finished on a screen and clips the totals on letter paper is the refund you can prevent in the same afternoon. If you buy the file yourself as a test, use that download to confirm the link, and do not count that purchase as a customer.

## How do you prove a printable works without fake reviews?

You prove a printable in Canada by printing it and showing that print, or by describing the page without a mockup you do not own. A star rating you wrote and a buyer count you guessed are not proof.

Photograph your own test print. If a person prints it and agrees you may say they tried the page, say that, and do not upgrade "tried the page" into "paid off their debt." Circle nothing that contains a real account number.

## How should you price the first printable in Canada?

Price the first printable in Canada as one number for one file, after you have subtracted the shop fee from the live page in your own notes. This guide does not set a market price and does not restate the fee. A low price with a file that does not print costs more in refunds than a higher price on a page you tested.

| Path | What the buyer gets | What you watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One PDF | The file, tested on letter paper | The fee on that shop's page, and refunds |
| A pack of twenty | A later product | Twenty untested files |

The live shop fee, refunds when the PDF clips, and hours you already spent drawing are the pricing factors. A second shop on day one means two fee pages to misread. If the page mentions school, point the buyer at their province's published calendar instead of pasting one set of dates and calling it Canadian. One tested file teaches you more than a folder of pages nobody has printed.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose you price the file at an assumed $8. That $8 is an assumption for the arithmetic, not a market rate. The shop fee is whatever the live page says, so this sketch does not invent a percentage. Suppose one buyer downloads it and does not refund. The cash before the fee is $8. Paper you already used to test the file is a startup cost, not a second subtraction on every sale, unless you are still reprinting for yourself. These totals are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first printable sale look like?

A 14-day plan for a first printable sale in Canada should produce one tested PDF, one listing, and a few asks to people who need that page. A sale in this window is not guaranteed. A second file waits until a stranger has downloaded the first.

### Days 1 and 2: Name the page

Write who prints it and what they fill in. Remove anything you do not have the right to sell. Decide the refund sentence.

### Days 3 to 5: Print it

Use letter paper. Fix the margin. Read the shop fee page and the Canva licence if you used Canva. Do not open a second shop.

### Days 6 to 9: List it and tell the right people

Publish one listing. Tell people who asked for this kind of page, or post where the community allows it. If you email, follow ISED's consent page. Do not invent a launch count.

### Days 10 to 14: Fix the file or leave the price alone

If someone buys, note which step confused them. If nobody buys, improve the page once and show it again. Do not cut the price in public every day.

## What mistakes block a first printable sale in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first printable sale in Canada are uploading a character you do not own, skipping the test print, and ignoring the shop fee until the payout looks small. One clean page is the business.

GI-065, dated August 2014, lists agendas and electronic and digital books among goods that do not qualify for the point-of-sale book rebate it describes. The rate table in that sheet is not today's HST table. Do not tell buyers the file is tax-free.

Fake reviews and borrowed mockups are how a shop gets a complaint. Ads for a file you have not printed multiply refunds.

## Which tax rules apply to a first printable sale in Canada?

Printable sales are self-employment income, including a shop payout, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table. The CRA's [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) is the further reading when a platform sits between you and the buyer.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

One small file does not by itself cross the small-supplier line. Add other side hustles to the same pot. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST. Once registered, confirm the rate with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Quebec charges GST and QST. Do not apply a printed-book story to a download. [GI-065](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-065/point-sale-rebate-on-books.html) is the rebate sheet, and its 2014 rates are not current.
