Charging for handyman work in Canada usually means an hourly rate with a minimum call-out, plus flat prices for common tasks like mounting a TV or assembling furniture, with materials billed separately. The public benchmark is Job Bank's handyman wage data: $16.55 (low), $25.00 (median), and $38.46 (high) an hour nationally for employees. A self-employed handyman prices above that because travel, quoting, tools, and both halves of CPP come out of the same rate. This guide suits someone who will stay inside small repairs and leave licensed trade work to the trades.

## What do handymen earn per hour in Canada?

Employed handymen in Canada earn a median of $25.00 an hour, according to [Job Bank's handyman/woman wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/14393/ca) (NOC 73201, reference period 2023–2024, updated November 19, 2025). Those are employee wages, often for building maintenance roles. They are a floor for a self-employed rate, not a price list.

| Area (Job Bank, handyman/woman) | Low | Median | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada | $16.55 | $25.00 | $38.46 |
| Ontario | $17.95 | $24.00 | $39.00 |
| British Columbia | $21.00 | $29.00 | $45.00 |
| Alberta | $23.00 | $28.00 | $42.25 |
| Quebec | $17.00 | $25.00 | $36.25 |

The provincial spread is wide. An Alberta or British Columbia median is several dollars above Ontario's, so read your own row. This guide does not publish a national handyman call-out fee because no public source we could verify collects one.

## Which pricing models work for handyman work in Canada?

Handyman work in Canada is usually priced hourly with a one- or two-hour minimum, as flat task prices for repeatable jobs, or as a half-day or full-day block for a list of small repairs. Materials are billed at cost with receipts or with a disclosed handling fee.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hourly with minimum | Rate × time, minimum 1–2 hours | Unknown scope, older homes | Clients who feel watched by the clock |
| Flat task price | One price per task (TV mount, shelf, door adjustment) | Repeatable jobs you have timed | Hidden problems: write "standard install" |
| Half-day / full-day block | Fixed price for a set number of hours on a written list | "Honey-do" lists | Lists that grow once you arrive |
| Materials | At cost with receipt, or cost plus a stated handling fee | Every job with parts | Fronting money for expensive parts |

Flat prices only work for tasks you have done enough times to know the range. The first few times, quote hourly with a written estimate.

## How do you calculate a handyman rate in Canada?

Calculate a handyman rate in Canada from a target hourly pay, the share of hours lost to quoting and driving, tool wear and consumables per job, and a minimum that covers the trip. Then compare with local listings.

### Step 1: Pick a target hourly pay

Read your province's row in the Job Bank table. Your take-home per working hour should not land under the employee low.

### Step 2: Count unpaid time

Quotes, supply runs, driving, and invoicing are not billable. Log a few weeks of real days to find your own billable share instead of guessing it.

### Step 3: Set a minimum charge

A minimum covers the drive, setup, and cleanup on a 20-minute job. Without it, small jobs lose money.

### Step 4: Add consumables and tool wear

Screws, anchors, caulk, blades, and bits. Larger tools are startup costs, recovered over many jobs.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both shares of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). The [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) covers the habit.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $32 an hour, between Job Bank's national median of $25.00 and high of $38.46. Suppose 70% of your working time is billable. The billable rate is $32 ÷ 0.70 = $45.71 an hour, which you might round to $46.

A TV mount on drywall with a stud mount, timed at 1.5 hours, is 1.5 × $46 = $69, plus an assumed $5 of anchors and screws: $74, rounded to $75 before tax. A one-hour minimum is $46 plus the trip. A four-hour half-day block is 4 × $46 = $184, which you might round to $185, with parts billed at cost.

Check: on the half-day, $185 ÷ 4 hours is $46.25 billable, or about $32.38 per working hour once the unpaid 30% is counted. Replace each number with your own timing.

## When should you raise handyman prices in Canada?

Raise handyman prices in Canada when you are booked out more than a week, when your timings show flat prices running long, or when fuel and consumables rise. Change new-client prices first and give regular clients notice before their next booking.

Raise flat task prices individually. If TV mounts always take two hours, the flat price was set for 1.5 hours and needs to move. Keep a log of actual time per task type for the first 20 jobs.

## What handyman pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common handyman pricing mistakes in Canada are skipping a minimum, quoting flat on unseen jobs, and fronting materials without a deposit. Each one turns small jobs into unpaid ones.

Ask for photos before quoting. Write "standard install" and what changes the price (no stud, tile, plaster). Take a deposit when parts cost more than you can lose. Do not quote work that needs a licence just because the client asks; the price does not matter if the work is not yours to do.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect handyman prices in Canada?

Handyman income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, counting every taxable side hustle you run.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work is done, from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html), on labour and on materials you resell. The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) explains voluntary registration and input tax credits on tools and supplies.

Materials at cost, deposits, and a half-day list are easier to track in an invoicing tool. <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can hold estimates, convert them to invoices, and attach receipts. A notebook works for the first few jobs.
