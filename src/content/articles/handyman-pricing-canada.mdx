HomeStars' national guide puts the typical rate at $85 to $110 an hour. On TaskRabbit, where many side hustlers start, city averages run $27 to $40 an hour and the average job comes to $64 to $107. Price a side hustle between the two: above the app averages once you have reviews, below the insured companies, with a minimum charge and flat prices for common tasks.

## Which pricing models work for handyman work?

Use three models together: an hourly rate with a minimum for unclear jobs, flat prices for repeat tasks, and half-day or full-day blocks for to-do lists. Materials are always a separate line.

| Model | Use it for | Published example | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hourly + minimum | Repairs where you can't see the problem yet | Vancouver Handyman Services: $100/hour, 2-hour minimum | Clients fear an open-ended bill, so give a time estimate |
| Flat task price | TV mounts, assembly, faucets, door hardware | HomeStars: faucet $140; aMaximum: TV mount $150 to $250 | Time each task twice before setting the price |
| Half-day or full-day block | A list of small jobs | aMaximum recommends booking half or full days for multiple tasks | Write the list down so it doesn't grow on the day |
| Materials | Everything you buy | Billed at cost with receipts, or cost plus a handling fee | Your time shopping is billable |

## How do you calculate a handyman rate?

Work out your rate from what you want to earn per billable hour, plus your costs, divided by the share of your time you can actually bill. Then set a minimum that covers travel, and check the result against the company and app rates above.

```text
Hourly rate    = (target pay per hour + costs per billable hour) ÷ share of hours billable
Minimum charge = rate × 1.5 to 2 hours (covers travel, setup, parking)
Flat task      = timed minutes ÷ 60 × rate, rounded up to the nearest $5 or $10
Quote          = max(minimum charge, hours × rate) + materials at cost
```

**Illustrative rate build.** The inputs are assumptions you replace with your own:

| Input | Assumed value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target pay | $40/hour | About 1.6 times the $25.00 Job Bank median |
| CPP on self-employment | 11.9% × $40 = $4.76 | 2026 self-employed rate |
| Tools, vehicle, insurance, phone | $8 per billable hour | Your own costs spread over billable hours |
| Share of hours billable | 75% | Quoting, driving and shopping are unpaid |
| **Rate** | ($40 + $4.76 + $8) ÷ 0.75 = **$70.35, round to $70** | |

$70 is below HomeStars' $85 to $110 range and above TaskRabbit's city averages, which suits an insured side hustler with a few reviews. With a 1.5-hour minimum, the call-out charge is 1.5 × $70 = $105.

## What does a worked handyman quote look like?

**Illustrative.** These quotes use the $70 rate above and assumed task times. The market comparisons are the published prices.

### Quote 1: mount a 55-inch TV on drywall

- Time: 60 minutes on site plus 15 minutes travel = 1.25 hours. 1.25 × $70 = $87.50, below the $105 minimum.
- Market check: aMaximum lists $150 to $250 in Toronto.
- **Quote: $130 flat, client supplies the mount.** Add $20 to hide cables in a wall kit the client buys.

### Quote 2: Saturday half-day list

- Hang three shelves (2 hours, the low end of HomeStars' 2–3 hours), replace a door handle (0.5 hours), re-caulk a tub (1 hour), plus 0.5 hours of setup and cleanup = 4 hours.
- Labour: 4 × $70 = $280. Materials at cost with receipts: shelf anchors, handle and caulk, about $65 on the client's receipt (assumed).
- Market check: TaskRabbit's estimates for the same three tasks add up to $80 + $100 + $100 = $280 at the low end and $180 + $250 + $200 = $630 at the high end.
- **Quote: $280 labour + materials at cost = $345 before tax.** A half-day block price of $280 for up to 4 hours is easier to sell than an open hourly rate.

### Quote 3: swap a bathroom faucet

- Time: 75 minutes plus 15 minutes travel = 1.5 hours = $105, the minimum.
- Market check: HomeStars' average is $140.
- **Quote: $120 flat, client buys the faucet.** Check that the shut-off valves work before you quote; seized valves are a separate line.

## What should a handyman price sheet look like?

A one-page sheet with an hourly rate, a minimum, half-day and full-day blocks and 8 to 12 flat-price tasks lets clients book without a phone call. **Illustrative** prices built on the $70 rate:

```text

Hourly: $70 · Minimum visit: $105 (covers the first 1.5 hours)
Half day (up to 4 h): $280 · Full day (up to 8 h): $540

Blinds, per window ..................... $50 (min. $105)
```

Every flat price sits under the published comparisons: aMaximum's $150 to $250 TV mount, TaskRabbit's $80 to $180 shelves and $100 to $200 caulking, and HomeStars' $140 faucet.

## How should you write a handyman quote and invoice?

Write the scope, the time estimate, the price model and what's excluded before you start. In Ontario, the province says a final price can't be more than 10% above a written estimate without the client's agreement ([Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-when-signing-or-cancelling-contract)).

```text
QUOTE #2026-031   Date: [date]   Valid 30 days
Client: [name, address]
Scope: hang 3 floating shelves (client supplies), replace front door handle
       (client supplies), remove and re-caulk tub surround
Estimate: 4 hours · Price: $280 half-day block
Materials: at cost with receipts (est. $65)
Not included: drywall repair behind old caulk if water damage is found
              (will stop and quote separately)
Total estimate: $345 + GST/HST if registered
Payment: on completion, e-Transfer to [email]
```

```text
INVOICE #2026-031   Date: [date]
From: [name, address, GST/HST # if registered]
Half-day block (4 h) .......................... $280.00
Materials (receipts attached) ................. $64.37
Subtotal ...................................... $344.37
HST 13% (registered only) ..................... $44.77
Total ......................................... $389.14
```

Estimates, receipts and deposits are easier to track in an invoicing tool once you have regular clients. <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can hold estimates, turn them into invoices and attach receipts. ## How do you raise handyman prices?

Raise prices when you're booked two weeks out, when you add insurance or a licence, or once a year. aMaximum says Toronto handyman rates rose 15% to 20% over the past three years, so a yearly increase of 5% to 7% keeps pace with that market.

1. **Raise the minimum first.** Moving from $105 to $120 changes small jobs without touching your half-day price.
2. **Move flat prices toward the published range.** If your $130 TV mount books every week, $150 still sits at the bottom of aMaximum's range.
3. **Add a priority fee.** Same-week or evening visits at 1.25 times your rate.
4. **Give regulars notice.** Send a short note 30 days before new rates start, and honour quotes already given.

The [guide to raising side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/) has more scripts. If you want to grow without raising rates, [adding a second service](/side-hustles/add-second-service-side-hustle-canada/) such as seasonal work is another route.

## What handyman pricing mistakes are common?

- **No minimum charge.** A 20-minute job across town loses money. Every company above uses a two-hour minimum or a minimum fee.
- **Pricing like an app forever.** TaskRabbit's $27 to $40 averages are a starting point, not a business rate once you're insured.
- **Free shopping trips.** Time spent buying materials is billable.
- **Open-ended hourly quotes.** Give an estimate in hours and say when you'll stop and re-quote.
- **Taking licensed work.** Electrical, gas and many plumbing jobs aren't side-hustle work in most provinces.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect handyman prices?

Handyman income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net earnings outside Quebec. You must register for GST/HST once your worldwide taxable supplies, from all your businesses, pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). After that, add the rate for the province where you do the work ([CRA rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)): a $280 half-day becomes $316.40 with Ontario's 13% HST or $294 with Alberta's 5% GST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
