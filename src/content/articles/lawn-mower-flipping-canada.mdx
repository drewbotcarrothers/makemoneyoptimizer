## When does lawn mower flipping work in Canada?

Lawn mower flipping in Canada works from the first warm weekends through early summer, when a buyer wants a machine that starts. Late fall and winter are when sellers give up on a mower they will not store. That is the buying window, and it is also the season you wait through.

A mower that will not fit in a shed, a balcony, or a corner of a garage you can vent is a pass in November. You will look at it until April. If you already run a spring lawn route, a spare mower can be a tool for that work and a sale later. Read the [lawn care startup costs](/side-hustles/lawn-care-startup-costs-canada/) before you mix the two.

Small engines in the same pattern include snow blowers, which sell on the opposite calendar, and trimmers. This guide stays with mowers because the demand is the spring grass season. Do not buy a riding mower you cannot move, register, or store. A push mower is the first test.

## How do you flip a lawn mower safely in Canada?

Flip a lawn mower in Canada by testing it outdoors, pricing parts before you buy a dead engine, and selling it with an honest start-up note.

### Step 1: See it start outdoors, or price it as a project

Ask the seller to start it outside, on flat ground, with the guards on. A video from last summer is not a start. If it will not start, you are buying a repair. Walk away when you cannot name the parts.

The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety says carbon monoxide comes from gasoline and other fuels that do not burn completely, and that internal combustion engines are a common workplace source. Its precautions say to avoid operating fuel-powered machinery indoors where possible, and: do not use gas-powered engines in an enclosed space. A garage with the door cracked is still a bad test. Take the machine outside.

### Step 2: Check the deck, the blade, and the bag

A bent deck, a welded frame, or a missing guard is a safety problem you would be passing on. Look under the deck before you pay. Check the brand and model on the [recalls site](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en).

### Step 3: Price a carburetor kit only after you know the model

Many non-starting mowers have stale fuel. A carburetor kit, a spark plug, fresh fuel, and the right oil are the usual small-engine list, and the prices belong on a receipt from a parts counter. Compare a Canadian hardware shelf, such as Canadian Tire, with an online listing so you do not pay shipping on one plug. A <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="small-engine tool kit" search="small engine carburetor screwdriver spark plug wrench" /> covers the screwdrivers and a plug wrench you will reuse. Buy the carburetor kit that matches the model number, not a random kit because it was cheap.

### Step 4: Drain old fuel and deal with used oil on purpose

Stale gasoline is a disposal item. Used oil from a small engine is too. Provinces and municipalities run collection for household hazardous waste, and the rules are local. Search your city's depot before you drain anything. Do not pour fuel or oil down a drain, into a storm sewer, onto soil, or into the household garbage.

### Step 5: Know which emissions rules are about new engines

The [Off-Road Small Spark-Ignition Engine Emission Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-355/FullText.html) (SOR/2003-355) say their purpose is to reduce hydrocarbons, oxides of nitrogen, and carbon monoxide from engines, to reduce listed toxic substances through hydrocarbon limits, and to align standards with those of the US Environmental Protection Agency. They apply to prescribed engines of the 2005 and later model years. They are written for the companies that manufacture and import those engines. Selling a used mower that is already in Canada is not the same job as importing a new engine. If you are bringing engines in from outside Canada, read the regulations before the shipment, not after.

### Step 6: List it in spring with a start video from that week

Say whether it starts cold, whether the self-propel works, and what you replaced. A buyer in April will ask. Store it so the deck is dry and the fuel is fresh or drained, depending on how long it will sit. Confirm any municipal second-hand dealer licence if you start operating as a shop. Cities differ, and this guide does not state a licence fee.

### Step 7: Scrap the one that will not come back

A mower that still will not start after the plug and carb work is metal and a fluid problem. [Junk removal](/side-hustles/junk-removal-side-hustle-canada/) is the honest end for a deck you should not sell as "needs a tune."

## What mistakes are common when flipping mowers in Canada?

Common mower-flipping mistakes in Canada are testing indoors, buying a riding mower you cannot move, and listing a project as "runs great" because it ran once in October.

- **A garage start.** Carbon monoxide is the reason the outdoor rule exists. CCOHS says not to use a gas-powered engine in an enclosed space.
- **A November purchase you need as December income.** Spring is the sale. Winter is storage.
- **The wrong carburetor kit.** Match the model number stamped on the engine.
- **Skipping the recall check.** A cheap mower with an open recall is a pass.
- **Dumping the old gas.** Use the municipal depot. Confirm it.

## Which tax rules apply to lawn mower flipping in Canada?

Lawn mower flipping in Canada is generally business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) when you buy machines to resell. Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income. Parts, fuel, and oil used on a flip are ordinary costs to record. A mower you keep as a tool for a lawn route may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. Selling the machine on is a different record from depreciating it. If you are unsure which one you are doing, ask a tax adviser.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Mower sales are not rides. Once you are registered, charge the rate for your province from the CRA's charge-and-collect table.
