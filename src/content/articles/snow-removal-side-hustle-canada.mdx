Snow removal in Canada is a seasonal side hustle: clearing driveways, walks, and sidewalks for nearby clients, usually before the workday, where snow is a repeating event. Snow removal suits people who can get up early, stay steady on ice, store a shovel and salt, and reach those clients with a vehicle that starts in the cold.

The money, when snow removal pays, comes at 5 a.m., in the dark, on ice, with a city plough burying the end of the driveway an hour after the clear is finished. People who do snow removal well sell a defined service to neighbours they can reach before work. People who get hurt sell "I'll just bring a shovel" with no insurance and no limit on how often they come.

If you already cut grass for the same houses, the summer half is [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada) and the gate and stair repairs after the melt are [handyman work](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada). If predawn physical work is the wrong winter plan, [house cleaning](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-side-hustle-canada) does not depend on whether the sky falls.

## Who does snow removal in Canada suit?

Snow removal in Canada suits people who can get up early, stay steady on their feet, store salt and a shovel, and live where snow is a repeating event rather than a rumour. A vehicle that starts in the cold, a back that can shovel a trial route before a machine purchase, and lawn clients who already trust the operator make a snow removal route more practical. Snow removal is a poor fit for someone with a cardiac history or a back injury, for a mild coastal city where a financed two-stage blower might only be used twice, for a day job that cannot move on storm mornings, for someone with nowhere legal to store fuel, or for someone who wants passive income.

Most of the value is a clear walk before the commute, which means working while the household is still asleep. If that schedule is unacceptable in October, the schedule will not improve at minus 20.

## Where in Canada is snow removal a real season?

Snow removal in Canada is a full season in some regions and a rare favour in others, because Canada does not have one winter. Any sidewalk deadline for snow removal is municipal, so the deadline and the rules for where snow may go come from that city's winter page.

- **Prairies, Ottawa, Montreal, Quebec City, and much of Atlantic Canada:** snow is a season. A route can be a real side income if the price is a season price. Storms stack. A plan is needed for the week the snow never stops.
- **Southern Ontario, away from the snowbelts:** variable. Some winters are a handful of events. Some are relentless. Seasonal contracts need a trigger depth and a visit cap, or a mild year feels overcharged and a harsh year feels ruinous. The snowbelts around the Great Lakes are a different climate from downtown Toronto. Price the street you are actually on.
- **British Columbia:** do not finance a blower on a mild coastal winter. The Fraser Valley, the interior, and the north still get real snow. A Surrey or Kelowna route is not a Kitsilano route.
- **The North:** fewer clients, harder cold, and machines that must start at temperatures southern manuals treat as a footnote.

Read the city's winter page before promising the sidewalk. Snow removal helps an owner meet a duty only when that page says the owner must clear. Do not push snow into the road, onto a hydrant, a bus stop, or a neighbour's cleared walk. The same page is where to see whether the city clears the sidewalk itself.

City ploughs create windrows, the ridge at the end of the driveway. Clients feel that ridge as the operator's problem even when the plough came after the clear. The agreement should say whether a return visit for the windrow is included, extra, or not part of the job.

## What should a snow removal agreement in Canada include?

A snow removal agreement in Canada should sell one defined product: per visit, a seasonal contract, or a hybrid with a visit cap, and the note should list surfaces, trigger depth, timing, salt, and price. A vague "call me when it snows" is not that product.

**Per visit.** Simple. You come when the client calls or when accumulation hits a number you both use. Income dies in a mild winter. You are also easy to forget at 5 a.m. because the client has not called yet.

**Seasonal contract.** A set price for the season, with a trigger depth (for example, you clear when settled snow reaches a stated depth), a daily time window, a list of surfaces, and a cap or a fair-use note so a once-in-a-decade winter does not mean unlimited free visits. This is the product that lets a client budget and lets you plan a route. The product needs writing.

**Hybrid.** A seasonal price for a defined number of visits, then a per-visit rate after that. Easier to explain than unlimited service. Useful in variable southern Ontario winters.

Write down, in plain language:

- The address and what is included: driveway, front walk, steps, city sidewalk, windrow
- Trigger depth and who measures it
- The time you will be done by, and what happens if a storm is still falling at that hour
- Start and end dates of the season
- Price, when it is due, and the extra-visit rate
- Salt or traction product: included, extra, or never, and whether the client wants a pet-safer product
- What you will not do: roofs, unless you are equipped and insured for them (most side hustlers should leave roofs alone), city streets, or the neighbour's half "as a favour" with no price
- How either of you can cancel
- A line that this note is your working agreement, not a lawyer's document

Roofs and ice dams stay off the menu. They injure people, and a casual policy will not treat them as a shovel job. Two centimetres and twelve centimetres are different jobs. Pick one written trigger, for example somewhere in the 2 to 5 centimetre range, and use that same depth on every driveway so the morning decision is fast. That range is an example to write down, not a survey of what operators charge.

## How do you start snow removal in Canada?

Starting snow removal in Canada means walking a route in daylight, reading the city's winter page, getting an insurance quote, and writing the offer before the first storm. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Decide the route shape in daylight

Walk the blocks you could clear before your other life starts. Time a pretend loop with an empty shovel. Note hills, gravel driveways, long townhouse walks, and hydrants. Gravel and a single-stage blower are a poor marriage. Stones go through windows.

### Step 2: Read the sidewalk rule and the dumping rule

Quote only what your city's winter page allows. The deadline, if the city sets one, is municipal. If the contract includes the sidewalk, the contract includes the sidewalk on the ugly days too.

### Step 3: Get an insurance quote with the real work described

Say residential driveways and walks, whether you use a blower, whether you apply ice melt, and that you do not clear roofs. Ask about slip-and-fall. Ask whether a home policy covers this business activity before you rely on that policy. A seasonal cheque without liability coverage is a bet against someone's broken wrist. This is not insurance advice. Ask a broker while they are still answering October calls.

### Step 4: Write the offer before the snow flies

One page: trigger depth, surfaces, time window, season dates, price, windrow rule, salt rule, extra-visit rate, payment dates. Use centimetres, not "a good dump."

### Step 5: Price three sample driveways on your street

Price a single-car driveway, a double, and the corner lot with steps. Add salt and the walk back. If you are far cheaper than a local operator, you missed something. A full route of underpriced driveways is how people quit in February.

### Step 6: Sign clients in the fall

October and early November are when responsible owners buy peace of mind. A polite door knock, a note, or your existing lawn clients are enough. Collect a deposit or the first instalment when they agree, and put the money in the separate account. Confirm the trigger by text so there is a record.

### Step 7: Stage the gear before the first forecast

Shovel by the door, lamp charged, salt stored so the bag does not turn into a brick, blower tested on a dry day, shear pins in the glove box. The first storm is when everyone else is buying the shovel you already own.

### Step 8: Run the first real storm as a drill

Start at the far end so you finish near home, unless a hill says otherwise. Clear to the trigger you promised, not to perfection on house one and a rush on the last. Text when you are done if you said you would. Do not block the neighbour.

### Step 9: Hold a mid-season check

After three events, look at hours, salt used, and whether the windrow rule is fair. Adjust extras for new clients. Honour the written deal for current ones. If a property is unsafe, such as black ice you cannot manage, an aggressive dog, or a driveway you cannot exit, cancel that one contract politely. A route you dread is how injuries happen.

## What should the first week of snow removal in Canada include?

The first week of snow removal in Canada should happen before the snow, in late September through November in much of the country: a walked route, an insurance quote, a one-page agreement, and one or two signed clients.

- **Day 1:** Walk a route and note ten properties. Read the city's winter page for sidewalks and where snow may be placed.
- **Day 2:** Insurance quote. If you cannot get coverage you understand, do not take seasonal money.
- **Day 3:** Write the one-page agreement. Pick the trigger depth and the done-by time.
- **Day 4:** Price three driveway types. Buy a shovel, a lamp, and one bag of ice melt. Test your boots on a slick surface.
- **Day 5:** Talk to five households, starting with people who already know you.
- **Day 6:** Follow up. Take one or two clients. Deposit any money in the separate account.
- **Day 7:** Stage the gear, order the route in your phone, and name a backup if you get sick.

## What is easy to miss on a snow removal route in Canada?

Snow removal in Canada goes wrong when salt replaces shovelling, when driveway edges are unmarked, and when windrow visits were never written down. Clear first, then use a thin, even melt where ice remains.

Piling salt on a surface you did not scrape wastes money and burns concrete and paws. Pet-safer products still are not candy. Tell clients if you use them, and follow the bag rate.

Mark edges in the fall. Stakes at the driveway corners and along garden beds save the auger from an ornamental rock. Clients who garden will thank you in May. Offer to place the stakes when you sign them.

Do the windrow return you wrote down, and do not add free ones all winter. Shear pins are supposed to break. Turn the machine off and pull the key before you clear a clog. Tell someone your route and when you should be home. Put a light-winter sentence in the October agreement: retainer, visit cap, or a small credit. Do not invent the refund in April.

## What snow removal mistakes are common in Canada?

Common snow removal mistakes in Canada are buying the two-stage blower before the clients, selling unlimited clears with no trigger depth, and pushing snow where the city's winter page does not allow it. Storage and a loan payment do not care that the route is still a theory, and a mild year will not cover that payment.

Unlimited clears with no trigger depth mean you are out for flurries, or you argue about flurries.

Pushing snow into the street, onto a hydrant, or onto the neighbour is a placement problem. Stack snow on the client's property, where the city allows.

No liability policy, plus more houses than you can finish before 7:30 a.m., means the last house gets the worst job and the first cancellation. Grow next storm, not this one.

## Which tax rules apply to snow removal in Canada?

Snow removal income in Canada is self-employment income, including a seasonal lump sum, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for snow removal follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

A deposit in November is still income to track. Keep the agreements, the e-transfers, and the receipts for salt, fuel, and repairs. Report snow removal on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add lawn, cleaning, and handyman revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Snow removal is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work happens. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.

If you use a vehicle to haul equipment, keep a kilometre log for any vehicle claim. Do not claim the family car as 100 percent business because you drove to a driveway. Your contract does not erase negligence. Clear what you promised and carry liability insurance that matches the work. Search your city for a business licence and use that city's fee if there is one. This guide does not state a licence fee from another city. If you pay a helper, check that province's workers' compensation rules before you call the person a favour. Store fuel the way the can and your municipality describe, not on a balcony.
