A snow removal side hustle in Canada means clearing driveways, walkways and steps for nearby homeowners on a written trigger (usually 5 cm), mostly before the morning commute, from November to April. Published 2025–26 seasonal prices for a single driveway run from about $316 to $649 in Ottawa, and Calgary plans cost $179 to $255 a month. You can start with a $33 to $78 shovel, so it suits early risers who live where snow keeps coming back and who can sign neighbours up in October.

## How does a snow removal side hustle in Canada make money?

A Canadian snow removal side hustle makes money in three ways: seasonal contracts paid up front or in instalments, per-visit fees after each storm, and add-ons such as walkways, steps, salting and windrow returns. Seasonal contracts are the core product because they let you plan a route and get paid before the first storm.

The published price lists show how operators package the work:

- **Seasonal contract with a trigger.** Stittsville Snow Removals clears "every time 5 cm or more of snow has accumulated within a 48-hour period" from November 1, 2025 to March 31, 2026, with no limit on visits. Half the fee is due when the client signs and the rest by January 1, 2026 ([Stittsville Snow Removals](https://www.stittsnow.ca/products/single-driveway-snow-removal)).
- **Seasonal contract with a snowfall cap.** Snowforge covers up to 250 cm of cumulative snowfall, then bills $45, $55 or $65 a visit by driveway size ([Snowforge](https://snowforgeottawa.ca/pricing)). BlueGuard covers up to 275 cm from October 15 to April 15 ([BlueGuard](https://snowremoval.blueguard.ca/snow-removal-cost-ottawa)). A cap protects you in a record winter.
- **Monthly plans.** Calgary's Golden Angel bills by the month: $179 with no driveway, $217 for a two-car driveway and $255 for a large or corner lot. Each plan includes the walkway, steps, public sidewalk and ice melt ([Golden Angel, July 23, 2026](https://goldenangel.co/snow-removal-cost-calgary/)).
- **Per visit.** BlueGuard charges $49, $79 or $99 plus HST for one-time clearing. Golden Angel puts Calgary per-visit pricing at roughly $30 to $70 and hourly work at around $70 per labour hour.
- **Add-ons.** Stittsville charges $150 a season to hand-shovel the front door, steps, porch and the strip in front of the garage. Snowforge charges $199 a season for a front walkway, $99 for front steps, or $249 for both on its 5 cm trigger.

Payment is usually by Interac e-Transfer, cheque or card. If you want to send estimates and card-payable invoices, Square's free plan includes Invoices at $0 a month plus processing fees ([Square Canada](https://squareup.com/ca/en/pricing)).

Days with snow is not the same as days that hit a 5 cm trigger. Many of those days are dustings. Ottawa's operators cap seasons at 250 to 275 cm, which tells you how much snow they budget for there.

The bylaw changes the sales pitch:

- **Calgary:** owners must clear public sidewalks next to their property "down to the bare surface" within 24 hours of a snowfall ending. Fines are $250 for a first offence, $500 for a second and $750 for a third within 12 months. If the City clears it, the owner pays at least $150 plus GST and an administration fee ([City of Calgary](https://www.calgary.ca/bylaws/snow-ice.html)). A corner lot has two sidewalks to clear.
- **Edmonton:** the Community Standards Bylaw sets no time limit. It says sidewalks must be kept clear of snow and ice, and failing to do so can bring a $100 fine plus the cost of clearing ([City of Edmonton](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/on_your_streets/sidewalk-snow-removal)). Complaints are accepted November 1 to May 1, once it hasn't snowed for 48 hours ([complaints page](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/bylaws/home-bylaw-complaints)).
- **Toronto:** the City clears about 98% of sidewalks once 2 cm falls. Below 2 cm, residents clear within 12 hours. Owners must clear driveways, steps, ramps and landings within 24 hours. Pushing snow onto a sidewalk, road or bike lane can bring a $500 fine plus a $110 victim surcharge ([City of Toronto](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-maintenance/winter-maintenance/clearing-snow-and-ice-from-your-property/)). In Toronto you sell driveways and steps, not the public sidewalk.

City rules for other places are summarized in the city guides for [Toronto](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-toronto-canada/), [Calgary](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-calgary-canada/), [Edmonton](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-edmonton-canada/), [Winnipeg](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-winnipeg-canada/), [Hamilton](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-hamilton-canada/) and [Montreal](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-montreal-canada/).

## What could a snow removal route earn in Canada? (worked math)

Seasonal revenue is **clients × seasonal price**. Your hourly rate is **revenue ÷ (trigger events × driveways × minutes per driveway ÷ 60)**, before gear, fuel, insurance and tax. The price inputs come from the operators above.

**Illustrative example: a 10-driveway shovel route.** The client count, number of trigger events and minutes per driveway are assumptions. The 20 to 30 minutes per shovelled driveway is the figure Calgary's Matt Wilson gives in his own account of his childhood route (see the examples section).

| Scenario | Price per season (source) | Revenue (10 clients) | Assumed events × minutes | Hours | Gross per hour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neighbourhood rate | $250 (Golden Angel's low end for a full season) | 10 × $250 = $2,500 | 20 events × 25 min | 20 × 10 × 25 ÷ 60 = 83.3 | $2,500 ÷ 83.3 = **$30.01** |
| Mid-market Ottawa | $418.50 (Stittsville detached) | $4,185 | 20 × 25 min | 83.3 | **$50.24** |
| Full-service Ottawa | $499 (BlueGuard single) | $4,990 | 25 × 25 min | 104.2 | **$47.89** |
| Heavy winter at the low price | $250 | $2,500 | 35 × 25 min | 145.8 | **$17.15** |

The last row is the reason operators write caps. At 35 events, the $250 season pays less per hour than the $20.75 median Job Bank lists for landscaping and grounds labourers. A cap like Snowforge's 250 cm, or a per-visit rate after a set number of visits, keeps a heavy year profitable.

**Illustrative example: per-visit pricing.** At BlueGuard's $49 single-driveway rate, a storm where you clear 8 driveways (assumed) brings in 8 × $49 = $392. At the low end of Golden Angel's Calgary range, $30, it's 8 × $30 = $240. Per-visit income disappears in a light winter, while seasonal money arrives in October and November.

**Illustrative example: what HST adds in Ontario.** If you're registered, a $499 contract becomes $499 × 1.13 = $563.87 with 13% HST ([CRA rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)). Below the $30,000 small-supplier line you usually don't charge it.

## What does a 90-day snow removal launch plan look like?

A 90-day plan for a Canadian snow removal side hustle runs from early October to early January: sign clients before the first snow, run the first storms as a drill, then adjust prices and add-ons before mid-season sign-ups. Snowforge, BlueGuard and Stittsville all sell their seasons in the fall, and Stittsville offered an early sign-up discount until October 10.

## What snow removal workflow and templates should you copy?

Batch the work into a storm-morning routine and keep every agreement in writing. Copy and edit the templates below.

### Door-hanger or community post

```text
Snow clearing for [street names], winter 2026-27
- Driveway + front walk cleared every time [5 cm] falls, done by [7:30 a.m.]
- Season: [Nov 15] to [Mar 31], up to [X] visits, then $[Y] per visit
- Single driveway $[ ]/season, double $[ ]/season. Steps and walk +$[ ]
- Half now, half by January 1. E-transfer or card invoice
- Local, insured, references from [lawn clients/neighbours]
Text [name] at [number] for a quote by [date]
```

### One-page agreement

```text
Client / address:
Surfaces: [ ] driveway  [ ] front walk  [ ] steps  [ ] city sidewalk  [ ] windrow return
Trigger: [5] cm of new snow, measured at [location]
Done by: [7:30 a.m.] on weekdays when snow stops by [4 a.m.]; otherwise within [X] hours of it stopping
Season: [start] to [end]. Includes up to [X] visits or [Y] cm total; after that $[Z] per visit
Ice melt: [included / extra $ / not used]. Pet-safer product: [yes/no]
Not included: roofs, ice dams, parking lots, neighbour's property
Price: $[ ] (+ GST/HST if registered). Payment: [50%] on signing, balance by [Jan 1]
Cancellation: either party with [7] days' written notice; refund = unused portion pro-rated by [month]
Damage: report within 48 hours. Client marks edges, garden beds and obstacles with stakes
```

### Storm-morning checklist

- Check the forecast at 9 p.m. and set two alarms
- Headlamp charged, cleats on, phone charged, route list open
- Start at the property with the earliest done-by time
- Clear to the trigger you promised on every property, not just the first one
- Salt only after clearing, thinly and evenly
- Don't push snow onto the road, sidewalk, a hydrant or a neighbour's lot
- Text "done" if the agreement says so, and log the visit (date, time, cm)

### Visit log columns

`Date | Storm cm | Property | Start | End | Salt (kg) | Windrow return Y/N | Notes`

Billing, receipts and the visit log can live in a spreadsheet. ## Which Canadians have run snow removal businesses?

Canadian news coverage shows snow removal as a starter business that grows through repeat clients and contracts signed before the snow. These are publicly reported examples. Dates matter because prices have risen since.

**Quinn Collins (Winnipeg, Manitoba).** The *Winnipeg Free Press* reported in November 2019 that the 14-year-old went door to door in the Crestview neighbourhood each winter and usually charged about **$10 to $15 per property**, finding clients through word of mouth, repeat customers and neighbourhood Facebook groups ([Winnipeg Free Press, November 8, 2019](https://www.winnipegfreepress.com/business/2019/11/08/no-business-like-snow-business)).

**OnTheStep (Winnipeg, Manitoba).** The same article reported that three University of Manitoba students launched the OnTheStep snow removal app in fall 2018 and did "well over 1,000 snow removal jobs" in a little under a year.

**Mason Muhm (Pickering, Ontario).** The 11-year-old started Mason's ASAP Snow Services after a neighbour gave him a snowblower. After his mother posted about it on social media, he cleared seven properties in a few days and had a lineup of customers for the next storm. He charges by driveway size and doesn't charge elderly residents ([Metroland](https://www.cambridgetimes.ca/news/pickering-boy-clears-snow-for-elderly/article_3097d5fe-4930-5459-a11b-e95ecb3b73a4.html); [All About Durham, February 11, 2026](https://allaboutdurham.com/11-year-old-snow-clearing-business-pickering/)).

**Matt Wilson (Calgary, Alberta; self-published account).** In a September 2024 LinkedIn post, Wilson describes signing neighbours to typed seasonal contracts at age 11: **$250 for the season, $150 up front and $100 in February** if the customer was satisfied, with snow cleared by 9 a.m. When other kids came canvassing after the first storm, the neighbours were already under contract, so he hired them. By year three he bought a used snowblower with a loan from his father ([LinkedIn, September 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattwilsonyyc_how-did-you-get-started-as-an-entrepreneur-activity-7239278971031330817-7GA_)). This is his own account, not a news report.

The pattern matches the price lists: sign contracts before the first snowfall, collect money up front, and add a machine only once the route is full.

## What mistakes do new snow removal operators make in Canada?

- **Pushing snow onto the sidewalk or road, or burying a hydrant.** Pile snow on the client's property, away from hydrants; city snow bylaws apply to you, not just the homeowner.
- **No liability insurance.** Slip-and-fall claims are the big risk. The FCAC's [home insurance page](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/insurance/home.html) says home insurance isn't business insurance and generally won't cover business claims, so ask a broker about snow clearing coverage before the season.
- **A vague agreement.** Put the trigger depth, done-by time, salting and extra-visit price in writing (see the [contract checklist](/side-hustles/snow-removal-contract-checklist-canada/)).
- **Clearing a clogged blower with the engine running.** CCOHS's [snow thrower page](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/landscaping/snow_throwers.html) says to stop the machine before inspecting or clearing it, to handle fuel with care, and to wait until the engine has cooled before refuelling.
- **Overexertion.** CCOHS's [snow shovelling page](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/snow_shovelling.html) notes that moving 5 kg of snow every 5 seconds adds up to over 70 kg a minute. Push rather than lift, use a proper lightweight snow shovel, and keep a steady pace.
- **No visit log.** Record each visit's date, time, snow depth and kilometres; it supports vehicle claims and settles billing disputes.

## Which tax rules apply to snow removal in Canada?

Snow removal income in Canada is self-employment income. Report it on Form T2125, pay both halves of CPP on your net profit, and register for GST/HST once your taxable supplies pass $30,000.

- **Income tax:** report seasonal contracts, per-visit fees and add-ons, minus expenses such as gear, salt, fuel, insurance and phone use, on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). A deposit received in October is income in the year you receive it, so keep the agreements and e-transfer records.
- **CPP:** self-employed people pay the full **11.9%** on net self-employment earnings between $3,500 and the 2026 ceiling of $74,600 ([Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html)). Quebec residents pay into the QPP instead.
- **GST/HST:** you're a small supplier until your worldwide taxable supplies, including lawn, cleaning and handyman income, pass **$30,000** in one calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). Snow removal isn't covered by the [taxi and ride-sharing rule](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) that requires registration from the first dollar.
- **Provincial rates once registered:** 13% HST in Ontario and 14% in Nova Scotia since April 1, 2025. Other provinces use HST, or 5% GST plus provincial sales tax where it applies, or GST plus QST in Quebec ([CRA rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html); [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html)).
- **Vehicle:** if you drive between properties, keep a kilometre log. Only the business share of vehicle costs is deductible.
- **Helpers:** if you pay friends to shovel, check your province's workers' compensation rules first.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. For monthly set-asides, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/), and for liability coverage, see [side hustle insurance in Canada](/side-hustles/side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Every price here comes from a dated public price list or retailer page, and all of them change. The Ottawa and Calgary figures belong to established operators with insurance and equipment, so a new side hustler usually prices below them. Snowfall averages are 30-year normals, and any single winter can come in well above or below them. The *Winnipeg Free Press*, Metroland and LinkedIn accounts describe individual people, not typical results. Bylaws change, so read your own city's page each fall. Amazon.ca prices change daily. The worked math uses assumed client counts, storm counts and minutes. Your own visit log after three storms will replace them. Nothing here promises income: a mild winter means fewer per-visit jobs, and a harsh one means more unpaid hours on an uncapped contract.
