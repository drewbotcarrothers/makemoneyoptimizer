## What does furniture refinishing pay per hour in Canada?

Employed furniture refinishers in Canada earn a median of $22.79 an hour, according to [Job Bank's furniture refinisher wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/12732/ca) (NOC 94210, reference period 2023–2024, updated November 19, 2025). Those are employee wages at shops that supply the space and materials. A flipper also carries the buying risk and the unsold pieces, so a flip's hourly result should land above them.

| Area (Job Bank, furniture refinisher) | Low | Median | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada | $17.00 | $22.79 | $30.00 |
| Ontario | $17.95 | $22.00 | $30.00 |
| British Columbia | $18.39 | $23.25 | $35.00 |
| Alberta | $23.00 | $26.00 | $37.39 |
| Quebec | $18.50 | $22.00 | $26.72 |

Use the median for your province as the minimum your hours should earn. If a flip works out below the low figure, you would earn more at an hourly refinishing job, without the risk.

## What do furniture consignment stores pay in Canada?

Consignment is the hands-off option: the store shows and sells the piece and keeps a share. Its payout is also a useful floor. If selling the piece yourself nets less than a store would pay you, consign it. These are published terms, checked October 9, 2026.

| [Recertified Furniture](https://recertifiedfurniture.ca/pages/selling) (Victoria, B.C.) | 50% of the net sale over $500; 40% under $500; 30% for dining and coffee tables, bed frames and mattresses | Card processing, pickup, cleaning and repair fees come off your payout; its cash buy offers are typically 30% to 40% below the estimated consignment payout |

## How much should you charge for driving to pick up furniture?

Count driving as a cost, not a favour. The [Department of Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/01/government-announces-the-2026-automobile-deduction-limits-and-expense-benefit-rates-for-businesses.html) set the 2026 tax-exempt automobile allowance at 73¢ per km for the first 5,000 km and 67¢ after (77¢ and 71¢ in the territories). That limit applies to allowances employers pay employees, but it is a reasonable per-km figure to price in.

For your own taxes, self-employed people deduct the business share of actual vehicle costs on T2125, not this rate. Keep a log of business kilometres either way.

A 30 km round trip at 73¢ is $21.90. On a $100 nightstand, that is more than a fifth of the price.

## How do you calculate a furniture flip price in Canada?

Calculate a furniture flip in Canada backwards. Start from a realistic sale price, subtract costs and your target pay, and the remainder is the most you should pay for the piece.

**Maximum buy = floor price − supplies − driving − (hours × hourly target)**

**Pay per hour = (floor price − buy − supplies − driving) ÷ hours**

### Step 1: Find three local comparables

Look for refinished pieces of the same type, size and condition near you. Note which ones sold or disappeared, not just the asking prices. Then check the IKEA table above for a new equivalent.

### Step 2: Price your supplies per piece

Divide each can across the pieces it covers. Add sandpaper, hardware and cleaning supplies from your own receipts.

### Step 3: Add driving

Round-trip kilometres for pickup, any supply run and delivery, at 73¢ per km.

### Step 4: Estimate hours honestly

Pickup, cleaning, repairs, finish, drying time you return for, photos, messages and showing the piece.

### Step 5: Check the safety questions before quoting

Old painted finishes can contain lead. Health Canada's [lead-based paint page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/lead-based-paint.html) says homes built before 1960 probably contain lead-based paint, and there is no known safe level of exposure. Do not sand an unknown old finish indoors. Check [recalls](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) for anything branded, and skip regulated children's products such as cribs unless you know the rules in Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html).

## What do worked furniture flip quotes look like?

The four quotes below are **Illustrative**. Supply prices, consignment terms, tax rates and the 73¢ figure come from the sources above. Comparable prices, buy prices, hours, distances and the sandpaper allowance are assumptions to show the arithmetic, not market data.

### Quote 1: Full chalk-paint refinish on a six-drawer dresser (Illustrative)

Assume three similar refinished dressers near you sold for about $280–$320, so you list at $300 with a $270 floor. You buy the dresser for $40.

- Paint: one quart, $52.95
- Clear Coat: half a pint, $18.48 (half of $36.95)
- Sandpaper and cleaning: assumed $10
- Supplies total: $81.43
- Driving: 30 km round trip × $0.73 = $21.90
- Hours: 9

Pay per hour at the floor: ($270 − $40 − $81.43 − $21.90) ÷ 9 = $126.67 ÷ 9 = **$14.07 an hour**. That is below Job Bank's national low of $17.00.

At a $25 target, the maximum buy price is $270 − $81.43 − $21.90 − (9 × $25) = **−$58.33**. No buy price makes this flip pay $25 an hour. The fix is fewer hours, cheaper supplies, or a piece whose comps sell higher.

### Quote 2: Light refresh on a pair of solid-wood nightstands (Illustrative)

Assume similar pairs near you sell around $200–$240. You list at $220 with a $200 floor and buy the pair for $50.

- Supplies: clear wax 4 oz, $20.95, plus assumed $5 for cleaning = $25.95
- Driving: 20 km round trip × $0.73 = $14.60
- Hours: 4 (clean, tighten, wax, photos)

Pay per hour at the floor: ($200 − $50 − $25.95 − $14.60) ÷ 4 = $109.45 ÷ 4 = **$27.36 an hour**. That is above the median in every province in the wage table. Matching the effort to the piece is what made the difference, not a higher price.

### Quote 3: Consign a refinished sideboard instead of selling it yourself (Illustrative)

Assume a refinished sideboard you would list at $400.

- **Juniper & Oak:** sold in the first 30 days at $400, your 50% is **$200**. After the first 25% reduction, the price is $300 and your share is **$150**.
- **Recertified Furniture:** a $400 sale is under $500, so you receive 40% of the net sale, **$160 before** its card processing, pickup, cleaning or repair fees. A sideboard that sold at $600 would pay 50%, **$300 before fees**.

Compare that with selling it yourself. If you would accept $350 after 3 more hours of messages and showings, consigning at $200 costs you $150 to save those hours, or $50 an hour. Consign when your time is worth more than that, or when the piece has sat for weeks.

### Quote 4: A registered seller in Ontario adds HST (Illustrative)

Assume you are registered for GST/HST because your business passed the $30,000 threshold, and you sell a refinished dresser in Ontario for $450.

- Price: $450.00
- HST at 13%: $58.50
- Buyer pays: $508.50
- HST you paid on supplies (one quart of paint and one pint of Clear Coat, $89.90 × 13%): $11.69, claimable as an input tax credit
- Net HST to remit for this sale: $58.50 − $11.69 = **$46.81**

The $450 is still your price. HST is added on top and passed to the CRA. If you are not registered, you charge no HST and claim no input tax credits. Ask CRA or an accountant how GST/HST applies to a piece bought from a private seller.

## What templates help you price and sell furniture?

Copy these and fill in the blanks. They contain no prices of their own.

**Pre-buy check (write it before you message the seller)**

> Piece: ___. Three comps: $___, $___, $___ (sold / still listed). New equivalent: $___ at ___. My floor: $___. Supplies: $___. Driving: ___ km × $0.73 = $___. Hours: ___. Target per hour: $___. Maximum buy: $___.

**Offer message to a seller**

> Hi, is the ___ still available? I can pick it up on ___ with cash or e-Transfer. Would you take $___? I can see ___ (water ring / loose drawer / worn finish) in the photos, so that is my top price.

**Listing text**

> Solid ___ (wood type if known) ___, refinished in ___ (colour) with a ___ (wax / clear coat) finish. Size: ___ W × ___ D × ___ H cm. Drawers: ___ (dovetailed / glide type). Known flaws: ___. Price $___. Pickup at ___ (area); delivery within ___ km for $___.

**Reply to a low offer**

> Thanks for the offer. The lowest I can do is $___, which covers the materials and the work. If that works, it is yours for pickup on ___.

**Price-change log**

> Listed ___ (date) at $___. Views/messages by day 14: ___. Change on ___ (date) to $___. Sold on ___ (date) for $___.

## When should you raise or lower furniture prices in Canada?

Raise prices when similar pieces sell within days at your price, when you get several messages on the first day, or when supply costs go up. Each of those means your floor is too close to the market.

Lower prices in one planned step when a piece has views but no serious messages for two to three weeks. One clear drop is easier for buyers to notice than several small ones.

Storage has a cost even at home. A piece that blocks your garage for two months may be worth selling at the floor, or consigning. Juniper & Oak's schedule (full price for 30 days, then 25% off every 30 days) is a reasonable model for your own markdowns.

Raise your rate, not just the price: if most pieces come out under your target per hour, buy pieces that need less work, or charge for delivery, rather than adding hours.

## What furniture flipping pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common mistakes are buying before checking comps, not counting hours and driving, and pricing from asking prices that never sold.

- **Full refinish on a light-refresh piece.** Quote 1 shows 9 hours of work paying under the lowest employee wage.
- **Forgetting the new price.** A used flat-pack dresser listed near IKEA's $249 will sit.
- **Ignoring supplies.** A quart of specialty paint and a pint of top coat cost about $90 before tax.
- **No written floor.** A hard bargainer sets it for you.
- **Hiding damage.** Describe chips, wobble and smells.
- **Sanding old paint indoors** to save time on a quote is never worth the risk.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect furniture flipping prices in Canada?

Regular furniture flipping for profit in Canada is generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), with the cost of pieces and supplies deducted. GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your businesses. Before that, you do not charge GST/HST.

Self-employed people pay both shares of CPP on net earnings; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). Keep each piece's buy receipt or a screenshot of the listing and message.
