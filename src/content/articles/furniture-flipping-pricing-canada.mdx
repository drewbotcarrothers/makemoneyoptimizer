## What does furniture refinishing pay per hour in Canada?

Those are employee wages at shops that supply the space and materials. A flipper also carries the buying risk, so the hourly result of a flip should land above them.

## Which pricing approaches work for flipped furniture in Canada?

Flipped furniture in Canada is usually priced from local comparables first, with a cost-and-hours check underneath. Pricing purely from your costs ignores what buyers will pay; pricing purely from comps ignores whether you made anything.

| Approach | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local comparables | Price near similar refinished pieces in your area | Common pieces (dressers, nightstands, tables) | Asking prices are not sold prices |
| Cost plus hours | Buy + supplies + transport + hours × rate | Checking a flip before you buy | Ignores what the market will pay |
| Light refresh vs full refinish | Clean and fix vs strip and paint | Matching effort to the piece | Spending 10 hours on a piece that sells like a 2-hour piece |
| Set firm vs room to negotiate | List a little above your floor | Local pickup buyers who haggle | A floor you have not written down |
| Sets and pairs | Price a matched pair above two singles | Nightstands, chairs | Splitting a set for one quick sale |

## How do you calculate a furniture flip price in Canada?

Calculate a furniture flip in Canada backwards: start from a realistic resale price, subtract costs and your target pay, and the remainder is the most you should pay for the piece. Then confirm the hours you can actually spend.

### Step 1: Find three local comparables

Look for refinished pieces of the same type, size, and condition near you. Note which ones sold or disappeared, not just the asking prices.

### Step 2: List supplies and transport

Paint, primer, sandpaper, hardware, and any van rental or delivery cost. Divide a can of paint across the pieces it covers.

### Step 3: Estimate hours honestly

Pick-up, cleaning, repairs, finish, drying time you have to return for, photos, and showing the piece to buyers.

### Step 4: Work out your maximum buy price

Maximum buy = expected resale − supplies − transport − (hours × your hourly rate). If the asking price is above it, negotiate or skip.

### Step 5: Check the safety questions before quoting

Old painted finishes can contain lead. Health Canada's [lead-based paint page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/lead-based-paint.html) says homes built before 1960 probably contain lead-based paint, and there is no known safe level of exposure. Do not sand an unknown old finish indoors. Check [recalls](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) for anything branded, and skip regulated children's products such as cribs unless you know the rules in Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose three similar refinished six-drawer dressers near you sold for about $280–$320, so you plan to list at $300 and accept $270.

Supplies are an assumed $45 (paint, primer, sandpaper, two new pulls). Transport is $0 because the piece fits your vehicle. You expect 8 hours in total and want $24 an hour, a little above Job Bank's national median: 8 × $24 = $192.

Maximum buy price at the $270 floor: $270 − $45 − $192 = $33. If the seller wants $80, the flip still works only if you accept 8 hours for $270 − $45 − $80 = $145, or about $18 an hour, which is below the national median refinisher wage. That tells you to negotiate or pass.

## When should you raise or lower furniture prices in Canada?

Raise flipped furniture prices in Canada when similar pieces sell quickly at your price, when a style is in demand, or when supplies cost more. Lower them, in one planned step, when a piece has had views but no serious messages for two to three weeks.

Storage has a cost even at home. A piece that blocks your garage for two months may be worth selling at the floor. Price drops work better as one clear change than as many small ones.

## What furniture flipping pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common furniture flipping pricing mistakes in Canada are buying before checking comps, not counting hours, and pricing from asking prices that never sold. Each one makes a beautiful piece a loss.

Write your floor before you list so a hard bargainer does not set it for you. Count drying days you have to return for. Do not hide damage to hit a price; describe chips, wobble, and smells. Disturbing old paint to save time on a quote is never worth it. ## How do GST/HST and income tax affect furniture flipping prices in Canada?

Regular furniture flipping for profit in Canada is generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), with the cost of pieces and supplies deducted. GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your businesses.

Self-employed people pay both shares of CPP on net earnings; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html).
