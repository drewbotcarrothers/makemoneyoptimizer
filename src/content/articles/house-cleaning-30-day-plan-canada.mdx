It suits organized, reliable people who are comfortable in other people's homes and follow product labels exactly.

## What happens in week 1 of a house cleaning launch plan in Canada?

Week 1 is setup: decide what you clean and what you do not, buy a lean kit, write a room-by-room checklist, and get an insurance quote.

### Days 1 and 2: Define your service menu

Choose a starting menu, such as a standard recurring clean and a deeper first clean. Write down what is excluded, such as high ladders, mould remediation, or outdoor windows.

### Days 3 and 4: Build a lean kit and learn your products

Buy a caddy, colour-coded cloths, sponges, bathroom, kitchen, and glass cleaners, gloves, and a mop. [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/chemicals/cleaning-products-working-safely.html) says never to mix different cleaning products and never to mix bleach and ammonia, so read every label before the first job. The [startup costs guide](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-startup-costs-canada/) has the ranges. Use the [house cleaning tools guide](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-tools-canada/) to compare a lean kit with upgrades before you buy.

### Days 5 to 7: Write a checklist and get an insurance quote

Write a room-by-room checklist so every clean is consistent. Ask a broker about liability insurance that covers damage in a client's home, and consider whether clients may ask for a police record check. In Ontario, the province's [police record checks page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/police-record-checks) explains the three types and says to ask the requesting person which type they need.

## What happens in week 2 of a house cleaning launch plan in Canada?

Week 2 is pricing and outreach: set a rate, publish a short offer, and book walkthroughs before quoting larger homes.

### Days 8 to 10: Set prices and policies

Use the [pricing guide](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-pricing-canada/) to choose hourly or flat rates, then write a short policy page covering cancellations, access and keys, pets, and payment.

### Days 11 and 12: Publish your offer

Tell friends and neighbours, post in community groups that allow service posts, and consider a free Google Business Profile; Google says you can [add or claim a profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778?hl=en) at no charge if eligible. Before emailing people who are not existing contacts, read the CASL [consent rules](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email).

### Days 13 and 14: Do walkthroughs and quotes

Walk through each home, or ask for photos, before you quote. Confirm size, priorities, pets, and supplies. A quote without seeing the home is a guess.

## What happens in week 3 of a house cleaning launch plan in Canada?

Week 3 is first cleans: follow the checklist, time each room, and fix whatever slowed you down.

### Days 15 to 17: Complete first cleans

Follow the checklist in order and note the time each room takes. Leave a short note listing what was done.

### Days 18 and 19: Adjust timing and supplies

Compare actual time with your quote. If a home took far longer, adjust the price for the next visit and explain why. Restock consumables and wash cloths.

### Days 20 and 21: Follow up

Message each client a day later to ask if anything was missed. Fix issues on the next visit, or offer a quick return for a clear miss.

## What happens in week 4 of a house cleaning launch plan in Canada?

Week 4 builds a route: offer recurring visits, ask for referrals, set up invoicing, and review your numbers.

### Days 22 to 24: Offer recurring visits

Offer weekly or biweekly slots on fixed days. Clustering homes in one area reduces unpaid travel time.

### Days 25 and 26: Ask for referrals and reviews

Ask satisfied clients for a review and a referral to a neighbour or friend.

### Days 27 and 28: Set up invoicing

A spreadsheet and e-transfer work for a few homes. If invoices pile up, <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> is one invoicing option; there is no need to pay for software before you have clients.

### Days 29 and 30: Review your numbers and plan month two

Total income, supplies, and travel. Decide whether to add homes, adjust rates, or change your service area.

### Illustrative example

A teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast: suppose by day 30 you have two biweekly clients at a flat price P each. Monthly revenue is roughly 2 clients × 2 visits × P = 4P. If P were $120, that is $480 a month before supplies, travel, insurance, and tax. Your price and client count will differ.

## What house cleaning launch mistakes should you avoid in Canada?

Common launch mistakes are quoting without seeing the home, mixing products, skipping a written checklist, accepting jobs you cannot reach safely, and booking clients spread across the city. More are in [house cleaning mistakes](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-mistakes-canada/).

## Which tax rules apply when launching house cleaning in Canada?

House cleaning income is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and the self-employed pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Keep receipts for supplies from day 1. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
