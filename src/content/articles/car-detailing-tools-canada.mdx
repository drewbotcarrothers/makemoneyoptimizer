## How should you choose car detailing tools in Canada?

Choose car detailing tools in Canada by what you will sell first (interiors are easier to do legally on a driveway than full exterior washes), by how products are labelled for each surface, and by how much water each tool needs. These are selection criteria, not test results.

| Criterion | What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service mix | Interior-first, or exterior with a water plan | Interiors avoid most runoff questions |
| Surface labels | Soap labelled for vehicles; interior products tested on a hidden spot | Dish soap and harsh cleaners can damage finishes |
| Water use | Bucket-based washing, or a machine that needs a water and drainage plan | [Toronto](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/water-environment/water-sewer-related-permits-and-bylaws/sewers-by-law/washing-cars-outdoors-surfaces/) says soaps and washwater must not flow into a catch basin |
| Towel separation | Enough towels that paint, glass, and interior never share | Cross-use causes streaks and scratches |
| Portability | Fits your car with room to spare | A mobile kit you cannot carry stays home |
| Power | Corded tools need a client outlet; battery tools need runtime | Ask before you plug in |

## What do Canadian rules say about car detailing water and chemicals?

The City of Toronto says no substances other than rain and melted snow may flow into an outdoor catch basin, including soaps and any water used for cleaning. For washing cars, its [page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/water-environment/water-sewer-related-permits-and-bylaws/sewers-by-law/washing-cars-outdoors-surfaces/) suggests a commercial car wash, a permeable surface such as gravel, or a pail, washcloth, and only a small amount of water, with the wastewater disposed of in the sanitary sewer through a laundry sink or toilet. Toronto also says both the person hiring and the mobile wash business are responsible for wastewater from the property. Other cities have their own bylaws; confirm locally.

On safety, [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/garages/wash_clean.html) tips for washing vehicles include non-slip rubber boots, reading product labels and safety data sheets, and protecting skin, eyes, and lungs when using solvents. CCOHS also says [never mix different cleaning products](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/chemicals/cleaning-products-working-safely.html), especially bleach and ammonia. Winter salt work has its own considerations in [winter car detailing and salt removal](/side-hustles/winter-car-detailing-salt-removal-canada/).

## What car detailing tool mistakes are common in Canada?

Common car detailing tool mistakes in Canada are using dish soap on paint, one towel for everything, letting soapy water run into the street grate, mixing interior cleaners, and buying an extractor or coating kit before anyone has paid for a basic interior.

## Which tax rules apply to car detailing tools in Canada?

Detailing income is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and the self-employed make the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. Soap, towels, and cleaners are ordinary supplies. An extractor or vacuum that lasts years is depreciable property, which the CRA says you [cannot deduct in full](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) in the year of purchase.

GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
