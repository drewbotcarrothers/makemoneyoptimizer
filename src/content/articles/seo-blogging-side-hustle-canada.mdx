An SEO blog side hustle in Canada is a website you own, on one subject, that earns from display ads, affiliate links, sponsored posts or your own digital products once search traffic arrives. It is slow: the 129 bloggers in the 2026 Blogging Income Survey took a median of 12 months to earn their first dollar. Hosting starts at about CAN$4.19 a month, and the money comes from traffic multiplied by revenue per 1,000 pageviews (RPM).

<QuickAnswer>
- **Time to income:** the 2026 Blogging Income Survey (129 bloggers, published May 2026) found a median of **12 months** to the first dollar and a mean of **49 months** to a full-time income; 24% reached full-time income within two years.
- **What a pageview is worth:** in the same survey, bloggers whose main income was ads averaged a **US$33.16 RPM**; those selling digital products averaged **US$535.90**. Affiliate-led blogs averaged US$34.66.
- **Ad network thresholds (checked October 9, 2026):** AdSense has no traffic minimum and pays 80% of revenue to publishers after the advertiser platform's fee. Journey by Mediavine needs 1,000 sessions a month from countries including Canada. Raptive needs 25,000 pageviews a month and a domain at least six months old.
- **Costs:** Hostinger's WordPress Premium plan is CAN$4.19 a month on a 48-month term (CAN$201.12 upfront, renews at CAN$15.39). WordPress.com Personal is US$4 a month billed yearly. Google Search Console and Ahrefs Free cost nothing.
- **Tax:** report the income on T2125, pay both halves of CPP on the profit, and register for GST/HST once taxable sales pass $30,000.

## Who does an SEO blog suit?

It suits people who already know a subject well enough to answer real questions about it, who like writing and research, and who can keep publishing for a year before the income is meaningful. It's flexible evening work, and the asset you build (a site with traffic) keeps earning between writing sessions.

It suits:

- **Hobbyists and specialists** with knowledge readers search for: fishing, gardening in short seasons, camping, sewing, home repair, cooking.
- **Professionals** who can write about their field with first-hand expertise (nurses, accountants, tradespeople, teachers), keeping employer and professional rules in mind.
- **Writers** who want an asset rather than per-article fees; see [side hustles for writers](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-writers-canada/).
- **Newcomers and people who've lived the process** (immigration steps, a credential recognition path, buying a first home in a specific province).

It doesn't suit people who need income within three months, who don't want to write, or who plan to publish scraped or mass-produced AI pages. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) name scraping and copying content with only slight changes as abuse, and its [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) lists mass-produced content on topics you don't know as a warning sign. If you'd rather write for someone else's site, see [freelance writing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada/); if you'd rather help a local business get found, that's [local SEO](/side-hustles/local-seo-side-hustle-canada/).

## How does an SEO blog make money?

A blog earns from five main streams, and most successful bloggers use more than one. The 2026 Blogging Income Survey found that bloggers with four or more revenue streams earned more than four times the RPM of bloggers with one.

| Stream | How it pays | When it becomes realistic | Source of the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display ads (AdSense) | Share of ad revenue per impression | After your site has original content and AdSense approves it | [AdSense eligibility](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724) |
| Managed ad networks | Higher-yield ads run by Mediavine, Raptive and others | 1,000 sessions (Journey) to 25,000 pageviews (Raptive) a month | [Mediavine](https://www.mediavine.com/mediavine-requirements/), [Raptive](https://help.raptive.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032840891-Who-is-eligible-for-Raptive) |
| Affiliate links | Commission on sales you refer | From the first post, once approved by a program | [Amazon.ca rates](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ) |
| Sponsored posts | Flat fee per post or link | Once you have traffic or an engaged audience | Your own rate card |
| Digital products and services | Your own templates, guides, courses or consulting | Anytime; strongest with an email list | Your own pricing |

**Display ads.** Google says publishers receive **80% of revenue** for AdSense for content after the advertiser platform takes its fee; when advertisers buy through Google Ads, publishers keep **about 68%** ([AdSense revenue share](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/180195)). Payouts happen once earnings reach the **payment threshold of C$100** for accounts reporting in Canadian dollars, or US$100 for USD accounts ([AdSense thresholds](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1709871)). You must be at least 18 and your content must be your own.

**Managed ad networks.** Mediavine's full program requires at least **$5,000 in annual ad revenue**, while its starter tier, **Journey by Mediavine**, accepts sites with at least **1,000 sessions in 30 days from Tier 1 countries, which include Canada** ([Mediavine requirements](https://www.mediavine.com/mediavine-requirements/), checked October 9, 2026). Raptive's minimum is **25,000 pageviews a month**, with 50% of traffic from the U.S., U.K., Canada, New Zealand or Australia at that level, plus a domain at least **six months old** ([Raptive eligibility](https://help.raptive.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032840891-Who-is-eligible-for-Raptive)).

**Affiliate links.** Amazon.ca's standard commission rates range from 1% (computers, video game consoles) to 10% (luxury beauty). Home, furniture, outdoor recreation and clothing pay 6%; kitchen and dining, books, power tools and sports pay 4%; grocery and gift cards pay 0%; and all other categories pay 2% ([Amazon.ca Associates schedule](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ), checked October 9, 2026). Software, finance and travel programs often pay more per sale. The full method is in [affiliate marketing](/side-hustles/affiliate-marketing-side-hustle-canada/).

**Sponsored posts and digital products.** The survey found digital products earned bloggers by far the highest RPM, and that sponsored-post RPMs roughly doubled year over year. A blog that sells a $15 planner to its own readers earns more per visitor than one waiting for ad impressions.

## How much do SEO bloggers earn?

Earnings depend on traffic, niche, reader location and how many streams you use, and most blogs earn little in their first year. The best published dataset is the Blogging Income Survey 2026 from Productive Blogging: 129 individual bloggers answered between January 2 and April 29, 2026, mostly from the U.S., U.K., Canada, Australia and New Zealand. All figures are in U.S. dollars.

**Average RPM by main revenue stream (Blogging Income Survey 2026, USD)**

| Main revenue stream | Average RPM (per 1,000 pageviews) |
|---|---|
| Digital products | $535.90 |
| Sponsored posts | $100.00 |
| Services | $100.00 |
| Physical products | $55.85 |
| Affiliates | $34.66 |
| Ads | $33.16 |

**Other findings from the same survey:**

- **Time to first dollar:** median 12 months, mean 21 months; just over a quarter earned something within six months.
- **Time to full-time income:** mean 49 months, median 48 months; 24% got there within two years.
- **Post count:** bloggers with 100–299 posts averaged US$1,987.52 a month, and those with 500–999 posts averaged US$9,460.09.
- **Ad networks:** Raptive and Mediavine had the highest ad RPMs, essentially tied, followed by Journey by Mediavine. AdSense was near the bottom.
- **Canada:** Canadian respondents had RPMs nearly as high as U.S. bloggers, but every Canadian respondent said most of their readers were in the U.S. Writing for Canadian readers usually means lower ad RPMs, offset by less competition on Canada-specific questions.
- **Email:** bloggers who did email marketing earned, on average, twice as much as those who didn't.

Survey RPMs combine all income divided by pageviews, so they're higher than what a new blog sees from ads alone. Your own figure lives in AdSense or your ad network's dashboard (revenue ÷ pageviews × 1,000).

## Worked math: what could a blog earn?

The formula is **monthly pageviews ÷ 1,000 × RPM = monthly revenue**. Convert U.S. dollars at the Bank of Canada rate: **US$1 = C$1.4240** on October 8, 2026.

**Illustrative example: ad revenue at the survey's average ad RPM.** The RPM (US$33.16) is sourced; the pageview levels are assumptions.

| Monthly pageviews | Calculation | Monthly revenue (USD) | In CAD |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5,000 | 5 × US$33.16 | US$165.80 | about C$236 |
| 10,000 | 10 × US$33.16 | US$331.60 | about C$472 |
| 25,000 (Raptive minimum) | 25 × US$33.16 | US$829.00 | about C$1,180 |
| 50,000 | 50 × US$33.16 | US$1,658.00 | about C$2,361 |

**Illustrative example: the same 10,000 pageviews monetized three ways.** The RPMs are the survey averages; the traffic is assumed.

| Approach | RPM (USD) | Monthly revenue (USD) | In CAD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ads only | $33.16 | $331.60 | about C$472 |
| Affiliate-led | $34.66 | $346.60 | about C$494 |
| Digital-product-led | $535.90 | $5,359.00 | about C$7,631 |

The digital-product row is the survey's average across bloggers who'd already built products and funnels; a new blogger's first product usually sells far less. The point of the table is direction: owning a product beats waiting for ad impressions.

**Illustrative example: one affiliate post.** Assume a post about cold-weather camping gear gets 3,000 visits a month, 4% click through to Amazon.ca (120 clicks), and 5% of those buy (6 orders) at an average of $80 in the outdoor recreation category, which pays 6%. Revenue: 6 × $80 × 6% = **$28.80 a month** from one post. Click-through and conversion rates are assumptions; the 6% rate is from Amazon.ca's schedule.

**Illustrative example: first-year costs.** Hostinger Premium on the 48-month term is CAN$201.12 upfront (CAN$4.19 × 48) and includes a domain free for the first year. That's about CAN$50 a year if you spread it over the term. Add $0 for Google Search Console and Ahrefs Free. If you write 50 posts at 4 hours each, the first year is 200 hours of unpaid work, which is why the survey's 12-month median to first income matters more than any RPM table.

## Which ad network or monetization path fits each stage?

Match the monetization to your traffic. Putting heavy ads on a tiny site earns little and slows pages; the survey's author suggests digital products or affiliates until you qualify for a managed network.

| Stage | Monthly traffic | Best first move |
|---|---|---|
| Launch (months 1–6) | Under 1,000 sessions | Affiliate links in genuinely useful posts; start an email list |
| Early traction | 1,000+ sessions from Tier 1 countries | Apply to Journey by Mediavine or AdSense; add a simple digital product |
| Established | 25,000+ pageviews, domain 6+ months old | Apply to Raptive; Mediavine once ad revenue reaches $5,000 a year |
| Mature | 50,000+ pageviews | Sponsored posts with a rate card, larger products, services |

## 20 blog niches that suit Canadians

Pick a subject you can write 50 genuinely useful posts about, where Canadian readers have questions U.S. sites answer badly.

1. **Canadian personal finance** (TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, RESP): high-value topic, and U.S. advice doesn't apply.
2. **Provincial camping and parks**: reservation systems, routes and gear differ by province.
3. **Cold-weather gardening**: short seasons and hardiness zones generic sites skip.

5. **Newcomer guides**: SIN, credit history, health cards, first apartment; you can write from experience.

7. **Canadian recipes and seasonal cooking**: food is a top-earning niche in the survey.
8. **Hockey, ringette and youth sports for parents**: gear sizing, budgets, tryouts.
9. **Fishing and hunting by province**: licences, seasons and regulations change by region.
10. **Canadian travel by region**: road trips, ferries, rail and lesser-known towns.
11. **Home renovation in a Canadian climate**: insulation, basements, ice dams.
12. **Pets in Canadian winters**: boots, coats, salt-safe paw care; pet food pays 9% on Amazon.ca.
13. **Student life and money**: student loans, budgeting, part-time work by province.
14. **Learning French as an anglophone** (or English as a francophone): huge, steady search demand.
15. **Small business and side hustle admin**: GST/HST, bookkeeping, tools for Canadian self-employed people.
16. **Cross-country skiing and snowshoeing**: gear and trail guides.
17. **Canoeing and paddling routes**: route guides earn links and loyal readers.
18. **Crafts and sewing**: arts and crafts was one of the top-earning niches in the survey.
19. **Local city guide**: restaurants, events and services in your city; sponsorships from local businesses.
20. **Retirement in Canada**: CPP, OAS and pension questions from people who read long posts.

## How to start: a 90-day plan

### Weeks 1–2: Subject and keyword list

- Write your subject in one sentence naming a reader and a job ("helping Ontario parents budget for minor hockey").
- Collect 60 questions from autocomplete, "People also ask", forums you read and questions friends ask you.
- Group them into 8–10 topic clusters. Each cluster gets one big guide and 4–6 supporting posts.
- Check each question: if the top results are big brands answering it perfectly, park it; if they're thin, U.S.-only or outdated, it's a target.

### Weeks 3–4: Site setup

- Buy hosting and a domain in your own name.
- Install WordPress, a fast lightweight theme, an SEO plugin and Google Search Console.
- Publish About, Contact, Privacy and Disclosure pages with your real name.
- Set up an email signup with one simple freebie (a checklist or printable).

### Weeks 5–8: First 12 posts

- Publish three posts a week, starting with the cluster you know best.
- Each post answers the question in the first two sentences, then adds steps, a table, original photos and sourced numbers.
- Link each supporting post to the cluster guide and back.
- Apply to one or two affiliate programs that fit your niche.

### Weeks 9–12: Measure and expand

- Check Search Console weekly: which queries show impressions? Write posts for related questions.
- Reach 25–30 posts by day 90.
- Update any post that gets impressions but few clicks (rewrite the title and opening).
- Plan one small digital product for your best-performing cluster.

### Months 4–12

- Keep a steady schedule (two posts a week is 80+ posts by month 12).
- Apply to Journey by Mediavine at 1,000 Tier 1 sessions a month, or AdSense once your content is substantial.
- Refresh posts every 6–12 months, especially anything with prices, rates or rules.
- Pitch sponsored posts once you have traffic and a rate card.

- [ ] Affiliate links disclosed clearly


**Disclosure line for posts with affiliate links**

> This post contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

The Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) expects material connections to be disclosed clearly, and Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) treat paid links that pass ranking credit as link spam, so mark them `rel="sponsored"`.

## What does Google actually reward?

Google publishes its rules, and they're short. Following them doesn't guarantee rankings, but ignoring them guarantees trouble.

- **Search is free.** [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) say it costs nothing to be eligible, and meeting the requirements doesn't guarantee Google will crawl, index or show a page.
- **People-first content.** The [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks whether the page shows first-hand expertise, such as having used a product or visited a place, and whether it adds original information or analysis.
- **No scraping or spun content.** The [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) cover copying content with slight changes and publishing without original value.
- **Core updates move rankings.** The [core updates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates) page says these broad changes don't target specific sites; the response is to improve content, not quick fixes.
- **AI Overviews need nothing special.** Google's [AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) page says there are no additional requirements or special markup to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode.

Because search changes can cut traffic overnight, build at least one channel you control: an email [newsletter](/side-hustles/newsletter-side-hustle-canada/), Pinterest (see [Pinterest affiliate marketing](/side-hustles/pinterest-affiliate-side-hustle-canada/)), or a [YouTube](/side-hustles/youtube-side-hustle-canada/) channel covering the same subject.

## Real bloggers and their numbers

These are publicly documented. They were chosen because they publish figures, not because they're typical.

**Kenna's Cooks (Calgary, Alberta).** In her 2025 creator income report, food blogger Kenna reported **$121,059.37 in gross income** in her fourth year: **$42,630.28 from Mediavine** ads on her blog and **$78,429.09 from sponsored content**. Monthly Mediavine income ranged from $2,751.28 to $4,365.62. The report doesn't state the currency, and expenses and taxes aren't included. **Lesson:** steady ad income from a mature blog plus brand deals; the blog was in its fourth year.

**Let's Glow with Bryana (Canada).** Canadian blogger Bryana Venos published an October 2025 income report five months into blogging and two months into a deliberate strategy: **$0 in ad revenue** after a second AdSense rejection, a **first affiliate cheque of $4.96**, $0 in product sales, and a single monthly cost of **$35 CAD for Tailwind** (a Pinterest scheduler). She noted that affiliate programs are "way more complicated as a Canadian blogger." **Lesson:** this is what month five usually looks like, which matches the survey's 12-month median to first meaningful income.

**Loonie Doctor (Canada, physician finance).** In his 2025 highlights, the Loonie Doctor reported that **93% of his traffic came from Canada**, readers averaged 2–3 pages per visit, and roughly **250 readers had used his Qtrade link with about $63 million invested**. He described the traffic as plateaued but strong for a niche Canadian finance blog. **Lesson:** a narrow, deeply useful Canadian niche can rank alongside banks and regulators.

**Johnny Africa (non-Canadian example, U.S. blogger).** Johnny reported about **US$76,000 in blogging profit for 2025**: US$24,005 from Mediavine ads, US$43,050 from sponsorships, US$7,346 from affiliate programs and US$1,800 from trip planning, against about US$400 in expenses. He reported 2,500–3,000 pageviews a day, about 80% from organic search, and an ad RPM just under US$30 per 1,000 sessions. His earlier reports put 2019 income at about US$12,000. **Lesson:** sponsorships out-earned ads, and income grew over six-plus years.

## Common SEO blogging mistakes

- **Buying a domain before choosing a subject.** Write the one-sentence subject and 60 questions first.
- **Applying for ads on day one.** AdSense wants substantial original content; Journey needs 1,000 Tier 1 sessions.
- **Publishing mass AI or rewritten content.** It's named in Google's spam and helpful-content guidance.
- **Ignoring email.** One search update can cut traffic; a list you own doesn't disappear.
- **Writing for everyone.** Narrow niches rank faster than "lifestyle" blogs with no focus.
- **Never updating.** Posts with prices and rules go stale within a year; schedule refreshes.
- **Hiding affiliate relationships.** Disclose clearly and mark paid links.
- **Quitting at month six.** The survey's median to first dollar is 12 months.

## Which tax rules apply to an SEO blog in Canada?

Blog income (ads, affiliate commissions, sponsorships and product sales) is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both the employee and employer share of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net profit (QPP in Quebec).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

- **GST/HST:** registration is required once worldwide taxable supplies pass **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). Count all your side hustles together. The [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule doesn't apply to blogs.
- **Rates once registered:** on sponsored posts and products sold to Canadian customers, charge the rate for the customer's province on Canadian sales: 13% HST in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia (since April 1, 2025), 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I., 5% GST elsewhere plus QST in Quebec. Check the [CRA rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **U.S.-dollar income:** record ad and affiliate payments paid in U.S. dollars in Canadian dollars on your return, using a consistent exchange rate such as the Bank of Canada's.
- **Deductions:** hosting, domain, themes and plugins, keyword tools, email software, stock photos, a share of home internet and home office, and a computer through capital cost allowance.
- **Set aside money:** see [how much to set aside for taxes](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

## Provincial notes

- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP and QST applies on top of GST once registered; businesses selling to Quebec consumers face French-language rules on commercial content, so a Quebec-focused blog selling products should check with the [Office québécois de la langue française](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/).
- **Atlantic Canada and Ontario:** HST rates differ (13%–15%), which matters only once you're registered and selling to Canadian customers.
- **All provinces:** some cities require a home-based business licence; check your municipality's site.
