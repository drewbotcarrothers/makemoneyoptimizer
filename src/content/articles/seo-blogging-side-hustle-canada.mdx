## How do you start an SEO blog in Canada?

Starting an SEO blog in Canada means choosing a subject you can check, publishing original pages, and leaving ads until the site has something to host them. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Write the subject in one sentence

The sentence should name a reader and a job. "How a renter in Canada reads a lease clause" is narrower than "life." If you cannot find official pages for the facts, pick a different subject.

### Step 2: List four questions that reader actually asks

Each question becomes a page. If two questions have the same answer, you have two titles and one page. Keep one.

### Step 3: Read the spam policies before you draft

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe scraping as taking content from other sites and hosting it to manipulate rankings. Examples include republishing without original value, and copying a page and changing it only slightly. Write from the source, in your own words, and link the source. Do not spin an article.

### Step 4: Draft the four pages offline

Answer the question in the first sentences. Put the official link next to any rule or fee. If you do not know the number, say you do not, and link the page that publishes it.

### Step 5: Register a domain you are allowed to hold

If you want .ca, read CIRA's [presence requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/legal-policy-and-compliance/canadian-presence-requirements/). Put the domain in your name. A domain in a friend's account is a dispute later.

### Step 6: Put the pages on a small host

Confirm the price. Turn on HTTPS if the host offers it. Add your name and a way to contact you. A site with no person on it is harder for a reader to trust, and harder for you to stand behind.

### Step 7: Do not apply for ads on the day you publish

AdSense's [eligibility page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724) says you need your own content and that applicants must be at least 18. Its [readiness page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7299563) says pages need unique content and that ads may not be placed on scraped or copyrighted content. Four thin pages are not an application. Publish, then wait until the pages are actually useful.

### Step 8: Update a page when the source changes

A tax rate, a fee, or a program rule will move. Put the date you checked next to the claim. A stale page that still sounds certain is worse than a short page that links the live source.

### Step 9: After eight pages, decide whether the subject is holding

If you are repeating yourself, narrow the subject. If people you know still cannot say what the site is for, the sentence in step 1 is wrong. Fix the sentence before you buy a second domain.

## What should the first week of an SEO blog in Canada include?

The first week should produce a subject, four drafted pages, and a domain decision. A published site is better. An AdSense account is not the goal.

- **Day 1:** Write the subject sentence and the four questions.
- **Day 2:** Read the spam-policy examples on scraping. Open the official pages you will cite.
- **Day 3:** Draft two pages. Link every rule.
- **Day 4:** Draft two more. Cut any sentence you cannot support.
- **Day 5:** Check CIRA if you want .ca. Compare one host's live price. Do not buy a three-year bundle to "save" on a site with no pages.
- **Day 6:** Publish if the pages answer the questions. Add your name.
- **Day 7:** Read the pages on a phone. Fix the first sentence of any page that does not answer the title.

## What SEO blog mistakes are common in Canada?

Common mistakes in Canada are buying a domain before a subject, copying a competitor's article, and applying for ads before the pages exist. Search Essentials say eligibility is not a promise of indexing. Spam policies name scraped pages as a problem. AdSense says scraped or copyrighted pages can violate its rules.

Another mistake is writing "Canadians earn" without a source. If a statistic is not on an official page, leave it out. A blog that invents income figures teaches the wrong habit and can mislead a reader. The Competition Bureau's [influencer page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) is the disclosure rule when a later page recommends a product you are paid to mention. A link with no disclosure is a poor surprise.

Do not sell "page one" to a local business from this site. That is a different offer, and Google does not let you sell the ranking.

## Which tax rules apply to an SEO blog in Canada?

Blog income in Canada, if it arrives, is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your own taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Recheck program rules and tax rates when you update a page.

Keep hosting receipts and any ad or affiliate reports. A year with no revenue is still a note. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. A blog is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Ad revenue from a platform is not the same question as tax you charge a Canadian client for a service. Read the platform's tax help and the CRA page before you treat a deposit as "already handled."

Search your city for a business licence and use that city's fee if there is one. This guide does not state a licence fee from another city.
