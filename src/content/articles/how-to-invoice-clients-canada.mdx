<QuickAnswer>

- **Every invoice needs:** your name or business name, the date, a unique invoice number, the client, what you did, the total, and how and when to pay.
- **If you're registered for GST/HST,** show the tax charged (or that it's included) and your registration number. For sales of $500 or more, the CRA also expects the buyer's name, a description, and payment terms.
- **If you're not registered,** don't charge GST/HST or show a tax line.
- **Keep a copy of every invoice** as part of your CRA records.

</QuickAnswer>

To invoice a client in Canada, send a dated document with a unique invoice number, your name or business name and contact details, the client's name, a description of the work, the amount owing, and your payment terms and methods. If you are registered for GST/HST, the invoice must also show the tax and your GST/HST registration number, because the CRA lists those details as what your business clients need to claim input tax credits ([CRA input tax credit records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html)). If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST at all.

## What must a Canadian freelancer's invoice include?

There is no single legal invoice format for a sole proprietor who is not registered for GST/HST, but an invoice should identify you, the client, the work, the amount, and the terms. For GST/HST registrants, the CRA's [input tax credit chart](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) sets out what business clients need on the document, by sale size:

| Information on the invoice | Under $100 | $100 to $499.99 | $500 or more |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Your business or trading name | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Invoice date | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Total amount payable | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| GST/HST charged, or a statement that the price includes it | No | Yes | Yes |
| Your GST/HST registration number | No | Yes | Yes |
| Buyer's name or trading name | No | No | Yes |
| Brief description of the goods or services | No | No | Yes |
| Terms of payment | No | No | Yes |

Most freelancers simply include every line on every invoice. It costs nothing and means a client never has to ask for a corrected copy.

## What does a simple invoice template look like?

A one-page layout with five blocks covers almost every side hustle. Copy this outline into a document, spreadsheet, or invoicing app.

**1. Header**
- The word "Invoice"
- Invoice number (for example 2026-014)
- Invoice date and due date

**2. From**
- Your legal name or registered business name
- Address, email, phone
- GST/HST registration number (only if registered)

**3. Bill to**
- Client's name or company name
- Contact person and email
- Client's purchase order or reference number, if they gave one

**4. Line items**
- Description of each service or product, with dates
- Quantity or hours × rate = line total
- Subtotal
- GST/HST line with rate and amount (only if registered)
- Total due, with currency (CAD or USD)

**5. Payment and terms**
- Accepted payment methods (Interac e-Transfer email, bank transfer details, card link)
- Payment terms (for example "due within 15 days")
- Any late-payment terms you agreed to in your contract
- A short thank-you line

### Illustrative example

These are assumed numbers. A freelancer who is not registered for GST/HST bills a client for 8 hours of editing at an assumed $60 an hour, plus a $50 flat fee for a style sheet.

- 8 × $60 = $480
- $480 + $50 = $530 total due
- No GST/HST line, because the freelancer is not registered.

Because the total is $500 or more, if this freelancer were registered the invoice would also need the GST/HST amount, registration number, buyer's name, a description, and payment terms under the CRA chart.

## How do you send an invoice and get paid?

Send it promptly after the work or milestone, by the method your client prefers, and follow up on the due date.

### Step 1: Number invoices in sequence

A simple year-and-number scheme (2026-001, 2026-002) keeps records tidy and makes missing invoices obvious.

### Step 2: Send as a PDF or through an invoicing tool

PDFs are hard to edit by accident. Free tools can send and track invoices: Wave's [pricing page](https://www.waveapps.com/pricing) lists unlimited invoicing on its free Starter plan, with card payment processing fees per transaction.

### Step 3: Make payment easy

List exact payment details. For Canadian clients, Interac e-Transfer or direct deposit are common. Card payment links cost you a processing fee, so price that in.

### Step 4: Follow up politely on the due date

A short reminder with the invoice attached usually works. If a deposit or late fee was agreed in your [contract](/side-hustles/freelance-contract-canada/), refer to it.

### Step 5: Record the payment

Mark the invoice paid and match it to the deposit in your [business account](/side-hustles/business-bank-account-side-hustle-canada/). This is the record the CRA expects you to keep.

## What invoicing mistakes do Canadian freelancers make?

The costly ones are tax mistakes and missing details.

- **Charging GST/HST without being registered.** Only registrants charge it. See [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Forgetting to start charging after you register.** The CRA's [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page explains when the obligation begins once you pass the small supplier limit.
- **Leaving out the currency.** A US client may assume USD; say CAD or USD.
- **No due date.** "Upon receipt" invites delay; a date does not.
- **Reusing invoice numbers.** Duplicates confuse both your records and the client's.

## How do invoices fit into your side hustle taxes in Canada?

Your invoices are part of the records that support the income you report on Form T2125, and the CRA's [business records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html) page requires you to keep them, generally for six years. An invoice sent in December but paid in January can raise a timing question, and the CRA's [Guide T4002](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002.html) explains the accounting methods that decide which year it belongs to; [record-keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) and [tax prep before January](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-prep-before-january-canada/) cover year-end.
