Getting a first graphic design client in Canada means selling one defined piece, such as a one-page flyer, to a business you can describe, with a sample they can see before they pay. Graphic design freelancing suits people who can take a short brief and return a file the client may actually use.

The first client is a paid test with a size, a revision cap, and a sentence about who owns the file. It is a poor target if you need rent from the first email, or if you will not send the note until the sample feels perfect. ## What offer gets a first graphic design client in Canada?

A first graphic design client in Canada usually buys one flyer, one menu, or a short set of posts, with a size, a date, and one round of edits. "Branding" is hard to purchase. "A one-page flyer for a house cleaner" is a purchase.

Pick a pair you can see: menus for cafés, a flyer for a cleaner, a simple price sheet for a tutor. If you cannot list ten real businesses that need that sentence, the offer is still a costume.

Say what is outside the first job. A full identity, a website, and a monthly retainer are later quotes. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office explains [copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright). Put who may use the file in the note. This is not legal advice.

## Where do Canadians find a first graphic design client?

Canadians find a first graphic design client among businesses whose current flyer they can already describe, among people who have seen them finish a piece, and on a marketplace only after the sample exists. A purchased list is a weak and often unlawful start.

Local businesses with a visible gap. A PDF that is hard to read, a menu with no prices, a social profile and no one-page leave-behind. You are offering to replace a gap you can point at.

People who already know you can make the piece. That is a conversation, not a scrape.

A marketplace profile, if you use one. This guide does not state a percentage. Read the platform's own page. A profile with no sample is a wish.

If the note is a commercial electronic message, [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies. ISED's [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page says you need consent before commercial emails or texts. Implied consent from a relationship may last up to two years, or six months after an inquiry, on that page's description. The message needs your name, a mailing address, contact details valid for at least 60 days, and an unsubscribe mechanism acted on within 10 business days at no cost. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first graphic design note say?

A first graphic design note in Canada should name the gap, point at one sample, state a capped paid test, and ask one question.

**Example message.** This is a generic example you can adapt. It is not a note that produced a client.

> Hello. Your shop explains the service and does not yet have a one-page flyer a customer could keep. I made a sample flyer for that kind of business, labelled as a sample, and I can send it. A first piece would be one page, one revision, on a date we write down, at a project fee we agree before I start. The note will say you may use the file for your business. Would you like the sample?

Keep a list of commercial sends, and honour a no. Do not add a free logo because the flyer felt small.

## How do you prove graphic design skill without fake reviews?

You prove graphic design skill in Canada with one finished sample in the niche you are selling, made from public information, with anything you assumed labelled as an assumption. A fake client logo or a borrowed poster is not a portfolio.

On the sample, write "Sample, not a client project" if nobody hired you. Canva's [Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/) allows a design containing Free or Pro content to go to a single client for that client's own use, with a written agreement, and it does not let you hand over Pro content as a standalone file. Read the current agreement before you export. It can change. When a real client later allows you to show the piece, put that permission in the note.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first graphic design client look like?

A 14-day plan for a first graphic design client in Canada should produce a niche sentence, one labelled sample, a one-page agreement, and a short list of real notes. A paid yes is a bonus. Silence is a reason to improve the sample, not a reason to buy a list. Copyright ownership and the other clauses are covered in [simple freelance contracts](/side-hustles/freelance-contract-canada/).

### Days 1 and 2: Fix the offer

Write the piece and the business type in one sentence. List fifteen businesses with a flyer or menu you can describe. Read the copyright page far enough to know the agreement needs a use line.

### Days 3 to 5: Build the sample and the note

Make one page. Remove any element you cannot hand to a client. Label the file as a sample. Write size, rounds, date, price, and who may use it.

### Days 6 to 9: Send a small batch

Send five notes that name a real gap. If the message is commercial email, follow ISED's consent page. On day 8, send five more only to businesses you have not already contacted.

### Days 10 to 14: Deliver the paid test or edit the sample

If someone says yes, send the agreement before you start, stay inside the cap, and invoice the same day. If nobody has replied, edit the sample once. Do not add a second niche to escape the silence.

## What mistakes block a first graphic design client in Canada?

Common mistakes are offering a full brand, using elements the licence does not let you transfer, and doing extra rounds because a no feels rude. One piece is enough until someone pays for a second.

A profile with no sample asks the client to imagine you. Invented testimonials are not proof. Scraping addresses skips the consent rules above.

Working for exposure with no cap is a gift. Call it a gift if you choose it, and cap the page. Do not describe it as a client.

## What do days 15 to 30 look like for a first graphic design client in Canada?

Days 15 to 30 turn the first 14 days into a 30-day plan: one more round of asks, one finished job or a clear reason there was none, and numbers you can review on day 30.

### Days 15 to 21: Deliver, then ask for the next piece of work

Deliver the files in the formats you agreed on the date you agreed, and invoice the same day. In the same message, ask whether a second piece (a social template, a flyer) is on their list. Send the same offer to five new contacts you have not asked yet.

### Days 22 to 26: Compare the hours with the quote

Write down how long the work really took, including revisions and calls. If the client asked for more rounds than the scope allowed, write the number of rounds into the next quote.

### Days 27 to 30: Review the month

Fill in the P&L below with what you invoiced, the payment fee, and what you paid for software. Decide one change for month two: the price, the scope, or the channel.

### Script: the follow-up after the first job

> Thanks for signing off on the logo files. If you need matching social templates or a one-page flyer, I can quote them this week using the same colours and fonts.

This is a template, not a quote from a client. Change the details to match the job you actually did.

## Which tax rules apply to a first graphic design client in Canada?

Graphic design income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement and the file you were paid for. One flyer does not by itself cross the small-supplier line. Add other side hustles to the same pot. Graphic design is not ride-sharing unless you also carry passengers for a platform. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Confirm it on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set the tax aside.
