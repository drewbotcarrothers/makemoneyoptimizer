## How much does snow removal cost to start in Canada?

Snow removal in Canada starts at about $90–$220 when you already own boots that grip, about $200–$450 when boots and a bit of stock are still to buy, and about $800–$2,500 when a liability quote, a salt stock, and a used single-stage blower wait until the route is real. The ranges are typical Canadian retail and used prices to verify locally. They are not quotes. They assume you can store a shovel and a bag of melt, and that you are not buying a truck.

| How you start | One-time cash, about | What you can clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean shovel route | $90–$220 | An ergonomic shovel, a smaller one for steps, a bag or two of ice melt, a headlamp, and boots you already trust on ice. You have asked about liability insurance even if the quote is still in your inbox. |
| Typical neighbourhood start | $200–$450 | Better boots if you needed them, a second bag of melt, spare lamp batteries, and gloves. Still no machine. |
| Doing it properly | $800–$2,500 | The shovel kit, boots you would wear at minus 20, a liability quote you accepted, a salt stock, and a used single-stage blower only after the route is real. A new two-stage machine is an upgrade for deep snow and gravel, often $1,500–$3,500, and it is the wrong first purchase. |

Canada does not have one winter. Prairie, Ottawa, Montreal, and much of Atlantic Canada can support a season. Southern Ontario away from the snowbelts is variable. A mild coastal B.C. street should not finance a blower. Price the street you are on, including a winter that barely comes. Sidewalk deadlines are municipal. ## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for snow removal in Canada?

Ongoing snow removal costs in Canada are ice melt, insurance that does not shrink in a green January, and fuel only after a blower exists, and the break-even sketch further down keeps this guide's assumed fees. Retail ranges in the tables are typical Canadian retail and used prices to verify locally, not a study. Snow removal break-even in the sketch is a method, not a contract price and not a snowfall forecast.

**Ice melt.** The repeating supply. An icy year burns bags. Store melt dry. Pet-safer melt is not candy. Scrape first, then follow the bag rate. Salt piled on uncleared snow wastes money and hurts concrete and paws.

**Fuel and shear pins**, only if you own a blower. A shovel route's fuel bill is breakfast and boots.

**Vehicle.** $0 if you walk the route. If you drive, log the kilometres and set aside wear. A plough windrow at the end of the driveway is not a fuel question. The agreement should say whether a return visit is included, extra, or not part of the job.

**Insurance.** The seasonal premium. The premium does not shrink in a green January.

**Unpaid scope.** Roofs and ice dams injure people, and a casual policy will not treat them as a shovel job. Free sidewalks all winter destroy the price.

**Weather risk.** Per-visit pay dies in a mild winter. A seasonal contract with no trigger depth and no cap dies in a harsh one. Write the trigger in centimetres before the snow.

### Step 1: Add the one-time gear

One-time gear means the shovel kit, or the shovel kit plus a blower if the route has already earned the machine.

### Step 2: Add the seasonal fixed costs

Seasonal fixed costs mean insurance, invoicing software, and a base stock of melt.

### Step 3: List the variable cost of the visits you did

Variable cost per visit means extra melt, blower fuel, kilometres, and the time.

For a seasonal contract, contribution for the winter equals total fees collected minus variable costs for the visits you actually did. Gear is earned back when contribution covers the startup. If a heavy winter pays less per hour than a hard day-job shift, rewrite the next client's cap. Honour the written deal for the current one. Sketch a light, typical, and heavy winter. A machine payment that a light year cannot carry is not affordable.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a contract price and not a snowfall forecast.**

Shovel, lamp, two bags of melt, and batteries cost $180. You already own boots. Liability insurance in this sketch is $200 for the season. You sign four small driveways at $350 each for the season, trigger at 5 cm, windrow return included, salt included. Fees = $1,400. You bought two more bags of melt at $30, so $60. You walked. No blower fuel.

Variable and seasonal supplies beyond the opening bags = $60. Insurance $200. Contribution after those = $1,400 − $60 − $200 = $1,140. Startup gear $180 is covered inside the first seasonal cheques in this sketch, with a large remainder.

Hour test: a light winter of 6 visits versus a heavy one of 18. Four driveways times 18 visits is 72 clears. At 25 minutes each, the heavy year is 30 hours and this sketch is $1,140 ÷ 30 = $38 an hour. The light year is only 10 hours, which is why the cap belongs in the October agreement. A financed $2,000 blower on a winter of three visits flips the sketch. Do not copy $350.

## Where should snow removal spending not be cut in Canada?

Snow removal spending in Canada should not skip liability insurance before seasonal money changes hands, boots and a lamp, or the city's winter page on where snow may go. Ask whether a home policy covers the business. Slip-and-fall is the serious case. This is not insurance advice. Ask a broker while they are still answering October calls.

Boots and a lamp matter because cheap smooth soles and a dead light are how people get hurt in a driveway they were paid to make safe.

Do not push snow into the street, onto a hydrant, a bus stop, or a neighbour's cleared walk. Read the winter page. A complaint is not a rounding error, and any penalty is whatever that page describes.

Shear-pin discipline applies if you graduate to a blower. Turn the machine off and pull the key before you clear a clog.

Your body is part of the budget. Cancel a stop with black ice you cannot manage. A blower payment is not a reason to keep an unsafe driveway.

## Which tax rules apply to a snow removal budget in Canada?

Snow removal income in Canada is self-employment income, including a seasonal lump sum, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both the employee and employer portions, on the net. Salt, fuel, and repairs are current expenses, while a blower that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion. GST/HST for snow removal uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

A deposit in November is still income to track. Keep the agreements, the e-transfers, and the receipts for salt, fuel, and repairs. Capital cost allowance recovers the cost of a lasting machine over time on the business portion, in the class the CRA assigns, not necessarily in full on the September receipt. Ask an accountant if the machine is a large part of the claim. If you drive, keep a kilometre log: date, destination, kilometres, purpose. Claim the business portion only.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add lawn, cleaning, and handyman revenue to the same pot. The CRA's when-to-register page explains the timing. Snow removal is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. This guide does not explain input tax credits. If you register, confirm those rules with the CRA or a tax professional. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate, including Nova Scotia and Quebec, on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set any tax you must collect aside. A seasonal deposit is not spending money until that tax is separated.

Your contract does not erase negligence. Search your city for a business licence and the sidewalk deadline on the city's winter page. This guide does not state another city's fee or bylaw. If you pay a helper, check that province's workers' compensation rules first. Store fuel the way the can and your municipality describe.
