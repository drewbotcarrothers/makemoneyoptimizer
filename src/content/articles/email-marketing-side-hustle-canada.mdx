## What CASL rules do email marketers need to know in Canada?

Email marketers in Canada need to understand consent, identification, and unsubscribes under CASL, because clients rely on you to set campaigns up correctly. ISED's [CASL overview](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) says the law protects consumers and businesses from spam and other electronic threats. Its [consent guide](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) explains the core rules:

- **Consent first.** Businesses must obtain consent before sending commercial electronic messages such as emails or texts, and be ready to prove it.
- **Two kinds of consent.** Express consent (someone agreed) has no time limit unless withdrawn. Implied consent applies only in certain situations, such as an existing business relationship, and ISED says it may be valid for up to 2 years, or just 6 months for inquiries or applications.
- **Identify the sender.** Each message must include the business name (and anyone it's sent on behalf of), a current mailing address, and a phone number, email, or website that stays valid for at least 60 days.
- **Unsubscribe.** Every message needs an unsubscribe mechanism, and each request must be actioned within 10 business days at no cost to the recipient.
- **No misleading content.** ISED asks senders to make sure no part of the message is false or misleading, and the Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) says "free" offers must disclose all conditions.

ISED also notes that businesses may have separate privacy obligations under PIPEDA. This is general information, not legal advice; point clients to a lawyer for complex questions, such as buying lists (avoid it) or importing old contacts.

## How do you start email marketing freelancing in Canada?

Starting email marketing freelancing in Canada means learning one platform well, building a small portfolio, and pitching businesses that already have a list.

### Step 1: Learn one platform deeply

Pick one widely used platform for ecommerce, learn flows, segments, and reporting, and get comfortable with its templates.

### Step 2: Learn CASL basics

Know consent types, sender identification, and unsubscribe rules so you can flag problems before they ship.

### Step 3: Build portfolio samples

Create a welcome series, abandoned-cart flow, and two campaigns for a demo store or a real friend's business.

### Step 4: Pick a niche

Ecommerce brands in one category (pet supplies, skincare, outdoor gear) are easier to pitch than "any business."

### Step 5: Pitch businesses with lists

Look for stores with a sign-up form but no welcome email. Offer a specific fix.

### Step 6: Scope and price in writing

Separate setup projects from monthly campaigns. List deliverables, revisions, and reporting.

### Step 7: Report results honestly

Share opens, clicks, and revenue attributed by the platform, with context, rather than promising fixed results.

## What mistakes are common in email marketing freelancing in Canada?

Common email marketing freelancing mistakes in Canada are ignoring consent, promising revenue numbers, and pricing everything hourly.

- **Sending to lists without consent.** CASL applies, and clients rely on you to spot problems.
- **Missing sender details or unsubscribe.** Every commercial message needs them.
- **Guaranteeing revenue.** Results vary; report what happened instead.
- **Hourly-only pricing.** Flat setup fees plus retainers are easier to sell.
- **Weak subject lines that mislead.** Misleading content can breach the law and hurts trust.

## Which tax rules apply to email marketing freelancing in Canada?

Email marketing freelance income in Canada is generally self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with expenses such as software and a portion of your internet. If you're over 18 and net self-employment earnings exceed $3,500, you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) outside Quebec. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable sales pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html).
