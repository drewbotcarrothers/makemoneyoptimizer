## Who does TikTok and Reels in Canada suit?

TikTok and Reels in Canada suit people who can demonstrate a task: a repair, a recipe step, a software click, a local place you have permission to film. A phone and a window with light in front of your face are enough for week one. The work is a poor fit if you need the money this month, if you will not show your face or your hands, or if the only idea is reacting to other people's clips you do not have rights to use.

Views are not a pay stub. A brand deal is a separate agreement. Your own product, if you have one, is a third path. Pick one path for the first month so you can tell whether the clips are the work or the waiting is.

## How do you start TikTok and Reels in Canada?

Starting TikTok and Reels in Canada means picking one subject, posting clips you filmed, and keeping paid work in a written note. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Pick a subject you can show

One action per clip. A tool, a step, a place. "My life" is not a subject. If the clip needs a fact, use a number you can point to, or leave the number out.

### Step 2: Read the rewards page and then ignore it as a plan

Open TikTok's [Creator Rewards](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-rewards-program) article. If Canada is still absent, do not buy gear in order to qualify for a program you cannot join. Re-check the page later. Do not build week one around it.

### Step 3: Film four clips on the phone

Vertical, light in front of you, one sentence at the start that says what the clip is. Watch them back. If the voice is muddy, then price a microphone. If the frame shakes, then price a tripod.

### Step 4: Post them on one account you control

Use your name or a clear topic name. Do not post the same borrowed clip to three accounts. Original means you made it.

### Step 5: Write a one-page note for any paid ask

Who pays, how many clips, where they may be posted, whether they can run the file as an ad, the date, and the fee. No note, no filming. A direct message that says "we'll send product" is not a fee.

### Step 6: Put the disclosure where it can be seen

The Competition Bureau's [influencer page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says disclosures should be visible without an extra tap, in plain language, on each platform you use. It says linking or tagging a brand, or dropping an affiliate link, is unlikely to be enough. Say that you were paid, or that you received the product, in the video or in the first line.

### Step 7: Do not promise results you did not get

The Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) is the general rule: a claim has to be true. "This drink changed my health" is a claim. If you did not experience it, do not say it.

### Step 8: Offer the note to two local businesses you already know

A shop, a tutor, a maker. Name the fee and the number of clips. Do not add a free extra video to fill the silence.

### Step 9: After eight clips, keep the subject or narrow it

If you cannot explain the subject in one line, the account is a pile of tests. Keep the line that a stranger understood. Stop the line that needed a caption to make sense.

## What should the first week of TikTok and Reels in Canada include?

The first week should produce four filmed clips, a posted account, and a one-page paid-work note you have not necessarily used. A brand deal is a bonus.

- **Day 1:** Write the subject. List four actions you can film at home.
- **Day 2:** Read the Creator Rewards country list. Write down that in-app pay is not the plan if Canada is absent.
- **Day 3:** Film two clips. Watch them on a phone, not a laptop.
- **Day 4:** Film two more. Note whether you need a tripod.
- **Day 5:** Post. Write the paid-work note, including a disclosure line.
- **Day 6:** Show the clips to three people who do not live with you. Ask what the clip was about. If they cannot say, reshoot the opening line.
- **Day 7:** If a business asks, send the note. If nobody asks, film next week's four. Do not buy a course to explain the silence.

## What TikTok and Reels mistakes are common in Canada?

Common mistakes in Canada are buying a camera to chase a rewards program that does not list the country, posting a paid clip with the disclosure buried in hashtags, and using music or footage you do not have rights to. The Competition Bureau says a disclosure should be visible without a tap. A hashtag pile is a poor place for it.

Another mistake is treating a view count as wages. Until a written fee arrives, the account is practice. Quitting after two clips teaches you nothing about a schedule. Eight honest clips teach you the hours.

Do not film inside a business, or film a person's face, without permission. A public street is not the same as a cafe's counter or a child's school event.

## Which tax rules apply to TikTok and Reels in Canada?

Short-form income in Canada, when a business pays you or a product sells, is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your own taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Recheck the Creator Rewards country list when you revisit this guide.

Keep the agreements and the e-transfers. A quiet year is still worth a note that the amount was zero or small. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Short-form video is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside. Free product is not automatically "no income." Ask a tax adviser if a brand sends goods instead of a fee. This guide does not assign a dollar value to a free product.
