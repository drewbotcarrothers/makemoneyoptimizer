A TikTok and Reels side hustle in Canada makes money from brand deals, affiliate commissions, Instagram Gifts and Subscriptions, YouTube Shorts revenue sharing, or your own products, not from TikTok paying per view. TikTok's Creator Rewards Program still doesn't list Canada (checked October 9, 2026), but Instagram Gifts and Subscriptions do, and YouTube shares 45% of allocated Shorts revenue with partners. A Toronto creator told CBC she charged about $3,000 per TikTok at 50,000 followers.

<QuickAnswer>
- **TikTok Creator Rewards isn't open to Canadians.** TikTok's Creator Academy lists only the U.S., U.K., Germany, Japan, South Korea, France, Mexico and Brazil (checked October 9, 2026). Where it runs, it needs 18+, 10,000 followers and 100,000 views in the last 30 days, and pays on videos over 1 minute.
- **Instagram pays Canadians directly:** Gifts on Reels need 500 followers and pay US$0.01 per Star; Subscriptions need 10,000 followers. Canada is on both country lists.
- **YouTube Shorts:** with 1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days you can join the YouTube Partner Program and keep 45% of your allocated Shorts revenue.
- **Brand deals pay most:** Toronto creator Tess Barclay told CBC in June 2024 she charged about $3,000 per TikTok at 50,000 followers.
- **TikTok stays in Canada:** in March 2026 Ottawa reversed its 2024 wind-up order and let TikTok Canada keep operating under new undertakings. Paid posts need clear disclosure, and income goes on T2125.
</QuickAnswer>

## How do Canadians actually get paid for short videos?

Canadians get paid for short-form video through five routes, and only two of them are platform payouts. The money in short-form for most Canadian creators is brand deals and selling something.

| Income route | Open to Canadians? | Threshold or rate (checked Oct 9, 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok Creator Rewards | **No** | Listed countries only; 18+, 10,000 followers, 100,000 views/30 days |
| Instagram Gifts on Reels | Yes | Professional account, 18+, 500 followers; US$0.01 per Star |
| Instagram Subscriptions | Yes | Professional account, 18+, 10,000 followers |
| YouTube Shorts revenue sharing | Yes | 1,000 subscribers + 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days; creator keeps 45% of allocation |
| Brand deals and paid partnerships | Yes | Negotiated per post or package |
| Affiliate commissions | Yes | Set by each merchant or network |
| UGC (brand posts your clip) | Yes | Negotiated per video plus usage rights |
| Your own product or service | Yes | Your price |

**TikTok in Canada in 2026.** CBC reported on March 9, 2026 that the federal government reversed its November 2024 order to dissolve TikTok's Canadian business after a further national security review, letting TikTok Canada keep operating and a physical presence in Canada under legally binding undertakings on data protection. The app itself was never banned for Canadian users. In June 2024, a TikTok spokesperson pointed CBC to Creative Exchange and Creator Marketplace, tools that connect creators with advertisers, as what's available to Canadians.

## How do TikTok, Reels and Shorts compare for Canadians?

Post the same vertical clip to all three, but know what each one can pay you directly.

| | TikTok | Instagram Reels | YouTube Shorts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct platform pay in Canada | None through Creator Rewards | Gifts (500 followers), Subscriptions (10,000 followers) | Shorts revenue sharing after joining YPP |
| Entry bar | n/a | 18+, professional account | 1,000 subscribers + 10M Shorts views in 90 days |
| What you keep | n/a | US$0.01 per Star received | 45% of your allocated Shorts revenue |
| Brand-deal tools | Creator Marketplace, Creative Exchange | Paid partnership label | Branded content setting |
| Best for | Discovery and trends | Local audiences and brands already on Instagram | Long-term views that also feed a long-form channel |

**How YouTube's Shorts share works.** YouTube's help page gives its own hypothetical: if your Shorts get 1% of the engaged views counted in a country's Creator Pool, you're allocated 1% of that pool's revenue (in its example, $900), and after the 45% share you keep $405. The pool and your share change every month, so YouTube Studio's revenue tab is where you'll see your real number. YouTube also says that from February 1, 2027, Shorts ads targeted to small groups of channels will earn direct revenue at the same 45% share.

## A weekly batching routine

Most part-time creators film in one or two blocks rather than every day.

| Day | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday | Pick 5 ideas, write hooks and shot lists | 45 min |
| Monday evening | Film all 5 clips in one setup | 90 min |
| Tuesday | Edit and caption in CapCut or Descript | 90 min |
| Wednesday–Sunday | Post one clip a day to TikTok, Reels and Shorts; reply to comments | 15 min/day |
| Saturday | Review retention, pitch 2 brands | 45 min |

That's about six hours a week. Track it, because your effective hourly rate on a brand deal includes all the unpaid clips that built the audience.

## Who does TikTok and Reels suit?

Short-form video suits people who can teach, show or entertain in under a minute, post several times a week, and are patient through months of small view counts. A phone, a window and a clear point are enough to start.

## What do brand deals pay Canadian creators?

There's no official rate card. The best published Canadian data points are from creators who've told reporters what they charge.

- **Tess Barclay (Toronto).** Told CBC News in June 2024: "When I had about 50,000 followers on TikTok, I would charge about $3,000 for a TikTok." She also said everyone she's worked with makes the most from brand deals rather than TikTok's creator fund.
- **Peace Ogebule (Winnipeg).** CBC reported in January 2025 that the University of Manitoba student had 82,000 TikTok followers, about half in the U.S., and that 60% of her 2024 brand deals came from American companies. Brand deals and commissions were her two main income sources.

Brands pay for reach, but also for fit: a niche audience that buys (home renovation in Calgary, budgeting for students, Quebec food) can command higher rates than a bigger general audience. Usage rights matter too. A brand that wants to run your clip as a paid ad for 90 days is buying more than one post, so price usage separately; see [UGC pricing](/side-hustles/ugc-pricing-canada/).

## Worked math: what does each income route pay?

**Illustrative.** Follower counts, Star volumes, view counts and deal prices below are assumptions for the arithmetic except where a source is named. Conversions use the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4240 CAD per USD on October 8, 2026.

**Instagram Gifts**

- Formula: Stars received × US$0.01.
- 5,000 Stars in a month × US$0.01 = US$50 × 1.4240 = **about $71 CAD**.
- 20,000 Stars × US$0.01 = US$200 = **about $285 CAD**.

**A brand deal priced from one documented data point**

- Barclay's figure works out to $3,000 ÷ 50,000 followers = $60 per 1,000 followers.
- Applying the same ratio to an assumed 10,000-follower account: 10 × $60 = $600 a post. Applying it to 25,000 followers: $1,500.
- Real offers vary widely with niche, engagement and usage rights; treat this as a ceiling to test, not a rate card.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

### Week 1: Pick the lane

- **Day 1:** Choose one niche and one audience (for example "budget meals for Calgary students").
- **Day 2:** Switch TikTok and Instagram to professional/creator accounts and write a one-line bio with who it's for.
- **Day 3:** List 30 video ideas as questions your audience asks.
- **Days 4–7:** Film and post four clips, each answering one question in under 45 seconds.

### Week 2: Find what works

- Post one clip a day, cross-posting to TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts.
- Check retention (how much of each clip people watch), not just views.
- Remake your best clip with a sharper first two seconds.

### Week 3: Build the business side

- Make a one-page media kit in Canva: niche, audience location, follower counts, average views, three best clips, contact email.
- Join affiliate programs that fit your niche and disclose every link; see [affiliate marketing](/side-hustles/affiliate-marketing-side-hustle-canada/).
- Pitch five local businesses (template below).

### Week 4: Review and repeat

- Keep the formats that held viewers, drop the rest.
- Set up a link-in-bio page and an email signup; see [newsletter](/side-hustles/newsletter-side-hustle-canada/).
- Check whether you qualify for Instagram Gifts (500 followers).

### Days 31–90

- **Weeks 5–8:** Post 4–7 times a week. Build two or three repeatable series ("$5 dinners", "Fix it Friday").
- **Weeks 9–10:** Offer a small paid product or service tied to the niche.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Use your clips as a portfolio to sell UGC or social media services to brands.

## Workflow and templates

**The clip workflow**

1. **Hook (0–2 s):** a claim, question or result on screen.
2. **Point (2–30 s):** one idea, shown not told.
3. **Proof:** the result, a number, or before-and-after.
4. **Call to action:** follow for part 2, comment a keyword, or link in bio.
5. **Caption:** one sentence plus 3–5 specific hashtags.

**Five hook templates**

- "Three things I wish I knew before [task] in [city]."
- "This [cheap thing] replaced my [expensive thing]."
- "Stop doing [common mistake]. Do this instead."
- "How much [thing] actually costs in Canada in 2026."
- "I tried [method] for 7 days. Here's what happened."

**Brand pitch email**

> Subject: Short video idea for [brand] – [your niche] creator in [city]
>
> Hi [name], I make short videos about [niche] for [audience], mostly in [city/province]. My last 10 clips averaged [X] views and [Y]% of viewers are in Canada.
> I'd like to make [2] clips showing [specific use of their product]. Package: [2 TikToks + 2 Reels], [30]-day organic use, $[price]. Paid-ad usage is extra.
> Examples: [link 1], [link 2]. Media kit attached. Could we talk this week?

**Disclosure checklist**

- [ ] "Ad", "Sponsored" or "Paid partnership" visible without tapping "more"
- [ ] Platform's paid-partnership label switched on
- [ ] Said out loud in the video when the clip is mostly spoken
- [ ] Gifted products disclosed too
- [ ] Affiliate links labelled as earning you a commission

The Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says a material connection with a brand must be disclosed clearly, and [false or misleading claims](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) about a product are an issue for both you and the brand.

## Which tax rules apply to TikTok and Reels income?

Brand fees, affiliate commissions, Gifts and Shorts revenue are business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net profit (QPP in Quebec).

This is not tax or legal advice.

- **Gifted products** received for promotion can be taxable income; keep a list with fair market values.
- **Foreign income** (US$ payouts from Instagram, YouTube or U.S. brands) is reported in Canadian dollars; use the Bank of Canada rate for the day or an annual average consistently.
- **GST/HST:** registration is required once worldwide taxable supplies pass **$30,000** in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). The [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule doesn't apply. Services supplied to non-resident businesses can be zero-rated in some cases; confirm the rate with the [CRA rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Provincial notes:** Ontario 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% (since April 1, 2025), New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I. 15%, Quebec adds QST to GST.
- **Deductions:** a share of your phone and plan, mics, lights, editing apps, props used only for content, and home-office space where it qualifies.

## Common mistakes

- **Planning around TikTok Creator Rewards.** It isn't open in Canada.
- **Chasing views instead of a niche.** Brands pay for an audience they can sell to.
- **Underpricing usage rights.** Organic posting and paid-ad usage are different products.
- **Hiding disclosures.** "#ad" buried after 20 hashtags isn't clear disclosure.
- **One platform only.** Cross-post to Reels and Shorts, where Canadians can earn platform payouts.
- **Using music or clips you don't have rights to** in branded content.
