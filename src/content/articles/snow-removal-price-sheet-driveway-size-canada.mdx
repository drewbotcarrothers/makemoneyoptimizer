A snow removal price sheet by driveway size lets you quote in seconds and stay consistent from street to street. Instead of inventing a number at each door, you sort driveways into a few tiers and add priced extras. The tiers come from the time each size takes you. City rules decide whether the public sidewalk is even part of the job. This guide is for anyone running a snow route who wants a rate card that holds up over a whole winter.

It builds on [snow removal pricing](/side-hustles/snow-removal-pricing-canada/), which covers measuring a driveway and timing a clear. Read the [snow removal side hustle guide](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/) first if you're just starting.

## How do you set driveway size tiers for snow removal in Canada?

### Step 1: Define the tiers by what you can see

Clients can't tell you square metres, but they know how many cars fit. Use plain tiers:

- **Tier 1:** single-car, short driveway.
- **Tier 2:** single-car, long, or tandem (two cars nose to tail).
- **Tier 3:** double-wide.
- **Tier 4:** triple-wide, long double, or a shared or commercial-style pad, quoted case by case.

### Step 2: Time one of each tier

Clear one driveway of each tier at a normal snowfall and write down the minutes, including setup and pack-up. The [pricing guide](/side-hustles/snow-removal-pricing-canada/) explains how to measure in metres and time a similar clear. Your times, not someone else's rates, set your prices.

### Step 3: Turn minutes into a price

Price per tier as: minutes ÷ 60 × your target hourly rate, plus a share of travel time and fuel, rounded to a clean number. Keep the gaps between tiers in proportion to the time gaps. If Tier 3 takes about twice as long as Tier 1, it should cost roughly twice as much.

### Step 4: Set the depth trigger and surcharge

Say what depth starts a visit, such as "service begins at a set number of centimetres." Add a per-visit surcharge above a higher depth, because deep or wet snow can double your time. Seasonal contracts need a visit cap or a storm clause, as covered in the [snow removal contract checklist](/side-hustles/snow-removal-contract-checklist-canada/).

## Which add-ons belong on a Canadian snow removal price sheet?

- **Walkway and steps.** A flat add-on, since they're shovel work even when you blow the driveway.
- **City sidewalk, only where the owner is responsible.** Toronto's [sidewalk page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-maintenance/winter-maintenance/clearing-snow-and-ice-from-your-property/) says the City clears public sidewalks once 2 cm or more falls, so residents clear only smaller snowfalls. Calgary's [bylaw](https://www.calgary.ca/bylaws/snow-ice.html) gives owners 24 hours to clear bordering sidewalks to bare surface. Hamilton's [by-law](https://www.hamilton.ca/home-neighbourhood/getting-around/streets-sidewalks/sidewalk-snow-clearing) gives owners 24 hours unless the property is on the City's route.
- **Windrow return visits.** State whether a return trip after the plow is included or extra.
- **Salt or ice melt.** Price per application so material costs don't eat the margin.
- **Where the snow goes.** Don't push it onto the road. Ottawa's [roads by-law](https://ottawa.ca/en/living-ottawa/laws-licences-and-permits/laws/laws-z/use-and-care-roads-law-no-2003-498) prohibits depositing snow on a road, and Toronto's page lists a fine for pushing it onto sidewalks or roads.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with made-up numbers, not a market rate. Suppose your timed clears were 15 minutes for Tier 1, 25 for Tier 2 and 30 for Tier 3, and your target is a round hourly figure you chose. Your sheet would keep Tier 2 at roughly 1.7 times Tier 1 and Tier 3 at about twice Tier 1. Walkway and steps would be a small flat add-on, and salt a per-application charge. Heavy-snow visits above your stated depth would add a surcharge. You'd print the sheet and quote from it at every door.

## How should a snow removal quote be written in Canada?

Put the tier, add-ons, depth trigger, surcharge, visit cap, and payment terms in writing before the first snowfall. In Ontario, the province says the final price [can't be more than 10% above a written estimate](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-when-signing-or-cancelling-contract) in the contract unless the client agrees to a new price. Federally, the Competition Bureau says advertising a price that [isn't attainable because of mandatory fees](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/drip-pricing) breaks the law, except government charges such as sales tax. If every visit includes a fuel fee, build it into the tier price.

## What mistakes do people make with snow removal price sheets in Canada?

- **One flat price for every driveway.** Big driveways subsidize small ones, and you lose both.
- **Pricing the sidewalk where the city clears it.** Check the local rule first.
- **No depth trigger.** Clients and you will disagree about what counts as a snowfall.
- **Advertising a base price, then adding a mandatory fee.** Quote the full price.

## How does GST/HST apply to snow removal prices in Canada?

You register for GST/HST once your taxable sales pass $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters, under the [CRA's small supplier rules](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). After that, add the tax to your price sheet. Sales tax is the one charge you can show separately.
