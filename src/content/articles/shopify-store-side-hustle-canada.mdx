A Shopify store in Canada is your own online shop, on your own domain, where you sell products you make, source, print on demand or create digitally. Unlike Etsy or eBay, there's no marketplace sending you shoppers, so you pay a monthly plan fee and do your own marketing. As of October 2026, Shopify's Canadian Basic plan is $37 a month billed yearly, and online card payments on that plan cost 2.8% + 30¢ CAD per transaction.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Plans (Shopify Canada, October 9, 2026):** Basic $37/month, Grow $99/month and Advanced $389/month when billed yearly. New stores could start with 3 free days, then $1/month for 3 months.
- **Card rates with Shopify Payments:** 2.8% + 30¢ CAD on Basic, 2.6% + 30¢ on Grow, 2.4% + 30¢ on Advanced. Online Amex and international cards are 3.5% + 30¢ on Basic.
- **Real Canadian stores:** apparel brand Province of Canada told Shopify it grew a $1,500 investment into a side hustle that hit its first $100,000 month in 2019, five years in. Card Dynasty, a Canadian hobby store founded in 2025, passed $1 million CAD in combined online and in-store revenue in six months, according to a vendor case study.
- **Traffic is your job.** Without marketplace search, most new stores need content, email, social media or paid ads to get visitors.
- **Tax:** report on Form T2125, pay 11.9% CPP on net earnings above $3,500, and register for GST/HST after $30,000 in taxable sales. Shopify can calculate tax at checkout, but registration is your responsibility.

</QuickAnswer>

## Who a Shopify store suits

A Shopify store is a good fit if:

- You have a product idea you can describe in one sentence, for a customer you can picture.
- You can create good photos and product pages, or are willing to learn.
- You're comfortable with marketing: social media, email, content or ads.
- You can wait months for momentum. Province of Canada said it took five years to reach its first $100,000 month.

It's a weaker fit if you want buyers to find you without marketing. Marketplaces like [Etsy](/side-hustles/etsy-vs-shopify-canada/) or [Amazon FBA](/side-hustles/amazon-fba-side-hustle-canada/) bring their own shoppers, at the cost of higher fees and less control. Many sellers start on a marketplace and add a Shopify store once they have repeat customers.

**When does Grow pay for itself?** Grow costs $62 a month more than Basic and saves 0.2 percentage points on standard online card sales. That only breaks even at about $31,000 a month in card sales ($62 ÷ 0.002), so almost every side-hustle store should start on Basic.

Other costs to budget for:

- **Domain name**, bought through Shopify or a registrar such as Namecheap.
- **Theme**: Shopify offers free themes; paid themes are optional.
- **Apps**: reviews, email marketing, subscriptions and upsells can each add a monthly fee. Add them one at a time.
- **Shipping supplies and labels**, unless you sell digital or print-on-demand products.
- **Marketing**: ads, samples, photography.

## Real Canadian Shopify stores

- **Province of Canada.** In a post on Shopify's Canadian blog, the founders wrote that they kept their day jobs as Shopify experts, building stores for other brands, while running Province of Canada as a side hustle that grew from a $1,500 investment. They reached their first $100,000 month in 2019 after five years, without spending on ads in that period, and say they now have months over $100,000. Their advantage was daily content and distinctive photography.
- **Card Dynasty.** A FoxEcom case study (FoxEcom sells the Shopify theme the store uses) says Card Dynasty is a Canadian hobby and trading card store founded by Antoine Donia in 2025 and crossed $1 million CAD in combined online and in-store revenue within six months. Restock alerts that turned new inventory into immediate sales were part of the approach.

Both are well above typical results. Read them for the tactics, consistent content and fast restocks, not as income forecasts.

## Physical, print-on-demand or digital?

| Model | What you handle | Margin per sale | Cash needed up front | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physical products you make or buy | Making or sourcing, storage, packing, shipping | Highest if you price well | Inventory and supplies | Makers and curators |
| Print on demand | Designs and marketing; the supplier prints and ships | Lower, because the supplier's base price is higher | Very little | Designers testing ideas |
| Digital downloads | Creating the file and marketing | Very high; no shipping | Your time | Templates, guides, patterns |

Many successful stores mix models: a core physical product plus digital add-ons, or print-on-demand to test designs before ordering inventory.

## Getting traffic: the channels that work for small stores

Shopify gives you the store, not the shoppers. Pick one or two channels and do them consistently before adding more.

1. **Daily social content.** Province of Canada credits years of daily content and strong photography, not ads, for its growth. Short videos showing the product being made, packed or used are easy to produce from your phone.
2. **Email.** Your list is the one channel you own. Offer something small for signing up and send a short email at least every two weeks. See [email marketing](/side-hustles/email-marketing-side-hustle-canada/).
3. **Search.** Write product pages and a few helpful articles around the words your customers search. This is slow but compounds over time.
4. **Collaborations.** Send products to small creators in your niche and ask for honest posts, with paid partnerships clearly disclosed under Competition Bureau rules.
5. **Paid ads.** Useful once you know your margin per order. Start with a small daily budget, track cost per purchase, and stop anything that costs more than the order earns.
6. **Markets and pop-ups.** Selling in person at local markets with Shopify POS builds an email list and gives you instant feedback.
7. **Restock drops.** Card Dynasty's approach of alerting customers the moment new stock lands can create bursts of sales for collectible or limited products.

## The math on one order

**Illustrative example.** You sell a $40 product (before tax) to a customer in Canada on the Basic plan, paid by a standard credit card. Assume the product costs you $14 to make or buy, packaging $1.50, and you offer free shipping that costs you $10 (assumptions; use your real costs).

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sale price | $40.00 |
| Shopify Payments fee (2.8% × $40 + $0.30) | –$1.42 |
| Product cost (assumed) | –$14.00 |
| Packaging (assumed) | –$1.50 |
| Shipping (assumed) | –$10.00 |
| **Profit before plan fee, marketing and tax** | **$13.08** |

At $37 a month for Basic, you need about three orders like this a month just to cover the plan. If you spend money on ads, divide your ad spend by $13.08 to see how many orders you need to break even on it. On an international or Amex card, the fee rises to 3.5% + 30¢, or $1.70 on the same order.

The full pricing method is in [how to price products on Shopify](/side-hustles/shopify-store-pricing-canada/).

## Your launch plan: the first 30 days

### Week 1: pick a product and a customer

- Write one sentence: "I sell [product] to [customer] who want [result]."
- Check demand: search the product on Etsy, Amazon.ca and Google, and look at what competitors charge.
- Order samples, or make your first batch, and photograph them.
- Calculate your margin with the table above before you commit.

### Week 2: build the store

- Start a Shopify trial and choose a free theme.
- Create 5 to 10 product pages with clear photos, measurements, materials and shipping times.
- Write your shipping, returns and privacy policies.
- Set up taxes and shipping zones, and buy your domain.

### Week 3: soft launch

- Place a test order and refund it to check checkout, emails and payouts.
- Tell friends, family and your social followers; ask for honest feedback.
- Set up an email signup with a small welcome offer. See [email marketing](/side-hustles/email-marketing-side-hustle-canada/).
- Post content every day on one social platform where your customer spends time.

### Week 4: first sales and fixes

- Fulfil every order fast and include a thank-you note.
- Ask first buyers for reviews and photos.
- Look at analytics: where do visitors come from, and where do they leave?
- The step-by-step plan is in [how to get your first Shopify sale](/side-hustles/shopify-store-first-sale-canada/).

### Days 31 to 90

| Weeks | Focus | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | Daily content and email; improve product pages | Steady traffic |
| 7–8 | Add 3 to 5 products based on what sold | Higher order value |
| 9–10 | Test a small ad budget or collaborations with creators | Know your cost per order |
| 11–12 | Review margins, drop slow products, plan the holiday season | A repeatable system |

## Planning for the holiday season

For many small stores, the stretch from Black Friday through Boxing Day (December 26) brings a large share of the year's orders. Plan it in September and October:

- **Stock and supplies:** order inventory and packaging early; suppliers and printers get busy.
- **Gift-friendly products:** bundles, gift cards and gift wrap options raise order value.
- **Shipping cut-offs:** check Canada Post's and your courier's holiday deadlines each year and show your last order date clearly on the site.
- **Email calendar:** plan an early-access email for your list, a Black Friday offer, a last-shipping-day reminder and a gift card push for late shoppers.
- **Customer service:** set expectations on reply times, and write saved replies for common questions about shipping, returns and sizing.
- **After the rush:** January is a good time to review what sold, clear slow stock, and ask holiday buyers to join your email list.

For ideas on buying stock during sales events, see [Black Friday and Boxing Day reselling](/side-hustles/black-friday-boxing-day-reselling-canada/).

## Shipping to the US and abroad

The US ended its de minimis exemption on August 29, 2025. CFIB reports that parcels sent through Canada Post now have US duties collected even on CUSMA-qualifying goods, while commercial couriers can still apply the CUSMA exemption. If many of your customers are American, compare courier and discount-shipping options carefully and consider showing duties clearly at checkout. Importing inventory into Canada falls under CBSA's guide to importing commercial goods.

## Taxes for Shopify sellers

- **Income tax:** report sales, cost of goods, Shopify fees, apps, shipping and marketing on Form T2125. See [how to report side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/).
- **CPP:** 11.9% of net earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026 (QPP in Quebec).
- **GST/HST:** you're a small supplier until taxable sales pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Once registered, you generally charge tax based on where the customer is, such as 13% HST in Ontario or 14% in Nova Scotia. Shopify can calculate taxes at checkout, but you must register and file.
- **Digital products:** CRA's digital-economy rules apply to some sales to Canadian consumers; check the CRA's overview if you sell downloads.

## Provincial notes

- **Ontario and Atlantic provinces** charge HST (13% in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia), so customer location matters once you're registered.
- **Quebec:** QST applies on top of GST for Quebec customers once registered, and Quebec's Charter of the French Language affects how businesses present products to Quebec consumers.
- **B.C., Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** provincial sales taxes are separate from GST and are administered by the province, so check each province's rules if you sell a lot to customers there.
- **Alberta and the territories:** GST only.

## What to watch out for

- **No traffic.** Most new stores fail from a lack of visitors, not a bad website. Plan marketing before you launch.
- **App creep.** Small monthly app fees add up; review them every quarter.
- **Thin margins.** Card fees, shipping and returns take a bigger share of low-priced products, so run the order math before you add a cheap item.
- **US customs changes** affect delivery costs and buyer experience.
- **Copyright and trademarks.** Don't sell designs using brand logos, characters or team marks without a licence.
- **Chargebacks and fraud.** Use Shopify's fraud analysis and don't ship high-risk orders without checking them.
