<QuickAnswer>

- **Business name:** no registration if you trade under your own name; otherwise a $30 name request plus a $40 registration with BC Registries.
- **Two sales taxes:** 5% GST (federal, $30,000 small-supplier test) and 7% PST (provincial, its own registration). Small sellers with $10,000 or less in eligible retail sales do not have to register for PST.
- **App workers:** ride-hail and delivery platform workers get at least $21.89 per hour of engaged time from June 1, 2026, plus a per-kilometre allowance, and keep 100% of tips.
- **City licences:** Vancouver requires a business licence for a home-based business; other cities set their own rules.

</QuickAnswer>

In British Columbia, a side hustle runs into two separate sales taxes and a city licence more often than in most provinces. You register a business name only if you use something other than your own name ($30 name request plus $40 registration), you register for 5% GST after $30,000 in taxable sales, and you check PST separately because B.C.'s 7% PST has its own registration rules and a small seller exemption. App-based delivery and ride-hail workers also have a provincial minimum of $21.89 per engaged hour. This guide suits anyone in B.C. starting a service, selling goods, or working for an app.

It is one of the [side hustle rules by province](/side-hustles/side-hustle-rules-by-province-canada/) guides. For depth, see [registering a sole proprietorship](/side-hustles/register-sole-proprietorship-canada/), [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/), and [tax on reselling and gig app income](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).

## Do you need to register a side hustle in B.C.?

Only if you use a business name. B.C.'s [sole proprietorship page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/permits-licences/businesses-incorporated-companies/proprietorships-partnerships) says that if you do business under your own name, you do not need to request a business name or register with the province.

### Step 1: Request and reserve a name ($30)

The name request checks that your name does not conflict with an existing business. An approved name lasts 56 days, so register before it expires. Priority service costs $100 if you need approval in one or two business days.

### Step 2: Register the sole proprietorship ($40)

Register online through the BC Business Registry or in person at Service BC. The CRA lists British Columbia among the provinces where a business number is issued automatically when you register ([When you need a BN](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/business-registration/business-number-program-account/need-bn.html)).

### Step 3: Keep the name separate from tax registration

Registering the name does not register you for GST or PST. Those are two more decisions, covered next.

## Do B.C. side hustlers charge GST, PST, or both?

Possibly both, and they are registered separately. GST is federal: 5% on the CRA's [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), with no registration needed until taxable sales pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). The CRA requires [taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) to register even as small suppliers.

PST is provincial at 7%. B.C. says you must [register to collect PST](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst/register) if you are located in B.C. and sell or lease taxable goods, or provide software or taxable services, in the ordinary course of business. Selling physical products is the most common trigger; whether a particular service is taxable depends on B.C.'s rules, which the province explains in Bulletin PST 001, linked from the registration page.

### Who counts as a small seller for B.C. PST?

[Bulletin PST 003](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/taxes/sales-taxes/publications/pst-003-small-sellers.pdf) says you are not required to register if you qualify as a small seller. The criteria include $10,000 or less in gross revenue from retail sales of eligible goods, software and services in the previous 12 months, an estimate of $10,000 or less for the next 12 months, and no wholesale sales. Regularly selling from established commercial premises (four or more occasions in 12 months) disqualifies you even under $10,000, while sales from your home or a garage sale can still qualify. Read the bulletin for the full list of criteria before you rely on it.

### Illustrative example

A teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast. A Kelowna maker sells $7,500 of candles a year at markets and online. That is under the $10,000 small seller figure and under the $30,000 GST threshold, so no PST or GST registration is required if the other small seller criteria are met. If sales grow to $14,000, PST registration is needed while GST still is not. A $40 candle would then be $40 plus $2.80 PST (7%).

## Is there a minimum wage for gig app workers in B.C.?

Yes. B.C.'s [minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) says online platform workers must receive a minimum hourly wage for engaged time, which starts when they accept an order or request and ends when it is completed or cancelled. The rate is **$21.89 per hour from June 1, 2026**.

On top of wages, the province sets a distance allowance during engaged time: at least $0.35 per km for delivery using any transport other than walking, and at least $0.45 per km for ride-hail while operating a motor vehicle. The province's [overview for app-based workers](https://engage.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/121/2026/07/Overview-of-Minimum-Standards-and-Protections-for-App-based-Workers.pdf) adds that tips are not counted toward the minimum, 100% of tips must go to the worker, and WorkSafeBC coverage applies to app-based ride-hail and delivery workers. Waiting time between orders is not engaged time. Compare apps in [Uber Eats vs DoorDash vs Skip](/side-hustles/uber-eats-vs-doordash-vs-skip-canada/).

## Which licences do side hustles need in B.C.?

Mostly municipal ones. Vancouver's [home-based business FAQ](https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/home-based-business-web-faq.pdf) says a business licence is required to operate a home-based business, with limits such as a maximum of three customers at one time and two non-resident employees. Remote employees working from a home office are not a home-based business ([Vancouver](https://vancouver.ca/doing-business/home-based-business.aspx)). Other cities set their own rules, so search [BizPaL](https://bizpal-perle.ca/) for your municipality.

Two provincial rules catch side hustlers:

- **Child care:** a licence-not-required provider can legally care for up to two children, or one sibling group, not related to them ([B.C. child care types](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/family-social-supports/caring-for-young-children/family-information/licensed-unlicensed-child-care)). See the [babysitting guide](/side-hustles/babysitting-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Food:** B.C. allows lower-risk foods at temporary food markets; the [cottage food rules by province](/side-hustles/holiday-baking-cottage-food-rules-by-province-canada/) post covers the details.

## What are the common B.C. side hustle mistakes?

- **Registering for GST and assuming PST is done.** They are separate accounts with different thresholds.
- **Ignoring PST because sales are small.** The small seller test has several conditions, not just the $10,000 figure.
- **Skipping the city licence.** Vancouver requires one for home-based businesses.
- **Counting waiting time as engaged time.** The $21.89 minimum applies from acceptance to completion.
- **Spending the km allowance.** It is meant to offset vehicle costs, which you also track for tax; see the [mileage log guide](/side-hustles/mileage-log-cra-side-hustle-canada/).

## Which tax rules apply to side hustles in B.C.?

You report profit on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with your T1, pay both halves of CPP on net self-employment earnings, and B.C. income tax is calculated on the same return. Track PST and GST you collect separately from income. [Record keeping for side hustles](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) covers what to keep, and tools like <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can track two tax rates on invoices if you outgrow a spreadsheet.
