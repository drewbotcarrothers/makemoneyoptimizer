Starting virtual assistant work in Canada, meaning paid admin on a client's existing tools, costs very little because the software you touch is theirs. What you're budgeting for is a headset for calls, a safe place to store client logins, an email address that looks like a business if you want one, insurance once you handle customer data, and a way to invoice. ## How much does virtual assistant work cost to start in Canada?

Virtual assistant work in Canada costs $0 with a computer you own and the client's tools, $39.55 with a USB headset, $213.89 with a paid password manager, business email and a domain for a year, $563.89 with a starting liability policy, or $1,240.75 with a second monitor and a year of invoicing software. Goods and software include 13% Ontario HST; insurance is added without HST. Use your province's rate.

| Kit | What's in it | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $0 start | Computer you own, free Bitwarden, a personal email you already check, the client's own tools | $0 |
| Lean | Logitech H390 USB headset ($35.00) | $39.55 with HST |
| Standard | Lean + Bitwarden Premium for a year (US$19.80, about C$28.20) + Google Workspace Business Starter for 12 months (CA$9.20/month, $110.40) + a .ca domain at NamesPro ($15.68) | $213.89 with HST |
| Standard with insurance | Standard + professional liability at Zensurance's starting freelancer estimate (about $350/year) | about $563.89 |
| Pro | Standard with insurance + Samsung 24" LS24D300G monitor ($94.99) + FreshBooks Plus for 12 months ($42/month, $504) | $1,240.75 |

The Bitwarden price is in US dollars, converted at the Bank of Canada's rate of 1.4240 on October 8, 2026, from its [daily exchange rates](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) table. Your card's rate will differ a little. Workspace and FreshBooks are spread across a year, so most of the pro kit doesn't leave your account in week one.

## Who should use a virtual assistant startup budget in Canada?

A virtual assistant budget fits people who can do ordinary admin carefully: inbox, scheduling, order lookups and a simple spreadsheet. The lean kit fits a paid week inside a client's accounts. The standard kit fits someone pitching small businesses who wants a business address and a proper way to hold logins. The pro kit fits someone with several retainers who reconciles hours and invoices monthly.

It's a poor fit if you disappear for days, if you want to own the brand, or if the budget assumes you'll subscribe to every tool a course listed.

## Do you need a licence or insurance to be a virtual assistant in Canada?

There's no licence for general admin work. Privacy and insurance matter more.

**Privacy.** If you handle a client's customer names, emails or orders, the federal [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) rules apply to the client's commercial activity, and you're part of how they protect it. Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec have their own private-sector privacy statutes. Don't keep copies of customer lists after the work ends. This is not legal advice.

**Insurance.** Zensurance's [virtual assistant insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/virtual-assistant-insurance) describes errors and omissions, general liability and cyber cover for VAs but doesn't publish a price. Its [freelancer insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/freelancer-insurance) says freelancer insurance generally starts from $350 to $500 a year for a professional liability policy with a $1 million limit. That's an estimate, not a quote, read on October 9, 2026. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance advice.

**Business name.** If you trade under a name that isn't your own, most provinces require a registration; in Ontario that's $60 online, per the province's [fee page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit).

### A first-week sketch

**Illustrative example, not a quote, a forecast or a survey of assistant rates.** Suppose the client invites you into tools they already pay for, so those seats are $0. Suppose you buy the lean kit, $39.55, and stay on Bitwarden's free plan. Suppose a client pays an assumed $300 for a written week of inbox and calendar work, and the log shows eight hours.

Cash out = $39.55. Remainder = $300 − $39.55 = $260.45. Hour sketch = $260.45 ÷ 8 = about $32.56 before a tax set-aside.

Month two doesn't subtract the headset again. If you add Workspace at CA$9.20 and FreshBooks Lite at CA$26, month two's fixed tools are $35.20 before tax. A retainer that arrives on the 1st and is spent on the 2nd fails this sketch even when the arithmetic works. Don't copy $300.

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Subscriptions in your name:** Workspace at CA$9.20 a month, FreshBooks from CA$26, Bitwarden Premium about C$28 a year, a domain renewal at $15.68.
- **Insurance,** from about $350 a year once you carry it.
- **Scope.** "Done" has to be written. A quick look at the inbox that becomes a weekend is the expensive line.
- **Client software.** If you paid for their email, their scheduler and their store apps, you financed their business. Stop.
- **Retainers.** A retainer paid on the 1st is unearned until the hours happen.

## Where can virtual assistant startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Use the client's tools** and Bitwarden's free plan for the first week.
- **Invoice from a personal address** you already check until the address itself is part of the offer.
- **Wait on the headset** until a call is on the menu, and on the monitor until a second retainer exists.
- **Skip a website.** The offer is the task list, sent to people who already have a backlog.

## Where shouldn't you cut virtual assistant spending?

Don't skip a password manager, a written "done," or a separate place for money that isn't your fee. A login in a chat thread is how a client loses an account and you lose the client. Don't skip insurance questions when a contract asks for coverage or when you'll see customer payment details.

Keep the fee in an account you can reconcile. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account that can separate assistant income from household spending. Money the client sends you to pay a supplier isn't your fee. Don't park it in spending.

## Which tax rules apply to a virtual assistant budget in Canada?

Virtual assistant income in Canada is self-employment income, including a retainer, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. A computer or monitor that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing and other side-hustle revenue to the same total. Client funds you pass on aren't your revenue. If you're registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, using the [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.
