## How much does virtual assistant work cost to start in Canada?

Virtual assistant work in Canada starts at about $0–$80 on a computer you own, about $40–$150 when a headset and one small subscription are in the first month, and about $200–$550 when Google Workspace at the list price, FreshBooks at the list price, a domain you confirmed, and an insurance quote are included. The ranges are not quotes. Client software seats are their bill.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What you can take on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0–$80 | Inbox and a spreadsheet on tools the client invites you into. A headset only if the test includes calls. A free password manager. |
| Typical | $40–$150 | The headset, Bitwarden Premium if you want the paid tier, and a time log. Still no business email of your own. |
| Doing it properly | $200–$550 | The typical kit, Workspace Business Starter at the list price rather than only the intro offer, FreshBooks Lite at the list price, a domain if clients need a real address, a course only after you read its checkout, and a broker's quote. |

The Canadian figure is approximate. Checkout rates can differ, and they move.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for virtual assistant work in Canada?

Ongoing virtual assistant costs in Canada are the subscriptions in your name, the hours you gave away in chat, and any tool you bought that the client should have owned. Virtual assistant break-even in the sketch is a method, not a national hourly rate.

**Scope.** "Done" has to be written. A quick look at the inbox that becomes a weekend is the expensive line.

**Client software.** If you paid for their email, their scheduler, and their store apps, you financed their business. Stop.

**Retainers.** A retainer paid on the 1st is unearned until the hours happen. It is not wages on the morning it lands.

### Step 1: Add only the gear the test requires

A headset for calls. Nothing for a spreadsheet test.

### Step 2: Add subscriptions that stay in your name

Password manager, your own email if you chose it, invoicing if the month needs it. Leave the client's seats off the page.

### Step 3: Log minutes, not a mood

Date, task, and minutes. The invoice comes from the log.

### Step 4: Separate client funds from your fee

If you are ever sent money that is not your fee, it is not your fee. It does not belong in the break-even as revenue.

### Step 5: Reprice the next month from the log

If five hours of inbox took twelve, the next menu is smaller or the price is higher. Honour the written week you already sold.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of assistant rates.**

Suppose the client invites you into tools they already pay for, so those seats are $0. Suppose you buy a headset at $30, inside the $25–$80 range, and Bitwarden Premium for the year at about $28 CAD, and you use the whole annual amount in this first-month sketch so you do not hide it. Suppose FreshBooks is still on the trial, so it is $0 this month. Suppose the paid test is an assumed $300 for a defined week. That $300 is an assumption, not a market rate. Suppose the log says eight hours.

Cash out = $30 + $28 = $58. Remainder = $300 − $58 = $242. Hour sketch = $242 ÷ 8 = $30.25 before tax set-aside.

Month two in this sketch does not subtract the headset or the annual password manager again. If you add Workspace at about $10 and FreshBooks Lite at about $33, month two's fixed tools are about $43 before any new gear. A retainer that arrives on the 1st and is spent on the 2nd fails this sketch even when the arithmetic looks fine. Do not copy $300.

Virtual assistant startup spending in Canada stays lower when the first week uses the client's tools, the free password manager, and a note for the time log. The headset waits for a call. Virtual assistant spending in Canada should not skip a password manager, a written "done," or a separate place for money that is not your fee. A login in a chat thread is how a client loses an account and you lose the client. A free tier is acceptable. A notebook of passwords is not the saving.

## Which tax rules apply to a virtual assistant budget in Canada?

Virtual assistant income in Canada is self-employment income, including a retainer, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. Subscriptions and a headset used for the work are generally current expenses. A computer that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement, the time log, and the e-transfers. Client funds you pass on are not your revenue. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing and other side hustles to the same pot. Virtual assistant work is not ride-sharing.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use. The CRA describes start-up costs and professional fees among purchases that may qualify, if you were a registrant and you have the documents. If you are not registered, do not charge the tax and do not claim the credit. Confirm the current rules with the CRA or a tax professional.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set it aside.
