Freelance podcast editing in Canada means turning raw recordings into finished episodes: cutting mistakes and long pauses, cleaning noise, levelling voices to the loudness podcast apps expect, adding music and exporting files ready to publish. Canadian studios publish editing rates from $99 to $155 per episode, and Job Bank puts the median sound editor wage at $32.86 an hour. You can start with free software and a $78 pair of studio headphones, and the work repeats every week a show publishes.

## Who does podcast editing suit?

Podcast editing suits people with a good ear and a tolerance for close, repetitive listening who like a predictable weekly deadline. You hear the clipped word, the chair creak and the 12-second pause, and you enjoy making a conversation sound like the host on a good day. It is a poor fit if long listening sessions drain you or if you'd rather make your own show.

Reliability matters as much as skill. A weekly show needs the file on the same day every week, and an editor who hits that day becomes part of the show's routine. That is why many podcast editing clients stay for months or years once the workflow is set.

## What does the math look like?

**Monthly income = shows × episodes per month × price per episode.** Your effective hourly rate is price ÷ hours per episode, and the hours depend on recording quality.

**Illustrative.** The per-episode prices are the published Canadian starting rates above; the hours per episode and the number of shows are assumptions.

| Scenario | Price per episode | Hours per episode (assumed) | Effective hourly | 2 weekly shows (8 episodes/month) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minimal edit (Organized Sound entry price) | $99 | 2 | $49.50 | $792 |
| Standard edit (ShowStarter Starter) | $150 | 3 | $50.00 | $1,200 |
| Edit plus clips (ShowStarter Standard) | $250 | 5 | $50.00 | $2,000 |
| Same $150 edit on a noisy recording | $150 | 6 | $25.00 | $1,200 |

The last row is the lesson: a poor recording halves your hourly rate. Set recording standards in the agreement (separate tracks, a quiet room, a decent mic) and price heavy noise repair as an add-on. A US$60 REAPER licence, or Descript's Hobbyist plan at US$16 a month billed annually (about C$22.78 at 1.4240), pays for itself with one episode at these prices.

## What podcast editing services can you offer? 18 ideas

You can sell more than "an edit." These services fit Canadian clients, each with a reason it sells.

1. **Basic clean edit.** Remove mistakes, long pauses and false starts; the entry product every show needs.
2. **Loudness mastering.** Deliver at about −16 LKFS with true peak at −1 dBFS, the level [Apple Podcasts recommends](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/893-audio-requirements).
3. **Noise and echo repair.** Rescue episodes recorded in kitchens and offices; price it separately.
4. **Multitrack interview sync.** Line up separate host and guest tracks from Riverside or Zoom.
5. **Narrative editing.** Restructure a long conversation into a tighter story; the premium tier.
6. **Intro, outro and ad-slot assembly.** Same structure every week, so it batches well.
7. **Show notes and timestamps.** Writing-adjacent and quick once you have the transcript.
8. **Transcripts.** Accessibility and search; Descript and similar tools draft them.
9. **Audiograms and short clips.** Social promotion for each episode.
10. **Video podcast edits.** Many shows now publish on YouTube; overlap with [video editing](/side-hustles/video-editing-side-hustle-canada/).
11. **Bilingual French–English shows.** Monthly listening among French Canadians reached 33% in 2025, and few editors work in both languages.
12. **Business and B2B podcasts.** Consultants, law firms and agencies publish shows as marketing and pay reliably.
13. **Nonprofit and community shows.** Recurring seasons, often grant-funded.
14. **Church sermon and talk audio.** Weekly recordings that need cleanup and publishing.
15. **Course and webinar audio.** Coaches repurpose recordings into podcast feeds.
16. **Newcomer-community shows.** Signal Hill found 76% of newcomers to Canada listen monthly; shows in other languages need editors too.
17. **Podcast launch package.** Trailer, first three episodes, artwork handoff and hosting setup.
18. **Back-catalogue remastering.** Re-level and re-export old episodes for a relaunch.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

Four weeks is enough to learn one editor, build a portfolio and land a first weekly show.

### Week 1: Learn one editor

- Install Audacity or REAPER. Learn cuts, crossfades, noise reduction, a compressor, a limiter and loudness measurement.
- Edit three public-domain or Creative Commons interview recordings to −16 LKFS and compare them with shows you like.
- Write your personal edit checklist (template below).

### Week 2: Build before-and-after samples

- Record a deliberately messy 10-minute conversation with a friend (with permission): background noise, crosstalk, ums.
- Produce three 60-second before-and-after samples: a clean edit, a noise rescue and a tightened interview.
- Put them on a one-page site or a shared folder with your rate card.

### Week 3: Pitch 15 shows

- Find shows that publish irregularly, have uneven volume or have obvious noise: small businesses, nonprofits, local news and community shows.
- Send a short pitch with one relevant sample. Offer a paid trial edit of one episode.
- Set up a marketplace profile as a second channel; [Fiverr vs Upwork in Canada](/side-hustles/fiverr-vs-upwork-canada/) covers the fees.

### Week 4: Deliver and convert

- Use the intake form below, edit the trial episode, ask for notes.
- Offer a four-episode monthly package billed at the start of the month.
- Ask for a testimonial and one referral. Every freelancer in the examples below says referrals became their main source of work.

## Real Canadian podcast freelancers on the record

These Canadian freelancers described how they started and found work in a July 23, 2024 issue of [Pod the North](https://podthenorth.substack.com/p/how-freelance-podcast-producers-get-clients), a Canadian podcast industry newsletter. None of them published income figures there.

- **The newsletter's author** began freelance producing in 2019. Her first client came from a post in a Bunz Facebook group, early clients came through a since-disbanded podcast freelancer marketplace run by Quill Inc., and by January 2020 she had enough work to quit her startup job. Most of her later work came from referrals and website inquiries.
- **Jess Schmidt, Calgary**, had freelanced for five years. She started while finishing an MA in media production at Toronto Metropolitan University, when an instructor connected her with a professor who needed a producer for an existing show, which she still worked on. Most later work came from referrals and inbound inquiries through her website; she noted that being Calgary-based helps with clients who want a local producer.
- **Kevin Sexton** (The Paul Wells Show, CANADALAND, Millennial Money, The Atlas Obscura Podcast) left a staff podcast job, kept that company as a freelance client, and found new work by identifying who had hiring power, introducing himself with past work and answering open pitch calls.
- **Michal Stein** freelanced for about four years from August 2020 on shows including The Decibel and It's Political with Althia Raj. Her first gig was editing HuffPost Canada's Follow-Up with Althia Raj, almost all later work came by referral, and she then took a contract as a podcast producer at The Globe and Mail.

The common thread is the plan above: a few well-chosen first clients, reliable delivery and referrals.

## What mistakes do new podcast editors make?

- **Cutting too much.** Removing every breath and "um" makes a conversation sound robotic. Match the host's style.
- **Ignoring recording quality in the price.** A noisy file can double your time, as the math table shows.
- **No revision limit.** Write one round into the rate card.
- **Using unlicensed music.** Use only music the client has the rights to.
- **Skipping loudness.** Episodes far quieter than other shows make listeners reach for the volume.
- **Missing release day.** A weekly show depends on you; build a buffer day into turnaround.

## Which tax rules apply to podcast editing in Canada?

Podcast editing income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Headphones, software, storage and a home-office share can be deductible; see [side hustle expense deductions in Canada](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all your side-hustle revenue: worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive quarters. Once registered, use the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table for Canadian clients. Many podcast clients are outside Canada; the CRA's page on [GST/HST on imports and exports](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-imports-exports.html) says certain services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated, and you must keep evidence of the customer's non-residency. USD payments from marketplaces are reported in Canadian dollars.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered. French-language shows are a growing niche: monthly listening among French Canadians was 33% in 2025.
- **British Columbia:** Job Bank's sound editor median is $36.26, the highest of the provinces listed; PST is separate from GST.
- **Alberta:** GST only; sound editor median $30.00.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

The per-episode prices are established studios' starting rates on October 9, 2026; a beginner may charge less until they have samples and testimonials, and studios change their rate cards. Job Bank wages describe employees and use 2023–2024 data. Upwork's ranges are typical profile rates in U.S. dollars, before its 0% to 15% service fee, and the CAD conversions use the Bank of Canada rate for October 8, 2026. Hours per episode in the math are assumptions; track your own for the first month. Software prices are in U.S. dollars and Amazon.ca prices move daily. The tax section is general information, not advice for your situation.
