## Where can bin wash water go in Canada?

Bin wash water in Canada has to go where that city's sewer bylaw allows, and Toronto has written the point in plain language. On the City of Toronto's page about [washing cars and outdoor surfaces](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/water-environment/water-sewer-related-permits-and-bylaws/sewers-by-law/washing-cars-outdoors-surfaces/):

- No substances other than rain and melted snow may be poured or allowed to flow into an outdoor catch basin.
- Wastewater from outdoor washing must not reach any catch basin, because soaps, cleaning products, and contaminants from the surface violate storm-sewer limits. The page includes sidewalk, alley, and other outdoor washing.
- Outside garbage areas should not be cleaned using running water. If wastewater from a garbage area reaches a catch basin, the page says it will violate the limit for suspended solids and possibly food products.
- If washing is necessary, re-route the water away from a catch basin, contain it, or dispose of it in an indoor drain. Disposing in an indoor drain may require treatment to meet sanitary-sewer limits.
- The person hiring and the mobile wash business are both responsible if wastewater leaves the property into the storm system.

A pressure washer does not cancel that page. Plain water that carries bin sludge is still wastewater. Other cities write their own bylaw. Read it before you quote. If you cannot name the drain, do not take the cart.

A practical beginner method is a brush, a small amount of water, and a bucket you empty into a sanitary drain the homeowner has given you permission to use, such as a laundry sink or toilet, the way Toronto describes for a contained car wash. Do not pour the bucket down a street grate. Do not assume every homeowner wants a bucket of bin water in the house. If they do not, you need another legal disposal plan or you skip the job.

## What does bin cleaning include in Canada?

Bin cleaning in Canada, at the start, means the outside and inside of the carts the client names, a rinse you can contain, and the carts returned lids-closed to the collection spot. It does not mean washing the alley, the pad under the carts, or a dumpster enclosure, unless that surface is in the price and the water still has a legal destination.

| Cart | What people usually want | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Garbage | Inside walls and the lid rim | Liquid at the bottom. That is the wastewater |
| Recycling | Sticky residue and the lid | Do not soak paper that is still in the cart. Ask the client to empty it |
| Organics / green bin | The smell after food scraps | Food residue is exactly what Toronto warns can hit sewer limits if it reaches a catch basin |
| A shared dumpster | Often a different site and a different rule | Do not treat it as a household cart. Confirm the property manager and the drain |

Toronto's green bin page says the city collects organics from houses and from many apartments. A condo may not let you wash carts in the loading area. Ask before you book a building.

## How do you start bin cleaning in Canada?

Starting bin cleaning in Canada means practising on your own cart, writing down where the water will go, and selling a street rather than a single bin.

### Step 1: Wash your own cart and time it

Use a brush and a bucket. Note how much water you used and where you emptied it. If you needed a storm grate, stop and change the method before you sell anything.

### Step 2: Read the sewer page

Toronto's outdoor-washing page is the worked example above. Find the equivalent page in your city. Keep the link in your phone.

Brush, bucket, gloves, a simple cleaner the label allows, and a way to carry dirty water. Dollar ranges are in the [startup cost guide](/side-hustles/bin-cleaning-startup-costs-canada/). A household might have two or three carts. Write a price for the set and a day that matches collection, usually the day after the truck, when the carts are empty. This guide does not publish a national rate. Time yours.

## What mistakes are common in bin cleaning in Canada?

Common bin cleaning mistakes in Canada are spraying into the street, buying a trailer before a route exists, and washing carts that still hold recycling or organics.

- **A catch basin as the drain.** Toronto treats that as a spill risk, not a shortcut.
- **Running water on a garbage pad.** The city's own page says outside garbage areas should not be cleaned that way.
- **A commercial rig in week one.** The machine does not replace the bylaw. Costs are in the [startup guide](/side-hustles/bin-cleaning-startup-costs-canada/).
- **One driveway across town.** Travel eats a small cart fee. Stack the street.
- **No glove and no eye protection.** Bin water splashes. The job is dirty on purpose.
- **Promising to haul the city's waste.** You are washing the cart, not replacing collection.
