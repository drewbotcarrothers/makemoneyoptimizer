Starting freelance writing in Canada costs less than most side hustles, because the main tool is a computer you probably already own. What you buy is a headset for client calls, a few subscriptions, possibly a domain and, if a client asks for it, insurance. This guide prices each item from a named seller on October 9, 2026, builds three kits with HST, and works out how many paid pieces cover them.

How to find clients, write samples and send pitches is in the [freelance writing side hustle guide](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada/). This page is the budget.

## How much does freelance writing cost to start in Canada?

Freelance writing in Canada costs about $40 to start if you already have a computer, about $132 for a first month with paid tools and a domain, and about $590 with a monitor and a year of liability insurance. All three figures use prices checked on October 9, 2026 and 13% Ontario HST. None includes a new laptop.

| Kit | First-month cash with HST | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $39.55 | Headset; free Google Docs and Grammarly Free; invoices from a template |
| Standard | $132.16 | Lean + laptop stand, .ca domain, Google Workspace email, FreshBooks Lite, Grammarly Pro |
| Pro | $589.50 | Standard + 24-inch monitor and about $350 for a year of liability insurance |

US-dollar prices are converted at the [Bank of Canada's](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) October 8, 2026 rate of 1.4240. A vendor that bills in US dollars converts at its own rate on the day you pay, so the Canadian amount will move.

## Who should use a freelance writing startup budget in Canada?

This budget suits people who can turn in a clean draft on a deadline, take edits without arguing, and name the kind of writing they sell, such as service pages, blog posts or newsletters. The lean kit suits a first paid test on a computer you already own. The standard kit makes sense once a client wants a proper email address and an invoice. The pro kit suits writers working long hours, or whose contracts ask for insurance.

Freelance writing is a poor fit if you need money this week. The first client usually comes after samples and pitches, not after buying tools.

## What does a domain cost for a freelance writer in Canada?

A .ca domain costs about $10 to $20 a year, according to [CIRA](https://www.cira.ca/en/resources/news/domains/how-much-does-a-ca-domain-cost/), which runs the .ca registry and charges registrars $10.50 a year wholesale. [NamesPro.ca](https://www.namespro.ca/Pricing.asp?sec=1) listed a .ca at C$15.68 a year for new registrations and renewals on October 9, 2026. A .ca domain requires you to meet CIRA's Canadian presence requirements.

You don't need a website to get your first client. A domain becomes useful when you want an email address with your own name on it, or a portfolio page to send with pitches. CIRA notes that some registrars discount the first year and charge more on renewal, so check the renewal price before you buy.

## What does insurance cost for a freelance writer in Canada?

Professional liability insurance for a freelance writer in Canada costs roughly $350 to $600 a year for $1 million of coverage, based on broker estimates. [Zensurance's freelancer page](https://www.zensurance.com/freelancer-insurance) says freelancer policies generally start from $350 to $500 a year for professional liability with a $1 million limit. Its [writer and author page](https://www.zensurance.com/writer-and-author-insurance) says a $1 million policy for writers usually costs around $50 a month. Both were read on October 9, 2026, and both are estimates, not quotes.

Professional liability (also called errors and omissions) covers claims that your work caused a client a loss, for example a factual error in published copy. Most first clients won't ask for it. Agencies and larger companies sometimes write it into contracts. Get a quote when a contract requires it, or when you write content where a mistake could cost a client money, such as financial or health topics.

Your home insurance policy may not cover business activity. Ask your insurer. This is not insurance advice.

## What are the monthly costs for freelance writing in Canada?

Monthly costs for freelance writing in Canada are $0 on free tools, or about $59.09 with HST for the standard stack: Google Workspace ($9.20), FreshBooks Lite ($26.00) and Grammarly Pro ($17.09) add up to $52.29 before tax. The .ca domain renews at $15.68 a year, and insurance, if you buy it, is about $350 to $600 a year.

Costs that don't show on a receipt:

- **Unpaid revisions.** A third round of edits that wasn't in the agreement is time you don't get paid for. Put a revision limit in writing before you start.
- **Research time.** A technical article takes longer than a service page of the same length. Count research when you quote.
- **Marketplace fees.** If you find clients on a platform, its fee comes off every invoice. Read the fee on the platform's own page. Direct clients have no platform fee.
- **Quiet months.** Subscriptions bill whether or not you have work. The lean kit keeps a quiet month at $0.

Put client payments in a separate account so tax money doesn't get spent. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian option for keeping writing income apart from everyday spending.

## How many paid pieces does it take to break even on freelance writing?

**Year one, standard kit.** First month $132.16, plus 11 more months of tools at $59.09 (11 × $59.09 = $649.99), comes to $782.15. That's 6 posts at $150 ($782.15 ÷ $150 = 5.21, rounded up).

**Year one, pro kit.** First month $589.50, plus $649.99 of tools, comes to $1,239.49. That's 9 posts at $150 ($1,239.49 ÷ $150 = 8.26, rounded up).

Break-even covers your costs only. It doesn't count your time, income tax or CPP. If a 1,000-word post takes 4 hours including research and one revision, $150 works out to $37.50 an hour before tax. ## Where can freelance writing startup costs be lower in Canada?

Freelance writing startup costs stay lower when you write samples before paying for any tool, buy the headset only when a call is booked, and wait to add FreshBooks until you have more invoices than a template can handle. Grammarly's free plan and Google Docs are enough for a first sample. A free public style guide does the job of a course.

Skip the domain until a client asks for your portfolio link or you want a professional email address. If you use an introductory price, such as FreshBooks Lite at $1 a month for the first year, note the regular $26 a month in your budget so year two isn't a surprise.

## Where shouldn't you cut freelance writing costs in Canada?

Don't skip a written agreement. It should cover scope, the number of revision rounds, the deadline, the price and who owns the finished piece. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office explains [copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright). If the agreement doesn't say who owns the work, you can end up in a dispute over a piece you wanted to use as a sample. This is not legal advice.

Don't skip records. Keep every invoice, payment and software receipt from the first dollar. Don't buy a new laptop on credit when the one you have still works. And don't skip insurance when a contract asks for it.

For the copyright, GST/HST and tax mistakes writers make most often, see the [freelance writing guide](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada/#what-to-watch-out-for).

## Which tax rules apply to freelance writing startup costs in Canada?

Freelance writing income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both the employee and employer portions of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net business income: 11.9% on earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026.

Software subscriptions, a headset used for the work, a domain and insurance premiums are generally current expenses. A monitor or computer that lasts for years is usually claimed through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion, not deducted in full in the year you buy it.

You must register for GST/HST once your taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, counting all your businesses together. Once registered, charge the rate for the client's province, which you can look up with the CRA's [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) pages. Registrants can generally recover GST/HST paid on business purchases as [input tax credits](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html). If you're not registered, the HST in the kits above is a real cost.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.
