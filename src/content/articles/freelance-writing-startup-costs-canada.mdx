## How much does freelance writing cost to start in Canada?

Freelance writing in Canada starts at about $0–$80 when the computer and internet already exist, about $80–$180 when a headset and one month of a writing or invoicing tool are in the first month, and about $250–$700 when you add list-price accounting, a domain you confirmed at a registrar, and you have asked for an insurance quote. The ranges are not quotes. A financed laptop is outside all three columns.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What you can deliver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0–$80 | The computer you have, a free writing tool, one sample, and a one-page agreement. A headset only if a call is already booked. |
| Typical | $80–$180 | The headset, Grammarly Pro if the checkout matches the figure below, and either a notebook or FreshBooks Lite for one month at the list price rather than the introductory dollar. |
| Doing it properly | $250–$700 | The typical stack, FreshBooks on a plan that fits the client count, a domain you priced yourself, a paid course only after you read that course's checkout, and a broker's quote for liability. A new computer is still a replacement, not a costume. |

USD prices in this guide use the Bank of Canada's daily rate of 1.4188 Canadian dollars per US dollar on 29 September 2026, the latest figure on the [daily exchange rates](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) table when this page was written. The Canadian amount is approximate. A vendor may bill in US dollars or convert at its own checkout rate, and the rate will move.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for freelance writing in Canada?

Ongoing freelance writing costs in Canada are the subscriptions you kept, a domain renewal if you bought one, and the hours of research you forgot to price. Freelance writing break-even in the sketch is a method, not a per-word rate and not a survey of Canadian fees.

**Subscriptions.** Cancel the ones the sample did not need. Grammarly Free can carry a first page. Pro is a choice after you have watched the trial change a draft.

**Platform fees.** A marketplace publishes its own cut. This guide does not state a percentage you heard second-hand. Direct clients have no marketplace fee. They do have late payment.

**Unpaid scope.** A second revision round that was not in the agreement is a cost. Write the cap before the draft.

**Quiet weeks.** A subscription billed in a month with no assignment is still a subscription. The lean column exists so that month can be $0.

### Step 1: Add the one-time gear

One-time gear means the headset, if you bought it, and nothing else until a paid piece exists.

### Step 2: Add the monthly tools you will still have next month

Monthly tools mean the list price, not the trial. Put Grammarly and FreshBooks on separate lines so a promo does not hide the later invoice.

### Step 3: List the hours, including research and the revision

Hours are the variable cost. A 400-word service page and a technical article are different jobs. This guide does not publish a national writing rate.

### Step 4: Subtract fees you can point at

A marketplace fee belongs only if that marketplace's page is the one you used. GST/HST you must collect, if you are registered, is the client's tax, not extra profit.

### Step 5: Compare the remainder with the hours

If the sketch is below what you need, the next price changes or the scope shrinks. Honour the written price for the client who already agreed.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of Canadian writing rates.**

Suppose the computer is already yours, so week-one gear is a headset at the low end of the $25–$80 line: $25. Suppose you skip Grammarly Pro. Suppose FreshBooks Lite is at the list price, about $33 CAD for the month, and you ignore the $1 introductory offer so the sketch survives month 13. Suppose a local business pays an assumed $200 for one service page. That $200 is an assumption for the arithmetic, not a market rate. Suppose the work takes four hours, including one revision.

Fees collected = $200. Cash out in this sketch = $25 + $33 = $58. Remainder = $200 − $58 = $142. Hour sketch = $142 ÷ 4 = $35.50 before tax set-aside.

A second page in a later month does not subtract the headset again. If you are registered for GST/HST, tax may sit on top of the $200. That tax is not wages. Do not copy $200.

## Which tax rules apply to a freelance writing budget in Canada?

Freelance writing income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both the employee and employer portions, on the net. Software, a headset used for the work, and platform fees are generally current expenses. A computer that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion, not necessarily the full price in the month you bought it. GST/HST for freelance writing uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreements, the e-transfers, and the software receipts. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add tutoring, virtual-assistant, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Freelance writing is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent the purchase is for commercial activity. The CRA says you generally need to be a registrant when the tax was paid or became payable, and you need records before you claim. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST and do not claim the credit. Confirm the current rules with the CRA or a tax professional.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set that tax aside. A deposit is not spending money until the tax is separated.
