## How do you write a Facebook Marketplace listing that sells in Canada?

### Step 1: Title with what buyers search

Lead with brand, item, and a key detail: "IKEA Hemnes 6-drawer dresser, white." Buyers search by brand and item type, and the title is what shows in results.

### Step 2: Take more photos than you think you need

Meta's [electronics selling tips](https://www.facebook.com/help/1252783238218358) say to upload as many high-quality photos as you can and include any issues or flaws. Use daylight, a plain background, and one photo with a tape measure for size. Show scratches up close. Buyers who see flaws in advance don't haggle at the door.

### Step 3: Set the real price

Meta says setting the price as $1 or another too-low amount can be confusing or misleading and goes against its Commerce Policies. Price from recently sold comparable items in your area and leave a little room to negotiate. [Marketplace flipping pricing](/side-hustles/facebook-marketplace-flipping-pricing-canada/) walks through comps.

### Step 4: Write the description buyers need

Include condition (new or used), dimensions, model number, what's included, any flaws, and the pickup area by neighbourhood, not your address. For electronics, Meta suggests wiping personal data and noting the serial number or IMEI before you sell.

### Step 5: Check the item is legal to sell

Meta's [Commerce Policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce) prohibit items such as recalled or banned products, stolen goods, animals, alcohol, and medical products. Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says sellers of used products must follow the Canada Consumer Product Safety Act. It bans items such as baby walkers and traditional drop-side cribs. Search [Recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before listing children's products, helmets or appliances.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch, not a real sale. Suppose you're selling a used dresser. You'd take eight daylight photos, including a close-up of a scuffed corner and one with a tape measure. The title would name the brand, model and colour. The price would come from similar dressers that sold nearby. The description would list dimensions and the scuff and give your neighbourhood for pickup. When it sells, you'd mark it sold and rate the buyer.

## How do you sell safely on Facebook Marketplace in Canada?

- **Check the buyer.** Meta suggests visiting the buyer's Marketplace profile to see ratings and when the account was created.
- **Meet in public.** Meta's [meeting-in-person tips](https://www.facebook.com/help/2329750133711372?locale=en_GB) recommend a public, well-lit place, telling someone your plan, and keeping your home address private.
- **Confirm payment first.** Meta's [scams page](https://www.facebook.com/help/721562085854101/) warns against payment screenshots and overpayment offers. Check that an e-transfer has landed in your bank app before handing over the item.
- **Mark it sold.** Then rate the buyer so other sellers can see how it went.

## What listing mistakes do Canadian Marketplace sellers make?

- **$1 placeholder prices.** Meta says they go against its Commerce Policies.
- **Hiding flaws.** It costs you at pickup, either a haggle or a walk-away.
- **Posting your address.** Give the neighbourhood and share the address only once the buyer is confirmed.
- **Selling recalled children's products.** Check the recalls site first.

## How are Facebook Marketplace sales taxed in Canada?

Selling your own used items at a loss usually isn't business income. Buying to resell for profit is. Report flipping profit on Form T2125 and register for GST/HST once your taxable sales pass $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters.
