A 30-day online tutoring launch plan for Canada uses week 1 to choose subjects, set up your space, and start a record check, week 2 to set rates and a trial-lesson offer, week 3 to find first students and teach, and week 4 to lock in a weekly schedule and sort out records and tax. It suits patient explainers with solid subject knowledge who can keep a steady weekly schedule.

## What happens in week 1 of an online tutoring launch plan in Canada?

Week 1 is preparation: pick what you teach, set up your space, and start any record check families may ask for.

### Days 1 and 2: Choose subjects and levels

Pick subjects and grades you can teach without preparing every answer, and note your province's curriculum for those courses.

### Days 3 and 4: Set up your space and tools

A computer, a headset, a stable connection, a free video-call tool, and a digital whiteboard or shared document are enough to start. The [startup costs guide](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-startup-costs-canada/) lists optional upgrades.

### Days 5 to 7: Look into a police record check

Parents may ask for one when you work with children. In Ontario, the province's [police record checks page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/police-record-checks) describes three types, says to ask the requesting person which one they need, and says vulnerable sector checks must be completed by your local police service. Other provinces have their own process.

## What happens in week 2 of an online tutoring launch plan in Canada?

Week 2 is the offer: rates, policies, and a trial lesson.

### Days 8 to 10: Set rates and policies

Use the [pricing guide](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-pricing-canada/) to set per-session or package rates. Write cancellation, rescheduling, and payment policies, and how you handle session recordings, if any.

### Days 11 and 12: Write a trial-lesson offer

Describe a short first session where you assess the student's level and goals. Make clear whether it is paid or free.

### Days 13 and 14: Prepare materials

Build a simple lesson template: warm-up, main concept, practice, and a summary for the parent or student.

## What happens in week 3 of an online tutoring launch plan in Canada?

Week 3 is finding students and teaching first sessions.

### Days 15 to 17: Reach your network

Tell friends, family, and community groups that allow service posts. If you email people who are not existing contacts, read the CASL [consent rules](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) first.

### Days 18 and 19: Consider a platform

Tutoring platforms can bring students in exchange for fees or commission. Read each platform's terms and fees on its own pages before joining.

### Days 20 and 21: Run trial and first sessions

Assess the student's level, agree on goals, and send a short note after each session.

## What happens in week 4 of an online tutoring launch plan in Canada?

Week 4 sets a routine and handles records.

### Days 22 to 24: Set a weekly schedule

Offer fixed weekly slots. Consistency helps students and your planning.

### Days 25 and 26: Send progress notes

Send parents or adult students a short progress summary every few sessions.

### Days 27 to 30: Records, GST/HST, and month two

Set up an income record and check how GST/HST applies to your subjects (see the tax section below). Plan for busy periods such as [exam season tutoring](/side-hustles/exam-season-winter-tutoring-canada/).

### Illustrative example

A teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast: suppose by day 30 you have four students with one weekly session each at a rate T. Weekly revenue is 4T. If T were $40, that is $160 a week before tools and tax. Your rate and student count will differ.

## What online tutoring launch mistakes should you avoid in Canada?

Common launch mistakes are offering too many subjects, skipping a written policy, not tracking session notes, ignoring a family's request for a record check, and assuming GST/HST treatment without reading the CRA's tutoring rules.

## Which tax rules apply when launching online tutoring in Canada?

Tutoring income is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and the self-employed pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. The CRA's [GST/HST Memorandum 20-6](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-6/tutoring-equivalent-services.html) says tutoring an individual in a course approved for credit by, or following a curriculum designated by, a school authority is an exempt supply, and that the exemption covers elementary and secondary level courses but not post-secondary ones. It also says general study-skills courses are not exempt, and that music lessons are treated as exempt equivalents.

For taxable tutoring, GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. If your work mixes exempt and taxable subjects, confirm with the CRA. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
