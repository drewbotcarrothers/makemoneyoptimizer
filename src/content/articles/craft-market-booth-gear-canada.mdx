Buy a 10 × 10 pop-up with a commercial frame if you'll do more than a few outdoor markets, and always weight every leg. Lightweight recreational canopies are fine for an occasional calm-day market.

## What should Canadian market vendors check before buying?

- **Market rules.** Indoor halls often ban tents; outdoor markets often require weights on every leg and may set other canopy rules. Read the vendor agreement before buying a canopy.
- **Cold.** CCOHS's [cold environments](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/cold/cold_working.html) guidance applies to long days at outdoor winter markets: dress in layers, take warm-up breaks and stand on something insulated rather than bare pavement.
- **Wind.** Sidewalls catch wind; take them down and weight the frame more heavily on gusty days.
- **Insurance.** Many markets ask vendors for liability insurance; see [side hustle insurance](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/).
- **Third-party listings.** Several picks above are third-party offers; check the seller, shipping time and return terms before a market date.

## How do taxes treat booth gear?

Booth fees and supplies are business expenses. CRA's [classes of depreciable property](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html) page puts most equipment of $500 or more, such as a commercial canopy, in Class 8 (20%); cheaper items may be expensed or fall in other classes, so check each one. Sales tax on gear varies by province ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)), and you must register for GST/HST once taxable sales pass $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters; see [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/). This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
