- **Fill your slow season.** Lawn care pairs with snow removal; dog walking pairs with holiday pet sitting.

To add a second service to a side hustle in Canada, pick one that the same clients already need or that fills your slow season, check whether it needs a licence, permit or different insurance, price it on its own, and keep its income and costs separate in your records. A second service often beats chasing new clients, because the people who already trust you are the easiest to sell to. The tax side stays simple if you plan it: the CRA asks for a separate Form T2125 for each business activity if you have two or more ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125.html)), and the $30,000 GST/HST threshold counts them all together.

Before adding anything, check that your first service is priced right with [the first-year price review](/side-hustles/first-year-side-hustle-price-review-canada/). ## How do you choose a second service for a Canadian side hustle?

Start from demand you can already see, not from a list of trending ideas.

### Step 1: List what clients already ask for

Every "do you also do…?" message is a lead. Write them down for a month.

### Step 2: Look at your calendar's empty months

Canada's seasons make outdoor hustles lumpy. A winter service for a summer business, or the reverse, uses the same client list all year.

### Step 3: Check equipment and skills

Choose something you can do well with gear you have or can afford. The tools guides, such as [snow removal tools](/side-hustles/snow-removal-tools-canada/) and [house cleaning tools](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-tools-canada/), list starter kits.

### Step 4: Test with existing clients first

Offer it to a few regulars before advertising. Their feedback shapes the price and the checklist.

## What licences and insurance change when you add a service in Canada?

Rules attach to the work, so check each new service on its own.

### Step 1: Check your city's licence list

Some cities license only named categories. Hamilton's licence guide, for example, lists Building Repair Contractor for work such as decks, roofing, siding and replacement windows: $92 to process plus a $341 licence fee ([City of Hamilton](https://www.hamilton.ca/sites/default/files/2026-01/businesslicence-reference-guide-jan2026.pdf)). Furniture assembly and small repairs aren't on that list, so a handyman adding deck repairs crosses a line that one adding shelf mounting doesn't.

### Step 2: Check permits tied to scale

Toronto issues commercial dog walker permits for walking four to six dogs ([City of Toronto](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/commercial-dog-walker-permit/)). Moving from solo walks to group walks can mean a permit.

### Step 3: Tell your insurer

A liability policy usually describes the work it covers. Ask before you start, not after a claim. See [side hustle insurance](/side-hustles/side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers. A lawn care side hustler has 15 weekly clients from May to October and almost no income from November to April. Six of them asked last year whether they also clear snow. Before winter, the hustler buys a snow shovel set and a used snowblower, confirms with their insurer that snow clearing is covered, writes a season contract using a [snow removal contract checklist](/side-hustles/snow-removal-contract-checklist-canada/), and offers it to those six first. They track snow income separately from lawn income, so next spring they can see whether winter was worth the equipment.

## How do you price and sell a second service?

Price it from its own costs and time, not as a discount on the first.

- **Build its own price.** Use the matching pricing guide, such as [snow removal pricing](/side-hustles/snow-removal-pricing-canada/) or [window cleaning](/side-hustles/window-cleaning-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Bundle carefully.** A combined price for two services can work, but make sure each part still covers its costs.
- **Tell clients in writing.** A short message with what's included, the price and how to book.

## What mistakes do side hustlers make when adding a service?

- **Adding a service nobody asked for.** Start with demand you can see.
- **Assuming the first licence or insurance covers it.**
- **Discounting the new service to get it started** and never fixing the price.
- **Mixing the numbers.** Without separate tracking you can't tell which service pays.
- **Spreading too thin.** If quality drops on the first service, you lose both.

## How does a second service affect side hustle taxes in Canada?

If the new service is a different business activity, the CRA asks for a separate T2125 for each ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125.html)). Both count toward the $30,000 GST/HST small supplier threshold, along with any other business you run ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). Equipment bought for the new service may be a capital cost rather than a one-time expense; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).
