## How should you choose handyman tools in Canada?

Choose handyman tools in Canada by the jobs you will actually advertise, battery-platform consistency, and whether you will own or rent. A small set of tools you keep beats a 200-piece case. These are selection criteria, not test results.

| Criterion | What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Job list | Shelves, mounts, assembly, caulking, patching | Buy for that list. Not for jobs you will decline |
| Battery platform | One brand's batteries across drill, light, and later tools | Spare batteries then work in everything |
| Canadian fasteners | Robertson (square) bits as well as Phillips | Square-drive screws are common in Canadian homes |
| Weight and case | One bag or tote you can carry up stairs | You will park down the street and walk in |
| Rent or buy | A rare saw or tool for one job | Rental for one day beats owning for a maybe |
| Scope limits | Electrical, gas, and plumbing work can be regulated provincially | A tool in the bag is not a licence. Confirm locally |

## What does CCOHS say about safe use of handyman tools?

For drills, [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/power_tools/drills.html) says to disconnect power before changing bits, secure the workpiece, never drill with one hand while holding the material with the other, inspect batteries for damage, and wear safety glasses. It also says not to free a jammed bit by starting and stopping the drill.

For ladders, [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html) says falls from portable ladders are a major source of serious injury, to use a ladder designed for the task with an appropriate load rating and CSA approval, to inspect it before and after each use, not to work from the top three rungs, and not to carry objects in your hands while climbing. Those rules are why a ladder is an upgrade with a clear purpose, not a default.

## What handyman tool mistakes are common in Canada?

Common handyman tool mistakes in Canada are buying a giant combo kit before the first job, mounting without finding a stud, mixing battery platforms, using a chair as a ladder, and letting a new tool expand your scope into regulated electrical, gas, or plumbing work. Decline those jobs and refer them out.

## Which tax rules apply to handyman tools in Canada?

Handyman income is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and the self-employed pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. Anchors, screws, and caulk are ordinary supplies. Power tools that last years are usually depreciable property; the CRA says you [cannot deduct the full cost](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) in the year of purchase and claim capital cost allowance instead.

GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
