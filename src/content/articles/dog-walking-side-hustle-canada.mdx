Dog walking in Canada is paid work walking someone else’s dog, and pet sitting is a separate drop-in or overnight service to add only after those walks are calm. Dog walking suits people who like dogs, can walk in local weather, and will write down medications, vet numbers, and how to get in the door.

The leash, the keys, and the sidewalk are the work. A lamp-post tangle, a silent client, or January ice can turn a short loop into a problem. ## Who is dog walking a good fit for in Canada?

Dog walking in Canada fits people who are calm when an animal is scared or rude, who can keep the same hour on the days they promise, and whose housing allows an emergency plan if a pet cannot stay in the client’s home. Dog walking is a poor fit for someone with an allergy, an unpredictable travel schedule, or a wish to group several unknown dogs on day one. Dog walking is also a poor fit when saying no is uncomfortable, including to a dog that lunges, guards food, or has no vaccines on record.

Pet sitting overnight is a different product from a 30-minute walk. Do not advertise both until the walk has gone well. Overnight sitting means sleeping in a stranger’s home or hosting a pet in yours. Insurance, keys, and trust all get heavier.

## How should dog walking be priced in Canada?

Dog walking prices in Canada vary with the city, the dog’s size, and whether the visit is one dog or a small group you already know. A downtown condo route and a suburban visit with a drive are different dog walking products, so price the trip and the update the owner expects, and check local ads before setting a number. The guide does not publish a national dog walking rate.

A practical list is a solo neighbourhood walk of about 30 minutes, a longer walk or a puppy who stops often, a drop-in priced apart from a walk, overnight sitting by the night with a written list, and extra dogs only after you know them. Holiday weeks need a written price and a weather rule before owners ask. Do not promise a walk during a weather warning. Promise an early message and a reschedule or refund under a rule you wrote down.

One regular weekday client is a stronger start than five one-off walks. Chasing a new dog every day is a more tiring business.

## How do you start dog walking in Canada?

Starting dog walking in Canada means picking a small area, writing limits before you meet a dog, checking the city’s leash pages, and doing one paid solo walk before any group. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Choose a service area you can reach calmly

Pick a few blocks, one condo cluster, or a suburb you already walk. Grouping clients is how the hourly rate survives. A single dog 40 minutes away is a favour, not a route.

### Step 2: Write your limits before you meet a dog

Set a maximum number of dogs, sizes you will not take, and a no for dogs with a bite history. Off-leash waits until the owner, the park rules, and your insurance all allow it. Intact dogs, reactivity, and medications are questions, not surprises. If you cannot physically hold the dog, you do not take the dog.

### Step 3: Check the city’s rules

Look up leash, off-leash, stoop-and-scoop, and business-licence pages for your city. A rule posted for one city is not a rule in another. Follow the leash and waste rules on the route you actually walk, and carry bags. This guide does not state a national dog-walking licence fee.

### Step 4: Set up keys and updates

Agree how you enter: a lock box, a concierge, or a family member. Keys get a code, not an address tag. After each walk, send two sentences and one photo of the dog. Keep the photo ordinary and the dog identifiable.

### Step 5: Do a paid meet-and-greet

Walk together once. Watch how the dog greets you, how the dog reacts to bikes and other dogs, and whether the owner minimizes a real problem. Ask about vaccines, the vet’s contact, and what to do if you cannot reach the owner. Collect an emergency number that is not only the owner’s phone.

### Step 6: Start with solo walks

One dog, same time, for two weeks. Add a second dog only if both are easy to handle. Group walks are a separate skill, and a loose dog in a group becomes your problem to explain.

### Step 7: Winterize the route

In much of Canada the sidewalk is the hazard. Boots with grip, a shorter leash on ice, and a salt rule belong in the offer. Wipe paws if the owner asks, and avoid heavily salted corners when paws are cracking. Extreme cold is a reason to shorten the outing. Say so in the message.

### Step 8: Price a package after three solo clients stick

Offer a weekly pack, such as five noon walks, paid at the start of the week or the month. Put the weather and illness rule in the same note.

### Step 9: Add sitting only when walks are calm

Overnight or vacation sitting needs a written list: plants are not your job unless listed, mail, medications with a log, and who may visit. Photograph existing damage when you arrive for a multi-day sit. That photo is kinder than a dispute about a scratched door.

## What can the first week of dog walking look like in Canada?

The first week of dog walking in Canada is a neighbourhood, a leash, one meet-and-greet, and one paid solo walk. A second client waits until your schedule and your knees agree.

- **Day 1:** Define the neighbourhood and the solo-walk offer. Check leash and business-licence pages for your city.
- **Day 2:** Buy a leash and bags. Draft a short questionnaire: vaccines, vet, reactivity, food, key plan, emergency contact.
- **Day 3:** Tell five people who live nearby, and post once in one local group.
- **Day 4:** Do one meet-and-greet. If the dog is a poor fit, say no the same day.
- **Day 5:** Do the first paid solo walk. Send a photo and a note within 15 minutes of finishing.
- **Day 6:** Repeat. Notice ice, construction, and off-leash park exits.
- **Day 7:** Decide whether a second client fits the same hour. If your knees or your schedule complained, stay at one dog.

## What is easy to miss on a dog walking route in Canada?

Dog walking in Canada depends on the hour the owner cannot cover, on keys stored without a street name, and on shortening the walk in extreme heat or extreme cold. A midday walk is one offer for owners who are away then. Evenings are full of owners who can walk their own dogs.

A reactive dog on a sidewalk is a handling problem. Training is a different service. Refer the owner out unless you are actually a trainer.

Do not store alarm codes in a phone contact named with the street address. A separate note with a code is safer than a labelled envelope in a bag.

Pavement that hurts your hand can hurt paws. Shorten the walk, carry water if the owner agrees, and skip midday asphalt in a heat wave. Extreme cold gets the same honesty.

If you drive dogs, ask your auto insurer before you transport animals for pay. Staying on foot in one neighbourhood avoids that question. This is not insurance advice.

## What mistakes do new dog walkers in Canada make?

New dog walkers in Canada get into trouble on dog walking routes by taking a crowd of unknown dogs, by skipping the vaccine question, and by going off-leash where the park is not off-leash. A photo of a pack is not a reason to add dogs. “He’s friendly” is not a health record.

Bylaw fines and lost dogs are yours to explain if the park rules were ignored. Holiday sits that are priced like an ordinary noon walk lead to an exhausted week in someone else’s house. Price the holiday as a premium or decline it.

The client’s home is not a place to host friends. You are a guest with a key. Leave the fridge and the mail alone unless the task list includes them.

## Which tax and safety rules apply to dog walking in Canada?

Dog walking fees in Canada are generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole Canada Pension Plan contribution on the net, as described under [Contributions to the Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). GST/HST for dog walking follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, veterinary, or insurance advice.

Track fees whether they arrive by e-transfer or an app. Keep supply receipts. An app may send a T4A. Your own record still matters. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies — you and associates — of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Under that test, registration is optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA’s when-to-register page. Confirm the rate for the province where the walk happens, including Quebec, on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

A bite, a fall involving a leash, or a pet that escapes can become a dispute. This guide does not state provincial liability rules. Ask your insurer whether paid dog walking is covered, and price a pet-care liability policy when you have regular clients. If you believe an animal is in danger, contact the humane society or animal-protection service in your province. You do not have to become the family’s counsellor.
