Dog walking in Canada is paid work walking other people's dogs, usually a 30-minute midday walk for owners who are at work, sold by the walk or as a weekly package. Rover's own data puts the average 30-minute walk at about $21 nationally and $20.73 in Toronto, and a walker who keeps five weekday regulars can bill around $600 a week. Start with solo walks in a few blocks you can reach on foot, then add drop-ins, sitting or permitted group walks.

## Who is dog walking a good fit for in Canada?

Dog walking fits people who are home or nearby on weekdays between about 10 a.m. and 3 p.m., can walk in rain, heat and ice, and are calm with strong or nervous dogs. It's one of the cheapest service businesses to start, and the work comes in recurring weekly slots.

It suits:

- **Remote workers and shift workers** with a free lunch hour or a gap between shifts.
- **Students** with flexible class schedules near dense neighbourhoods.
- **Retirees and semi-retirees** who want daily exercise and steady, small income.
- **Condo and townhouse dwellers** in dense areas, where five clients can sit within a 10-minute walk.
- **People already doing neighbourhood work** like [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada/), [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/) or [house cleaning](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-side-hustle-canada/), who can sell to the same households.

It's a poor fit if you have pet allergies, can't commit to the same hour every weekday, or find it hard to turn down a dog that lunges, guards food or has no vaccine record. Overnight pet sitting is a different product, with keys, a stranger's home and longer liability, so add it only after your walks are running smoothly.

## How does dog walking pay in Canada?

Dog walkers charge per walk, not per hour, and most sell 30- and 60-minute walks, with extras for added dogs, puppies and holidays. You get paid either directly by the owner (e-transfer or card) or through an app, which takes a percentage.

**Published Canadian dog walking prices**

|---|---|---|
| Rover, Toronto price guide (September 2025 data) | 30-minute walk | $16–$25, average $20.73 |
| Rover, Toronto price guide (September 2025 data) | 60-minute walk | $28.33–$37.33, average $33.06 |
| Rover, national figure in the same guide | 30-minute walk | $21 average |
| Rover Toronto search page (October 2026) | Walk, 1 dog, incl. 11% owner fee | Average $22, median $20, most $14–$34 |
| Rover Vancouver search page (October 2026) | Walk, 1 dog, incl. 11% owner fee | Average $23, median $20, most $13–$41 |

All prices checked October 9, 2026. The search-page figures include Rover's 11% owner booking fee, so the walker's listed rate is lower. Independent Toronto companies publish their own price guides, and these often sit above the Rover average because they include insurance, staff and scheduling.

**Employee wage benchmark.** Job Bank groups dog walkers under pet groomers and animal care workers (NOC 65220). Its wage report, updated November 19, 2025, shows a **$18.00 national median** (range $15.00–$25.50). Medians by province: British Columbia $19.50, Ontario $19.00 (Toronto region $20.00), Quebec $18.57, Prince Edward Island $17.30, Alberta $17.00, Nova Scotia $17.00, Manitoba $16.40, New Brunswick $16.25, Saskatchewan $15.70. This is what employees of kennels and walking companies earn per hour. As your own boss you set a per-walk price, so the number to compare is your price ÷ the time each walk really takes, including travel.

### How much does Rover take?

Rover's Canadian help centre says it applies a **20% service fee** per booking. Sitters who live in or near Saskatoon, Regina, Québec, Nanaimo, Calgary, Vancouver and Ottawa-Gatineau are in a pilot with tiered fees by client: **30%** while that client's booking history totals $499 or less, **15%** from $500 to $999, and **10%** at $1,000 or more (both pages checked October 9, 2026). The pilot rewards keeping clients on the platform for a long time. Elsewhere it's a flat 20%.

The fee pays for the client search, payments, background check, reviews and the Rover Guarantee, which Rover says covers up to $25,000 of eligible vet care. Direct clients cost nothing per walk but need your own insurance, booking and invoices. The [Rover review for Canadian sitters](/side-hustles/rover-review-canada/) covers payouts and T4A slips in detail.

## Worked math: what does a dog walking route earn?

The two levers are price per walk and how many walks you fit into one block of time without long gaps or travel.

**Illustrative.** The walker's rate, the number of clients, the travel time and the weeks worked are assumptions. The rate is close to Rover's published Toronto average.

**Scenario A: five weekday solo walks**

- 5 walks × $25 = $125 a day.
- Each walk takes 30 minutes plus about 10 minutes to reach the next dog: 5 × 40 min = 3 hours 20 minutes.
- $125 ÷ 3.33 hours = **about $37.50 an hour** before tax and costs.
- 5 days × $125 = $625 a week. Over 48 weeks: **$30,000 a year**, which is exactly the GST/HST small-supplier line, so a route this size needs to plan for registration.

**Scenario B: the same walks booked through Rover**

| Fee level | Walker's rate | Fee | Walker keeps | 5 walks × 5 days × 48 weeks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard (20%) | $20 | $4.00 | $16.00 | $19,200 |
| Pilot city, new client (30%) | $20 | $6.00 | $14.00 | $16,800 |
| Pilot city, client past $1,000 (10%) | $20 | $2.00 | $18.00 | $21,600 |

**Scenario C: a permitted group walk in Toronto**

- 4 dogs on a 60-minute group outing at an assumed $22 each = $88 for the hour.
- Fixed costs: the Commercial Dog Walker Permit at $305.99 a year, plus $2 million of commercial general liability insurance (get a quote; premiums vary).
- If you run one group a day, five days a week for 48 weeks: 240 hours × $88 = **$21,120** before the permit, insurance and any vehicle costs. The same 240 hours of one-dog walks at $25 per 30 minutes (two walks an hour, no travel) would bill $12,000.
- Group walks earn more per hour but need dogs that get along, the permit, insurance and either a vehicle or a park within walking distance of every client.

The single biggest variable is clustering. If every dog sits within a few blocks, travel shrinks and the hourly rate climbs. A route that zig-zags across a city can halve it.

## What are the rules for dog walkers in Canadian cities?

Most cities don't license someone walking one to three dogs, but several regulate how many dogs one person can control and where. Check your city's parks and animal bylaw before you take a fourth dog.

- **Toronto:** No one may control more than three dogs in a city park without a **Commercial Dog Walker Permit**, and the maximum is six. Fees from January 1, 2026 are **$305.99 a year**, $153.00 for six months or $76.50 for three months, before tax. Applicants must show **$2,000,000** of commercial general liability insurance naming the City as an additional insured, and every dog must wear a valid Toronto licence tag.
- **Calgary:** The Responsible Pet Ownership Bylaw lets one person have up to six dogs off-leash at once in an off-leash area. A walker with a Dogwalker Permit can exceed that, but the city can set a lower maximum as a permit condition.
- **West Vancouver** and other B.C. municipalities set their own dog-walker counts; see the [Vancouver guide](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-vancouver-canada/).
- **Everywhere:** stoop-and-scoop, leash zones and off-leash-park hours apply to you as the person in control of the dog, and fines are yours to pay.

City-by-city notes are in the [Toronto](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-toronto-canada/), [Calgary](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-calgary-canada/) and [Hamilton](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-hamilton-canada/) guides.

## 18 dog walking services and client types to target

1. **Midday weekday walks for office workers.** The core product: same dog, same time, five days a week.

7. **Post-surgery owners.** Short-term bookings while someone recovers.
8. **Condo towers.** One building can hold several clients; ask the property manager about a lobby notice.
9. **Shift workers (nurses, trades).** Odd-hour walks command a premium.
10. **High-energy breeds.** 60-minute or running walks for huskies, shepherds and retrievers.
11. **Weekend and holiday coverage.** Owners away for the day.
12. **Drop-in visits.** Feeding, water, a yard break and a photo.

## Do dog walkers need insurance in Canada?

Insurance isn't legally required for one to three dogs in most places, but Toronto requires $2 million of commercial general liability insurance for its group-walk permit, and owners increasingly ask. A dog bite, a leash that trips a cyclist or a dog that slips its collar and gets hit are the claims to ask a broker about. Look for general liability plus care, custody and control (sometimes called animal bailee) coverage, which covers the dogs in your care.

Rover's guarantee covers eligible vet costs on Rover bookings only. For direct clients, get quotes; [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/) compares online options. If you drive dogs, tell your auto insurer first.

## Real Canadian dog walkers

These businesses are publicly documented. Their results aren't typical.

**Dogs on the Go! (Richmond, B.C.).** CBC News reported on June 25, 2022 that owner Kari Hanninen started the company in 2019 walking one or two dogs once a day. By 2022 she was taking two groups of six dogs out twice a day, usually to the off-leash area at McDonald Beach Park, and offered up to two overnight boarding spots. She told clients who knew their days to book early because spots filled quickly. **Lesson:** a waitlist is a signal to raise prices or move to permitted group walks.

**Millbrook Pet Care (Peterborough County, Ontario).** kawarthaNOW reported on March 14, 2025 that Riley Friesen, who worked as a pet care provider while studying at Trent University, launched the business in November 2023. Each weekday morning she picks up about a dozen dogs in a painted "doggy bus" for an hour-long off-leash pack walk on a 191-acre family farm, and also offers solo walks for reactive dogs, drop-in sitting and overnight care. In her first year she drove the dogs in her own car until she ran out of room. **Lesson:** in rural areas, pickup and a safe private space can be the product.

**Roger Leonhardt (Toronto).** Canadian Immigrant reported on June 24, 2025 that Leonhardt left a tech sales job in the early 2000s to walk dogs and at his peak walked up to 40 dogs a day in Toronto's east-end parks and trails. He later launched a natural tick repellent, Chase Away, selling at pet expos and online. **Lesson:** a walking route builds the local trust and audience for other pet products and services.

## How to start dog walking: a 30-day plan

### Week 1: Set up the offer

- **Day 1:** Choose a service area you can walk or bike between in under 10 minutes. Note dense blocks, condos and parks.
- **Day 2:** Read your city's parks and animal bylaws: leash zones, dog limits and any walker permit.
- **Day 3:** Write your limits: dogs per walk, sizes, no bite history, vaccines required.
- **Day 4:** Set prices: 30-minute walk, 60-minute walk, extra dog, puppy visit, holiday surcharge. Use the Rover averages above as your benchmark.
- **Day 5:** Buy the minimum kit: backup leash, bags, first aid kit, and cleats if it's winter.
- **Days 6–7:** Write your intake form and service agreement (templates below). Ask a broker or online insurer for a liability quote.

### Week 2: Get the first clients

- Tell neighbours, friends and family; post once in local community groups following each group's rules.
- Put a flyer on condo and vet-clinic notice boards where allowed.
- Optional: build a Rover profile with clear photos and your limits. See [first dog walking clients](/side-hustles/dog-walking-first-clients-canada/).
- Claim a free Google Business Profile if you qualify; Google says you can [add or claim a Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778?hl=en) at no charge. If you email people who aren't already contacts, read the CASL [consent rules](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) first: commercial electronic messages generally need express or implied consent.
- Book meet-and-greets with the owner present.

### Week 3: Run solo walks

- Walk each new dog alone for at least two weeks before pairing.
- Send a photo and a two-line note after every walk.
- Track every walk's start, end and travel time.

### Week 4: Measure and package

- Work out your real per-hour rate including travel.
- Offer a weekly package (five walks paid in advance).
- Ask your happiest client for a review and a referral.

### Days 31–90

- **Weeks 5–6:** Add drop-in visits and cat care for existing clients.
- **Weeks 7–8:** Fill gaps in your midday block; decline clients outside your area or price them higher.
- **Weeks 9–10:** If you want group walks, get the permit and insurance your city requires, and test pairs of dogs that already know each other.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Book holiday sitting early and post your December and summer rates.

## How do you grow beyond solo walks?

Most walkers grow in one of three directions.

1. **More services per client.** Drop-ins, cat visits, overnight sitting and holiday coverage raise the value of each household without more marketing. An overnight sit is priced by the night, so one booking can be worth several walks.
2. **Group walks.** Permits, insurance and careful dog matching, as Hanninen and Friesen did.
3. **Hire walkers.** Once your midday block is full, hire or contract another walker; you'll need clear rules on whether they're employees or contractors, and software like Time To Pet's Team plan.

If you'd rather add people work alongside dog work, see [personal training](/side-hustles/personal-training-side-hustle-canada/) or [babysitting](/side-hustles/babysitting-side-hustle-canada/), including provincial limits on unlicensed child care.

## Which tax rules apply to dog walking in Canada?

Dog walking fees are self-employment income. Report them on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and pay both the employee and employer share of [Canada Pension Plan contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on your net profit (QPP in Quebec).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

- **GST/HST:** registration is required once your worldwide taxable supplies pass **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA small-supplier rule](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). The first-dollar rule for [taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) doesn't apply. Scenario A above lands right at $30,000, so track your running total.
- **Rates once registered:** charge the rate where the walk happens: 13% HST in Ontario, 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I., 14% in Nova Scotia (since April 1, 2025), and 5% GST elsewhere, plus QST in Quebec. Confirm with the [CRA charge-and-collect table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Platform income:** Rover may issue a slip, but report all fees, including e-transfers and tips.
- **Deductions:** bags, leashes, first aid kits, permits, insurance, booking software, phone (business share) and app fees. Keep receipts.
- **Vehicle:** if you drive between dogs, keep a kilometre log for the business share.

## Common dog walking mistakes

- **Taking unknown dogs into a group.** Walk every new dog alone first.
- **Skipping the vaccine and bite-history questions.** "He's friendly" isn't a health record.
- **A scattered route.** Travel time is unpaid; decline far-off clients or price them higher.
- **Off-leash where it isn't allowed.** Fines and lost dogs are on you.
- **Labelled keys.** Store keys with a code, never an address.
- **Underpricing holidays.** December and long weekends sell out; charge a surcharge.

More in [dog walking mistakes](/side-hustles/dog-walking-mistakes-canada/). For tasks that aren't pets, see [odd-job apps](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-side-hustle-canada/).

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Rover's averages describe prices listed on one platform at a point in time (September 2025 for the Toronto guide, October 2026 for the search pages) and include its owner fee where noted. Independent walkers, companies and small towns price differently. The Job Bank figures are employee wages, not self-employed earnings. The route math is illustrative and leaves out unpaid admin, cancellations, sick days and seasonal dips. City permit fees and dog limits change; check your own city's bylaw before taking a fourth dog. The operators above were chosen because their stories are documented, not because they're typical.
