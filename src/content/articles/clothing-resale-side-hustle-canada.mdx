It suits people who can measure a garment, photograph its flaws, and pack a parcel the same week. Start with clothes you already own, and buy stock only after a few sales show what moves.

The two apps charge very differently, so the same $40 jacket nets a different amount on each. Children's clothing has federal safety rules that apply to used items too.

## Who does clothing resale in Canada suit?

Clothing resale in Canada suits people who know sizes and brands in at least one style, can measure a garment flat, and will answer messages and ship within a few days. A category you already wear, such as outdoor jackets, vintage denim, or running shoes, is enough to start.

It is a poor fit if you dislike handling other people's clothes, have no space for a bin or two, or need fast cash: shipped sales depend on a buyer finding the listing, which can take weeks. Clothing also returns more questions about fit than most categories, so measurements matter.

## Which apps can Canadians use to resell clothes?

Canadians can resell clothes on Poshmark Canada and Depop, plus general marketplaces such as eBay.ca and local-pickup apps. Vinted is the one that often comes up and does not appear to serve Canada.

**Poshmark Canada.** Poshmark's [getting started guide](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/get_started_on_poshmark) says listing is free, sales under C$20 pay a flat C$3.95, and sales of C$20 or more pay 20%, with a prepaid label included. The [shipping guide](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/how_to_ship) says to print that label and drop the parcel at a mailbox if it fits, or at Canada Post. The [label upgrade page](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/how_to_get_overweight_shipping_label) says the label covers up to 2.5 kg without an extra fee, and heavier parcels need an upgrade deducted from earnings.

**Depop.** Depop's [seller fees page](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001791127-Seller-fees-and-charges) says sellers outside the UK, US, and Australia pay a 10% selling fee on the item price, excluding taxes, and on the shipping cost if they did not use a Depop shipping label. Those sellers pay payment processing through PayPal. Boosted listings cost an 8% boosting fee for sellers outside the UK and US. [PayPal Canada's fee page](https://www.paypal.com/ca/business/paypal-business-fees) lists 2.90% + C$0.30 for domestic commercial transactions, with extra percentages for international payments and currency conversion. Check the fee in your own PayPal account, as Depop suggests.

**Vinted.** Vinted's [international sales help page](https://www.vinted.com/help/1555) describes connections between the US and the UK. This guide found no Vinted help page for Canada. Treat Vinted as unavailable unless its sign-up accepts a Canadian address when you try it.

## What would an assumed clothing sale look like in Canada?

An assumed clothing sale in Canada, using the published fee rules above, shows why the same price nets differently on each app. The figures are assumptions, not typical results.

### Illustrative example

Suppose you sell a jacket you already own for C$40.

- **Poshmark:** 20% × $40 = $8. You keep $40 − $8 = $32, and the label is included up to 2.5 kg.
- **Depop:** suppose the buyer pays $40 plus $12 shipping, and you buy your own $12 label. The selling fee is 10% × ($40 + $12) = $5.20. PayPal, at the domestic rate, is 2.90% × $52 + $0.30 = $1.81. You keep $52 − $5.20 − $1.81 − $12 = $32.99 before tax.

If the jacket was thrifted for an assumed $10, subtract that too: $22 on Poshmark and $22.99 on Depop in this sketch. A real PayPal fee can differ by account. These figures are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## How do you start clothing resale in Canada?

Starting clothing resale in Canada means picking one style, listing clothes you already own with measurements and flaw photos, and shipping promptly before you buy anything to resell. The steps below follow that order.

### Step 1: Pick one style you can judge

Choose sizes and brands you know. You will spot fakes, wear, and fit problems faster.

### Step 2: Pull 10 to 20 items from your closet

Wash them. Check seams, zips, pilling, and stains. Leave anything you would not buy yourself.

### Step 3: Photograph and measure

Shoot in daylight on a plain background: front, back, label, and every flaw. Measure pit-to-pit, length, and inseam flat, and write them in the listing.

### Step 4: Check children's items against federal rules

Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says all drawstrings should be removed from children's clothing. It says children's sleepwear rules cover sizes up to 14X, including flammability, and that loose-fitting sleepwear in cotton, cotton blends, or rayon ignites readily and likely does not meet the requirements. If you are unsure, do not list children's sleepwear. Search [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) for children's products before you sell them.

### Step 5: Choose the app per item

Under C$20, Poshmark's flat C$3.95 is a large share, so cheaper items may suit Depop or a bundle. Heavier items need Poshmark's 2.5 kg limit checked.

### Step 6: Describe condition honestly

Name the flaw in the first lines. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) covers claims made to sell a product. "Like new" means like new.

### Step 7: Ship within the app's window and log the sale

Pack in a clean mailer, ship promptly, and record the date, app, sale price, fee, label, and what you paid.

### Step 8: Source only after five completed sales

Five sales show which items and prices your buyers respond to. Then take a small budget to a thrift store, in the same style.

## What should the first week of clothing resale include?

The first week of clothing resale in Canada should produce ten measured listings from your own closet and no buying trip.

- **Day 1:** Pick a style. Pull and wash ten items. Set children's sleepwear aside.
- **Day 2:** Photograph in daylight. Measure every item.
- **Day 3:** Read the Poshmark and Depop fee pages. Price each item from the fee backward.
- **Day 4:** List five items on the app that suits each price.
- **Day 5:** List the other five. Answer questions with measurements.
- **Day 6:** Ship anything that sold. Log the payout.
- **Day 7:** Review which listings got questions or likes. Rewrite titles on the rest.

## What clothing resale mistakes are common in Canada?

Common clothing resale mistakes in Canada are buying stock before selling anything, skipping measurements, and hiding flaws. Each one turns into returns, low prices, or a bin of unsold clothes.

Listing items under C$20 on Poshmark without noticing the C$3.95 fee eats the margin. Selling a children's hoodie with a drawstring or cotton sleepwear ignores the federal guidance. Counterfeit brand items can get your account removed and mislead buyers.

## Which tax rules apply to clothing resale in Canada?

Clothing resale income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase notes, payout reports, PayPal statements, and supply receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Reselling clothes is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
