Clothing resale in Canada means buying (or pulling from your own closet) used clothes, shoes and accessories, listing them on apps like Poshmark and Depop, and shipping each sale. It's low-cost to start and easy to do around a day job, but margins live or die on sourcing and platform fees. The best-documented Canadian example, Aurora, Ont. reseller Satu Tuohimaa, told the Financial Post she made $47,000 on Poshmark in 2024 selling vintage, while paying for a $500-a-month storage unit and giving up 20% of each sale.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Fees:** Poshmark Canada charges a flat C$3.95 on sales under C$20 and 20% on sales of C$20 or more, with a prepaid shipping label included. Depop charges sellers in Canada a 10% selling fee, plus PayPal processing.
- **Demand:** a 2025 DIG360 and Angus Reid Institute poll found 77% of Canadian adults bought at least one pre-owned item in the past year (reported August 11, 2025).
- **Real results:** Tuohimaa sold 880 items in 2024 and made $47,000 (Financial Post, November 18, 2025). Toronto Depop seller Bessie Barlis made $900 to $1,700 a month part time (The Kit, April 2021).
- **Startup cost:** $0 from your own closet; a garment steamer ($41.97), shipping scale ($29.99) and 100 poly mailers ($16.75 to $17.99) on Amazon.ca on October 9, 2026 cover the basics.
- **Tax:** selling your own used clothes is generally personal-use property; buying to resell is business income on T2125, with CPP and GST/HST rules.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does clothing resale suit?

It suits people who already thrift, know brands and sizes in one style, and can photograph, measure and ship a few times a week. The work fits in small blocks: sourcing on a Saturday, listing in the evening, a Canada Post drop on the way to work.

Good fits:

- **Thrifters** who can spot quality fabrics and labels. Tuohimaa built her shop around bohemian and vintage pieces from the 1960s and 1970s.
- **Students**, who often know current trends; Depop's audience skews young. See [side hustles for students](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-students-canada/).
- **Parents** reselling kids' clothes they already buy and outgrow.
- **People with a closet clean-out** who want to test the apps before buying stock.
- **Sneaker, outdoor and workwear fans** who know which brands hold value.

It's a weaker fit if you need steady income (Depop seller Jenna Taylor told The Kit she would sell 30 items one week and none the next), have no storage, or dislike answering questions about fit late at night.

## How much can clothing resellers earn?

There's no official data on resale income, so the most useful numbers come from Canadian sellers who've shared theirs:

- **Satu Tuohimaa (Aurora, Ont.):** $47,000 in 2024 from Poshmark sales of vintage clothing; 880 items sold in 2024 and 943 so far in 2025 at slightly lower prices. Monthly overhead included a $500 storage unit, $42 for a cross-listing app, and over $100 for shipping supplies and gas. Her best flip: a 1990s Dolce and Gabbana dress bought for $12 at Value Village and sold for about $1,100 (Financial Post, November 18, 2025).
- **Bessie Barlis (Toronto):** $900 to $1,700 a month selling 1990s mall brands on Depop part time, alongside a full-time job (The Kit, April 2021).
- **Chloe Penny (Ontario):** $5,500 in November 2020 after going full time on Depop, working "9 to 9, sometimes 9 to 11 p.m." (The Kit, April 2021).
- **Jenna Taylor (Orillia, Ont.):** some weeks 30 items at about $40 each, other weeks none (The Kit, April 2021).

The wider market is growing. A Boston Consulting Group and Vestiaire Collective report cited by the Financial Post estimated global second-hand fashion and luxury at US$210 billion to US$220 billion, possibly reaching US$360 billion by 2030, growing three times faster than first-hand.

## How do you price and photograph clothing?

**Pricing.** Search the item's brand and style on the app you're selling on and filter to sold listings, not active ones. Price new listings slightly above the typical sold price so you have room to accept offers; Tuohimaa listed her Dolce and Gabbana dress at $1,200 after seeing one sell for $2,000 elsewhere, then accepted a lower offer. On Poshmark, remember the fee changes at C$20: an item priced at $19 pays $3.95 (you keep $15.05), while one at $20 pays $4 (you keep $16). Drop prices on stale listings in steps, or bundle them, rather than relisting at the same price for months.

**Photos.** Shoot in daylight next to a window against a plain wall or door. Steam first. Use the same background for every item so your shop looks consistent. The first photo should show the whole item clearly, either flat, on a hanger or on a mannequin; Liz Guber wrote in The Kit that likes and followers started to come in once she styled outfits and shot in early-morning light. Add close-ups of the brand tag, fabric tag, texture and every flaw.

**Descriptions.** Lead with brand, item, colour and size, then flat measurements, fabric content and an honest condition note. Buyers search by brand and size, so put those words in the title.

## Where do resellers source clothing?

- **Your own closet** and family clean-outs. Tuohimaa started with her own and friends' and family's clothes.
- **Thrift stores** such as Value Village, Salvation Army thrift stores and local charity shops. Retail analyst Bruce Winder told the Financial Post some resellers time visits to when new stock goes out.
- **Estate sales, church sales and garage sales**, especially for vintage.
- **Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji bundles** ("bag of women's clothes, $20").
- **Consignment from people you know**, which Tuohimaa does for items that fit her niche.
- **End-of-season clearance** for new-with-tags items; see [Black Friday and Boxing Day reselling](/side-hustles/black-friday-boxing-day-reselling-canada/).

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

**Week 1: test with your own closet.**
- Pull 20 items you no longer wear; wash and steam them.
- Open Poshmark and Depop accounts; read each app's fee page.
- List 10 items on each with measurements, flaw photos and fair prices based on sold listings.

**Week 2: learn what moves.**
- Share and refresh listings; respond to offers within a day.
- Ship every sale within two days.
- Note which brands and categories get likes, offers and sales.

**Week 3: first sourcing trip.**
- Set a $50 budget for one thrift store visit.
- Buy only items you've checked against sold prices on your phone, with at least 3x margin after fees.
- Photograph and list everything within 48 hours.

**Week 4: build the system.**
- Set up a bin system by category with item numbers matching your listings.
- Track every item in a spreadsheet: cost, list date, sale price, fee, label, profit.
- Decide your niche (vintage, outdoor, sneakers, kids) and your weekly listing target.

**Months 2 and 3:** list a set number of items every week (consistency matters more than bursts), cross-list your best pieces, and drop or bundle anything unsold after 60 days. The first sale step by step is in [how to make your first clothing resale in Canada](/side-hustles/clothing-resale-first-sale-canada/), and pricing is in [how to price used clothing in Canada](/side-hustles/clothing-resale-pricing-canada/).

## Templates and checklists

**Sourcing check (before you pay):**

- [ ] Brand and style have sold recently at 3x your cost or more
- [ ] No stains, holes, pilling or odours you can't fix
- [ ] Zippers, buttons and snaps work
- [ ] Fabric content label present
- [ ] Not a counterfeit

**Listing template:**

> **[Brand] [item] – [colour] – size [ ]**
> Measurements (flat): pit to pit [ ] cm, length [ ] cm, sleeve [ ] cm / waist [ ] cm, inseam [ ] cm.
> Fabric: [from label]. Condition: [excellent / good / fair]; [list flaws, see photos].

## What mistakes do new resellers make?

- **Ignoring the fee floor.** Poshmark's C$3.95 on a $10 item is nearly 40%.
- **Buying without checking sold prices.** Tuohimaa notes some expensive brands resell poorly.
- **Missing measurements.** Fit questions and returns drop when you list flat measurements.
- **Hiding flaws.** The Competition Bureau's guidance on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) applies to condition claims.
- **Selling unsafe kids' items.** Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says drawstrings should be removed from children's clothing and covers flammability rules for children's sleepwear; also check [recalls](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en).
- **Counterfeits.** They can get your account removed and mislead buyers.
- **Overbuying.** Inventory you can't list in a week is clutter, not stock.

## Which tax rules apply to clothing resale in Canada?

Selling clothes you bought for yourself is generally personal-use property: under the CRA's [Schedule 3 rules](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/line-12700-capital-gains/completing-schedule-3.html), if both the cost and the sale price are $1,000 or less, there's no gain or loss to report. Buying clothes to resell is a business. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Cost of goods sold, platform fees, labels, mailers, a share of your phone plan and sourcing kilometres can be deducted. Since 2024, platforms have had to [report seller information to the CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/reporting-rules-digital-platforms.html), with an exclusion for goods sellers with fewer than 30 sales that total no more than $2,800 in the period. That exclusion isn't a tax exemption. More detail is in [tax on reselling and platform income in Canada](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count other side-hustle revenue too. Clothing resale isn't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered; check Revenu Québec. French-language listings reach Quebec buyers.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** PST rules are separate from GST; check whether PST applies to your used-goods sales and when you must register.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Operator figures are self-reported to journalists: Tuohimaa's in the Financial Post (November 2025), and Barlis, Penny and Taylor's in The Kit (April 2021), when fees and app algorithms were different. None are typical. Platform fees were read on October 9, 2026 and can change; PayPal fees vary by account. Amazon.ca prices that day change often. Buy prices, sale prices, item counts, hours and shipping costs in the worked examples are assumptions. The global market estimate is a Boston Consulting Group and Vestiaire Collective projection as reported by the Financial Post.
