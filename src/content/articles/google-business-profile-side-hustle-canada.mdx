## What are Google's rules for managing a profile for a client?

Google's rules for managing a client's profile cover eligibility, representation, access, reviews, and posts. Each one shapes what you may offer.

**Eligibility.** Google's [policy overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en) says a profile requires in-person contact with customers during stated hours, and online-only businesses are not eligible. A P.O. box can lead to suspension.

**Representation.** The [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say to use the business's real-world name, with no keywords, taglines, or phone numbers added, and to choose the fewest categories that describe the core business. Service-area businesses that do not serve customers at their address should hide it.

**Access.** Google's [owners and managers page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en) says owners have full control and can add or remove users, while managers have mostly the same access except they cannot add or remove users or remove the profile. The owner invites you by email to your own Google Account. You never need their password.

**Reviews.** Google's [review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) say reviews should reflect genuine experiences, offering incentives for reviews is prohibited, and businesses can share a review link or QR code and reply to reviews. The Maps [prohibited content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) also bars paid or incentivised reviews, selectively asking only happy customers, reviews by people with a conflict of interest, and posting from multiple accounts.

**Posts.** The [photos, videos, and posts policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en) says posts with unverified contact information may be removed. Use the phone number and website already on the profile.

## How do you start Google Business Profile management in Canada?

Starting profile management in Canada means learning Google's rules, building a sample plan, and serving one local business with manager access. The steps follow that order.

### Step 1: Read Google's profile rules

Read the representation guidelines, the policy overview, the owners and managers page, the review tips, and the Maps prohibited content policy. Write down what you will refuse to do.

### Step 2: Practise on a profile you are allowed to manage

If you, a family member, or a friend has an eligible business, ask to be added as a manager. Never claim a profile without the owner's consent.

### Step 3: Write a monthly checklist

Posts, photos, review replies, hours checks before holidays, and a check for suggested edits. Put each item and its frequency on one page.

### Step 4: Draft a review-reply guide

Write reply templates for positive, neutral, and negative reviews. Never offer a discount for a changed review.

### Step 5: Build a review-request kit without incentives

Use the profile's review link or a QR code for the business to share with all customers, as Google's review tips describe. No gifts, no discounts, and no asking only happy customers.

### Step 6: Price a monthly plan

Set a flat monthly fee for the checklist, with photo visits and extra posts priced separately.

### Step 7: Find a first client

Start with businesses you know. The first-client guide covers outreach and consent.

### Step 8: Report monthly

Send a short summary of what you did. Do not claim ranking changes you cannot attribute to your work.

## What profile management mistakes are common in Canada?

Common profile management mistakes in Canada are asking for the owner's password, stuffing keywords into the business name, and offering discounts for reviews. Each one can get the profile suspended or break Google's policies.

Promising a top-three Maps position is a promise you cannot keep. Posting a phone number or website not on the profile risks the post being removed. Emailing every business in a directory skips anti-spam consent; ISED's [anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies to commercial email.

## Which tax rules apply to Google Business Profile management in Canada?

Profile management income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

Keep client agreements, monthly invoices, payments, and a mileage log for photo visits. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Managing Business Profiles is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST.
