## Who does Google Business Profile management suit?

Profile management suits people who like small, recurring tasks, can write a calm reply to an angry review, and can take decent photos with a phone. Clients are local businesses that serve customers in person: trades, clinics, salons, restaurants, gyms and shops. The work fits around a day job because most of it is a few hours per client per month, done whenever you like.

It is a poor fit if you want to promise rankings, if you can't say no to an owner who asks for fake reviews, or if you prefer projects with a clear end. The value is in showing up every month.

## How is profile management different from local SEO?

Profile management is the monthly upkeep of a Google Business Profile, while a local SEO project is usually a one-time cleanup of the profile, its citations and the matching website. Many freelancers sell both: a cleanup first (Elevate Web Design and North SEO publish one-time setup fees of $400 and $500), then a monthly plan.

A monthly plan typically covers weekly or biweekly posts, new photos, review replies, Q&A, holiday hours and checking suggested edits from the public. The cleanup and website alignment are covered in [how to start local SEO freelancing in Canada](/side-hustles/local-seo-side-hustle-canada/).

## What are Google's rules for managing a profile for a client?

Google's rules cover eligibility, representation, access, reviews and posts, and following them is most of the job.

**Eligibility.** Google's [policy overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en) says a profile requires in-person contact with customers during stated hours, and online-only businesses are not eligible. A P.O. box can lead to suspension.

**Representation.** The [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say to use the business's real-world name, with no keywords, taglines or phone numbers added, and to choose the fewest categories that describe the core business. Service-area businesses that don't serve customers at their address should hide it.

**Access.** Google's [owners and managers page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en) says owners have full control and can add or remove users, while managers have mostly the same access except they can't add or remove users or remove the profile. The owner invites you by email to your own Google Account; you never need their password.

**Reviews.** Google's [review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) say reviews should reflect genuine experiences, offering incentives for reviews is prohibited, and businesses can share a review link or QR code and reply to reviews. The Maps [prohibited content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) also bars paid or incentivized reviews, selectively asking only happy customers, reviews by people with a conflict of interest and posting from multiple accounts.

**Posts.** The [photos, videos and posts policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en) says posts with unverified contact information may be removed. Use the phone number and website already on the profile.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

### Week 1: Learn the rules

- Read Google's representation guidelines, owners and managers page, review tips, prohibited content policy and posts policy. Summarize each in one line.
- Read ISED's [anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) pages before you send any commercial email.
- Practise on a profile you're allowed to manage: your own side hustle, a family business or a volunteer group.

### Week 2: Build your plan and samples

- Write a monthly checklist (template below) and two tiers, for example a posts-and-replies tier and a tier that adds photos and a report.
- Draft four sample posts and three sample review replies for an imaginary café.
- Build a review request kit: link, QR card text and follow-up message.

### Week 3: Find a first client

- List 20 local businesses you know with visible gaps: unanswered reviews, no posts in months, wrong holiday hours.
- Approach the ones you have a real connection with in person or by phone first; for email, follow CASL's consent rules. [How to get your first Google Business Profile client](/side-hustles/google-business-profile-first-client-canada/) covers scripts.
- Offer a one-month trial at a set price with a report at the end.

### Week 4: Deliver and report

- Get manager access from the owner. Do the checklist, take photos, reply to every review in the last 90 days.
- Send the first monthly report and propose the ongoing plan, billed on the first of the month.
- Document the system so the second client takes half the time; [a side hustle client acquisition system](/side-hustles/side-hustle-client-acquisition-system-canada/) helps scale outreach.

## What mistakes do new profile managers make?

- **Promising a top-three Maps position.** Google decides rankings; sell the routine and the report.
- **Asking only happy customers for reviews.** Selective soliciting breaks Google's policy; send the same request to every customer.
- **Offering a discount or gift for reviews.** Incentives are prohibited.
- **Posting a different phone number or website.** Posts with unverified contact information can be removed.
- **Emailing every business in a directory.** CASL requires consent, identification and an unsubscribe mechanism for commercial email.
- **Taking the owner's password.** Ask to be added as a manager.

## Which tax rules apply to Google Business Profile management in Canada?

Profile management income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Tool subscriptions, phone and internet shares and mileage for photo visits can be deductible; see [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Ten clients at $300 a month is $36,000 a year, which crosses the threshold, so plan for registration as you grow. Once registered, use the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). The [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule doesn't apply to this work.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.
