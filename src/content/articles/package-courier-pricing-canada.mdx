Package couriers on apps in Canada are usually paid per block or per route, not per kilometre, so what you earn per hour after costs depends on how fast you finish, how far the route runs, and what your vehicle costs. Amazon Flex's Canadian site says most drivers earn $22–$27 an hour, with a note that actual earnings depend on location and how long deliveries take; Job Bank lists employed courier service drivers at a $23.00 national median. Both figures are before your fuel and vehicle costs. This guide suits drivers who want to turn an advertised hourly number into a net one.

## How does package courier pay work in Canada?

App-based package courier pay in Canada is typically a set amount for a scheduled block or route, shown before you accept, plus any surge or tip features the app offers. The [Amazon Flex Canada site](https://flex.amazon.ca/) says you reserve a block, make deliveries, and get paid, and that most drivers earn $22–$27 an hour, with actual earnings depending on location, how long deliveries take, and other factors.

The Amazon range and the Job Bank wages are gross. An employed courier usually drives a company van; an app courier pays for fuel, wear, and the drive to the station. In BC, check with your platform whether its blocks fall under the provincial delivery rules.

## What do package couriers earn per hour after costs in Canada?

What a package courier in Canada keeps per hour is block pay minus vehicle costs, divided by all the time the block really takes, including the drive to the delivery station, waiting to load, and the drive home.

### Step 1: Record block pay

The amount shown when you accepted, plus any extras.

### Step 2: Record total kilometres

From home to the station, the route, and home again. The [vehicle expenses guide](/side-hustles/vehicle-expenses-delivery-rideshare-canada/) explains the logbook.

### Step 3: Set your cost per kilometre

From your own fuel, maintenance, tire, and depreciation records. This guide does not supply one.

### Step 4: Divide by total hours

Include the drive to the station and the wait to load, not only the block length.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Courier income is self-employment income, and self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast, average, or survey. Suppose a 3-hour block pays $72, which is $24 an hour on the block's stated length. Suppose the route is 70 km and the round trip to the station is another 20 km, for 90 km total. Suppose your own cost is $0.25 per kilometre: 90 × $0.25 = $22.50.

Suppose the drive to the station, loading, and the drive home add 45 minutes, so the block really takes 3.75 hours. Net before tax is $72 − $22.50 = $49.50, and $49.50 ÷ 3.75 = $13.20 per hour.

The same block on a dense 35 km route, with the same 20 km round trip, costs 55 × $0.25 = $13.75. Net is $72 − $13.75 = $58.25, or $58.25 ÷ 3.75 = $15.53 per hour. Route density matters as much as block pay.

## How do you decide which courier blocks to accept in Canada?

Decide which courier blocks to accept in Canada by checking block pay against your target hourly pay for the real total time plus your cost per kilometre for the expected distance. Blocks from a far-away station carry hidden unpaid time.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose your target is $20 an hour net and your cost is $0.25 per kilometre. A block that will take 4 hours in total, including the station trip, and about 100 km must pay at least 4 × $20 + 100 × $0.25 = $80 + $25 = $105.

| Block (illustrative) | Total hours | Total km | Floor at $20/h + $0.25/km | Block pay | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Near station, dense route | 3.5 | 60 | $70 + $15 = $85 | $72 | Decline at this target |
| Near station, dense route, surge | 3.5 | 60 | $70 + $15 = $85 | $90 | Accept |
| Far station, rural route | 4.5 | 150 | $90 + $37.50 = $127.50 | $96 | Decline |

If very few blocks clear your floor, the honest conclusion is that your target is above what the app pays in your area after costs.

## How can package couriers raise earnings per hour in Canada?

Package couriers in Canada raise earnings per hour by choosing stations close to home, preferring dense routes, organizing the cargo area to cut stop time, and keeping vehicle costs low. You cannot set the block price, so the levers are which blocks and how efficiently you run them.

A tidy load order saves minutes at every stop. A fuel-efficient vehicle lowers the per-kilometre cost that every block has to cover.

## What package courier earnings mistakes are common in Canada?

Common package courier earnings mistakes in Canada are judging pay by the block length instead of total time, ignoring the drive to the station, and not logging kilometres. Each makes an advertised hourly figure look like take-home pay.

Do not compare a gross app figure with an employee wage as if they were the same. Keep fuel and maintenance receipts. Watch for blocks that routinely run past their scheduled end.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect package courier earnings in Canada?

Package courier income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all your side hustles; the first-dollar taxi and ride-sharing rule applies to passenger transport, not parcels.

If you register, the rate depends on the province of supply, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [platform income tax guide](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/) covers what platforms report.
