Moving help in Canada is paid labour for a household that is changing addresses: carrying boxes, taking a bed apart, and loading a vehicle, for a written time and a written price. Moving help suits people who can lift, who will stop when a staircase is unsafe, and who will walk the job before they name a number.

The money, when moving help pays, comes from hours you can finish without a trip to a clinic. People who do this well sell labour on a walk-through. People who get stuck sell "I'll bring a friend and a truck" and then discover the sofa does not turn on the landing.

## Who does moving help in Canada suit?

Moving help in Canada suits people who can carry a labelled box down a real staircase, say no to a piano, and keep a Saturday free. A strong back is not a plan. A walk-through, a second person when the piece needs one, and a price that includes the stairs are the plan. Moving help is a poor fit if you will not turn down a load, if you are about to finance a truck, or if you need income that does not depend on someone else's moving day.

Apartment elevators book up. Parking tickets are a cost. Ask about both before you quote.

## What should a moving-help agreement in Canada include?

A moving-help agreement in Canada should name hours or a load, the addresses, what you will not carry, and who supplies the vehicle. In Ontario, the province's [Hiring a mover](https://www.ontario.ca/page/hiring-mover) page says moving companies are not licensed by the province, and that consumer contracts for moving services must be in writing if they are worth more than $50. The page says those contracts include the mover's name and contact information, a description and itemized prices, the total, start and end dates, and payment terms. It also says a mover cannot charge more than 10 percent above the estimate unless the customer needs additional supplies or services and agrees to the increase.

Other provinces publish their own consumer pages. Read yours. This guide uses Ontario as the example it can cite, not as a national code. Write the note even when the job is under the Ontario threshold, so the landing and the piano are not a surprise at noon.

## How much can moving help earn in Canada?

Moving help earnings in Canada cannot be promised as an hourly rate, because a studio with an elevator and a third-floor walk-up are different jobs. What you keep depends on the list below, and this guide does not publish a national moving rate.

- Hours you actually worked, including the load that would not turn
- Stairs, distance from the truck to the door, and a broken elevator
- Pieces you refused, and pieces you should have refused
- Parking, fuel if you drove, and a second person you paid
- Damage, and whether the note said who carries that risk
- The estimate you beat by "just finishing," which is free labour
- Weather, ice, and a day that ran past the light

In Ontario, the hiring-a-mover page's 10 percent ceiling on an estimate is a reason to walk the job first. An estimate you invent from a text message is how that ceiling becomes your problem.

## How do you start moving help in Canada?

Starting moving help in Canada means selling labour on a walk-through, with a written limit, before you supply a truck. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Decide you are labour only

The client books the truck or uses vehicles they already have. You carry, you pad, you do not finance a cube van this month. If a later job needs your vehicle, you will read that province's rules for commercial use before you advertise it. This guide does not state a weight limit.

### Step 2: Read your province's consumer page

In Ontario, read [Hiring a mover](https://www.ontario.ca/page/hiring-mover) before you take a job over $50. Note the writing requirement and the 10 percent estimate rule. If you are elsewhere, find that province's page and follow it. Do not copy Ontario's numbers into another province.

### Step 3: Write the no-list

Pianos, safes, gun cabinets, and any piece that needs two experienced movers when you are alone. Appliances you are not equipped to disconnect. Hazardous products. The no-list goes in the message you send.

### Step 4: Set a minimum and an hourly or flat price

A two-hour minimum keeps a "quick favour" from eating a Saturday. Stairs are a line, not a surprise. Payment is due when you finish, by a method you named.

### Step 5: Get an insurance quote that matches the carrying

Say household moves, stairs, and that you are not supplying a truck yet. Ask who is responsible for a scraped wall. This is not insurance advice. Ontario's hiring-a-mover page also suggests the contract name the insurance provider and who is responsible for damage. Use that as a checklist where it applies.

### Step 6: Walk one apartment before you quote it

Count stairs. Look at the landing. Ask about the elevator reservation and where the truck may stop. A quote from a photo of a sofa is a guess.

### Step 7: Tell five people who are actually moving

Friends, a building board, a note at a workplace. The offer is a date range and a price, not "text me anytime."

### Step 8: Run the first job with a clock

Start when you said. Lift with your legs. Stop if a piece needs a second person you do not have. Do not hold the client's goods because you want a higher price. Ontario's consumer pages treat holding goods to force a new price as a problem. Finish, invoice, and leave the pathway clear.

### Step 9: After two jobs, rewrite the estimate

If both ran long, the next quote includes that time. Honour the written deal for the client who already signed. If you bring a friend, check that province's workers' compensation rules before you direct their work.

## What should the first week of moving help in Canada include?

The first week of moving help in Canada should produce a no-list, a one-page note, an insurance quote, and five conversations. A moving day on the calendar is a bonus.

- **Day 1:** Choose labour only. Read the provincial page. In Ontario, that is the hiring-a-mover page.
- **Day 2:** Write the no-list, the minimum hours, and the price.
- **Day 3:** Buy gloves, a few blankets, and straps if you do not own them. Practise padding a table.
- **Day 4:** Insurance quote.
- **Day 5:** Walk a friend's place or your own stairs and time a pretend load of ten boxes.
- **Day 6:** Tell five people. Ask for the addresses and the date, not for a vague maybe.
- **Day 7:** If someone books, walk it before you confirm the number. If nobody books, the offer stays specific. Do not add a truck to the ad to make it sound bigger.

## What is easy to miss on a moving day in Canada?

Moving help in Canada goes wrong when the elevator was never reserved, when parking is a hydrant, and when the last load is a mattress in the rain with no bag. Ask about the elevator and the loading zone the day before. A mattress bag is a small purchase that saves a wet argument.

Label boxes with the room, not with "miscellaneous." Keep a lane on the stairs, and slow down if the entrance is icy.

## What moving help mistakes are common in Canada?

Common moving help mistakes in Canada are quoting from a text message, financing a truck before a single paid Saturday, and treating a friend with a bad back as free labour. The walk-through is the estimate. Ontario's 10 percent rule, where it applies, punishes the fantasy number.

Holding furniture until the client pays more than the written price is how a side hustle becomes a complaint. Deliver what you agreed, then invoice.

Junk from the move is not "just dump it on the way home." If the client wants items gone, that is a separate [junk removal](/side-hustles/junk-removal-side-hustle-canada/) offer with a facility that accepts the load. A ravine is not a facility.

## Which tax rules apply to moving help in Canada?

Moving help income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for moving help follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the written notes, the e-transfers, and the receipts for blankets and fuel. Report moving help on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add handyman, junk removal, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Moving help is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work happens. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.

If you drive to the job, keep a kilometre log for any vehicle claim. Search your city for a business licence and use that city's fee if there is one. This guide does not state a licence fee from another city. Ontario's hiring-a-mover page says the province does not licence moving companies. A municipal licence can still exist. If you pay a helper, check that province's workers' compensation rules before you call the person a favour.
