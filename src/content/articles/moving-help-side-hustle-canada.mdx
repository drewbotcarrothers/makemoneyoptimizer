Moving help in Canada usually starts as labour only: you load and unload a truck the client rented, carry furniture up and down stairs, and wrap pieces so they arrive unscratched. It needs strength, a partner, about $150 to $250 of gear and a clear written agreement. Taskrabbit's October 2026 cost guide puts average moving-help rates at $24 to $35 an hour across nine Canadian cities, and Job Bank's median wage for furniture movers is $22.00 an hour. Adding your own truck raises what you can charge, along with your insurance and liability.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Market rates:** Taskrabbit's cost guide (updated October 1, 2026) lists average moving-help rates of $34/hour in Toronto, $35 in Montreal, $33 in Vancouver, $29 in Calgary and $24 in Halifax, with average invoices of $52 to $118.
- **Wage benchmark:** Job Bank lists furniture movers at $16.55 low, $22.00 median and $30.29 high per hour, and mover helpers at $17.00, $22.00 and $30.00 (2023 to 2024 data, updated November 19, 2025).
- **Startup cost:** on Amazon.ca on October 9, 2026, a VEVOR furniture dolly 2-pack was $55.90, Forearm Forklift lifting straps $37.75, an Amazon Basics 6-pack of moving blankets $53.76 and leather work gloves $20.99.
- **Ontario rule:** moving contracts over $50 must be in writing with itemized prices, the total, dates and payment terms; a mover can't charge more than 10% over an estimate in the contract without agreement.
- **Tax:** report on T2125, pay both CPP shares on net income, and register for GST/HST once taxable supplies pass $30,000.

## Who does moving help suit?

It suits people who are fit, punctual and calm under pressure, and who can work with a partner. Moving days are stressful for clients; being organised is half the job.

Good fits:

- **Students and athletes** with flexible schedules. Toronto's Hustle Moving Co. was built around varsity athletes who couldn't hold regular shifts.
- **Pairs of friends** who can work as a two-person crew and split jobs.
- **Owners of a pickup, cargo van or trailer** who can add small moves and Marketplace deliveries.
- **People already doing odd jobs** such as assembly, who can sell disassembly and reassembly with the move.
- **Gym-goers and labourers** who already lift safely.

It's a poor fit if you have back problems, can't work weekends and month-ends, or would be tempted to move a piano or a safe alone.

## What kinds of moving help can you offer?

Start with labour only. Each step up adds income and risk.

| Service | What you do | What the client provides | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labour only | Load and unload, wrap furniture, carry boxes | Rental truck, boxes, packing | Low to medium |
| Packing and unpacking | Box and label, unpack and set up the kitchen | Boxes or a budget for them | Low |
| Disassembly and reassembly | Beds, tables, wardrobes before and after the move | Nothing | Low |
| In-home moves | Move furniture between rooms or floors | Nothing | Medium |
| Small moves with your vehicle | Dorm rooms, studio apartments, single items | Payment for vehicle time | Medium to high |
| Truck-assisted moving | Labour plus a large vehicle, priced as one hourly rate | Nothing | High |

Taskrabbit added a truck-assisted moving category in all its active Canadian markets in March 2026; Taskers in that category set hourly rates that include vehicle use ([Taskrabbit release, March 24, 2026](https://www.taskrabbit.ca/press/release/taskrabbit-launches-vehicle-integrated-moving-category-across-90-cities-in)). The same release cites a figure that nearly 27% of Canadians who moved reported unexpected fees in the past year, which is why clear pricing wins jobs.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

**Week 1: decide your service and read the rules.**
- Decide labour only or vehicle; most people should start labour only.
- Read your province's consumer page. In Ontario that's [Hiring a mover](https://www.ontario.ca/page/hiring-mover); in Quebec, the Office de la protection du consommateur's [moving tip sheet](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/consumer/good-service/housing-renovation/moving/tip-sheet/).
- Write your no-list: pianos, safes, pool tables, hot tubs, gas appliances, anything over a weight you name.
- Find a partner and agree how you split pay.

**Week 2: gear, insurance and pricing.**
- Buy gloves, blankets, a dolly and straps.
- Get a commercial general liability quote. Quebec's consumer office suggests clients look for movers with at least $1 million in third-party liability insurance.
- Set an hourly rate per helper and a minimum booking (for example, 3 hours). Compare Taskrabbit's city average and Job Bank's $22.00 median.
- If you'll use Taskrabbit, register ($25 one-time fee) and choose moving help and heavy lifting.

**Week 3: first jobs.**
- Tell people who are actually moving: friends, a campus housing board, a local buy-and-sell group.
- Walk or video-call the space before quoting larger jobs. Ask about stairs, elevators, parking and the biggest item.
- Run the first job with a clock and a photo log of existing damage.

**Week 4: review.**
- Compare quoted hours with actual hours. Rewrite your estimate formula.
- Ask every client for a review and a referral; property managers and realtors can send repeat work.
- Book month-end dates in advance; they fill first.

The first-job walkthrough is in [how to get your first moving client in Canada](/side-hustles/moving-help-first-client-canada/), and rates and add-ons are in [how much to charge for moving help in Canada](/side-hustles/moving-help-pricing-canada/).

## What should a moving-help agreement include?

Put it in writing even if your province doesn't require it for your situation. Ontario's [Hiring a mover](https://www.ontario.ca/page/hiring-mover) page says consumer moving contracts over $50 must be in writing and must include:

- the mover's name, address and contact information
- a description of the services and an itemized price list
- the total the client will pay
- start and end dates
- the terms of payment

The same page lists what a contract can also include:

- the estimate (by law, the mover can't charge more than 10% above the estimate in the contract unless the client needs extra supplies or services and agrees to the increase)
- the insurer's name and policy number
- who is responsible for damage or loss
- the number of staff, estimated hours and vehicle size

Ontario also gives consumers a 10-day cooling-off period on contracts made in their home, and a mover can't hold belongings to force a renegotiation. In Quebec, the OPC says a contract made by phone or online must be confirmed in writing within 15 days, and advises consumers to pay little or no deposit before the move.

**Copyable agreement template:**

```text

Helper(s): [names], [phone], [email], [address]

Signed: ______________  Date: ________
```

## Templates and checklists

**Quote questions to send before every job:**

> Hi [name], to give you an accurate quote: how many bedrooms, which floor at each end, stairs or elevator (and is it booked?), parking for the truck, and your three heaviest items. Will everything be boxed before we arrive?

**Moving-day checklist:**

- [ ] Confirm time, addresses and elevator booking the day before
- [ ] Gloves, blankets, dolly, straps, stretch wrap, tape, tool bag with hex keys
- [ ] Walk the path at both ends; clear ice and salt in winter
- [ ] Photograph existing damage to furniture and walls

Agree on commands ("lift on three", "set down") before the first carry.

**Job log columns:** date, client, crew, quoted hours, actual hours, rate, tips, travel, gear used, damage notes, profit per helper.

## What is easy to miss on moving day?

- **Elevator and loading dock bookings.** Condo moves run to a time slot; Taskrabbit's guide mentions building move fees of $100 to $500, and missing the slot can cost the client.
- **Parking.** Dense cities may need a street-occupancy permit for a truck. Ask who's arranging it.
- **Winter.** Icy stairs and salt in hallways slow everything; put the extra time in the quote.
- **Late arrivals.** Quebec's consumer office told CBC that a mover arriving late is one of the most common moving complaints, and 200 to 300 of its roughly 30,000 complaints are linked to moving (June 30, 2026).
- **Items that won't fit.** Measure sofas and doorways before carrying.
- **Gas and water hookups.** Disconnecting a gas dryer or stove is licensed work. Leave it to the client's contractor.

## What moving-help mistakes are common?

- **Quoting by the bedroom instead of by the stairs.** A third-floor walk-up takes far longer than a ground-floor unit.
- **No written scope.** Ontario's rules exist because surprise charges are common.
- **Working alone.** Two people is the minimum for furniture.
- **Taking a large deposit.** Quebec's OPC advises consumers against it; asking for one makes you look like the movers it warns about.
- **Skipping insurance.** One dropped TV or gouged floor can wipe out a season's profit.
- **Not photographing existing damage.** Do it before the first carry.

## Which tax rules apply to moving help?

Moving help is generally self-employment income, whether paid through an app, cash or e-transfer. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Deductible expenses can include gloves, blankets, wrap, insurance premiums, the business share of phone and vehicle costs, and platform registration fees; a dolly or hand truck may need to go through capital cost allowance. If you split jobs with a partner, decide whether you're each running your own business or one of you is paying the other, and keep records either way. See [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Moving help isn't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply. Once registered, use the rate for the province where you supply the service; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) pages. <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can produce same-day invoices with hours and add-ons, and <AffiliateLink program="wave" label="Wave" /> is a free alternative.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** 13% HST once registered; the written-contract and 10% estimate rules above apply to moving services.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP and QST applies alongside GST once registered. Demand spikes around July 1, so the [Montreal city guide](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-montreal-canada/) explains how to plan a moving calendar. The OPC asks whether heavy vehicles are registered with the Commission des transports du Québec.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** GST plus a separate PST; check whether PST applies to your services.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered.
- **Atlantic provinces:** HST; Nova Scotia's rate is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Workers' compensation** is provincial. If you hire helpers rather than splitting jobs as equals, check your province's board.
