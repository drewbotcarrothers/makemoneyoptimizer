## Should you buy a 3D printer or a laser engraver first?

Buy the machine that makes the product you already know will sell. A 3D printer is slower per item but makes three-dimensional objects from cheap filament; a laser is fast per item but needs blanks (wood, leather, acrylic, slate) and more safety gear.

| Machine | Good for | Running cost | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FDM 3D printer | Organizers, planters, cosplay parts, replacement parts | Filament, about $12–$22 per kg on Amazon.ca | Hours per print; failed prints |
| Resin 3D printer | Miniatures, jewellery masters, fine detail | Resin, gloves, isopropyl alcohol | Fumes, skin contact, post-curing |
| Diode laser | Wood signs, coasters, leather, coated metal | Blanks and electricity | Open frames need eye protection and an enclosure |
| CO2 laser | Clear acrylic, faster cutting | Blanks, tube wear, water cooling | Size, venting and invisible infrared beam |

## Which 3D printer should a Canadian maker buy?

Start with an open-frame FDM printer for PLA and PETG; move to an enclosed printer only when you need ABS or ASA. Bambu Lab lists ABS and ASA as "Not Recommended" on its open-frame [A1 Mini](https://bambulab.com/en/a1-mini/tech-specs) and [A1](https://bambulab.com/en/a1/tech-specs), which matters if a customer wants heat-resistant parts.

## What safety gear and supplies should you add?

Add eye protection rated for your laser's wavelength, a fire extinguisher, and a way to move fumes outside. ## What should Canadian makers check before buying?

- **Cold rooms.** An unheated garage or basement in winter makes prints warp and adhesive fail. An enclosure or enclosed printer such as the P1S helps; so does keeping the machine in a heated room.
- **Venting in winter.** Running a duct out a window in January costs heat. Plan the vent route before you buy a laser, not after.
- **Recalls.** Search the model on the Government of Canada's [Recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before you order.
- **Seller and warranty.** Some Amazon.ca listings are third-party offers; check who sells and ships the machine, because that decides how a warranty claim works.
- **Home insurance.** Tell your insurer you run a laser or printer for business; see [side hustle insurance](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

## How do taxes treat a 3D printer or laser?

Filament, resin and blanks are supplies you generally expense. A machine is claimed through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html). CRA's [classes of depreciable property](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html) places tools costing less than $500 in Class 12 (100%) and tools of $500 or more in Class 8 (20%), and it excludes electronic data processing equipment from Class 12, so confirm the class for your machine. See [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/). This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
