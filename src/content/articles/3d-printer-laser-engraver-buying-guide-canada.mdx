<QuickAnswer>

- **Short answer:** pick the machine for the product you will sell. A 3D printer makes objects (organizers, figurines, replacement parts); a laser engraver marks and cuts flat goods (signs, coasters, personalized gifts).
- **3D printers:** FDM (filament) for most sellable items; resin for fine detail, with more handling and ventilation needs.
- **Laser engravers:** diode lasers for wood, leather and coated metals; CO2 lasers for faster cutting and clear acrylic.
- **Safety:** both need ventilation; lasers need the right eye protection and an enclosure. Prices: see current price.

</QuickAnswer>

## What should you look for in a 3D printer?

| Spec | What it means | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technology | FDM melts filament; resin cures liquid with light | FDM for most products; resin for miniatures and jewellery masters |
| Build volume | Largest part it can print | Big enough for your best-selling item |
| Auto bed levelling | Machine calibrates the print bed | Saves failed prints and setup time |
| Enclosure | Cover around the print area | Steadier temperatures, contains fumes and particles |
| Materials | PLA, PETG, ABS/ASA, TPU | Check the hotend and bed temperatures support what you plan to use |
| Speed and reliability | Real-world print speed, not just the headline | Look for a large user community and spare parts in Canada |

## What should you look for in a laser engraver?

| Spec | What it means | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laser type | Diode (common, lower cost) vs CO2 (glass tube, faster cutting) | Diode for engraving wood and leather; CO2 for acrylic and volume cutting |
| Optical power | Output power of the laser module | Compare optical, not input, power |
| Work area | Largest piece it can engrave | Fits your coasters, signs or cutting boards |
| Enclosure | Box around the laser | Strongly preferred at home: contains light and smoke |
| Air assist and exhaust | Blows smoke away; vents fumes outside | Cleaner cuts and safer air |
| Safety features | Flame detection, emergency stop, lid interlock | The more the better for a home shop |

## What should Canadian makers know before buying?

- **Ventilation and fumes.** CCOHS lists [fumes from materials](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/lasers.html) as a laser hazard and recommends local exhaust ventilation; its [3D printing fact sheet](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/additive_manufacturing.pdf) covers emissions too.
- **Eye protection.** Laser glasses must match the laser's wavelength; generic sunglasses do not protect.
- **Never leave a laser running unattended.** Materials can catch fire.
- **Recalls.** Check models on [Recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en).
- **Taxes.** Machines used for business are generally claimed through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html).
