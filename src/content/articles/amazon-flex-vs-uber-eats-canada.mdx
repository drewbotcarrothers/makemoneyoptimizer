## How do the schedules differ on Amazon Flex and Uber Eats?

On Amazon Flex you commit to a block in advance. On Uber Eats you decide minute by minute. For most side hustlers that difference matters more than the pay rate.

**Amazon Flex.** You set your availability in the app's calendar and receive block offers for those dates, or tap Check For Available Blocks for offers open to everyone, released up to 24 hours ahead. The station address appears at least one hour before the block. If you cannot make it, forfeit the block at least 45 minutes before it starts. A last-minute block that starts in under 45 minutes can be cancelled within 5 minutes without affecting your reliability rating. Undelivered parcels go back to the station by 10 a.m. the next day. Amazon says blocks fluctuate week to week and are not guaranteed, and if no spots are open you go on an interest list.

**Uber Eats.** There is no shift to book. You can stop after one order. Orders cluster around lunch and dinner, so pay per hour online depends on when you log on and how long you wait between offers.

The combination many drivers land on is both: a Flex block on a weekday morning and Uber Eats in the dinner rush.

## How does insurance differ between Amazon Flex and Uber Eats in Canada?

Both require your personal auto insurance to stay in force, and both add some coverage while you work, but the details change by province. Tell your insurer before you start.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario | Amazon's policy with Northbridge is supplemental to yours. Loss or damage cover for your car only mirrors coverages you already carry (all perils, collision, comprehensive, specified perils). It does not apply if you live in Quebec and deliver in Ontario ([Amazon Flex FAQ](https://flex.amazon.ca/faq)) | Economical commercial policy. Waiting for a request: $1,000,000 third-party liability. Request accepted to drop-off: $2,000,000. Contingent collision and comprehensive with a $2,500 deductible, only if your own policy has them ([Uber insurance](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/deliver/insurance/)) |
| Alberta | Same supplemental Northbridge policy as Ontario | Personal policy applies first while waiting, with $1,000,000 back-up; $2,000,000 liability plus collision and comprehensive ($2,500 deductible) once a request is accepted |
| British Columbia | Amazon says it cannot get coverage for independent delivery partners in B.C. You need an ICBC Delivery policy (rate class 013), or a personal policy your insurer confirms allows limited commercial days | Uber says most basic ICBC policies allow up to 6 days a month of commercial use; beyond that you need business coverage |
| Manitoba and Saskatchewan | No Flex service areas | Manitoba: most MPI policies allow up to 4 days a month of commercial use. Saskatchewan: basic SGI liability applies while delivering |
| Quebec and Atlantic Canada | No Flex service areas | Economical commercial coverage, with $1,000,000 civil liability in Quebec and $2,000,000 in the Atlantic provinces once a request is accepted |

The B.C. row is the biggest practical difference. An Uber Eats driver in Vancouver can start on a basic ICBC policy and stay under 6 days a month. A Flex driver in Vancouver gets no Amazon policy at all. This is not insurance advice. Confirm with your insurer or broker in writing.

## What pay floors apply to Amazon Flex and Uber Eats?

Ontario and B.C. set minimum pay for app-based delivery, and both apply to time on an assignment, not time waiting for one.

**Ontario.** The [Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers) requires at least the general minimum wage, which is [$17.95 an hour from October 1, 2026](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/minimum-wage), checked per work assignment or per pay period. Tips do not count toward it. Amazon's FAQ links its own Ontario DPWRA information. A Flex block paying Amazon's stated $22 to $27 an hour is above the floor. On Uber Eats the floor matters on slow orders with long restaurant waits.

**British Columbia.** The province's [minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) sets $21.89 per hour of engaged time from June 1, 2026, for online platform workers who pick up online orders and deliver them. Engaged time runs from accepting an order to completing or cancelling it. On top of that, delivery workers get at least $0.35 per km of engaged travel by any means other than walking. The $0.45 per km figure that appears in some guides is the ride-hail allowance for carrying passengers, not the delivery rate.

## How much could you keep on Amazon Flex versus Uber Eats?

Neither app publishes a guaranteed hourly rate, so compare your own logs after vehicle costs. The kilometre cost decides more than the sticker pay.

For the per-km cost, use your own fuel, insurance, maintenance and depreciation if you track them. If not, the CRA's [2026 reasonable allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/benefits-allowances/automobile/automobile-motor-vehicle-allowances.html) of **$0.73 per km** for the first 5,000 business km is a full-cost benchmark. It is the rate an employer can pay tax-free for an employee's own car, not a deduction you claim. A fuel-type cost is lower, so the example shows both.

### Illustrative example

**Assumed numbers for teaching, not a quote, an average or a typical result.** The block pay sits inside Amazon's stated range. The Uber Eats fees, tips, kilometres and hours are assumptions.

A Toronto driver works one 4-hour Amazon Flex block paid $96 (equal to $24 an hour) and drives 90 km including the trip to and from the station. On another day the same driver spends 4 hours online on Uber Eats, earns $72 in delivery fees and $18 in tips, and drives 70 km.

| | Amazon Flex (4-hour block) | Uber Eats (4 hours online) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pay + tips | $96.00 | $90.00 |
| Kilometres | 90 | 70 |
| Cost at an assumed $0.35/km (fuel-type cost) | $31.50 | $24.50 |
| Net at $0.35/km | $64.50 ($16.13/hr) | $65.50 ($16.38/hr) |
| Cost at the CRA's $0.73/km | $65.70 | $51.10 |
| Net at $0.73/km | $30.30 ($7.58/hr) | $38.90 ($9.73/hr) |

At a fuel-type cost the two apps tie. At a full-cost rate, Uber Eats wins by $8.60 for the shift because it drove 20 fewer kilometres, even though Flex paid $6 more. Where you live relative to the Amazon station changes that answer: a driver 25 km from the station adds about 50 km a block before the first parcel.

**Floor check, same assumptions.** Suppose 2.5 of the 4 Uber Eats hours were engaged time (from accepting each order to delivering it), covering 55 km.

- **Ontario:** 2.5 × $17.95 = $44.88. The $72 in fees is above it, so no top-up.
- **B.C.:** 2.5 × $21.89 = $54.73, plus 55 × $0.35 = $19.25, for $73.98. The $72 in fees is $1.98 short, so the platform owes a top-up of $1.98. Tips do not count toward either floor.

Swap in your own block offers, order pay, kilometres and engaged time. GST/HST and income tax are not shown.

## What mistakes do Canadian drivers make with Amazon Flex and Uber Eats?

The common mistakes are skipping the insurance call, counting gross pay as income, and accepting work your car cannot carry.

- **Not telling your insurer.** Both companies say your personal policy must stay in force. In B.C., Amazon's coverage does not exist at all.
- **Ignoring kilometres.** Neither app pays mileage. The drive to the Flex station is part of the block's cost. Log every business kilometre.
- **Booking a Flex block you cannot keep.** Forfeit at least 45 minutes ahead. Late forfeits and parcels not returned by 10 a.m. the next day can affect your standing.
- **Turning up with a full car.** Amazon says you may be turned away if parcels will not fit, and the instance goes in your delivery history.
- **Counting waiting time toward the floor.** Ontario and B.C. floors cover assignment or engaged time, not time online waiting for an order.
- **Paying for daily cash-outs by habit.** Five Instant Pay transfers a week at $1.49 is $7.45, or about $387 a year. Use the free weekly payout when you can wait.

## Which tax rules apply to Amazon Flex and Uber Eats drivers in Canada?

Both are generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net income. Amazon's FAQ says it typically does not issue year-end tax forms to Flex drivers, so your own records are the record. Download the earnings screens.

The CRA's mandatory GST/HST registration rule for [taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) is about carrying passengers, so parcel and food delivery follow the normal [small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) of $30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, counting all your side hustles. If you also drive passengers for Uber or Lyft, the passenger rule makes you register from the first dollar, and the registration covers your delivery income too. Uber's delivery agreement says it pays applicable sales tax on your delivery fee once you are registered and your tax profile is complete.

Vehicle costs are claimed by business-use share, as explained in the CRA's [motor vehicle expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125/calculating-motor-vehicle-expenses.html) page and in [vehicle expenses for delivery and rideshare](/side-hustles/vehicle-expenses-delivery-rideshare-canada/). A mileage and receipt tool such as <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can help, but a spreadsheet works too. Where to keep the money is compared in [Wealthsimple vs a bank business account](/side-hustles/wealthsimple-vs-bank-business-account-side-hustle-canada/).
