## How do you find the resale price of an item in Canada?

Find the resale price of a flip in Canada by searching your local area for the same item, brand, and condition, and paying most attention to listings that disappear quickly. Asking prices on stale listings that sit for weeks are not market prices.

Compare across a few sources: local listings, other classified sites, and sold listings on shipping platforms for similar items. Local prices vary by city and season; patio furniture and bikes sell for more in spring than in November.

## How do you calculate a maximum buy price for a flip in Canada?

Calculate a maximum buy price in Canada as expected resale price minus target profit minus all other costs. If the seller will not go that low, walk away.

### Step 1: Estimate the resale price

Use quick-selling local comps, not hopeful asking prices.

### Step 2: Choose a target profit

A dollar amount or a share of the resale price that pays for your time.

### Step 3: Add up the other costs

Travel, cleaning, parts, and paint.

### Step 4: Subtract to get your maximum buy price

Resale − profit − costs.

### Step 5: Set the list price and floor

List a little above the resale price; the floor is the price at which you still hit your target.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not an average or a market price. Suppose quick-selling local comps for a solid-wood dresser cluster around $180 (assumed). You want $70 profit. Pickup travel is $10, and cleaning and new knobs are $20: costs $30. Maximum buy price is $180 − $70 − $30 = $80.

You buy it for $65. You list at $200 and set a $165 floor, because $165 − $65 − $30 = $70 still meets your target. If it sells at $180, profit is $180 − $65 − $30 = $85.

## When should you lower or raise flip prices in Canada?

Lower a flip price in Canada when an item gets views but no messages for a week or two, and relist with new photos before cutting deeply. Raise prices when items sell within hours or several buyers ask at once.

Seasonal items can be worth holding: a snowblower listed in April may sell for more in November. Holding has a cost too, in storage space and cash you cannot reuse.

## What Facebook Marketplace pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common Facebook Marketplace pricing mistakes in Canada are paying close to resale price, forgetting travel and cleaning costs, and listing with no floor, which leads to accepting low offers on the spot.

Safety and honesty rules apply to private resellers too. Scams, such as fake payment screenshots and overpayment requests, can be reported to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/index-eng.htm).

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect Facebook Marketplace flipping in Canada?

Buying items to resell for profit in Canada is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html); selling your own used belongings occasionally is generally not. Keep a record of buy prices and costs, as the CRA's [keeping records page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records.html) explains. GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters.
