## What does a flip really cost in Canada?

A flip costs the buy price plus driving, cleaning, parts and your time. This guide covers local pickup sales paid in cash or e-transfer, where Facebook Marketplace charges no listing fee; shipped sales and platform checkout are outside it.

| Cost line | Benchmark |
| --- | --- |
| Driving | 73¢ per km for the first 5,000 km and 67¢ after, the 2026 tax-exempt allowance limits announced by the [Department of Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/01/government-announces-the-2026-automobile-deduction-limits-and-expense-benefit-rates-for-businesses.html) on January 14, 2026 (77¢ and 71¢ in the territories) |
| Cleaning and supplies | Your receipts |
| Parts, paint, hardware | Your receipts |
| Your time | Job Bank lists a $22.00 median hourly wage for [furniture movers](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/16660/ca) in Canada (updated November 19, 2025) |
| Items that never sell | Track them in the margin sheet; they're a cost of the whole business |
| Paid promotion on Kijiji | Kijiji's Bump Up and Top Ad prices vary by city and category and are shown when you post |

The 73¢ figure is what an employer can pay tax-free per kilometre, so it covers gas, wear and insurance together. It's a pricing benchmark; for your tax return you claim actual vehicle expenses for the business share of your driving, backed by a logbook.

## How do you calculate a maximum buy price?

Estimate the local sale price from listings that sold quickly, then subtract everything else.

```text
Expected sale price = median of 5+ local comps that sold fast, same item/condition
                      (never above the current new price)
Travel              = round-trip km × $0.73
Max buy price       = expected sale − target profit − travel − cleaning/parts
List price          = expected sale + 10% to 20% negotiating room
Floor               = buy price + travel + cleaning/parts + target profit
Time check          = (sale − buy − travel − cleaning/parts) ÷ hours ≥ $22
```

The 10% to 20% negotiating room and the profit target are your choices; set them before the first message arrives so you're never deciding a floor mid-conversation.

## What do worked flip examples look like?

**Illustrative.** The expected sale prices, buy prices, distances, supply costs, profit targets and hours are my assumptions. New prices, the 73¢ rate, the $22.00 wage and the consignment splits are from the sources above.

### Example 1: KALLAX shelf (new $99 at IKEA Canada)

- Assumed local sale price: $50. Round trip 16 km: 16 × $0.73 = $11.68. Cleaning $3. Target profit $20.
- Max buy: $50 − $20 − $11.68 − $3 = **$15.32**, so offer $15.
- Profit at $50: $50 − $15 − $11.68 − $3 = $20.32. Over 1.5 hours that's **$13.55 an hour**, below the $22.00 benchmark.
- Low-ticket flat-pack items only pass the time check when they're nearby or picked up alongside other buys.

### Example 2: solid-wood dresser vs selling on consignment

- Assumed local sale price $180 (well under the $429 to $699 new price of IKEA's 6- and 8-drawer dressers). Round trip 30 km: $21.90. Knobs and cleaning $20. Target profit $70.
- Max buy: $180 − $70 − $21.90 − $20 = **$68.10**. Bought for $60.
- List at $210 (about 17% above the expected sale). Floor: $60 + $21.90 + $20 + $70 = **$171.90**, round to $172.
- Sold at $185: profit $185 − $60 − $21.90 − $20 = **$83.10**; over 4 hours, $20.78 an hour.
- Same dresser on consignment at $185: Juniper & Oak's 50% pays $92.50, a loss of $9.40 after costs; Recertified's 40% pays $74.00, a loss of $27.90.

### Example 3: KitchenAid Artisan mixer (new $379.99 at Best Buy Canada)

- Assumed local sale price $180. Round trip 20 km: $14.60. Cleaning $5. Target profit $60.
- Max buy: $180 − $60 − $14.60 − $5 = **$100.40**. Bought for $90.
- Profit at $180: $180 − $90 − $14.60 − $5 = **$70.40**; over 2 hours, $35.20 an hour.
- Test it runs at every speed before paying, and show it running in a short video in the listing.

### Example 4: DeWalt 20V drill and impact kit (new $299.99 at Best Buy Canada)

- Assumed local sale price $150 with battery and charger. Round trip 10 km: $7.30. Target profit $50.
- Max buy: $150 − $50 − $7.30 = **$92.70**. Bought for $80.
- Buyer negotiates to $140: profit $140 − $80 − $7.30 = **$52.70**; over 1 hour, $52.70 an hour.
- Tools with batteries sell on proof they hold a charge; photograph the charger light and include the battery's amp-hour rating.

## Which templates help price and sell flips?

A comp check before you message a seller:

```text
COMP CHECK — [item, brand, model, size]        Date: [date]
New price today: $____ at [retailer] (link)
Local listings, same condition (price / days listed / still up?):
  1. $___ / __ days / Y-N    2. $___ / __ days / Y-N    3. $___ / __ days / Y-N
  4. $___ / __ days / Y-N    5. $___ / __ days / Y-N
Expected sale (fast-moving comps): $____
Max buy = ____ − profit ____ − travel (__ km × $0.73) ____ − supplies ____ = $____
```

An offer message that states your number once:

```text
Hi [Name], is the [item] still available? I can pick it up [day/time]
with cash or e-transfer for $[offer]. If that works, I'll confirm and
come by. Thanks!
```

A listing with room to negotiate:

```text
[Brand] [item] — [key spec] — $[list price]
Condition: [honest description, including any marks or repairs]
Tested: [what you checked, e.g. runs at all speeds / battery holds charge]
New: $[price] at [retailer] on [date]
Pickup in [area]. Cash or e-transfer on pickup.
```

Counteroffer replies:

```text
Thanks for the offer. The lowest I can do is $[floor]. It's [reason:
cleaned / new hardware / tested]. Happy to hold it until [time] for you.
```

```text
PRICE-DROP SCHEDULE
Day 0   list at expected sale + 15%
Day 7   new cover photo, same price
Day 14  drop to expected sale
Day 30  drop to floor, or move it (consignment, bundle, donate)
```

If you quote a new price in a listing, use the real current price and date; the Competition Bureau's rules on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) cover price and condition claims.

## How do you raise flip prices?

- **Clean, repair and photograph better.** New hardware on a dresser or a video of a mixer running supports a list price at the top of your comps.
- **Sell in season.** Patio furniture and bikes sell for more in spring than in November; a snowblower bought in April can wait for the first snowfall, if you have the space.
- **Bundle small items** (a KALLAX with storage bins, a drill with bits) so one pickup carries a higher price.
- **Raise the profit target as you get faster.** If your $/hour column runs above $22, keep the same max-buy formula with a larger target.
- **Cut buys, not list prices.** If margins shrink, offer less or drive less; the travel line is often the easiest cost to lower. For general rate-raising scripts, see [how to raise your side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/).

## What Facebook Marketplace pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common Facebook Marketplace pricing mistakes in Canada are paying close to resale price, forgetting travel (a 40 km round trip is $29.20 at 73¢), listing with no floor, and pricing above a current sale price at a big-box retailer.

Safety rules apply to private resellers too. Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says the Canada Consumer Product Safety Act prohibits selling dangerous products, bans items such as baby walkers, and prohibits traditional drop-side cribs; check [recalls](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before listing. Scams, such as fake payment screenshots and overpayment requests, can be reported to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/index-eng.htm).

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect Facebook Marketplace flipping in Canada?

Buying items to resell for profit in Canada is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html); selling your own used belongings occasionally is generally not. Record buy prices, supplies and kilometres, as the CRA's [keeping records page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records.html) explains. GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters; after that, add the GST/HST for your province on top of your sale prices.
