The Halloween side hustles that fit Canada in October 2026 are short, local, and safety-sensitive: setting up and taking down outdoor decorations, carving pumpkins for busy households, face painting at parties and events, making or altering costumes, and reselling used costumes and decor. Halloween falls on Saturday, October 31, 2026, so paid work tends to cluster in the two weekends before it and the first week of November for takedown. Health Canada's Halloween guidance shapes how to do each one safely: check the recalls database for costumes and decorations, prefer LED lights over open flames in jack-o'-lanterns, and patch-test face paint, because products labelled "hypoallergenic" can still cause reactions.

## How do you run a Halloween decor set-up service safely?

Run decor work as a scheduled, two-visit job: set-up before Halloween and takedown afterward, booked at the same time. Health Canada advises checking the [recalls and safety alerts database](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) for decorations including string lights, and suggests LED candles or lights in jack-o'-lanterns to reduce open-flame risk. If the client supplies the decor, check it before installing, and keep cords and props clear of walkways where trick-or-treaters trip in the dark. Ladder and roofline habits carry straight into the Christmas season, covered in [Christmas light installation](/side-hustles/christmas-light-installation-side-hustle-canada/).

## What safety rules apply to face painting and costumes in Canada?

For face painting, Health Canada's [Halloween safety update](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/staying-safe-halloween) recommends a patch test before use, warns that "hypoallergenic" products can still cause allergic reactions, and suggests checking ingredients when a child has a known skin allergy. Ask the parent before you paint, and use products sold as cosmetics for skin, not craft paint.

For costumes, the same guidance says to avoid flowing skirts, capes, baggy sleeves, and oversized accessories near open flames, notes that nylon or heavyweight polyester may be safer choices, recommends bright colours and reflective tape for visibility, and says masks should let a child see and breathe easily. Build those choices into anything you sew for children.

## Can you resell used Halloween costumes in Canada?

Yes, but the seller is responsible for safety. Health Canada's [guide to second-hand products](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says to check whether a product has been recalled, confirm all parts are present and working, and make sure items are sanitary; if you are unsure a product is safe, do not sell it or give it away. ## What can a Halloween weekend look like?

A Halloween weekend is a handful of short bookings packed into Saturday and Sunday. The labelled example shows how to price and count them.

### Illustrative example

All prices and times below are assumptions to show the arithmetic, not Canadian market rates.

- **Saturday:** three decor set-ups at an assumed $80 each, about 1.5 hours each including travel: 3 × $80 = $240 for 4.5 hours.
- **Sunday:** one children's party, face painting for an assumed 2 hours at $60 an hour: $120.
- **Supplies:** assume $40 of zip ties, hooks, and face-paint replacements for the weekend.
- **Weekend total before tax:** $240 + $120 − $40 = $320 for about 6.5 hours of work, or about $49 an hour.
- **Takedown visits** the following weekend add income only if you booked and priced them with the set-up.

Swap in your own local prices; the method is the point.

## What mistakes do people make with Halloween side hustles in Canada?

The common mistakes are starting too late, forgetting the takedown, and treating safety as optional.

- **Advertising in late October.** That leaves little time to book; start in early October.
- **Not booking takedown at the same time.** An unbooked takedown is an easy visit to lose.
- **Using open flames in display pumpkins.** Health Canada suggests LED lights instead.
- **Skipping the face-paint patch test.** "Hypoallergenic" does not mean no reactions.
- **Reselling recalled items.** Check the recalls database first.
- **Ladders on wet or icy steps.** Late October can bring frost; leave a job rather than risk a fall.

## Which tax rules apply to Halloween side hustles in Canada?

Paid Halloween work you do as a business is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and supplies you buy for the work are generally deductible. Add Halloween income to everything else you earn in your side business for the GST/HST [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). Seasonal store jobs are employment, with tax withheld by the employer.
