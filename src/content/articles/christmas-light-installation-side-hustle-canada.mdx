## How do you set up a ladder for Christmas lights in Canada?

Set up a Christmas-light ladder in Canada the way the [Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html) describes for portable ladders: the right duty rating, a firm level base, the 4-to-1 angle, and no climb near overhead power. CCOHS guidance is national plain language. Your province's occupational health rules still apply when you have workers, and a sole proprietor can fall just as hard.

### Step 1: Pick a ladder that matches the load

CCOHS describes portable ladder grades as light duty or grade 3, medium duty or grade 2, and heavy duty or grade 1, and it says to consider strength, type, length, and Canadian Standards Association approval. Read the label on the ladder you own. A light-duty household step stool is the wrong tool for an eavestrough. For work near electricity, CCOHS says not to use an aluminum ladder. A fibreglass ladder is the usual choice beside outdoor cords. Confirm the duty rating and the material on the label before you buy.

### Step 2: Inspect, angle, and climb

CCOHS says to inspect a ladder before each use and to remove one with loose rungs, damaged feet, cracks, or twisted rails. Ladders must rest on both side rails, not on a rung. The 4-to-1 rule is: for every 4 feet (1.2 metres) up, place the base 1 foot (0.3 metres) from the wall, about 75 degrees, on firm, level, non-slippery footing. Do not shim the feet with a barrel, box, or other loose object. Snow and ice are reasons to wait. Secure the base and the top. Securing the foot does not stop a side slip at the top. Do not rest the top on a gutter or a window. A stabilizer that bears on the wall is the piece to use when the trough cannot take the load.

Face the ladder, stay between the rails, and keep three-point contact: two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand. Do not carry clips or a coil of lights in your hand. Do not stand on or above the top two rungs. Do not climb in high winds. One person on the ladder. If you cannot reach, climb down and move the ladder.

### Step 3: Treat three metres and power lines as stop rules

CCOHS says to check your jurisdiction when working near or above 3 metres (10 feet), and that a harness and a proper anchor may be necessary, with training. Ontario's mandatory working-at-heights program applies to workers on construction projects who use listed fall-protection methods. The province says workers do not have to complete that approved program if their workplaces are not covered by the construction-projects regulation. Plug-in lights on a house are not automatically that kind of project. Read [Ontario's working-at-heights page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/training-working-heights) if you work there, and your own province's page if you do not. This guide does not say whether a certificate is required for your job.

Stay at least 3 metres from overhead power lines. That distance is on the Electrical Safety Authority's [holiday safety page](https://esasafe.com/safety/home-electrical-safety/holiday-safety/), which is Ontario guidance. CCOHS says local rules can set the distance. If the tree or the peak is inside it, decline the job.

## Which electrical rules apply to holiday lights in Canada?

Holiday lights in Canada belong outdoors only when they are rated for outdoor use, carry a Canadian certification mark, and plug into a ground-fault protected outdoor outlet. You are decorating. You are not modifying the building's wiring.

Health Canada's [holiday safety page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/tips-holiday-safety.html) says to buy lights with a Canadian certification mark such as CSA, cUL, or cETL, to stay inside the package wattage, to check recalls, and to keep indoor lights indoors. Use a ground-fault circuit interrupter outlet outdoors, avoid overloading an outlet, and turn lights off before bed or when leaving. Discard frayed cords, exposed wires, loose connections, and broken sockets.

The Electrical Safety Authority's holiday page, for Ontario, says to use clips and to avoid staples or nails, and to keep connections off the ground, away from puddles, driveways, and walkways. It says to follow manufacturer instructions and to connect no more than three light strings together. If the package states a different maximum, stop and read both the live page and the package before you add another string.

Health Canada has assessed decorative lighting strings against criteria in CSA C22.2 No. 37 and says certain strings that do not conform likely pose a danger. Search [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before you install a client's old box or a set bought online.

A new receptacle, a hardwired display, or an opened electrical box is regulated electrical work. In British Columbia, regulated electrical work generally requires an appropriate certificate. A homeowner permit exists for an owner working in their own fully detached dwelling, within stated limits, with inspections. That path is not a paid installer wiring a client's house. Use a licensed contractor. Do not remove the third prong from a plug. ESA says that pin is there to prevent shock.
