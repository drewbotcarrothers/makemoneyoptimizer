Event staffing means working short shifts at weddings, galas, festivals, conferences, sports games and private parties: serving, bartending, setting up and tearing down, checking coats, running registration or helping with crowds. In Canada most of this work is paid by the hour through a caterer, a venue, a staffing agency or a shift app, and Job Bank's latest wage data puts the national median at $18.50 an hour for banquet servers and $20.00 for bartenders. The barrier to entry is low: black clothes, comfortable non-slip shoes and, if you'll pour alcohol, a provincial certificate such as Ontario's Smart Serve ($44.95) or B.C.'s Serving It Right.

<QuickAnswer>
- **What it pays:** Job Bank medians (2023–2024 data, updated November 19, 2025) are $18.50/hour for food and beverage servers in banquet roles, $20.00 for bartenders and $16.55 for caterer helpers nationally, with tips on top where guests or the host provide them.
- **What it costs to start:** Smart Serve's full course is $44.95 (no HST) and valid five years, according to the AGCO; a waiter's corkscrew starts around $9 on Amazon.ca; most people already own black pants and a black or white shirt.
- **How you get shifts:** catering companies, hotel and convention banquet departments, event staffing agencies such as Toronto's Harrison Staffing and The Server Collective, and shift apps such as Instawork, which lists Toronto event roles from bartenders to ushers.
- **Employment status:** most event staff are employees of the caterer, venue or agency (T4 income). If you invoice hosts directly you're self-employed and report on Form T2125.
- **Ontario rule to know:** the Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act, in force since July 1, 2025, covers ride share, delivery and courier apps only, so it does not give event-shift app workers its minimum-wage guarantee. Employment status still decides your rights.
</QuickAnswer>

Two things stand out. First, the median banquet server in Ontario earns about the provincial minimum wage, which rose to $17.95 an hour on October 1, 2026, [CBC News reported](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/manitoba/minimum-wage-manitoba-saskatchewan-ontario-pei-nova-scotia-9.7364970). The same report lists $17.00 in Nova Scotia, $17.30 in P.E.I., $16.40 in Manitoba, $15.70 in Saskatchewan and $15.00 in Alberta, which hasn't raised its rate since 2019. Base pay at the bottom of the range is set by law, not by your skill.

Second, the high end runs to $30 an hour for servers and bartenders nationally, and Job Bank's high for Quebec bartenders is $40.00. Those higher rates usually go to experienced bartenders, captains and team leads at bigger events. Bartending is the clearest step up: the national median is $1.50 an hour more than banquet serving, and it's the role that needs the alcohol certificate.

Tips are the wild card. Some weddings and private parties pay staff a gratuity, some catering contracts add a service charge, and some events pay nothing extra. There's no reliable public dataset on event tips in Canada, so treat the base wage as your plan and any tips as a bonus.

### Why hosts and caterers need you

Event work is lumpy. A caterer might run one 40-person dinner on Tuesday and three 200-person weddings on Saturday, so it keeps a roster of on-call staff instead of a large full-time team. Danny Murrell, founder and CEO of Toronto's Harrison Staffing, said on the Forward Motion podcast in February 2026 that the company can field up to roughly 300 staff a day at its busiest, serving Toronto, Ottawa and Montreal ([Forward Motion, Sound Off Podcast Network](https://www.podpage.com/network/sound-off/show/forward-motion/danny-murrell/)). Ontario also proclaimed a Hospitality Workers Appreciation Day through a 2025 act; the episode notes it honours more than 426,700 workers in restaurants, hotels and catering.

## Do you need Smart Serve or Serving It Right?

If you'll sell or serve alcohol, almost certainly yes, and the rules are provincial.

**Ontario.** The Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario lists approved training programs on its [responsible service training page](https://www.agco.ca/en/alcohol/responsible-liquor-sale-service-and-delivery-training). The Smart Serve full course "meets the training requirements for all liquor licensees and employees (including bars and restaurants, grocery stores, convenience stores and delivery licensees)." It costs $44.95 (no HST), is available online and in person, is offered in English, French, Chinese, Korean, Punjabi and Spanish, and is valid for five years. The AGCO says all Ontario liquor training certifications must be renewed every five years. A cheaper Smart Serve Retail course ($24.95) covers retail settings only, so it's the wrong pick for events.

**British Columbia.** [Responsible Service BC](https://www.responsibleservicebc.gov.bc.ca/who-needs-serving-it-right) says that for licensed caterers, "licensees, managers and staff serving liquor at events must have a SIR certificate," and that anyone with a catering endorsement needs all servers working events to complete Serving It Right. Events under a Special Event Permit, such as weddings, community festivals and outdoor concerts in non-licensed spaces, have a separate abridged Special Event Server course. Check the current course fee on the Responsible Service BC site before you buy.

**Other provinces.** Alberta, Quebec, the Prairies and Atlantic Canada each set their own responsible-service programs. Ask the caterer or agency which certificate it requires before you pay for a course.

Food handler certification is a separate question. Some caterers want it for staff who plate or handle food. Ask before you buy a course.

## A 30-day plan to land your first shifts

### Week 1: Get eligible

- Check the minimum age and certificate rules for your province. In B.C., some Serving It Right settings also require staff to be at least 19.
- Take Smart Serve (Ontario), Serving It Right (B.C.) or your province's equivalent if you want bartending or bar-service shifts. Save the certificate as a PDF on your phone.
- Put together your uniform: black pants, a black and a white collared shirt, non-slip black shoes.
- Write a short event resumé. Lead with customer service, cash handling, any serving, retail or hospitality work, and your availability.

### Week 2: Apply everywhere at once

- Make a list of 10 to 15 caterers, hotel banquet departments, convention centres, golf clubs and staffing agencies near you.
- Apply to two or three agencies and two or three caterers. Agencies fill your calendar faster; caterers give you a steady crew.
- Sign up for a shift app if one covers your city, and read how it classifies workers before you take shifts.
- Tell everyone you know who has worked a wedding or catering job. Referrals are how many rosters fill.

### Week 3: Work your first shifts

- Arrive 15 minutes early with your certificate, corkscrew, two pens and a phone charger.
- Learn the event flow: guest arrival, cocktail hour, dinner service, speeches, dessert and teardown.
- Ask the captain how you'll be scored and who decides who comes back.
- Log the date, hours, pay rate and tips after every shift.

### Week 4: Become someone they rebook

- Message the coordinator to say thanks and give your availability for the next month.
- Ask about bartending, captain or lead roles once you've done a few events.
- Block the season's big dates early: wedding Saturdays, December party weekends and festival weeks.
- Compare your hourly take across routes and drop the one that pays least for your time.

### Shift confirmation checklist

- Venue address, loading or staff entrance, and parking or transit plan
- Call time and expected end time (teardown included?)
- Uniform: colour, tie, apron, shoes
- Role: server, bartender, setup or floater
- Point of contact on site and their phone number
- Meal break and staff meal arrangements
- How you'll be paid and when, and how tips are handled

### Bag checklist

- Alcohol-service certificate (digital and printed)
- Waiter's corkscrew, two pens, small notepad, lighter if the venue allows
- Hair ties, lint roller, spare socks, blister bandages
- Phone, charger and transit card
- Water bottle and a snack for the trip home

### Self-employed booking terms (direct to hosts)

> Service: bartending for [number] guests at [address] on [date], [start] to [end], including 30 minutes of setup and teardown.
> Rate: $[X] per hour, [minimum hours], payable [deposit / day of event].
> Alcohol: supplied by the host; I serve only guests of legal drinking age and may refuse service to anyone intoxicated.
> Cancellation: [terms].

Check your province's rules on who may serve alcohol at private events and whether a permit is needed. Liquor rules for events differ a lot across Canada.

## Worked math: what a weekend side hustle adds up to

*Illustrative example using Job Bank median wages. Your rate, hours and tips will differ.*

| Scenario | Shifts per month | Hours per shift | Hourly rate | Monthly base pay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario banquet server, wedding season | 4 | 8 | $17.95 (Ontario median) | $574.40 |
| Bartender, national median | 4 | 7 | $20.00 | $560.00 |
| Mix: 2 server shifts + 2 bartending shifts | 4 | 7.5 | $17.95 / $20.00 | $569.25 |
| Busy December: 6 holiday-party shifts as a bartender | 6 | 6 | $20.00 | $720.00 |

The mixed-shift row: 2 × 7.5 × $17.95 = $269.25, plus 2 × 7.5 × $20.00 = $300.00, for $569.25.

Startup costs are small next to that. A $44.95 Smart Serve certificate spread across its five-year validity costs less than $1 a month. A $8.63 corkscrew pays for itself in the first half hour of a shift.

Now subtract what you spend to get there. A 60-minute round trip by transit to a suburban wedding venue doesn't count as paid time, so 8 paid hours can mean 9 or 10 hours away from home. If you drive, parking at downtown hotels and the cost of a late-night trip home add up. Your real hourly rate is total pay plus tips, divided by every hour from leaving home to getting back.

If you're an employee, CPP and EI come off your pay and income tax may be withheld. The self-employed route has no withholding, so you'll need to set money aside yourself.

## Taxes: employee or self-employed?

Event staff hired by a caterer, venue or agency are usually employees, even for a single shift. If a caterer, venue or agency sets your schedule, supplies the equipment and directs the work, you're likely an employee, and you'll get a T4 slip. The CRA explains the tests on its [determine the employment status](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/canada-pension-plan-cpp-employment-insurance-ei-rulings/employee-self-employed/determine-employment-status.html) page. If you're unsure, you can ask the CRA for a ruling.

**Tips.** Tips and gratuities you receive are taxable. Tips that don't show on a T4 are generally reported as [other employment income on line 10400](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/line-10400-other-employment-income.html). Keep a simple log of tips by date and event.

**Self-employed.** If you invoice hosts directly as a bartender or server, report the income and expenses on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both the employee and employer shares of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on your net profit. Certificates, uniform items used only for work, bar tools and work-related travel may be deductible; keep receipts.

**GST/HST.** Self-employed workers must register once taxable supplies pass the CRA's [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive quarters. Employment income doesn't count toward that total.

For tracking and filing, see [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) and [how to report side hustle income to the CRA](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/). - **Quebec:** Job Bank's medians are $20.00 for banquet servers and $22.44 for bartenders; that bartender median is the highest of the provinces listed here, and the bartender high is $40.00. Self-employed income also counts for QPP, which replaces CPP, and QST alongside GST once registered.
- **Nova Scotia:** minimum wage is $17.00 from October 1, 2026 (CBC); Job Bank medians are $17.00 for banquet servers and $18.00 for bartenders.

## What should you keep in mind before you commit?

Event staffing is real money for flexible hours, but it's uneven. Wedding Saturdays and December parties fill up while January and February can go quiet, and an agency or app can only offer the shifts its clients book. Base pay in several provinces sits at or near minimum wage, so don't count on tips that may not exist at a given event. The work is physical: eight hours on your feet carrying trays, plus late finishes and travel home after transit has slowed down. If a platform treats you as an independent contractor, you may give up employment-standards protections, so read the terms. Ontario's platform-worker law doesn't cover event apps. Plan your budget around base wages and a quiet winter, and treat busy-season tips as extra.
