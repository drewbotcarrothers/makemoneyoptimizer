Bank account sign-up bonuses in Canada are cash or credits a bank pays after you open a deposit account and do what the offer's terms require. They suit someone who can read those terms, park money they will not need during the hold, and walk away if the monthly fee is larger than the bonus. They do not suit someone who would move rent money, open a credit product they do not understand, or treat a promotion as income they can spend before it arrives.

This is not tax, legal, or financial advice. Offers are marketing. The contract is the terms on the bank's site the day you accept them.

How a side hustle should separate its own money is in [opening a business bank account](/side-hustles/business-bank-account-side-hustle-canada/) and [Wealthsimple versus a bank business account](/side-hustles/wealthsimple-vs-bank-business-account-side-hustle-canada/). A bonus on a personal chequing account is a different decision from the account you use for clients.

## How does a bank account sign-up bonus work in Canada?

A bank account sign-up bonus in Canada works as a conditional payment. You open a specific account, you meet the conditions in the offer, you wait until the bank's payout date, and the bank deposits the bonus if you still qualify. If you miss a condition, the usual result is that the bonus is not paid. Some terms also take a bonus back if you close the account early. Read that sentence on the offer you are looking at. This guide will not guess a universal clawback rule.

| Condition you will usually see | What to find on the bank's page | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who counts as new | Whether you, or a household member, held an account there recently | A bonus for new clients excludes people the bank still counts as customers |
| Minimum balance | The dollar amount and how many days it must stay | Money you might need for rent is a bad balance to lock |
| Direct deposit | A payroll or other recurring deposit, sometimes with a minimum | A side hustle that pays by e-Transfer may not count. The terms define what counts |
| Hold period | The date the bonus is paid | A bonus next quarter is not money today |
| Account staying open | Whether closing early cancels or reclaims the bonus | The "free money" math includes the months you must stay |
| Monthly fee and waiver | The fee, and the balance or deposit that waives it | Fees over the hold can be larger than the bonus |
| What product it is | Chequing or savings, versus a credit card or line of credit | A credit offer is a borrowing product. This guide is about deposit accounts |

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada explains the right to open a personal deposit account, and the identification banks can ask for, on its [opening a bank account](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/banking/opening-bank-account.html) page. A promotion does not remove those rules. It also does not require a bank to offer you a bonus.

Check whether the institution is a CDIC member if deposit insurance matters to you, and read what [CDIC](https://www.cdic.ca/) actually covers. This guide does not restate a coverage limit. Confirm it on CDIC's site before you rely on it.

## What should you watch for before taking a bank bonus in Canada?

Watch the fee, the definition of "new," the payout date, and whether the account is a deposit account or a credit product. A bonus is a bad trade when the only way to earn it is to keep a balance you cannot spare, or to accept a monthly fee that continues after the promotion ends.

The FCAC's briefing for the House of Commons finance committee, dated for an appearance on 13 April 2026, says that as of 1 December 2025, participating institutions offer modern low-cost and no-cost accounts costing no more than $4 a month, and that 14 federally regulated financial institutions had signed the commitment so far. That cap is about everyday accounts under the commitment. It is not a cap on promotional accounts. If a bonus account charges more than a basic account would, write both monthly fees down and subtract them from the bonus over the months you must stay.

Also watch:

- **Payroll that cannot move.** If your employer needs a form and a pay cycle to change direct deposit, the offer's deadline may pass before the first deposit lands.
- **A side-hustle payout that is not a direct deposit.** Read the definition. Do not assume an e-Transfer or a payment-app withdrawal qualifies.
- **Credit cards and overdraft bundled into the application.** A deposit bonus does not require you to borrow. If the page is selling credit, stop and read that product on its own. This site does not cover casino or sportsbook offers, and it does not treat credit-card churn as a side hustle.
- **A business account you do not need yet.** Some banks require a business account for business activity. That question is the [business account guide](/side-hustles/business-bank-account-side-hustle-canada/), not a reason to open three personal accounts for bonuses.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a current offer, a survey, or a recommendation to open any account.

Suppose you open a bank's own offer page and it shows an assumed bonus of $300 if you keep an assumed $4,000 in the account for an assumed 180 days and set up a qualifying direct deposit. Suppose the monthly fee is an assumed $16.95 unless that balance condition is met. If you miss the waiver for six months, fees are 6 × $16.95 = $101.70. Subtracted from the assumed $300, that is $198.30, and the $4,000 was unavailable for those six months. If you meet the waiver, the assumed fees are $0 and the assumed bonus is $300, paid on the bank's date, not on day one. Tax is not subtracted in this sketch because this guide does not state a tax result. Do not copy $300, $16.95, or $4,000. Check the current terms on the bank's page. If the fee over the hold is larger than the bonus, the offer fails on its own arithmetic.

## Are bank sign-up bonuses taxable in Canada?

This guide does not state that every bank sign-up bonus is taxable, or that every bonus is tax-free. A search of CRA pages for a single rule aimed at customer account promotions did not produce a page this guide can quote as the answer. Employment gifts and employee taxable benefits are a different topic. They are about an employer and an employee, not about a bank and a retail customer.

What you can do without inventing a rule:

- Keep the offer terms, the date the bonus landed, and the account statements. The CRA's [business records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html) page is about business transactions. A personal bonus still belongs in your own file.
- Ask the bank whether it will issue a tax slip for the payment.
- If the account is the one you use for a side hustle, do not confuse a promotional credit with business revenue, and do not deduct a personal account fee as a business expense unless it meets the CRA's [business expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses.html) rules. Side hustle profit still goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). The [guide to reporting side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/) is the filing overview. Survey rewards, cashback, and bank bonuses are compared in [are rewards and bonuses taxable](/side-hustles/are-rewards-and-bonuses-taxable-canada/). ## Is chasing bank bonuses a side hustle in Canada?

Chasing bank bonuses in Canada is not a side hustle in the way mowing lawns or invoicing a client is. There is no skill you sell, and the amount is capped by the offer and by how much of your own cash you must park. The hours are the reading and the errands. The risk is a fee, a locked balance, or a credit product you did not mean to open.

People who already run a side hustle still need one clear place for client money. Opening and closing accounts to collect bonuses can make that record worse. ## What mistakes do people make with bank bonuses in Canada?

Common mistakes with bank bonuses in Canada are skipping the fee schedule, moving money you need before the hold ends, and assuming a blog's dollar amount is still the offer. Another mistake is counting the bonus as spent on the day you apply. It is not paid until the terms say it is paid.

Also common: letting a "new customer" definition fail because a joint account or a recently closed account still ties you to the bank, and treating a credit-card application as if it were a free chequing perk. Read the product name at the top of the page. A text that is not the bank's own site, and that asks you to send money to unlock a bonus, is not an offer.
