## Who does window cleaning in Canada suit?

Window cleaning in Canada suits people who enjoy methodical work, can be outside in changeable weather, and are disciplined about safety. It's a poor fit if you're uncomfortable on a ladder, if you need steady income through a Canadian winter, or if you'd be tempted to take a three-storey job because the client asked.

Storefront windows are a different rhythm from homes. A small shop on a main street may want its glass cleaned on a regular schedule, which gives you a route rather than one-off jobs. Homes tend to book seasonally, often in spring and fall.

## How do you stay safe cleaning windows in Canada?

Staying safe cleaning windows in Canada starts with not climbing at all for your first jobs: ground-floor windows and storefronts can be reached from the ground or with a short extension pole. When you do use a ladder, the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety's page on [portable ladders](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html) says falls from portable ladders are a major source of serious injury.

The same CCOHS page recommends using a ladder designed for the task, inspecting it before and after each use, keeping ladders away from power wires, facing the ladder when climbing, and not working from the top three rungs. It also says not to carry objects in your hands while on a ladder. Rules for workers vary by province, so check your provincial occupational health and safety regulator if you hire help.

Practical rules for a beginner:

- Write a height limit into your offer, such as ground floor and first-floor windows reachable from the ground with a pole.
- Never set a ladder near an overhead power line.
- Don't work alone on a ladder; have someone foot it when needed.
- Skip icy or windy days. Wet soles and a gust are a bad combination.

## How much can window cleaning earn in Canada?

Window cleaning earnings in Canada vary with the number and size of windows, access, and how dirty the glass is, so this guide does not publish a national rate. What you keep depends on:

- Number of panes, and whether you clean inside, outside, or both
- Screens, tracks, and sills, if included
- Height and access; anything needing a ladder takes longer
- Travel between jobs, and how tightly you can group a route
- The season: winter work is limited in much of Canada

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote or a market rate. Suppose a starter kit costs $90. Suppose you clean the front windows of four small shops on one street every two weeks at an assumed $30 a visit, taking 30 minutes each: 4 × $30 = $120 per round, over 2 hours, which is $60 an hour before travel and a tax set-aside. Two rounds, $240, cover the $90 kit with $150 left over. Real routes include travel, weather days, and shops that cancel.

## How do you start window cleaning in Canada?

Starting window cleaning in Canada means practising on your own windows, setting a height limit, and selling a simple service locally. The steps below follow that order.

### Step 1: Practise on your own glass

Learn the scrub-then-squeegee rhythm on your own windows until you can do a pane without streaks. Time yourself.

### Step 2: Set your service limits

Ground-floor exterior glass, storefronts, and interiors where invited. Write down what you won't do: second storeys, roofs, and anything near power lines.

### Step 3: Buy the basic kit

Squeegee, scrubber, bucket, scraper, and cloths. Add a pole when you need it. Skip ladders until a job justifies one, then follow the [CCOHS ladder guidance](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html).

### Step 4: Price by the window or the job

Count panes on three real homes or shops and write prices that include setup and travel. A simple menu (per pane, or per storefront) is easier for clients than an hourly rate.

### Step 5: Get an insurance quote

Describe residential and storefront glass, your height limit, and whether you clean interiors. This is not insurance advice.

### Step 6: Walk a main street

Small storefronts are visible prospects. Offer a first clean, then a regular schedule. Leave a card with your price and phone number. Homes tend to book when you knock a street of larger ground-floor glass and ask each finished client for one neighbour on that block. Count the panes before you quote. The prices are in [how to price window cleaning in Canada](/side-hustles/window-cleaning-pricing-canada/), and the first-street steps are in [how to get your first window cleaning clients in Canada](/side-hustles/window-cleaning-first-clients-canada/). Signs, scripts, and the rules on follow-up email are in [door-to-door marketing for side hustles in Canada](/side-hustles/door-to-door-marketing-side-hustle-canada/). Keep the height limit. The [CCOHS portable ladder page](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html) is why a third storey is not a first-customer tactic.

### Step 7: Build a route

Group clients by street or neighbourhood so travel time doesn't eat the margin. Keep a simple calendar of who's due when.

## What mistakes are common in window cleaning in Canada?

Common window cleaning mistakes in Canada are taking jobs above your height limit, working in freezing weather, and pricing without counting travel. Each one costs more than the job pays.

- **Climbing for a one-off job.** A fall costs far more than any window is worth.
- **Ignoring the weather.** Water freezes on glass in Canadian cold, and wind turns a ladder unsafe.
- **Scratching glass.** Use a scraper only on wet glass and stop if something grinds.
- **Scattered clients.** One-off jobs across town lose money to travel. Build routes.
- **No written price.** Confirm what's included (screens, sills, interiors) before you start.

## Which tax rules apply to window cleaning in Canada?

Window cleaning income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all your side-hustle revenue.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work happens, using the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) page. Keep receipts for tools, supplies, and insurance, and a kilometre log if you drive between jobs. Some cities require a business licence for home services; check your municipality's website.
