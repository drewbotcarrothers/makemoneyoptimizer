Window cleaning in Canada means washing residential and storefront glass, usually exterior first, with a squeegee, a scrubber and later a water-fed pole, from April to October. HomeStars' 2026 price guide puts an exterior-only clean on an average detached house at $150 to $300 and inside-and-out at $250 to $500, with $5 to $12 per standard window. You can start for under $100 in tools, the work repeats every spring and fall, and an Orillia teen grew a bucket-and-squeegee start at 13 into 125 clients by 16.

- **Speed:** HomeStars says an exterior-only clean of a two-storey house with 15 to 20 windows usually takes one to two hours.

## Who does window cleaning suit?

Window cleaning suits methodical people who like visible results, can work outside in changeable weather, and are disciplined about safety. It's a classic student summer business: the Ontario examples below were started at 13, run through university franchises, or owned by college students. It also works as a weekend add-on for anyone already doing lawn care, pressure washing or house cleaning.

It's a poor fit if you're uncomfortable on a ladder, need steady income through a Canadian winter, or would be tempted to take a three-storey job because the client asked. Start with ground-floor windows and storefronts and grow into height with the right equipment.

## How do you stay safe?

Start with no climbing: ground-floor windows and storefronts can be reached from the ground or with a short extension pole. When you do use a ladder, the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety's page on [portable ladders](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html) says falls from portable ladders are a major source of serious injury, and advises using a ladder designed for the task and inspecting it before each use.

- Use a pole, not a ladder, wherever you can. Lightfoot saved his first summer's profit for a water-fed system that reaches four storeys from the ground so he doesn't climb.
- Never set a ladder on ice, soft soil or a deck without footing.
- Keep three points of contact and don't overreach.
- Turn down roofs, steep grades and anything above your training; HomeStars notes that buildings above four storeys need rope access or platforms, which is commercial work.
- If you hire helpers, check your province's workplace safety and workers' compensation rules first.

## How does window cleaning pay?

Most pros charge per window or per pane rather than by the hour, so the price scales with window count, HomeStars says. Interior cleaning, screens, tracks and hard water stains are add-ons.

Recurring clients often get a lower per-visit rate.

## What does the math look like?

**Illustrative.** Prices come from HomeStars' 2026 ranges; job counts, travel time and supply costs are assumptions.

Formula: windows × price per window + add-ons − supplies and travel = profit; profit ÷ total hours = hourly.

| Scenario | Job | Price | Total hours incl. travel (assumed) | Supplies/fuel (assumed) | Profit | Per hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bungalow, exterior | 12 standard windows × $8 | $96 | 1.25 | $6 | $90 | $72.00 |
| Two-storey, exterior | 18 windows × $8 + 8 upper × $5 | $184 | 2.0 | $8 | $176 | $88.00 |
| Two-storey, inside and out | $184 + 18 × $5 interior + 18 screens × $3 | $328 | 4.0 | $10 | $318 | $79.50 |

Check the middle row: 18 × $8 = $144, plus 8 × $5 = $40, totals $184; $184 − $8 = $176; $176 ÷ 2 = $88 an hour.

Season math: 4 exterior jobs a weekend at about $184 for 24 weekends is 4 × $184 × 24 = $17,664 in revenue in this sketch, before rain-outs. Spring-and-fall repeat clients are what make that realistic: Lightfoot's 125 clients were mostly repeat customers.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

### Week 1: Tools and technique

- Buy a squeegee, scrubber, bucket and cloths (about $45 to $70 at the prices above).
- Clean every window at your own home and two relatives' homes. Time each window.
- Practise the "fan" squeegee technique on a large pane until there are no streaks or edge lines.

### Week 2: Offer and prices

- Set per-window prices from the HomeStars table, adjusted for your town.
- Write a one-page flyer: exterior price per standard window, a sample house price, and your season.
- Get an insurance quote and register your business name if you'll use one.

### Week 3: First clients

- Door-knock or flyer one neighbourhood of similar houses (see [door-to-door marketing](/side-hustles/door-to-door-marketing-side-hustle-canada/)), and walk storefront strips asking for the owner.
- Offer neighbours a same-day discount so you can clean three houses on one street.
- Book 5 to 10 jobs.

### Week 4: Deliver and lock in repeat work

- Do each job, photograph before and after, and ask for a review.
- Offer every client a fall visit at a recurring-client price.
- Track hours per job and adjust your prices.

## What mistakes do new window cleaners make?

- **Climbing too early.** Build the business on ground-floor work, then buy a water-fed pole before you buy a taller ladder.
- **Pricing by guesswork.** Count windows and use per-window prices; a "flat $100" can be a loss on a big house.
- **Working in full sun.** Solution dries before you squeegee, leaving streaks.
- **Skipping the pre-job walk.** Point out cracks and old stains before you start, not after.
- **Forgetting the second visit.** Book fall when you finish spring; repeat clients are the business.
- **Underquoting hard water stains.** They're a separate line in every published price guide.
- **No insurance.** One broken pane or fallen ladder can cost more than a summer's profit.

## Which tax rules apply to window cleaning in Canada?

Window cleaning is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people 18 and over pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Tools, supplies, insurance, advertising and the business share of vehicle costs are deductible; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/) and [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count pressure washing, light installation and other side-hustle revenue. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work happens; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered. Summer Company supports entrepreneurs aged 15 to 29; Job Bank's Ontario window-cleaner median is $19.25.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered. Job Bank's Quebec median is $21.00.
- **British Columbia and Alberta:** Job Bank medians are $20.00; B.C. has PST separate from GST, Alberta has GST only.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Everywhere:** if you hire, provincial workplace safety and workers' compensation rules apply to ladder work.
