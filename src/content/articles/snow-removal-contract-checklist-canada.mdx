A snow removal contract in Canada, for a side hustle, is a one-page note that names the address, the surfaces, the trigger depth, the time window, the price, salt, the windrow, and how either person can stop. Snow removal means paid clearing of nearby driveways and walks. The checklist suits someone signing a few neighbours, not someone who wants a lawyer's template pasted without reading it.

This page is not legal advice, not a form that is enforceable because it appears here, and not a substitute for a lawyer if the route, the property, or the risk is larger than a handful of houses. ## What should a snow removal checklist in Canada cover?

A snow removal checklist in Canada should cover scope, trigger, timing, money, salt, the windrow, exclusions, and cancellation, in sentences both people can repeat. If a line is missing, the first storm becomes the negotiation.

| Line | Question the note answers | Leave it blank and |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parties and address | Who is clearing which property | You may be argued into the house next door |
| Surfaces | Driveway, walk, steps, sidewalk, windrow, or not | The sidewalk appears for free |
| Trigger | How deep, and who measures | You are out for flurries, or you argue about flurries |
| Clock | Done-by time, and what happens if snow is still falling | The client expects a clear during the storm |

## Which scope lines belong on a snow removal agreement in Canada?

Scope lines on a snow removal agreement in Canada should list the surfaces you will clear and the surfaces you will not touch. Quote the sidewalk only after you read that city's winter page. The duty is municipal, and it changes.

Toronto's [sidewalk clearing page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-maintenance/winter-maintenance/clearing-snow-and-ice-from-your-property/) says that when accumulation is less than 2 cm, residents and businesses clear sidewalks next to their property within 12 hours of the end of a snowfall, and that city crews clear public sidewalks at 2 cm or more. Private driveways, parking spaces, steps, ramps, and landings are described there as a 24-hour owner responsibility. The page also says snow must not be pushed onto sidewalks, roads, or bike lanes, and it lists a fine of $500 plus a $110 victim surcharge. Confirm the live page. That fine is Toronto's, and the program has changed before. Do not paste Toronto's hours into a contract in another city.

Ottawa's [Use and Care of Roads By-law](https://ottawa.ca/en/living-ottawa/laws-licences-and-permits/laws/laws-z/use-and-care-roads-law-no-2003-498) says no person shall throw, push, plow, dump, or otherwise deposit snow or ice on a highway, with an exception for a city employee or agent engaged in moving snow or ice. If your note includes a sidewalk or a windrow, it should also say where the snow will sit. "On the client's property, where the city allows" is the shape of the sentence. Your city supplies the exact rule.

### Step 1: Name the property and the person

Write the street address and who is agreeing. If a tenant is paying and an owner must allow access, say who lets you in the gate. Do not clear a house because the neighbour waved.

### Step 2: List surfaces in plain words

Driveway, front walk, steps, city sidewalk, and the plough windrow are separate lines. A windrow is the ridge at the end of the driveway after a city plough. Say whether a return visit is included, extra, or not part of the job.

### Step 3: Write the exclusions in the same list

Roofs and ice dams stay off the menu for a typical side hustle. So do public streets, the neighbour's half "as a favour," and any surface you cannot exit safely. Equipment limits are in the [tools guide](/side-hustles/snow-removal-tools-canada). A plough on a private lot is still not a contract to plough the road.

## Which trigger, clock, and season lines belong on the note?

Trigger, clock, and season lines tell a snow removal client in Canada when you come and when the job is over. Pick one depth, in centimetres, and use it on every house. The beginner guide's example band, somewhere in the 2 to 5 centimetre range, is an example to write down, not a survey of contracts.

Say who measures. "Settled snow at the driveway, not a drift against the step" is clearer than "a good dump." Say the hour you aim to be done by, and that a storm still falling at that hour means you finish after it stops or you do a pass and return. Write the calendar dates. A season with no end date is how May becomes a dispute.

The mild-winter sentence belongs here. Options you can write, and then actually follow, include treating the fee as a retainer to be on call, capping included visits, or offering a credit you defined in October. Inventing the policy after a green winter is the mistake.

## What snow removal contract mistakes are common in Canada?

Common snow removal contract mistakes in Canada are a verbal "I'll just come," an unlimited season, and a sidewalk you never priced. The other pattern is a long template neither person understands. A short note you both follow beats a form you copied from another province.

Promising the city's deadline as if it were your personal guarantee is a mistake when the storm is still falling. Promising to push snow into the street because the lawn is full is a bylaw problem, not a customer-service favour. Adding roofs in a postscript because the client asked nicely is how a driveway price becomes a dangerous job. Taking the deposit and then changing the trigger in January breaks the record you wrote. ## Which tax rules apply to a snow removal agreement in Canada?

Snow removal income in Canada is self-employment income, including a seasonal lump sum, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for snow removal follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

A deposit in November is still income to track, even when the note calls it a retainer. Keep the note, the texts, and the transfers. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add lawn, cleaning, and handyman revenue. If you are registered, the agreement should show the rate for the province where you work. Confirm it with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). If you are not registered, do not charge the tax. Set aside any tax you collect before you spend the instalment.
