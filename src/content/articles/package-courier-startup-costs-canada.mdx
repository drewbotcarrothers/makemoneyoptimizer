## How much does package courier work cost to start in Canada?

Package courier work in Canada costs about $93.29 for a lean gear kit, $234.51 for a standard kit, or $404.74 for a pro kit, all with 13% Ontario HST, before any insurance change. Use your province's rate. The kits assume a car, hatchback or SUV with a flat cargo area, a smartphone and a licence, and they don't include a vehicle payment.

## Who is this package courier budget a fit for in Canada?

This package courier budget in Canada fits people who can lift boxes repeatedly, wait in a pickup queue without treating that time as free, and keep cargo from becoming a projectile on a ramp. Early mornings and evenings are common. The lean kit fits a first week of Spark offers. The standard kit fits Flex blocks with dozens of parcels. The pro kit fits heavier, bulkier offers and long days.

The same budget is a poor fit if the only vehicle is a two-seater full of child seats, if your back is already the limiting factor, or if you live outside the zones. Unpaid waiting is the cost that never shows on a gear table.

## How do pay floors change the break-even?

Ontario requires at least the general minimum wage, $17.95 an hour from October 1, 2026, for time spent on an assignment, with tips not counted, per [Ontario's digital platform workers page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers). B.C. requires $21.89 an hour of engaged time plus $0.35 per km for delivery by any means other than walking, from June 1, 2026, per [B.C.'s minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage). Waiting for an offer isn't engaged time.

## What are the ongoing costs?

Ongoing costs for package courier work in Canada are fuel, wear, failed-delivery kilometres, small replacements, phone data, insurance, and unpaid waiting at pickup. Hours start when you arrive for pickup, not when you tap complete on the last stop.

**Fuel and wear.** Highway blocks burn more than neighbourhood hops. Include the empty drive back. The tax claim, if you make one, is usually actual expenses times a business-use percentage from a log.

**Failed deliveries.** The drive back to return a parcel is unpaid distance. Log it on that block.

**Supplies that wear out.** Gloves, straps and a blanket. A new $18.99 pack of gloves, not a new kit every month.

**Insurance.** The monthly number from your quote, for the province where the car is plated. In B.C., that's an ICBC delivery policy for Flex.

**Waiting.** Station check-in and store pickups take time with no receipt. It still counts in the hourly math.

## Where can a package courier budget in Canada stay smaller?

- **Measure the cargo area** with a tape before you buy storage.
- **Buy the hand truck after the first heavy offer,** not because a video said so.
- **Use clean blankets you already own** for the first week.
- **Decline offers that don't fit.** A photo of the open trunk is free.
- **Skip invoicing software** unless a local courier company asks you to bill them as a contractor. Read whether that company wants an employee or an invoice before you hear a rate.

## Where should a package courier budget in Canada refuse to shrink?

Keep the insurance quote, gloves, a stable load, and a mounted phone, because delivering goods for pay is business use of the vehicle in the province where the car is plated. A personal policy that excludes that use is not a saving. This guide is not insurance advice. Get the quote while you can still say no.

A box through a taillight, or a hand sliced on a frozen latch, costs more than the organizer. Decline an offer your back or your cargo area cannot finish.

## How do taxes and vehicle claims work for package courier work in Canada?

Package courier income in Canada is generally self-employment income, GST/HST follows the small-supplier test, and the vehicle claim is the business portion from a kilometre log. This guide is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Package delivery is not the ride-sharing first-fare rule.

Keep offer summaries and receipts. Amazon's FAQ says it typically doesn't issue tax slips to Flex drivers resident in Canada, so your log and the app's earnings screen are your records. The whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution generally applies to net self-employment income. Report the business on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with your T1.

**Log and CCA.** A kilometre log, with date, destination, kilometres and purpose, supports the business-use percentage of the vehicle. Apply it to fuel, insurance, and repairs. Gloves and straps are ordinary expenses. A vehicle, trailer, or other lasting asset is [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion.

**GST/HST.** The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and your other side hustles. The CRA explains the timing on its [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page. If you also carry passengers for a platform, those fares follow the first-fare rule on the CRA's [ride-sharing page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html).

**Input tax credits.** Registrants can often claim an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) for GST/HST paid on business purchases used in commercial activity. Keep receipts that show the tax. Non-registrants do not charge GST/HST and do not claim the credit.

Charge the provincial rate once you are registered. Quebec adds QST. Some provinces use HST, including Nova Scotia at 14% as of 1 April 2025 ([Charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)). Others charge GST and a separate PST. Use that table or the CRA [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Some B.C. municipalities require a business licence; check your city's page.
