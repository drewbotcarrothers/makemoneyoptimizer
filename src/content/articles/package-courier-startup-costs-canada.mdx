Where Spark, Amazon Flex, and local courier work exist, and how to test a week, is the [package courier guide](/side-hustles/package-courier-side-hustle-canada). This page is the spending. The expensive asset is the vehicle you already own. The cheap items are what keep you from breaking merchandise or your shoulders.

## The quick answer

Figures are typical Canadian retail ranges, not a bid. They assume a car, hatchback, or SUV with a flat cargo area, plus a smartphone and insurance you are willing to discuss with the insurer. They do not include a vehicle payment.

| How you start | One-time cash, about | What that covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $80–$180 | Work gloves, a cargo bin or two, a phone mount, a charger, and a headlamp. You decline offers that need a dolly until you have seen them. |
| Typical | $200–$450 | A folding hand truck, a trunk organizer, moving blankets or a liner, straps, and the lean list. |
| Doing it properly | $400–$900, plus insurance | The typical kit, a fuel float for the first blocks, and the insurance quote for delivering goods for pay. A roof pod or a trailer is not in this column. Those are later, after you know offer sizes. |

Spark Driver is the clearest Canadian example of store-pickup delivery. Walmart’s Spark Driver Canada page describes independent-contractor drivers paid per completed delivery, choosing which offers to accept, and it lists pickup areas that change. Amazon Flex lists its own Canadian cities. If your town is on neither list, a dolly does not create a zone. Check the app before you buy.

Signup language on the Spark Canada Play listing has said you need a car, a smartphone, and vehicle insurance, and that a background check is part of joining. Follow the checklist inside the Canadian app. Do not copy a U.S. Flex document list onto a Canadian application.

## Who this budget fits

**It fits** if you can lift boxes repeatedly, wait in a pickup queue without pretending that time was free, and keep cargo from becoming a projectile on a ramp. Early mornings and evenings are common.

**It is a poor fit** if the only vehicle is a two-seater full of child seats, if your back is already the limiting factor, or if you live outside the zones. Unpaid waiting is the cost that never shows on a gear table.

Hot food is a smaller kit and a different rhythm: [food delivery startup costs](/side-hustles/food-delivery-startup-costs-canada). Choosing the groceries yourself is [grocery shopping startup costs](/side-hustles/grocery-shopping-startup-costs-canada). Passengers are [rideshare startup costs](/side-hustles/rideshare-startup-costs-canada), with a stricter GST/HST rule.

## Ongoing costs, and a simple break-even

**Fuel and wear.** Highway blocks burn more than neighbourhood hops. Use your consumption and the price you paid, and include the empty drive back to the store or home. A planning set-aside per kilometre keeps wear money from getting spent. The tax claim, if you make one, is usually actual expenses times a business-use percentage from a log.

**Failed deliveries.** The drive back to return a parcel is unpaid distance. Log it on that block.

**Supplies that disappear.** Straps, gloves, and a blanket wear out. Budget a small replacement, not a new kit every month. Often $0 in a quiet month and $20–$40 when something tears.

**Platform pay.** Spark’s Canada page says you are paid for each completed delivery and that you choose offers. It does not publish a wage. Where tips exist, confirm the current in-app terms. Amazon Flex blocks, in cities that have them, show an amount before you accept, and the time can run long. Do not import a U.S. block rate.

**Phone and insurance.** Data add-on $0–$30 if you need it. Insurance is the monthly number from your quote.

**Waiting.** Arrival at the store until the order exists is part of the shift. It has no receipt and it still counts in the hourly math.

### How to calculate break-even

1. One-time startup.
2. Monthly fixed costs (insurance difference, phone).
3. Variable cost of one block: fuel, wear set-aside, parking, and the return kilometres.

Contribution = pay for the completed block, plus any tip the app actually confirmed, minus variable cost.

Blocks to recover gear = startup ÷ contribution.

Hours should start when you arrive for pickup, not when you tap complete on the last stop.

### Illustrative example

**This sketch uses tidy numbers so the division is obvious. It is not a Spark or Flex quote.**

You spent $240 on a folding dolly, bins, gloves, and a mount. Insurance in this sketch is $60 a month extra. One block pays $85. You drove 70 km including the drive back. At 8.5 L/100 km and $1.55/L, fuel is 70 × 0.085 × 1.55 = $9.22. Wear at $0.12/km is $8.40. No parking fee.

Variable cost = $17.62. Contribution = $85 − $17.62 = $67.38. You were out for 3 hours including a 40-minute wait. This sketch is about $22 per hour before the monthly insurance.

Gear payback = $240 ÷ $67.38, or about four blocks like this one. The $60 insurance takes roughly one such block a month. A block that pays $85 and sends you 140 km, or makes you return three failed stops, may not clear fuel and wear. Decline patterns you have now measured. Do not average this paragraph into a weekly plan.

## Where you can spend less

Measure the cargo area with a tape before you buy storage. Offers that do not fit are declines, not puzzles. A photo of the open trunk is free.

Buy the dolly after the first heavy offer, not before you are off the waitlist. A waitlist is not a business.

Use blankets you already own for the first week if they are clean and dark enough that a scuff does not matter. Replace them if customers’ goods are sliding.

Skip the trailer. Four bins and a dolly move a surprising number of grocery-sized orders. Televisions and flat furniture are a vehicle question, not a strap question.

Apps pay you per completed delivery. You do not need invoicing software unless a local courier company asks you to bill them as a contractor. Read whether that company wants an employee or an invoice before you hear a rate.

## Where not to cut corners

Insurance is the non-negotiable. Delivering goods for pay is business use of the vehicle. A personal policy that excludes it is not a saving. This is not insurance advice. Get the quote while you can still say no.

Do not skip gloves and a stable load. A box through a taillight, or a hand sliced on a frozen latch, costs more than the organizer.

Do not use a phone in your hand on the ramp. Mount it. Provincial distracted-driving penalties are a worse line item than a $30 mount.

Do not accept an offer your back or your cargo area cannot finish, then “make it work” with three trips the pay did not include.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Package-delivery income is generally self-employment income. Keep offer summaries and receipts. A T4A may arrive. You still report the income if it does not. Both shares of CPP generally apply to net self-employment income. Report the business on form T2125 with your T1.

**Log and CCA.** A kilometre log — date, destination, kilometres, purpose — supports the business-use percentage of the vehicle. Apply it to fuel, insurance, and repairs. Do not claim 100 percent of a family car. Bins and gloves are ordinary expenses. A vehicle, trailer, or other lasting asset is capital. Capital cost allowance (CCA) recovers that cost over time on the business portion, in the class for that year. Do not write off a car in the month you deliver your first parcel. Confirm the class when you file.

**GST/HST.** Same-day package work is not taxi or ride-sharing. The small-supplier test applies: worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and your other side hustles. The CRA explains the timing on its [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page. If you also carry passengers for a platform, those fares follow the first-dollar rule on the CRA’s [ride-sharing page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html).

**Input tax credits.** Registrants can often claim an input tax credit for GST/HST paid on business purchases used in commercial activity. Keep receipts that show the tax. Non-registrants do not charge GST/HST and do not claim the credit.

Charge the provincial rate once you are registered. Quebec adds QST. Some provinces use HST, including Nova Scotia at 14% as of 1 April 2025. Others charge GST and a separate PST. Use the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set the tax aside.

Zones, background checks, and any municipal licence are local. Read the Canadian app and your city’s business-licence page. Do not budget a fee you have not looked up, and do not assume every city charges one.
