A browser extension or mobile app side hustle in Canada builds one small tool, publishes it on the Chrome Web Store, Google Play or the App Store, and charges once or by subscription. Publishing costs are small (a one-time Chrome Web Store fee, US$25 once for Google Play, 99 USD a year for Apple), and both app stores offer a 15% commission tier on the first US$1 million of a developer's yearly earnings. Every fee below was read on the store's or processor's own page on October 19, 2026.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Chrome Web Store:** a one-time registration fee before you can publish; Chrome Web Store payments are deprecated, so paid extensions take payment through your own processor ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/register)).
- **Google Play:** US$25 one-time registration fee; prepaid cards are not accepted ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435)).
- **Google Play fee (markets including Canada, until the updated fees roll out):** 15% on the first US$1 million a year for developers enrolled in the 15% tier, 30% above that, and 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions ([Service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622)).
- **Apple:** the Developer Program is 99 USD per membership year, shown in local currency at enrollment; the Small Business Program cuts the commission to 15% for developers with up to US$1 million in proceeds the prior year ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)).
- **Selling your own subscription:** Stripe Canada charges 2.9% + CA$0.30 per successful domestic card payment, +0.8% for international cards and +2% if currency conversion is required ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing)).

</QuickAnswer>

## How do browser extensions and mobile apps make money?

Browser extensions and mobile apps make money from a one-time price, a subscription, or in-app purchases, and the store you sell through decides who takes the fee. The three routes:

| Route | Who takes payment | Fee you pay (October 19, 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chrome extension with your own checkout | You, through a processor | Stripe Canada: 2.9% + CA$0.30 per domestic card payment |
| Android app or in-app purchase on Google Play | Google Play billing | 15% on the first US$1M a year (15% tier), 30% above; 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions |
| iPhone or iPad app on the App Store | Apple In-App Purchase | 15% in the Small Business Program up to US$1M; the standard rate above that |

Google's page says 97% of developers distribute on Google Play at no charge, and that of those subject to a fee, 99% are eligible for 15% or less. It also lists different, newer fee tables for users in Australia, the European Economic Area, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States, with rollout dates of June 30, 2026 (EEA, UK, US) and September 30, 2026 (Australia, Japan). If many of your buyers are in the U.S., read that table: it uses 10% to 25% plus a 5% billing fee, depending on the transaction type and install date.

## What does it cost to publish?

Publishing costs a registration fee per store and nothing per listing. What each store's own page says:

- **Chrome Web Store.** You register as a developer and pay a one-time registration fee before publishing. Google suggests a new email address used only for publishing, because the account email cannot be changed later.
- **Google Play.** A US$25 one-time registration fee, paid by Mastercard, Visa, American Express or Visa Electron (outside the U.S.); prepaid cards are not accepted.
- **Apple.** 99 USD per membership year, listed in local currency during enrollment. Nonprofits, accredited educational institutions and government entities that meet requirements can request a fee waiver.

So the first-year platform cost of shipping on all three is the Chrome fee, plus US$25, plus 99 USD. Your computer, test devices and design tools are the larger costs; price them from your own receipts.

## Illustrative example: when do store fees and memberships break even?

This is an illustrative example. The fees are sourced from the pages above; prices and sales counts are assumptions. Store amounts are in US dollars, as the stores state them.

**iPhone app, one-time price (Apple Small Business Program):**

- Assumed price: US$2.99.
- Your proceeds: US$2.99 × (1 − 0.15) = US$2.54 a sale (before any sales tax handling by the store).
- Sales to cover the 99 USD membership: 99 ÷ 2.54 = 39 sales a year.

**Chrome extension, subscription through Stripe Canada:**

- Assumed price: CA$4.00 a month, paid by domestic cards.
- Stripe fee: 4.00 × 0.029 + 0.30 = CA$0.42. You keep CA$3.58 a month per subscriber.
- 100 subscribers: 100 × 3.58 = CA$358 a month, before tax and hosting.
- An international card adds 0.8% (about CA$0.03 on CA$4.00), and currency conversion adds 2%.

| Subscribers (assumed) | Monthly revenue at CA$4 | Stripe fees | You keep |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25 | CA$100.00 | CA$10.40 | CA$89.60 |
| 100 | CA$400.00 | CA$41.60 | CA$358.40 |
| 400 | CA$1,600.00 | CA$166.40 | CA$1,433.60 |

The CA$0.30 fixed fee is why tiny monthly prices hurt: at CA$1.00 a month, the Stripe fee is CA$0.33, a third of the payment. Annual billing (one charge a year) cuts that.

A first 90 days builds one tool, gets it in front of 20 real users, and only then adds a price.

## What mistakes do new extension and app developers make?

- **Planning on Chrome Web Store payments.** They're deprecated, so a paid extension needs its own payment processor.
- **Registering for Google Play with a prepaid card.** Play's US$25 registration fee doesn't accept prepaid cards.
- **Not enrolling in the reduced-fee tier.** Google Play's 15% rate on the first US$1 million is for developers enrolled in that tier. Apple's Small Business Program also needs an application.
- **Forgetting Apple's yearly fee.** The Developer Program is 99 USD every membership year, whether or not the app earns.

## Which tax rules apply to app and extension income in Canada?

App and extension income is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), with developer fees, processing fees and a share of your computer as expenses. GST/HST registration follows the $30,000 small-supplier test on the CRA's [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page. The CRA's [digital-economy page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/cross-border.html) explains that registered distribution platform operators, such as app stores, charge and collect GST/HST on digital products sold to Canadian consumers by non-resident vendors who are not registered under the normal regime. How a store handles tax for a Canadian-resident developer is set in that store's agreement, so read it before you register for GST/HST. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
