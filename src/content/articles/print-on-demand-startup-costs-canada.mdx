Starting print-on-demand in Canada can cost about $0–$40 if you design on a free tool and list through a marketplace you already use, about $40–$150 for a typical start that includes one or two sample orders, or about $150–$450 when you add a paid storefront month, a few more samples, and a paid design tool. Printful's Canada page says signing up, designing, and setting up a store are free and that you pay only when a sale happens, so most of the cost is samples and the storefront you pick. The ranges are retail and platform figures to confirm, not quotes. They suit people who have one original design and can wait for an order.

## How much does print-on-demand cost to start in Canada?

Print-on-demand in Canada starts at about $0–$40 with free sign-up and a marketplace listing, about $40–$150 when you order samples of what you sell, and about $150–$450 with your own store subscription. The printer charges its product and shipping price when an order arrives, which comes out of the sale price you set.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0–$40 | Free printer account, a free design tool, and marketplace listing fees, for example a few Etsy listings at 0.20 USD each |
| Typical | $40–$150 | The lean column plus one or two sample orders so your photos and descriptions match the real product |
| Fuller | $150–$450 | Samples in more colours, a Shopify month on a plan you chose, and a paid design or mockup tool |

## Who should use a print-on-demand startup budget in Canada?

A print-on-demand startup budget in Canada fits people who own the rights to their artwork and want to test it without holding stock. The lean column fits a designer with a finished file. It is a poor fit if the art uses a team logo, a character, or someone else's file, or if you need income before the first order.

If you sew, paint, or pack the object yourself, compare [Etsy shop startup costs](/side-hustles/etsy-shop-startup-costs-canada/). If the buyer downloads a file, see [printables startup costs](/side-hustles/printables-startup-costs-canada/).

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for print-on-demand in Canada?

Print-on-demand break-even in Canada is the retail price minus the printer's cost, shipping you absorb, and platform fees, and then the storefront subscription spread over the orders in that month. One order rarely pays for a month of a store plan.

### Step 1: Read the printer's cost for the exact blank and size

A larger size or a different colour can cost more. Price the one you list.

### Step 2: Decide who pays shipping

If you offer free shipping, the printer's shipping charge comes out of your margin, and on Etsy the 6.5% applies to the full displayed price.

### Step 3: Subtract the platform's fees

Use the page for the platform you sell on, not a fee from a video.

### Step 4: Spread the subscription over real orders

A $37 store month with one order is a $37 cost on that order.

### Step 5: Order a sample before you write the description

A description that does not match the real print is a refund risk and, if it misleads, a problem under the Competition Bureau's guidance on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations).

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.**

Suppose a shirt sells on Etsy to a Canadian buyer for $42 CAD with shipping included, which is an assumption. Suppose the printer's product and shipping price that day is $24, also an assumption until you read it. Etsy transaction fee: 0.065 × $42 = $2.73. Processing: 0.03 × $42 + $0.25 = $1.51. Regulatory fee: 0.005 × $42 = $0.21. Remainder: $42 − $24 − $2.73 − $1.51 − $0.21 = $13.55 before the listing fee, a tax set-aside, and your time. If the same shirt sold through a Shopify Basic store on the yearly rate, and it was the only order that month, the $37 subscription would turn the sketch negative. These totals are assumptions. Do not copy $42.

## Where can print-on-demand startup spending be lower in Canada?

Print-on-demand startup spending in Canada stays lower when you start with one design on one product, list it on a marketplace you already use, and order only the sample you need to photograph. Skip a store subscription until you have a way to send visitors. Skip paid mockup tools when a free tier makes an honest image.

## Where should print-on-demand spending not be cut in Canada?

Print-on-demand spending in Canada should not skip a sample of what you sell or a check that you own the artwork. A sample shows colour, fit, and print quality before a buyer sees it. A design that copies a brand or character can be removed, and the money you spent promoting it is gone.

## Which tax rules apply to a print-on-demand budget in Canada?

Print-on-demand income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep order exports, the printer's invoices, and platform statements. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Print-on-demand is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
