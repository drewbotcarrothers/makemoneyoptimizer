Teaching English online from Canada means running paid video lessons for learners who want to speak, write or test better in English: through a fixed-rate platform like Cambly, a marketplace like Preply or italki where you set your price, or with private students you find yourself. Fixed-rate platforms pay about $14.50 CAD an hour, marketplaces pay what you charge minus a 15% to 33% commission, and direct students keep the whole fee but take longer to find.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Fixed rate:** Cambly pays US$0.17 a minute (US$10.20 an hour) for adult lessons and US$0.20 a minute (US$12 an hour) on Cambly Kids, about $14.52 and $17.09 CAD at the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4240 on October 8, 2026. Cambly says it is limiting new tutor sign-ups.
- **Marketplaces:** Preply keeps 100% of each trial lesson with a new student, then 33% falling to 18% as your total hours grow; it says English tutors charge US$15 to US$25 an hour on average. italki charges 0% on trials, 21% on single lessons and 15% on 15- and 20-lesson packages.
- **Benchmark:** Job Bank lists language school instructors (NOC 41210) at a $45.00 median hourly wage in Canada for 2023–2024, an employed-instructor figure, not a platform rate.
- **Credentials:** TESL Canada certification covers adult ESL programs and doesn't replace provincial K–12 teaching certificates. Most adult conversation marketplaces don't require it, but schools and employers often do.
- **Tax:** income goes on Form T2125 with both CPP shares. GST/HST registration applies past $30,000; a CRA memo exempts English or French second-language programs only when a school or a business operated primarily to teach languages supplies them.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does teaching English online suit?

It suits fluent English speakers who like conversation, can explain a grammar point in one plain sentence, and can keep a schedule that matches learners in other time zones. Your best hours often land early morning or late evening in Canada, because many adult learners book before or after their own workday in Europe, Latin America or Asia.

Good fits:

- **Certified teachers and TESL graduates**, who can charge more and teach exam or workplace programs.
- **Professionals in a field** (nursing, IT, finance, law, trades) who can teach the vocabulary of that field to people preparing for jobs or interviews.
- **Bilingual Canadians**, who can explain English using the learner's first language, especially French, Spanish, Portuguese, Mandarin or Arabic.
- **Students and retirees** with flexible early-morning or evening hours.

It's a weaker fit if you need fixed income from week one, dislike repeating the same correction many times, or can't work when your learners are awake.

## How does teaching English online pay?

There are three models, and most teachers combine two of them.

1. **Fixed-rate platforms.** The company sets the rate and supplies the students. Cambly's tutor page, checked October 9, 2026, lists US$0.17 a minute (US$10.20 an hour) on Cambly and US$0.20 a minute (US$12 an hour) on Cambly Kids, paid weekly by direct deposit, with no minimum hours. The same page says Cambly is temporarily limiting new tutors to protect current tutors' schedules, so profile processing may be slow.
2. **Marketplaces.** You set your price and the platform takes a commission. Preply charges 100% of every trial lesson with a new student, then 33% for new tutors, dropping to 28% after 20 total hours, and down to 18% for its most experienced tutors; trial hours count toward those thresholds. italki charges 0% on trial lessons, 21% on single lessons, 19% on 5-lesson packages, 17% on 10-lesson packages, 15% on 15- and 20-lesson packages and 30% on group classes.
3. **Direct students.** You find adults through your network, community groups, workplaces, a simple website or a YouTube channel, and take payment yourself. No commission, but no stream of new students either.

italki pays in US dollars as italki Credits with a US$30 minimum withdrawal. italki doesn't charge to withdraw, but the payment provider does: its help page lists PayPal at about 2%, Hyperwallet at about 1.5% and Payoneer at US$3 per transfer, plus about 2% for currency conversion when Payoneer pays out in local currency. Preply's teach page lists PayPal, Payoneer, Skrill and Transferwise as payout methods.

## What does the math look like over a month?

**Worked example 1, a new Preply tutor (illustrative).** Assumptions: US$20 an hour, 8 trial lessons with new students and 24 regular lessons in the first month, all 60 minutes. Commission rates are Preply's published tiers.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| 8 trial lessons (Preply keeps 100%) | US$0.00 |
| 12 regular lessons in hours 9–20 at 33% (12 × US$13.40) | US$160.80 |
| 12 regular lessons in hours 21–32 at 28% (12 × US$14.40) | US$172.80 |
| Total before payout fees | US$333.60 |
| In CAD at 1.4240 | $475.05 |
| Per teaching hour (32 hours) | $14.85 |

The trial lessons are the main drag in month one. If half those trial students book a weekly lesson for the next three months, month two looks very different, which is why conversion from trial to regular is the number to watch.

**Worked example 2, an italki teacher selling packages (illustrative).** Assumptions: four regular students each buy a 10-lesson package at US$25 and use it within the month, and you withdraw twice through Payoneer to a Canadian bank.

- 40 lessons × US$25 × (1 − 17%) = US$830.00 in italki Credits
- Two Payoneer transfers at US$3 each = US$6.00, leaving US$824.00
- About 2% conversion = US$16.48, leaving about US$807.52
- At 1.4240 = about $1,149.91 CAD, or $28.75 per teaching hour

**Worked example 3, direct students (illustrative).** Assumptions: 8 adults each book one 60-minute lesson a week at $35 CAD for 46 weeks of the year.

- Month (4 weeks): 8 × 4 × $35 = $1,120
- Year: 8 × 46 × $35 = $12,880, well under the $30,000 GST/HST threshold
- Add 15 minutes of prep and notes per lesson and your effective rate is $35 ÷ 1.25 = $28 an hour

## Do you need TESL or TEFL certification?

For marketplaces and conversation platforms, usually not; for language schools, settlement programs and many employers, yes. TESL Canada's national standards are for instructors of English as a second or foreign language in adult programs, and its own page says a TESL Canada certificate doesn't replace provincial professional certificates where they exist. Teaching in a public K–12 school is regulated by each province (in Ontario, the [Ontario College of Teachers](https://www.oct.ca/)).

What to say on your profile:

- If you hold TESL Canada, CELTA or a provincial teaching licence, name it exactly and give the year.
- If you don't, describe what you do ("conversation practice for working adults") rather than implying a credential.
- If a learner is under 18, the [RCMP's vulnerable sector check page](https://rcmp.ca/en/criminal-records/criminal-record-checks/vulnerable-sector-checks) explains that checks are requested by an organization for a position of trust. Have a parent book and stay nearby, and start with adults until you know the rules that apply to you.

Read the [TESL Canada certification FAQ](https://tesl.ca/certification/tesl-canada-professional-certification/certification-faq) before you pay for any course, and check that the program is on TESL Canada's recognized list if you want that certificate.

## What should you teach? 18 niches that suit Canadians

Narrow offers book faster and justify a higher price than "English for everyone."

1. **Job interview practice** for professionals applying to North American companies.
2. **Workplace email and meeting English** for remote workers on English-speaking teams.
3. **Nursing and health care vocabulary**, if you've worked in the field.
4. **IT and software team English** (stand-ups, code reviews, tickets).
5. **Accent and pronunciation work** for intermediate speakers who are already fluent.
6. **Newcomer settlement English** (banking, rental, doctor's visits), taught with care for privacy.
7. **IELTS or CELPIP speaking practice**, using publicly released sample tasks; never promise a score.
8. **Small talk and Canadian culture** for people moving here for work or study.
9. **Business presentation rehearsal**, ideal for package sales.
10. **English for trades** (construction, electrical, automotive) for workers preparing for Canadian jobs.
11. **Teen conversation practice** with a parent present, once you've checked your obligations.
12. **French speakers learning English**, taught by bilingual Canadians who can explain in French.
13. **Spanish and Portuguese speakers**, a large learner market on most platforms.
14. **Reading clubs** built around one short article or story a week.
15. **Writing feedback** on cover letters and LinkedIn profiles, delivered live on screen.
16. **Hospitality and tourism English** for hotel, restaurant and airline staff.
17. **Academic English** for students preparing for Canadian college or university.
18. **Group conversation classes** of 3 to 5 adults, once you have regular students (italki charges 30% on group classes; a direct group needs a meeting tool without a 40-minute cap).

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

### Week 1: Choose a niche and set up

- Pick one learner type from the list above and write a one-sentence offer.
- Read the TESL Canada FAQ and decide what you can honestly put on a profile.
- Test your audio and camera on a call with a friend; buy a headset only if they struggle to hear you.
- Apply to Cambly (it may waitlist you) and create a Preply or italki profile with a 1- to 2-minute intro video.

### Week 2: Build three lesson templates

- Write a 30-minute trial lesson, a 60-minute conversation lesson and a 60-minute skills lesson (see the templates below).
- Set your marketplace price. A common approach is to start near the low end of Preply's US$15 to US$25 average so you collect first reviews, then raise it.
- Open early-morning and evening slots that match your learners' time zones.

### Week 3: Teach your first paid lessons

- Teach every trial as if it were the sale: find the learner's goal in the first five minutes and show progress by the end.
- Offer a package or a weekly slot at the end of each trial.
- Message five people in your own network about direct lessons for adults they know.

### Week 4: Review and adjust

- Count trials, conversions to regular students, teaching hours and take-home in CAD.
- If fewer than one in three trials convert, rewrite your trial lesson before you change your price.
- Move to 10-lesson packages on italki or weekly bookings on Preply for students who stay.

### Days 31–90: Raise the rate and add direct students

- After 10 to 15 reviews, raise your marketplace rate by US$2 to US$3 for new students.
- Build a simple booking page for direct students and a written cancellation policy.
- Track which niche books fastest and drop the ones that don't.

## Templates you can copy

### Profile intro (marketplace)

```
Hi, I'm [first name] from [city], Canada. I help [learner type] speak
confidently in [situation: job interviews / team meetings / daily life].
In our lessons you'll practise real conversations, get clear corrections
on the 2–3 errors that matter most, and leave with phrases you can use
the same day. [Credential, if you hold one: e.g. "TESL Canada certified, 2024."]
Book a trial and tell me your goal.
```

### Message to a potential direct student

```
Hi [name], I'm offering 60-minute English conversation lessons online
for working adults who want to sound more natural in meetings and interviews.
Lessons are $[price] CAD, booked weekly, by video call. If you or someone
you know would like a first lesson, reply and I'll send a booking link.
```

### 60-minute lesson plan

```
0–5 min    Warm-up: what happened this week (learner talks)
5–15 min   Goal task: role-play the real situation (interview, meeting, call)
15–25 min  Feedback: 2–3 corrections, written in the shared doc
25–45 min  Repeat the task using the corrections
45–55 min  New phrases for next time; learner uses each in a sentence
55–60 min  Recap, homework (one short task), next booking
```

### After-lesson recap

```
Today: [task]. Great: [one specific strength].
Fix next time: [error 1] → [correct form]; [error 2] → [correct form].
New phrases: [3 phrases]. Homework: [one task]. Next lesson: [date/time].
```

### Direct-student agreement checklist

- Lesson length, price in CAD and how payment is made (before the lesson).
- Cancellation window (for example, 24 hours) and what happens with no-shows.
- Recording only with written consent.
- What you won't promise: test scores, visas, jobs.
- How you store notes and contact details; collect only what you need under [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/).

## Real online English teachers on the record

These are public examples. They show how teaching went for these people, not what you'll earn.

**Bob the Canadian, Canada.** The "Learn English with Bob the Canadian" YouTube channel had 2.6 million subscribers, 1,664 videos and 122.4 million total views as of October 7, 2026, according to Inside Créateurs' channel statistics. Its first video was published January 3, 2017. Inside Créateurs estimates the channel's ad revenue at $5,086 to $18,619 a month; that's a third-party estimate, not income the creator reported. The channel shows how free lessons can become a large audience over years of regular publishing.

**Anastasiia Stoianova, Toronto, Ontario.** In episode 12 of her English Canada podcast (February 2, 2026), Stoianova, a self-employed ESL instructor with more than a decade of teaching experience, describes the costs of working for herself in Ontario: tax of around 30% on her income, no paid vacation or dental coverage, and competition from lower-priced tutors in other regions. She stresses written contracts, clear scheduling boundaries and planning for low-income months, and says her pricing reflects her qualifications and the cost of living in Toronto.

**Jessica Webster, British Columbia.** Writing for CBC in 2017, Webster described teaching English by webcam for a program that provides one-on-one language and settlement training to newcomers across Canada who can't attend in-person classes. She spent at least half an hour a week with each student, and students often stayed with the same teacher for years. She had worked in language teaching for more than 14 years.

**Dave Trotter (not Canadian; based in the US when he started).** In a Medium post dated May 20, 2020, Trotter, who earned his CELTA in Montreal, reported italki earnings of $87.55 for 6.5 hours in his first month, $182.75 for 11.25 hours in his second and $351.90 for 20 hours in his third. In October 2019 he taught 83 hours for $1,421.73 and later held an average near $19 an hour. His figures appear to be US dollars, and he added a note that italki changes since then make the post partly outdated.

What they share: steady income came from regular students and repeat formats, not from one-off trials.

## What mistakes do new online English teachers make?

- **Underpricing forever.** A low launch price helps collect reviews; keeping it after 20 reviews leaves money behind.
- **Teaching the trial like a regular lesson.** The trial's job is to diagnose a goal and show quick progress, then offer a plan.
- **Talking too much.** Learners pay to speak. Aim for them to talk most of the lesson.
- **Correcting everything.** Pick two or three patterns per lesson and write them down.
- **Claiming credentials you don't hold.** Describe your experience instead.
- **Ignoring time zones.** Open slots when learners are awake, not when it suits your evening.
- **Forgetting payout fees.** Count PayPal, Payoneer and conversion costs in your real hourly rate.

## Which tax rules apply to teaching English online in Canada?

Tutoring income is self-employment income, including platform payouts in US dollars converted to CAD. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with your T1, and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Deductible costs include platform commissions, payout fees, a headset or webcam bought for the work (larger items through capital cost allowance), software, and a reasonable share of internet. Keep platform statements and convert each payout at the rate on the day you received it or a consistent average.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count other side-hustle revenue too. Teaching isn't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply.

Don't assume English lessons are tax-exempt. [GST/HST Memorandum 20-7](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-7/second-language-instruction.html) (December 2019) exempts instruction in English or French as a second language only when the courses form part of a program of second-language instruction and are supplied by a school authority, vocational school, public college, university or a business established and operated primarily (more than 50% of annual revenue) to provide language instruction. Conversation practice, interview coaching and test prep may fall outside that exemption, and the CRA says each case is a question of fact. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered. Teaching in Ontario public schools requires Ontario College of Teachers certification.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered; check Revenu Québec. Bilingual teachers here can offer English to French speakers and the reverse.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** provincial sales tax rules are separate from GST; check whether your service is taxable provincially.
- **Alberta and the territories:** 5% GST only once registered.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?
