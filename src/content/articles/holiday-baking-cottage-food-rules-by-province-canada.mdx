You can sell holiday baking made in a home kitchen in much of Canada, but the rules are provincial and they differ a lot. Alberta allows low-risk home-prepared foods with no permit or routine inspection if you label them correctly. Saskatchewan allows low-risk home-processed foods with a specific label and a required food safety course. B.C. allows lower-risk home-prepared foods at temporary food markets such as farmers' and craft markets, but not sales from home or in stores. Ontario requires you to contact your local public health unit and treats you as a food premises subject to inspection, though low-risk home businesses are exempt from some requirements. Quebec requires a MAPAQ permit, an inspection, mandatory hygiene training, and caps home-kitchen production at 100 kg a month. Cookies, most breads, and cakes with icing that doesn't need refrigeration are the typical low-risk items; anything with cream, custard, or meat usually is not.

## What are the home baking rules in each province?

Each province sets its own home-based food rules, and five are summarized below from their official pages as fetched in October 2026. For any province or territory not listed, contact your provincial health authority or local public health unit before selling.

| Province | Permit or approval? | Where you can sell | Key conditions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alberta | No permit, commercial kitchen, or routine inspection for low-risk foods | From home, online or mail order, special events, farmers' markets | Specific label; household members prepare; no meat, poultry, seafood, raw milk |
| Saskatchewan | No routine inspection; approved food safety course required | Direct to consumers, retail or wholesale outlets that do not process food, restaurants | Low-risk foods only; specific 12-point label |
| British Columbia | No application for lower-risk prepackaged foods at temporary food markets | Temporary food markets only, not from home or retail stores | Lower-risk foods only; higher-risk foods need Health Authority approval |
| Ontario | Contact your local public health unit; subject to inspection | As approved locally; check zoning | Low-risk foods have some exemptions |
| Quebec | MAPAQ permit, inspection, and mandatory hygiene training | Retail only from a home kitchen; no wholesale | 100 kg a month cap; kitchen not used for personal purposes at the same time |

## What are Alberta's rules for selling home baking?

Alberta allows low-risk home-prepared foods to be sold without a permit, a commercial kitchen, or routine inspection, a change in place since June 1, 2020, per the province's [low-risk home-prepared foods page](https://www.alberta.ca/low-risk-home-prepared-foods). According to Alberta's [fact sheet](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/7981d37f-185e-496f-81ae-a935831635ca/resource/c462be1f-77ab-4409-bced-b29f3e5a37b7/download/health-low-risk-home-prepared-food-fact-sheet.pdf):

- You can sell from home, online or by mail order, at special events, and at farmers' markets.
- Meat, poultry, seafood, raw milk, and home-bottled water are not allowed.
- Labels must state that the food was "prepared in a home kitchen not subject to inspection" and is "not for resale," with your name and contact information, the name of the food, and the date it was made.
- Only household members prepare the food, and at special events it must be packaged at home.
- Food safety training is encouraged.

## What are Saskatchewan's rules for selling home baking?

Saskatchewan's [Home Food Processing fact sheet](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/91663/formats/108565/download), updated September 2026, allows low-risk foods (water activity of 0.85 or less, or pH of 4.6 or less) to be made at home. You may sell directly to consumers, to retail or wholesale establishments that do not process food, and to restaurants, but not to food processing facilities or facilities serving vulnerable people. Labels must read "Made in a Home Kitchen That is Not Inspected by a Government Agency" in at least 12-point font, with your name, address, phone number, and the date. An approved food safety course is required, and there are no routine inspections.

## What are B.C.'s rules for selling home baking?

In B.C., the BC Centre for Disease Control's [temporary food market guideline](https://www.bccdc.ca/resource-gallery/Documents/Guidelines%20and%20Forms/Guidelines%20and%20Manuals/EH/FPS/Food/Guidelines%20-%20Sale%20of%20Foods%20at%20Temporary%20Food%20Markets_current.pdf) (revised August 2020) says home-prepared, prepackaged lower-risk foods, those with a water activity of 0.85 or less or a pH of 4.6 or less, can be sold at temporary food markets without an application. The guideline does not allow selling home-prepared food from your home or in retail stores, and higher-risk foods need Health Authority approval. MarketSafe or FOODSAFE training is recommended. Check with your regional health authority and the market organizer.

## What are Ontario's rules for selling home baking?

Ontario's [Guide to Starting a Home-Based Food Business](https://www.ontario.ca/files/2024-03/moh-guide-to-starting-home-based-food-business-en-2021-11-01.pdf) says you must contact your local public health unit, and that a home-based food business is subject to the Health Protection and Promotion Act and the Food Premises Regulation (O. Reg. 493/17), including periodic inspection. The guide lists low-risk foods such as most breads, baked goods without custard, cakes with icing that does not need refrigeration, chocolate, hard candy, fudge, toffee, and granola. Low-risk home businesses are exempt from requirements such as a dedicated handwashing station, commercial dishwashing, and food handler certification. Check your municipality's zoning rules for home businesses too.

## What are Quebec's rules for selling home baking?

Quebec's government page on [selling home-cooked food](https://www.quebec.ca/agriculture-environnement-et-ressources-naturelles/agriculture/agriculture-urbaine/reglementation/don-vente-aliments-frais-ou-cuisines-a-la-maison) (in French, updated May 11, 2026) says that selling prepared food requires:

- A permit from the ministère de l'Agriculture, des Pêcheries et de l'Alimentation (MAPAQ), with an inspector visit to check your facilities.
- Mandatory food hygiene and sanitation training.
- A kitchen that is not used for personal purposes at the same time as food is prepared for sale.
- In a home kitchen, no wholesale or sales to an intermediary for resale, and no more than 100 kg of food a month.
- Labelling that meets the food labelling rules.

Giving food away for free is not regulated, according to the same page.

## What mistakes do home bakers make selling in Canada?

The common mistakes are assuming one province's rules apply everywhere, selling higher-risk foods, and skipping labels.

- **Following another province's rules.** Alberta's no-permit model does not apply in Quebec or Ontario.
- **Selling cream-filled, custard, or cheesecake items** as if they were low-risk.
- **Missing the required label** wording in Alberta or Saskatchewan.
- **Selling from home in B.C.** when the guideline covers temporary food markets.
- **Ignoring allergens.** List ingredients and keep nut-containing batches separate.
- **No zoning check** in Ontario or other municipalities that regulate home businesses.
