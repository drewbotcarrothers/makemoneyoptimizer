Calendly, Square Appointments and Acuity Scheduling are the three booking tools Canadian tutors, music teachers, coaches and personal trainers compare most often. All three give clients a link to pick a time. They differ on whether you can take payment at booking, how many session types you get for free, what happens on a no-show, and whether you pay in Canadian or US dollars.

Every price below comes from the vendor's pricing page on October 14, 2026. US-dollar prices are converted at the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4222 CAD per USD published for October 13, 2026. Your card issuer will add its own foreign-exchange margin on USD subscriptions, so the real charge is usually a little higher.

## What do payments cost on each tool?

The subscription is only half the bill once clients pay at booking. Each tool routes payments differently:

| Payment type | Square Appointments (Canada) | Calendly Standard or Acuity via Stripe (Canada) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Online prepayment at booking | 2.8% + 30¢ | 2.9% + C$0.30 |
| No-show or late-cancel fee charged to card | 2.8% + 30¢ (Plus and Premium) | Depends on the tool's settings and Stripe |
| In-person credit (tap, dip, swipe) | 2.5% | Not a Calendly or Acuity feature |
| In-person debit (chip and PIN or tap) | 0.75% + 7¢ | Not a Calendly or Acuity feature |
| Manually keyed or card on file | 3.3% + 15¢ | Stripe's rates apply |

Square rates are from [Square Appointments' Canadian pricing page](https://squareup.com/ca/en/appointments/pricing) and are the same on all three plans; Stripe's standard Canadian card rate is from [Stripe's Canadian pricing page](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing), which also lists extra charges for international cards. Calendly Standard connects Stripe or PayPal; Acuity lists Stripe, Square, PayPal and Venmo, so an Acuity user could also route through Square.

On a C$50 session paid online, Square takes $50 × 2.8% + $0.30 = **$1.70** and Stripe takes $50 × 2.9% + $0.30 = **$1.75**.

### Worked example: a coach picking a tool

**Illustrative example.** Assumed: a career coach sells single 60-minute calls at C$120 and a three-call bundle, and about a quarter of new clients book from the United States.

1. **Packages rule out Calendly and Square Free.** Acuity Standard (≈ C$38.40 a month billed annually) is the first plan in this comparison that sells packages.
2. **Time zones favour Acuity or Calendly.** Both convert times automatically for clients.
3. **Fees.** One C$120 call through Stripe costs $120 × 2.9% + $0.30 = $3.78; Stripe's page lists added charges for international cards, so US clients cost more to process.
4. **Result.** Acuity Standard fits, at about $38.40 a month plus processing. A coach who drops the bundle could use Calendly Standard at about $14.22.

## Common mistakes with booking tools

- **No cancellation policy on the booking page.** Put your 24-hour rule where clients agree to it before they book.
- **Taking card payments without pricing in fees.** Build roughly 3% into the rate or offer e-Transfer.
- **Too many session types.** Start with one or two so clients do not stall.
- **No buffers.** Add travel or reset time between sessions.
- **Sharing a personal calendar.** Connect a separate calendar for tutoring or coaching.
- **Paying monthly when you'll stay a year.** Both USD tools discount yearly billing.

## The caveat: USD prices and plan changes

Calendly and Acuity bill in US dollars, so the Canadian cost moves with the exchange rate and your card's foreign-transaction fee, typically a few percent on top of the Bank of Canada conversion used here. All three vendors also change plan names, limits and feature placement often. The tables above reflect the pricing pages on October 14, 2026; recheck the live page before you commit, particularly for features (such as text reminders) that depend on your country.

## How are booking tools and tutoring income taxed?

Tutoring and coaching income is generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Subscriptions to booking software and card-processing fees used for the business are generally deductible expenses, and a USD subscription is recorded in Canadian dollars at the rate on the day you paid. Once your taxable revenue passes $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters you generally need to register for GST/HST, although some tutoring and instruction is exempt; see [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/). Foreign vendors like Calendly and Acuity may also charge you GST/HST on the subscription. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
