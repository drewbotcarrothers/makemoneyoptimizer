## How do rates work on odd-job apps in Canada?

On Taskrabbit in Canada, Taskers choose their categories and, in most categories, set their own hourly rates, according to Taskrabbit's [Tasker Rates & Minimum Hours Policy](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260502667035-Tasker-Rates-Minimum-Hours-Policy). The same policy says hourly tasks have a one-hour minimum charge, time beyond the first hour can be invoiced in 15-minute increments, and Taskers may opt into a two-hour minimum if they disclose it.

| Platform rule (Taskrabbit, Canada help centre) | What it means for your price |
| --- | --- |
| Taskers set their own hourly rate in most categories | You can price assembly, mounting, and moving differently |
| One-hour minimum on hourly tasks | A 20-minute job still bills an hour |
| Optional two-hour minimum, disclosed to clients | Useful for tasks with long travel or setup |
| Some categories are fixed-price, paid on completion | Your hourly rate does not apply there |
| Client price includes the Tasker rate plus a percentage service fee | Clients see a higher number than your rate |
| Taskers receive 100% of reimbursements | Parts you buy for the client are reimbursed separately |

The client-side [service fee page](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260411872155-What-s-the-Taskrabbit-Service-Fee) explains that the rate clients see includes both your rate and the service fee. Other apps use different rules; read the current help centre for the app you use.

## Which pricing models work on odd-job apps in Canada?

On odd-job apps in Canada, the main lever is a different hourly rate for each category, plus the choice of a one- or two-hour minimum and which fixed-price tasks you accept.

| Choice | When it helps | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category-specific rates | Skilled tasks (mounting) pay more than simple ones (waiting in line) | Underpricing your best skill |
| Two-hour minimum | Tasks with long travel or tool setup | Fewer bookings on small jobs |
| Fixed-price categories | Quick, repeatable tasks you have timed | A slow job pays the same as a fast one |
| Off-app direct clients | Repeat clients you met on your own | Conflicting with the app's terms for clients you met through it |

Read the app's terms on off-platform work before taking a client you met there direct.

## How do you calculate an odd-job app rate in Canada?

Calculate an odd-job app rate in Canada from a target hourly pay, the unpaid travel between tasks, tool wear for that category, and the minimum the app lets you set.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay per category

Use the Job Bank median for the matching job as a floor.

### Step 2: Count travel between tasks

Travel time to a task is usually not billed. If you spend 25 minutes driving to a one-hour task, about 30% of that block is unpaid.

### Step 3: Add tool and vehicle costs

Drill bits, anchors, fuel, and parking. Parts for the client are reimbursed separately.

### Step 4: Use the minimum

A one-hour minimum protects short tasks. A two-hour minimum suits categories with long setup.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target for furniture assembly is $26 an hour, a little above Job Bank's $25.00 handyman median. Suppose 70% of your app hours are billable. The billable rate is $26 ÷ 0.70 = $37.14, which you might set as $38 an hour.

A bookshelf and dresser assembly takes 1 hour 15 minutes. With 15-minute increments after the first hour, the task bills 1.25 × $38 = $47.50. Suppose you drove 20 minutes each way and spent $4 on fuel. Your time was 1.25 + 0.67 = 1.92 hours, so ($47.50 − $4) ÷ 1.92 = about $22.66 per working hour before tax. That is below the $26 target because this task had long travel, which is when a two-hour minimum or a higher rate earns its place.

## When should you raise your odd-job app rates in Canada?

Raise odd-job app rates in Canada when your calendar books out several days ahead, when your reviews are strong, or when a category keeps running longer than priced. Raise one category at a time and watch bookings for two weeks.

Strong reviews in one category support a higher rate there. Leave simple categories lower if they fill gaps between better-paid tasks.

## What odd-job app pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common odd-job app pricing mistakes in Canada are one rate for every category, ignoring travel, and racing the lowest Tasker in your city. Each one fills the calendar while lowering pay per hour.

Do not accept tasks across the city at your cheapest rate. Do not forget that clients see your rate plus the service fee. Do not accept work that needs a licensed trade because the app lists it.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect odd-job app rates in Canada?

Odd-job app income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, counting all your side hustles.

When registered, the rate depends on the province where the service is supplied, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html); check how your app handles tax on invoices.
