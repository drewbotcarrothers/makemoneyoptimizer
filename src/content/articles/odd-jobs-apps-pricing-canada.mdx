On Taskrabbit-style odd-job apps in Canada, you set your own hourly rate for each task category. The app adds its fees to the client's bill, so clients see a higher number than you set. This guide covers what Taskrabbit's own cost guides say clients pay, Job Bank wage benchmarks, the platform rules that shape your price, a rate formula, four worked examples and templates.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Market rates:** Taskrabbit's cost guides (updated October 1, 2026) list client-facing furniture assembly averages of $24 to $31 an hour and moving help averages of $24 to $35 an hour across nine Canadian cities.
- **What you keep:** 100% of your self-set hourly rate and tips. Taskrabbit's service fee and Trust & Support fee are added to the client's bill. Joining costs a one-time $25 registration fee.
- **Platform rules:** hourly tasks have a one-hour minimum and bill in 15-minute increments after the first hour. You can opt into a 2-hour minimum if you disclose it. Since February 2026, gas, parking and stairs must be built into your rate, not added on.
- **Formula:** rate per category = (target hourly pay + tools and travel per billable hour) ÷ billable share.
- **Wage benchmark:** Job Bank's national median for handypersons is $25.00 an hour.

</QuickAnswer>

## How does Taskrabbit pricing work for Taskers in Canada?

Taskrabbit uses three earning structures, according to its [September 15, 2025 Tasker blog post](https://www.taskrabbit.ca/blog/how-pricing-works-on-taskrabbit-and-what-it-means-for-your-earnings/):

| Structure | How it pays | Where you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-Set Hourly | You set an hourly rate per category and keep 100% of it plus 100% of tips | Most categories, including cleaning, moving and furniture assembly |
| Task-Based | A fixed payment per task, shown up front | IKEA assembly and partner tasks |
| Pre-Set Hourly | A pre-set hourly rate | Direct-hire jobs where a client picks you |

The platform rules that affect your price:

| Rule (source) | What it means for your price |
| --- | --- |
| One-hour minimum on hourly tasks ([Tasker Rates & Minimum Hours Policy](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260502667035-Tasker-Rates-Minimum-Hours-Policy), effective June 23, 2025) | A 20-minute job still bills an hour |
| Time beyond the first hour is invoiced in 15-minute increments (same policy) | Long jobs bill closer to actual time |
| Optional 2-hour minimum, which must be in your category description and chats (same policy) | Useful where travel or setup is long |
| Fixed-price tasks pay on completion, not hours (same policy) | A slow job pays the same as a fast one |
| Gas, tolls, parking, stairs and vehicle costs must be built into your rate ([2026 Tasker update](https://www.taskrabbit.ca/blog/taskrabbit-2026-tasker-update-built-from-your-feedback/), February 11, 2026) | No travel or stairs add-ons on the app |
| Service fee and Trust & Support fee are added to the client's bill ([service fee page](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260411872155-What-s-the-Taskrabbit-Service-Fee)) | Clients see more than your rate |
| One-time $25 registration fee ([Taskrabbit Support](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260480151579-What-s-the-Registration-Fee)) | A start-up cost, not a per-task cut |

Hardware or parts you buy at the client's request are reimbursed 100% to you, separately from your rate. Other apps work differently. Jiffy, for example, [asks its Pros](https://intercom.help/jiffy/en/articles/2176611-become-a-jiffy-pro) for a tax ID, licences in their field and at least $2 million in liability insurance, so read the current help centre for each app you use.

## How do you calculate an odd-job app rate in Canada?

On Taskrabbit, travel and parking can't be charged separately, so they have to go into your hourly rate. Work out a rate for each category with this formula:

**Rate per category = (target hourly pay + tools and travel per billable hour) ÷ billable share**

1. **Target hourly pay.** Use the matching Job Bank median as a floor and your city's Taskrabbit average as a market check.
2. **Tools and travel.** Add up drill bits, anchors, shoe covers, fuel, transit and parking over a typical week, then divide by your billable hours. The [odd-job apps guide](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-side-hustle-canada/) priced a basic assembly kit at $94.55 on Amazon.ca on October 9, 2026.
3. **Billable share.** Travel between tasks, messages and quotes are unpaid. If you're paid for 7 of every 10 hours you work, your billable share is 0.70.
4. **Minimum.** Keep the one-hour minimum for quick, nearby tasks. Opt into a 2-hour minimum for categories with long setup or travel, and disclose it.
5. **Tax and CPP.** Set aside money for both CPP shares and income tax. See [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

**Illustrative rate card.** Assume $1.50 of tools and travel per billable hour and a 0.70 billable share. These are teaching numbers, not market averages.

| Category (illustrative) | Target pay | (Target + $1.50) ÷ 0.70 | Posted rate | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TV and shelf mounting | $30 | $45.00 | $45 | 1 h |
| Furniture assembly | $26 | $39.29 | $39 | 1 h |
| Moving help (labour only) | $23 | $35.00 | $35 | 2 h |
| Cleaning | $22 | $33.57 | $34 | 2 h |
| Yard work | $22 | $33.57 | $34 | 1 h |

These are the rates you set. Clients see more, because Taskrabbit's fees are added on top. A $39 assembly rate sits above Toronto's $31 client-facing average, so a new Tasker with no reviews may start lower and raise the rate as reviews come in.

## What do worked odd-job app examples look like?

These examples are **Illustrative**. Rates, job lengths, tips and travel are assumptions. Fuel uses an assumed $1.70 a litre and 9 L/100 km. Taskrabbit's fees are paid by the client, so they don't reduce these numbers.

**Example 1: a Saturday dresser and bed frame assembly in Toronto, by transit.**

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Self-set rate | $40.00/hour |
| Time on task | 2.5 hours |
| Task pay (2.5 × $40) | $100.00 |
| Tip | $10.00 |
| Transit, round trip | −$7.00 |
| **Profit before tax** | **$103.00** |
| Total time including 1 hour of travel | 3.5 hours |
| **Profit per hour** | **$29.43** |

Taskrabbit's item estimates put a dresser at $90 to $220 and a bed frame at $80 to $150 for the client, so $100 of labour for both is at the low end of what the client pays.

**Example 2: a 20-minute shelf mount with the one-hour minimum.** At $45 an hour, the minimum bills $45. Driving 20 km round trip uses 20 × 0.09 L × $1.70 = $3.06 of fuel. Twenty minutes on site plus 40 minutes of driving is one hour, so profit is $41.94 for that hour. Without a minimum, short tasks like this would barely cover the drive.

**Example 3: moving help with a 2-hour minimum.** At $35 an hour, a 1.25-hour couch carry bills $70 under a disclosed 2-hour minimum, or $43.75 under the standard one-hour minimum and 15-minute increments. With an hour of travel, that's 2.25 hours of your time: $31.11 an hour with the 2-hour minimum, $19.44 without. The 2-hour minimum may cost you some bookings, but it keeps short, far-away jobs worth taking.

**Example 4: a fixed-price assembly that runs long.** A task-based job pays a set amount however long it takes. If it pays $60 and you finish in 1.5 hours, you earn $40 an hour. If a missing part stretches it to 3 hours, you earn $20 an hour. Track your times on fixed-price items so you know which ones to accept.

## How do you raise odd-job app rates in Canada?

Raise your rates when your calendar books out several days ahead, when reviews in a category are strong, or when a category keeps taking longer than your rate allows.

- **Change one category at a time,** then watch bookings for two weeks before changing another.
- **Start with your best-reviewed skill.** The $56.50 and $96.05 Toronto profiles show that clients pay well above the average for proven Taskers.
- **Keep a cheaper gap-filler.** A simple category at a lower rate can fill gaps between better-paid tasks.
- **Price in the 2026 rule.** If stairs, parking or long drives keep costing you, raise the rate for that category, because you can't add them as separate charges.

## What templates help with odd-job app pricing?

**Category description with a 2-hour minimum** (Taskrabbit's policy requires the disclosure):

> Labour-only moving help: loading, unloading and carrying furniture. I have a dolly, straps and moving blankets. Please note: I have a 2-hour minimum for this category, which covers travel and setup. Tell me the floor, elevator access and the large items when you book.

**Chat message confirming the minimum:**

> Thanks for booking. Just confirming that this category has a 2-hour minimum, so a shorter job is billed as 2 hours. If you'd like, list any other small tasks and I can do them in the same visit.

**Rate change note to a repeat client** (for direct work you set up yourself, within the app's terms):

> Hi [name], from [date] my rate for [category] goes to $[42] an hour. Anything you book before then stays at the current rate. Thanks for the repeat work.

## What odd-job app pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common odd-job app pricing mistakes in Canada are using one rate for every category, forgetting travel, and undercutting the lowest Tasker in your city. Each one keeps you busy while lowering your pay per hour.

Don't accept tasks across the city at your cheapest rate. Remember that clients see your rate plus fees. Don't try to add parking, gas or stairs as separate charges on Taskrabbit, which the February 2026 policy doesn't allow. Don't accept work that needs a licensed trade just because the app lists it.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect odd-job app rates in Canada?

Odd-job app income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). You must register for GST/HST once your taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), either in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. That total counts all your side hustles together. Odd jobs aren't ride-sharing, so the CRA's first-dollar rule for [taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) doesn't apply.

Once you're registered, charge the rate for the province where the service is supplied, from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html), and check how your app handles tax on invoices. For example, a registered Ontario Tasker's $40 hour carries $5.20 of HST at 13%. The [platform income tax guide](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/) and [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) cover the rest.

App payouts usually arrive in 1 to 3 business days, and tax set-asides are easier from a separate account. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account option for keeping app income apart from household money.
