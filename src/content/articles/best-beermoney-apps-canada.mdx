## What counts as a beermoney app in Canada?

A beermoney app pays a small reward for a short task or for shopping you were going to do anyway. It is not a shift, a client, or a product you have to stock. The useful split in Canada is four types: academic and market-research studies, survey panels, moderated or recorded user tests, and cash-back or receipt apps.

Studies and user tests pay more per sitting than a five-minute survey, and they are also easier to get screened out of. Cash-back does not create new income so much as it returns a slice of money you already spent. Treat those as different jobs, even when they live in the same app.

## Which popular apps should Canadians skip?

Skip anything that asks you to pay to unlock earnings. That pattern is a task scam, not a slow payout. The [side hustle scams guide](/side-hustles/side-hustle-scams-canada/) collects the red flags, and [mystery shopping](/side-hustles/mystery-shopping-side-hustle-canada/) already covers fake-cheque shopper offers.

Three closures and country locks are documented on the companies' own pages:

- **InboxDollars** says it welcomes individuals living in the 50 US states, age 18 or older, and that the country on the profile must be the country you live in and log in from ([Who can join](https://help.inboxdollars.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044247092-Who-Can-Join-InboxDollars)).
- **Checkout 51's** public homepage says the offer is available to United States members only. The same page includes a Français (CA) language toggle, so if you already have an older Canadian account, check that account rather than the US homepage.
- **Amazon Mechanical Turk** says it is permanently closed as of September 30, 2026 ([MTurk help](https://www.mturk.com/help)). It is not a current side hustle in any country.

Ibotta's help centre says additional functions of the app do not work outside the United States, and that a phone number based outside the US cannot be verified for a withdrawal. Fetch Rewards did not publish a country list on a page this guide could confirm, so check the store listing for your region before you install it. ## How much do beermoney apps pay in Canada?

Most of them do not publish a Canadian average, and this guide will not invent one. What they do publish is a floor or a per-task amount:

- Prolific tells researchers the minimum reward is £6 or US$8 per hour, and it recommends at least £9 or US$12. The participant is paid the fixed reward for that study, not an hourly wage that changes with how fast they finish ([payment model](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445230-prolific-s-payment-model)).
- User tests and Respondent studies show the incentive before you apply. Respondent lists incentives in US dollars.
- Survey panels invite people whose profile matches the study. Leger says you may see surveys weekly, monthly, or quarterly depending on that match. A quiet week is normal.
- Cash-back is a percentage or a fixed rebate on a purchase, paid only after the merchant confirms it. Rakuten.ca holds payment until confirmed cash back reaches $5.01.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed time, not a forecast or an average. Suppose one Prolific study pays the published US-dollar floor for a task the researcher estimated at 20 minutes: US$8 × 20/60 = US$2.67. At the [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) daily average of 1.4254 CAD per US dollar on October 5, 2026, that is about $3.81 CAD before any PayPal conversion. Four studies at that floor in a week are about $15.22 CAD. A study can pay more than the floor. Screen-outs, gaps between studies, and PayPal's own conversion can make the week smaller. The arithmetic uses Prolific's published minimum and an assumed number of studies.

## How do you start without wasting the first week?

### Step 1: Open two study accounts and one cash-back habit

Create a Prolific account if you live in Canada and can verify your identity, and create a User Interviews profile. Add Rakuten.ca or Caddle only for shopping you already do. Three logins are enough. Ten half-finished signups are not.

### Step 2: Use your real profile

Panels pay for a specific person: age, city, job, devices. Inventing answers to qualify for more surveys gets accounts closed and makes the data useless. Prolific, Leger, and the user-testing platforms all tie the account to identity or to a consistent profile.

### Step 3: Turn on PayPal for GBP and USD

Prolific pays through PayPal in the study's currency and tells participants to enable both GBP and USD. UserTesting also pays PayPal. A Canadian PayPal account can hold those currencies or convert them. The rate in the PayPal receipt will not match the Bank of Canada rate exactly. Keep the receipt either way. Reporting those dollars is covered in [earning in USD from US platforms](/side-hustles/usd-platform-income-tax-canada/).

### Step 4: Set a stop rule

If a week of surveys pays less than you want after screen-outs, stop adding panels and keep the one or two that actually send work. Cash-back stays, because it rides on spending you were doing anyway. AI-training projects, if you qualify, are the step up from this tier.

## What mistakes do Canadians make with beermoney apps?

- **Installing the US app.** Rakuten.com and Rakuten.ca are separate accounts. Rakuten.ca pays CAD. The .com account pays US dollars. InboxDollars is not the Canadian version of Swagbucks.
- **Paying to join or to withdraw.** Legitimate panels in this guide do not charge a membership fee. A fee to "unlock" a balance is a scam.
- **Counting screen-outs as income.** A disqualification is unpaid time. Track approved rewards only.
- **Using a VPN.** InboxDollars forbids tools that hide your country. Other platforms reject mismatched locations during ID checks. Use the connection where you actually live.
- **Treating marketing "up to" rates as a wage.** A role page that says "up to" a rate is a ceiling the company chose to advertise. It is not what every task pays.

## Which tax rules apply to beermoney apps in Canada?

Study pay, user-test pay, and AI-training pay are generally self-employment or other income you report in Canadian dollars. The form for business and professional income is [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). The GST/HST small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000, not a tax-free income allowance ([when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). Whether survey points, grocery rebates, and bank bonuses are taxable is a separate question with less room for a flat yes or no. That discussion is in [are rewards and bonuses taxable](/side-hustles/are-rewards-and-bonuses-taxable-canada/).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
