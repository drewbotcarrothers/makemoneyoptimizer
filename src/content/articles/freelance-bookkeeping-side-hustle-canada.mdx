Freelance bookkeeping in Canada means keeping small businesses' books for a monthly fee: categorizing transactions, reconciling bank and credit card accounts, tracking GST/HST and sending a monthly report. Canadian firms publish monthly packages from $300 to $1,500, and Job Bank's median wage for employed bookkeepers is $28.02 an hour. You don't need a licence to keep books, but you can't call yourself a CPA unless you are one.

## Who does freelance bookkeeping suit?

Bookkeeping suits detail-oriented people who like a reconciliation that ties to the cent, can explain numbers in plain English, and are trusted with private financial data. It's steady, remote-friendly work that can be done in evenings once systems are set.

It suits:

- **People with accounts payable, payroll or office admin experience**; see [side hustles for parents with admin experience](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-parents-with-admin-experience-canada/).
- **Accounting students and accountants** who want small clients on the side; see [side hustles for accountants](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-accountants-canada/).
- **Retirees** from finance or office roles; see [side hustles for retirees](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-retirees-canada/).
- **Virtual assistants** who want a higher-value specialty; see [virtual assistant work](/side-hustles/virtual-assistant-side-hustle-canada/).

It doesn't suit people who guess at numbers to make a column balance, who want to give tax advice they're not qualified to give, or who can't keep client data secure.

## Which titles and rules apply to Canadian bookkeepers?

Titles are set by provincial law. In Ontario, the [Public Accounting Act, 2004](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p08) says a person isn't required to be licensed under that Act for providing services as a bookkeeper or engaging in bookkeeping solely by virtue of those activities. The [Chartered Professional Accountants of Ontario Act, 2017](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/17c08) restricts the CPA designation and initials and holding yourself out as a Chartered Professional Accountant, Chartered Accountant, Certified Management Accountant or Certified General Accountant, while saying it doesn't interfere with a non-member's right to practise as an accountant. Other provinces have their own statutes; read your provincial CPA body's rules before you print a business card.

What this means in practice:

- Call yourself a **bookkeeper**. Don't use "CPA" or "chartered" unless you hold the designation.
- Don't sign audits or review engagements, and don't issue financial statements that claim assurance.
- If you'll speak to the CRA for a client, they must [authorize you as a representative](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/representative-authorization/how.html) first.
- The CRA's [business records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html) page says records must generally be kept for six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to. Agree in writing who keeps what.
- Client financial data is personal information; private-sector privacy law ([PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/)) applies in most provinces. Use two-factor authentication and never email bank passwords.

This is not legal advice.

## Worked math: what a small client roster earns

**Illustrative.** Client fees, hours per client and software costs are assumptions. The fees sit below the published firm packages above, as a new freelancer might charge.

| Client type | Monthly fee | Hours/month | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor, 1 bank + 1 card, ~40 transactions | $250 | 3 | $83/hr |
| Small retailer, 2 banks, Shopify payouts, ~150 transactions | $450 | 6 | $75/hr |
| Contractor with GST/HST filing and 2 employees' payroll via an app | $650 | 9 | $72/hr |

Four sole-proprietor clients + two retailers + one contractor:

- Revenue: 4 × $250 + 2 × $450 + $650 = **$2,550 a month**.
- Hours: 4 × 3 + 2 × 6 + 9 = **33 hours a month**, about 8 a week.
- Costs: assume $60/month for your own invoicing, storage and a password manager; client software is billed to the client.
- Net before tax: $2,490, or **about $75 an hour**.
- Annualised: $30,600 in revenue, just past the **$30,000 GST/HST small-supplier line**, so a roster this size needs to register.

**Cleanup jobs.** A client who's a year behind is a separate fixed quote: estimate the transactions, multiply by your minutes per transaction, and add a buffer. For example, 1,200 transactions × 1.5 minutes = 30 hours; at an assumed $55/hour that's $1,650.

The first-year lesson from Financly (below) is that pricing too low, not too few clients, is what breaks small bookkeeping businesses.

## How do you price a bookkeeping client?

Price by the work the month creates, not by the hour. Ask for three months of bank and card statements before you quote, count the transactions, and note the extras.

| Pricing input | Why it matters | How to charge for it |
|---|---|---|
| Transactions per month | The main driver of time | Bands, e.g. under 50, 50–150, 150–300 |
| Number of accounts | Each bank, card, PayPal or Stripe account needs its own reconciliation | Include 2–3; charge per extra account |
| GST/HST tracking and return prep | Quarterly or monthly deadlines | Add-on per return |
| Payroll | Remittance deadlines and T4s | Add-on per pay run or per employee |
| Accounts receivable chasing | Ongoing client contact | Add-on or a higher tier |
| Inventory or e-commerce payouts | Fees, refunds and split payouts are slow to match | Higher tier |
| Late or missing receipts | Rework and follow-ups | Late fee or catch-up charge |

Publish two or three tiers rather than a custom price for every client; both national packages above use tiers. Review each client after three months against your effective hourly rate and reprice the ones that take longer than planned.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

### Week 1: Prove you can do it

- **Day 1:** Pick two ledgers to learn (QuickBooks Online plus Xero or Wave).
- **Day 2:** Sign up for the free accountant or partner program and start the vendor certification.
- **Days 3–5:** Do a full month of your own household or side-business books: categorize, reconcile bank and card to the cent, produce a P&L.
- **Days 6–7:** Write your service menu and an engagement letter (template below).

### Week 2: Set up the business

- Register a business name if you won't use your own; open a separate bank account; set up your own invoicing.
- Get a quote for errors and omissions (professional liability) insurance.
- Set up a password manager and secure file sharing.
- Read the rules for your province's protected accounting titles.

### Week 3: Find the first clients

- Tell your network: one specific post ("I keep monthly books for trades and small shops in [city]").
- Offer a fixed-price cleanup to two small businesses you already know.
- Contact local accountants: many refer small clients they don't want, or subcontract year-end cleanup.

### Week 4: Deliver and systemise

- Run your first month-end close using the checklist below.
- Send a one-page monthly report with three numbers the owner cares about.
- Ask for a testimonial and a referral.

### Days 31–90

- **Weeks 5–8:** Add one client a fortnight; track hours per client.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Reprice any client whose effective rate is under your floor.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Add GST/HST return preparation for clients (they still file and sign) and plan for tax season.

## Templates and checklists

**Month-end close checklist**

- [ ] Bank feeds imported for every account, including PayPal, Stripe and Shopify payouts
- [ ] All transactions categorized; uncertain items listed for the client
- [ ] Bank and credit card accounts reconciled to the statement balance
- [ ] Receipts attached for expenses over the client's threshold
- [ ] GST/HST collected and paid (input tax credits) reviewed
- [ ] Accounts receivable and payable aged and reviewed
- [ ] P&L and balance sheet exported; month locked
- [ ] Summary email sent with questions

**Engagement letter (short)**

> Services: monthly bookkeeping for [business] in [software]: categorizing transactions, reconciling [list accounts], and a monthly P&L and balance sheet by the [15th].
> Not included: tax returns, tax advice, audits or reviews, payroll remittances unless listed.
> Client responsibilities: provide statements and receipts by the [5th]; approve and file all CRA returns.
> Fee: $[X]/month for up to [N] transactions; extra transactions $[Y] each. Cleanup quoted separately.
> Records: the client keeps original records for at least six years. Either party may end this with 30 days' notice.

**Monthly report email**

> Hi [name], your [month] books are closed. Revenue $[ ], expenses $[ ], profit $[ ]. Cash in the bank: $[ ]. GST/HST owing so far this quarter: $[ ]. Three questions for you: [list]. Full reports attached.

## Real Canadian bookkeepers

These are publicly documented. Their results aren't typical.

**Balanced Bookkeeping (Yukon).** Yukonstruct profiled Shanti Morrison-Doherty on March 16, 2026. She started the business in spring 2022 after more than a decade working for a former boss who encouraged her to go out on her own, and provides bookkeeping onsite and remotely. She said she carried forward strong professional relationships from that job, and that the biggest challenge is establishing trust and credibility while doing every part of the business alone. **Lesson:** your past employer and colleagues are often your first clients and referrers.

**Financly (Canada).** Pure Bookkeeping's case study says co-founders Teresa Slack and Connie Smith grew from 5 to 10 to 20 to 30 bookkeeping clients, plus over a hundred tax clients, partly by running financial literacy seminars with Intuit Canada. After hiring three part-time staff without written processes, clients got upset and the owners stopped getting paid; their pricing was wrong. On The Successful Bookkeeper podcast (February 3, 2026), Slack described starting in 2014 charging $18 an hour and not paying herself for nearly three years. After fixing pricing and systems, the case study reports a 63% increase in business. **Lesson:** write procedures before you hire, and price on value and volume rather than a low hourly rate.

## Which tax rules apply to your bookkeeping income?

Your fees are self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net profit (QPP in Quebec). Your own records follow the same six-year rule as your clients'.

This is not tax or legal advice.

- **GST/HST:** register once worldwide taxable supplies pass **$30,000** in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). The [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule doesn't apply to you, though it does to driver clients.
- **Place of supply:** for services to businesses in other provinces, the rate generally follows the client's location. Ontario 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% (since April 1, 2025), New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I. 15%, other provinces 5% GST, with QST in Quebec. Confirm with the [CRA rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Deductions:** software, insurance, training, a share of home office, internet and phone.
- **Tax prep:** if you also prepare returns, read [Wealthsimple Tax vs TurboTax vs H&R Block](/side-hustles/wealthsimple-tax-vs-turbotax-vs-hr-block-canada/) and keep tax advice within your training.

## Common freelance bookkeeping mistakes

- **Hourly pricing that punishes speed.** Sell a monthly package by volume.
- **Unlimited "quick questions."** Tax advice and CFO-style calls are separate services.
- **Taking on cleanup inside the monthly fee.** Quote it separately after seeing the records.
- **Using a protected title.** "Bookkeeper" is fine; "CPA" isn't unless you are one.
- **Weak security.** Passwords by email and shared logins put client data at risk.
- **Plugging differences.** Never force a reconciliation; flag the gap.
