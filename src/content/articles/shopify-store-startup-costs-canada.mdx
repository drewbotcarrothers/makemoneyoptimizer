Starting a Shopify store in Canada, meaning your own checkout for a product you can describe and deliver, can cost about $1–$50 in an introductory month if you already hold the product, about $50–$150 in a typical month once Basic is at its standard yearly or monthly price plus a few mailers, or about $250–$800 when inventory, a domain you bought, apps, and a real returns buffer are in the plan. Those Shopify figures use Shopify's Canada pricing page for the plan and card rates, and typical Canadian retail ranges for supplies. They suit people who can answer a customer while sales are still uncertain, and they do not treat the $1 offer as the ongoing cost.

How to list one product and read the plan is the [Shopify store side hustle guide](/side-hustles/shopify-store-side-hustle-canada). This page is the cash. One product is the catalogue.

## How much does a Shopify store cost to start in Canada?

A Shopify store in Canada starts at about $1–$50 while an introductory offer on the pricing page is in force and you already have the product, about $50–$150 in a typical month at Basic's standard yearly or monthly price plus a small shipping kit, and about $250–$800 when you add stock you paid for, a custom domain, one paid app you can name, and money you do not spend so a refund is possible. The ranges are not quotes. Grow and Advanced are outside a first store.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What you can sell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean, during the intro offer | About $1–$50 plus the product you already have | Basic on the offer the pricing page describes, photos in daylight, a myshopify.com address, no paid apps. The offer ends. |
| Typical, after the offer | About $50–$150 | Basic at $37 CAD a month if you pay yearly, or $49 CAD a month if you pay monthly, a few mailers if you ship, and card fees only when an order exists. |
| Doing it properly | About $250–$800 before a large inventory bet | The standard plan, a domain you priced at checkout, packaging for more than one order, a sample you ordered to yourself if you do not make the item, FreshBooks if the order count needs it, a course only if you priced it, insurance if you store stock, and a returns buffer. |

Shopify's [Canada pricing](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing) page is the source. As published there, Basic is $49 CAD a month, or $37 CAD a month billed yearly. Grow is $132 CAD a month, or $99 CAD a month yearly. Advanced is $517 CAD a month, or $389 CAD a month yearly. The page describes a 3-day trial and then $1 a month for 3 months on many plans, after which standard pricing applies. There is no setup fee on the plans described there. Online standard card rates on Basic are 2.8% + 30¢ CAD. Third-party payment providers add a transaction fee, 2% on Basic on that page. Hosting, a free TLS certificate, and a myshopify.com address are included. A custom domain is optional. Confirm every figure. The $1 offer is not the ongoing cost.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for a Shopify store in Canada?

Ongoing Shopify costs in Canada are the plan at the standard price, the card fee on each order, the product and the mailer, refunds, and ads if you run them. Shopify break-even in the sketch is a method, not an average store profit. A new store can sell nothing.

**The intro cliff.** Month four at $1 is not month four at $49 or at the yearly equivalent. Sketch the standard price.

**Card fees.** They scale with the sale. They do not replace the plan.

**Ads.** They can exceed sales. They are not in the lean column.

**Duties.** Imported stock has a border cost. Put it in the product cost before you set the price.

### Step 1: Write the standard plan price, not only the offer

$1 and $37 and $49 are three different months. Pick the one you will actually be on.

### Step 2: Add the card fee for one realistic order

Use 2.8% + 30¢ on Basic unless your card type is the higher line on the page.

### Step 3: Add the product cost and the mailer

A digital file has no mailer. A shipped good does. A refund keeps the outgoing postage.

### Step 4: Hold a buffer you do not spend

One return should not empty the account that pays next month's plan.

### Step 5: Add an app only after the core editor fails

The pricing page includes hosting, themes, and a free Academy. That is the stack until a task breaks.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of store profits.**

Suppose the intro has ended and you use Basic at the yearly rate, $37 for the month. Suppose one order arrives at an assumed $40 product price. That $40 is an assumption, not a market price. Online standard card rates on Basic are 2.8% + 30¢ CAD, so 0.028 × $40 = $1.12, plus $0.30, which is $1.42. Suppose the item cost you $12 and the mailer is $2. Cash in = $40. Cash out = $37 + $1.42 + $12 + $2 = $52.42. This one order loses about $12.42 before your time.

Suppose instead you sell a digital file with no product cost and no mailer, and ten orders arrive at the same assumed $40. Card fees are 10 × $1.42 = $14.20. Remainder = $400 − $14.20 − $37 = $348.80 before hours, refunds, and tax set-aside. Ten physical orders at a $12 cost and a $2 mailer would subtract another 10 × $14 = $140, leaving $208.80 in this sketch before hours. These totals are assumptions. Confirm the live card rate. Do not copy $40. One order does not carry the plan.

## Which tax rules apply to a Shopify budget in Canada?

Shopify store income in Canada is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) when you are carrying on the activity for profit, and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. The plan, card fees, mailers, and the cost of goods are generally current expenses. A computer or equipment that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Digital products can also sit on the CRA [digital-economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) pages. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Shopify can calculate tax at checkout. That feature does not decide whether you must be registered. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over four consecutive quarters. Add other side hustles. A store is not ride-sharing.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases, including the plan if the receipt shows the tax, can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use. If you are not registered, do not add GST/HST and do not claim the credit. Confirm the current rules with the CRA or a tax professional before you turn tax on in the admin.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set collected tax aside. A payout is not spending money until fees, refunds, and that tax are separated.
