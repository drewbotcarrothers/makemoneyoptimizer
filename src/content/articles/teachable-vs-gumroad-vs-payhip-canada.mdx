Teachable, Gumroad and Payhip all sell digital products to buyers anywhere, pay out to Canadian bank accounts, and charge in different ways: Gumroad takes a percentage of each sale and nothing monthly, Payhip trades a monthly plan for a lower percentage, and Teachable charges a monthly plan built around courses. For a Canadian seller, the decision comes down to monthly sales, whether the product is a course or a download, and who handles sales tax. Plan prices are converted to Canadian dollars at the Bank of Canada's [daily rate](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) of 1.4240 CAD per USD for October 8, 2026.

### Where do the lines cross?

- **Payhip Plus beats Payhip Free** when 3% of monthly sales passes the $41.30 plan: $41.30 ÷ 0.03 = **$1,376.67 a month** in sales.
- **Payhip Pro beats Plus** when 2% of sales passes the $99.68 difference between the plans: **$4,984 a month**.
- **Teachable Builder beats Starter** when 7.5% of sales passes the plan difference: $71.20 ÷ 0.075 = **$949.33 a month** on monthly billing, or $56.96 ÷ 0.075 = **$759.47** on yearly billing.
- **Payhip Free always costs less than Gumroad direct** on a card sale: its 5% is half Gumroad's 10%, with no $0.50 per sale. Gumroad's case rests on the sales tax handling and the Discover marketplace, not on price.

The fixed fee per sale matters more as prices fall. On a $5 file through Gumroad, $0.50 USD is $0.71, which with the 10% ($0.50), 2.9% processing ($0.15) and $0.43 of fixed processing takes $1.78, about 36% of the sale. On Payhip Free the same sale costs 5% + 2.9% + $0.30 = $0.70.

## Which platform fits a Canadian digital product seller?

- **Payhip** fits most Canadians selling downloads: ebooks, printables, Notion and Canva templates, presets and small courses. Start on the free plan, move to Plus when monthly sales pass about $1,375, and to Pro above about $4,980. Every plan has every feature, so upgrading is purely a fee decision.
- **Gumroad** fits a first test with no monthly cost, sellers whose buyers are spread across many countries and who would rather not track foreign sales tax, and products that suit the Discover marketplace. It cost $62.68 more than Payhip Free at $750 a month in sales in the example above.
- **Teachable** fits a course business: video lessons, drip schedules, quizzes, certificates on higher plans, coaching and memberships. A course priced at $200 or more reaches Builder's crossover quickly; a $25 download does not need a course platform.

If the product is a newsletter or a membership rather than a file, compare [Substack vs beehiiv vs Kit](/side-hustles/substack-vs-beehiiv-vs-kit-canada/). For physical goods, see [Etsy vs Shopify](/side-hustles/etsy-vs-shopify-canada/).

## What does merchant of record mean for a Canadian digital seller?

A merchant of record is the seller of record to the buyer, so it calculates, collects and remits sales tax on the sale. Gumroad took on that role on January 1, 2025 and collects tax in regions where it has obligations as merchant of record. Teachable collects and remits tax on many sales through Teachable Payments, but for a Canadian school its help centre lists Canadian GST, QST and Saskatchewan PST as the creator's responsibility on domestic sales. Payhip collects EU and UK VAT and lets you set up other taxes, which you then remit. None of the three files your income tax; you still report the income to the CRA.

## What mistakes do Canadian digital sellers make choosing a platform?

- **Paying monthly before the first sale.** A Teachable plan with no sales is $41.30 to $55.54 a month gone.
- **Leaving processing out of the comparison.** Payhip and Teachable both add about 2.9% + $0.30 on top of their own fee.
- **Pricing small files on Gumroad.** The $0.50 fixed fee takes a large share of anything under $10.
- **Forgetting the payout minimum.** Gumroad's standard payouts need a $100 USD balance.
- **Assuming merchant of record covers Canadian GST/HST everywhere.** On Teachable, domestic Canadian tax stays with you; on Payhip, all non-EU/UK tax does.
- **Using unlicensed fonts or images.** Design in a tool whose licence covers commercial use, such as <AffiliateLink program="canva" label="Canva" />, and read the terms.

## What could change this comparison?

All three companies change prices without notice, and Teachable runs promotions such as three months for the price of one. The pricing pages for Teachable and Payhip show dollar amounts without naming a currency to Canadian visitors; this comparison treats them as U.S. dollars and converts at 1.4240, so a weaker Canadian dollar raises every plan line. Payment processing differs for international cards (Stripe Canada adds 0.8%; Teachable's international rate is 3.7% + CA$0.30) and PayPal. Gumroad's 30% Discover fee applies only to buyers who find you through Gumroad's marketplace. This is not tax or legal advice.

## Which tax rules apply to digital product sellers in Canada?

Digital product sales are business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), converted to Canadian dollars, and platform and processing fees are generally deductible. You register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html); how sales made through a platform count is covered on the CRA's [digital economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) pages. Steps are in [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) and [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/). This is not tax or legal advice.
