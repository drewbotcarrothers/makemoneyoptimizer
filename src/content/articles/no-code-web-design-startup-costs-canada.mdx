## How much does no-code web design cost to start in Canada?

No-code web design in Canada costs $0 with a computer you own and a free builder plan, $39.55 with a USB headset, $374.15 with your own portfolio site and a second monitor, $724.15 with a starting liability policy, or $1,454.13 with a year of design and invoicing software. Goods and software include 13% Ontario HST; insurance is added without HST. Use your province's rate.

The Hostinger plan is the line most people can swap. If you'd rather show your work on the platform you sell, a Squarespace Basic plan billed annually or a paid Framer plan does the same job. The pro kit's software lines are spread over a year, so they don't all leave your account in week one.

## Who should use a no-code web design startup budget in Canada?

A no-code web design budget fits people who can turn a short explanation into a page a stranger can use on a phone, and who will write down who owns the domain before the first payment. The lean kit fits one brochure page for a trade, a clinic or a studio. The standard kit fits someone pitching local businesses with a portfolio site of their own. The pro kit fits someone handling several projects a quarter with deposits and care plans.

It's a poor fit if you want to write custom software, if you'll hold the domain hostage, or if the plan depends on promising a spot on the first page of Google.

## Do you need a licence or insurance for web design in Canada?

There's no provincial licence to build websites. Two things matter more.

**Domains and privacy.** CIRA's [Canadian Presence Requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/legal-policy-and-compliance/canadian-presence-requirements/) explain who may register a .ca name. Categories include Canadian citizens, permanent residents and Canadian corporations, among others. The client, if they qualify, should be the registrant. If a page collects names or emails through a form, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner's [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/) page is the federal starting point. Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec have their own private-sector privacy statutes. A business that operates in Quebec may have French-language duties for public commercial content; send that client to the Office québécois de la langue française. This is not legal advice.

**Insurance.** Zensurance's [freelancer insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/freelancer-insurance) lists web developers among the freelancers it covers and says freelancer insurance generally starts from $350 to $500 a year for a professional liability policy with a $1 million limit. Its [web developer insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/web-developer-insurance) says most web designers and developers carry $1 million of professional liability coverage and suggests cyber liability as well. Both are estimates, not quotes, read on October 9, 2026. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance advice.

**Business name and licence.** If you trade under a name that isn't your own, most provinces require a registration; in Ontario that's $60 online, per the province's [fee page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit). Search your city for a home-business licence if you advertise locally. This guide does not state a city licence fee.

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Renewals you kept by accident.** If the agreement says the client pays, their card should be on the account before you call the job done.
- **Your own portfolio plan.** Hostinger renews at CAN$15.39 a month after the promo term; Squarespace Basic is CA$21 a month on an annual plan.
- **Insurance,** from about $350 a year once you carry it.
- **Software,** such as Canva Pro at $142 a year and FreshBooks from $26 a month, if you pay for them.
- **Scope.** "Also make it rank" and a contact form that stores a customer list are different jobs from a brochure.
- **Photos and copy.** Time you spend chasing a logo is unpaid if the agreement assumed the client would send it.
- **Quiet months.** A plan you opened for a client who never paid a deposit is a subscription in search of a page.

## Where can no-code web design startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Build the sample on a free plan.** Framer and Wix both have one.
- **Skip the paid portfolio** until a client asks for your own URL. A free subdomain and a shared folder of screenshots are enough for the first pitches.
- **Wait on the monitor and software** until a second project is booked.
- **Don't register the client's .ca in your account** to save a step. The step you save is the one that causes the dispute.

Don't buy a laptop. Don't buy a year of hosting for a business that hasn't paid a deposit.

## Where shouldn't you cut no-code web design spending?

Don't skip the written handoff, the privacy reading if the page collects names, or a backup you can give the client. A site only you can log into isn't a completed sale. Don't promise a Google ranking. Don't keep a copy of the client's customer list after the handoff.

When the client pays, <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can hold the deposit and the final invoice. Take the deposit before you build. A finished page with no invoice is the cost you can't get back. When a scope call is booked, a <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="USB headset" search="USB headset microphone" /> on Amazon.ca is a cheap comparison shop against a local price.

## Which tax rules apply to a no-code web design budget in Canada?

No-code web design income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. A computer or monitor that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion. GST/HST for this work uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add design, writing and other side-hustle revenue to the same total. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, using the [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025. Quebec charges GST and QST. If you're registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases like the monitor or a builder plan can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html). If you aren't registered, don't charge GST/HST.

Keep the agreements, the e-transfers and any receipts you bill back. This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.
