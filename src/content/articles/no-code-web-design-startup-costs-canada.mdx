## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for no-code web design in Canada?

Ongoing no-code web design costs in Canada are hosting renewals you accidentally kept in your name, a domain you forgot to transfer, and edits that were not in the page count. No-code break-even in the sketch is a method, not a site price. Hosting dollars belong on the host's page, not in a remembered number.

**Renewals.** If the agreement says the client pays, the card on the account should be theirs before you call the job done. A renewal in your name is a cost you did not mean to own.

**Scope.** "Also make it rank" and a contact form that stores a customer list are different jobs from a brochure. Privacy and anti-spam questions belong in the note, with the client pointed at the official pages, not with you playing lawyer.

**Photos and copy.** Time you spend chasing a logo is unpaid if the agreement assumed the client would send it. Write that assumption down.

**Quiet months.** A hosting plan you opened before a client exists is a subscription in search of a page. The lean column uses a free preview.

## Which tax rules apply to a no-code web design budget in Canada?

No-code web design income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. A laptop that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion. GST/HST for this work uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreements and the e-transfers. If you pay for hosting and bill it back, keep that receipt separate from your fee. Report the work on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add design, writing, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Web design is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. This guide does not explain input tax credits. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set any tax you must collect aside.
