Getting a first English-teaching client in Canada means selling one adult lesson, with a length, a price, and a cancellation rule, to a learner you can actually schedule. Teaching English online from Canada suits people who can model the language clearly and who will not promise an exam score or an immigration outcome.

The first client is a paid conversation, not a language school. It is a poor target if you need income that does not depend on someone showing up, or if the learner would be a child without a parent in the plan. Starting the work is covered in [how to start teaching English online in Canada](/side-hustles/teach-english-online-side-hustle-canada/). The gear is in [teaching English online startup costs](/side-hustles/teach-english-online-startup-costs-canada/).

A school subject is [online tutoring](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-side-hustle-canada/). The same lesson as a document is [freelance writing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada/).

## What offer gets a first English-teaching client in Canada?

A first English-teaching client in Canada usually buys one 45-minute adult conversation or a workplace phrase list, with a date and a no-show rule. "English coach" is hard to purchase. "One conversation about your job, 45 minutes, a price we write down" is a purchase.

Say what you will not promise. Exam scores, immigration results, and a child's lesson are outside the first offer. TESL Canada's [certification FAQ](https://tesl.ca/certification/tesl-canada-professional-certification/certification-faq) says its professional certification is intended for adult ESL or EFL environments and does not replace provincial K–12 certification. Do not advertise a certificate you do not hold. Some employers require one. A private adult lesson is a different question.

## Where do Canadians find a first English student?

Canadians find a first English student among adults who asked for practice, in communities that allow a lesson offer, and on a marketplace only after they have read that marketplace's fee page. This guide does not state a platform cut.

People who already want practice. A coworker learning workplace phrases, a newcomer who asked, an adult in a group you belong to. That is a conversation.

A public place that allows the offer. Read the group's rules before you post. Do not scrape members.

A marketplace profile, if you use one. Read the fee and the payout currency on that site's page. Put the same lesson sentence on the profile. A profile that says "certified" without the certificate is a misrepresentation.

If you email, follow ISED's [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page. Commercial messages need consent, identification, and an unsubscribe path acted on within 10 business days. This is not legal advice.

The RCMP's [vulnerable sector](https://rcmp.ca/en/criminal-records/criminal-record-checks/vulnerable-sector-checks) page says there is no federal legislation that requires any organization to conduct those checks, and that the hiring organization requests one for a position of trust over children or vulnerable persons. Prefer adults. If the learner is under 18, a parent books and is present, and you read that page and your province's rules first.

## What should a first English-lesson note say?

A first lesson note in Canada should name the length, the goal, the price, the cancellation rule, and what you will not promise.

**Example message.** This is a generic example you can adapt. It is not a note that produced a student.

> Hello. I teach one 45-minute adult conversation on a video call. A first lesson can focus on workplace phrases you choose, with a short text I wrote. The price is a number we agree before the call. If you cancel with less than a day of notice, the note says whether the hour is still billed. I do not promise an exam score or an immigration result. I am not advertising a certificate I do not hold. Would Thursday at 7 p.m. Eastern work?

Do not fill silence with a discount that removes the cancellation rule.

## How do you prove English-teaching skill without fake reviews?

You prove the skill by running the lesson plan out loud and timing it, and by speaking clearly enough that a headset test survives. Invented student quotes and a borrowed "TESL certified" line are not proof.

Use a text you wrote. Do not photocopy a textbook chapter. If an adult agrees you may say they took a trial conversation, say that, and do not upgrade it into a score improvement. In Ontario, the Ontario College of Teachers regulates teaching in publicly funded schools. Other provinces have their own regulators. A private conversation is not that title.

## How should you price the first English lesson in Canada?

Price the first English lesson in Canada as a fee for a defined length, with prep counted in your own sketch even if the invoice says 45 minutes. This guide does not publish a national rate. A marketplace cut, if you use one, comes off only after you read that page.

| Path | What the learner buys | What you watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct lesson | One timed conversation, a cancellation rule | A no-show, prep you forgot |
| Marketplace hour | Whatever that profile allows | The fee on that platform's page |

A direct lesson and a marketplace profile are different prices because the cut, if any, is on that platform's page and this guide does not guess it. Cancellations, prep, and a lesson that runs past the timer are the other factors. Count prep in your own sketch even when the invoice says 45 minutes. A headset that lets the learner hear consonants is gear, not a reason to raise the first fee after you already wrote it down.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose you assume $40 for 45 minutes, and prep takes another 25 minutes, so the block is about 1.2 hours. That $40 is an assumption for the arithmetic, not a market rate. The sketch is $40 ÷ 1.2, about $33 an hour before a tax set-aside, if the lesson is direct and this sketch includes no marketplace fee. A headset bought the same week is not subtracted from every future lesson. These totals are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first English student look like?

A 14-day plan for a first English student in Canada should produce a timed lesson, a cancellation rule, and a few asks to adults who want practice. A paid hour is a bonus. Silence is not a reason to advertise a certificate or to recruit children.

### Days 1 and 2: Write the lesson

One goal. A short text you wrote. A timer. Read the TESL Canada FAQ far enough to know which words you will not use.

### Days 3 to 5: Say it out loud

Time the 45 minutes. Fix the headset. Write the no-show rule and the line that you do not promise scores or immigration outcomes.

### Days 6 to 9: Ask adults who want practice

Five conversations or posts where the community allows it. If you email, follow the consent page. Do not message minors.

### Days 10 to 14: Teach the hour or tighten the plan

If someone books, stop at the timer and invoice as the note says. If nobody books, edit the lesson once. Do not add a children's offer to fill the calendar.

## What mistakes block a first English-teaching client in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first student in Canada are promising results, using a title you do not hold, and teaching children as the easy start. One adult lesson is enough.

A marketplace profile with no lesson plan asks the learner to imagine you. A no-show rule you invent after the fact is a dispute. Scraping a newcomer group that forbids promotion skips both the group's rules and anti-spam consent.

A free month of lessons with no cap is a gift. Call it a gift if you choose it. Do not describe it as a client.

## Which tax rules apply to a first English lesson in Canada?

Teaching income is self-employment income, including a marketplace payout, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST Memorandum [20-6](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-6/tutoring-equivalent-services.html) describes an exemption for tutoring an individual in a course approved for credit by, or that follows a curriculum designated by, a school authority, and says that exemption does not apply to post-secondary courses. A private adult conversation is not automatically that supply. Do not tell a learner the hour is tax-free unless it meets the memorandum.

For taxable supplies, the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) applies, not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

One lesson does not by itself cross the small-supplier line. If you are not registered, and the supply is taxable, do not charge GST/HST. If you are registered, confirm the rate with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set the tax aside.
