Reselling can work as a side hustle when you treat it like retail: buy carefully, price for fees and time, and stay honest about condition. This sample article is for Canadians testing marketplace sales — not flipping fantasies.

## What “good enough” looks like

Successful part-time resellers usually:

- Know a category (outdoors gear, kids’ items, tools, vintage electronics)
- Factor in platform fees, shipping, returns, and their own hours
- Photograph accurately and write clear descriptions

If you hate customer messages or storage clutter, this path will feel draining fast.

## Common Canadian channels

- Local pickup apps and classifieds (lower shipping hassle)
- Larger marketplaces with buyer protection (higher fees, wider reach)
- Specialty forums for collectibles or camera gear

Start local to learn photography, pricing, and negotiation before you add shipping complexity.

## Sourcing ethically

- Prefer thrift, estate sales, and clearing your own unused items first.
- Avoid counterfeit goods and “mystery” electronics with wiped serials.
- Check recall lists for kids’ products and safety gear.
- Don’t misrepresent authenticity or working condition.

## Math that keeps you honest

Before you buy inventory, sketch:

`Sale price − fees − shipping − cost of goods − your time estimate = real outcome`

If the leftover is tiny, skip the item. Volume only helps when margins survive fees.

Compare with service hustles like [freelance writing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-canada) or [gig delivery](/side-hustles/gig-delivery-canada) if capital and storage aren’t appealing.

## Shipping and customer trust

- Weigh packages; underestimating postage eats profit.
- Use sturdy materials; damaged arrivals create refunds and bad ratings.
- Reply promptly; most disputes escalate from silence.
- Keep proof of postage and condition photos.

## Taxes and records (overview only)

Reselling income may be taxable. Track purchases, sales, and fees. CRA guidance on business vs. hobby depends on facts — get professional advice when unsure. See also our broader [guides hub](/guides).

## A low-risk test week

1. List 5–10 items you already own.
2. Price using sold comps, not wishful asking prices.
3. Note hours spent photographing, messaging, and packing.
4. Decide whether the hourly outcome is worth continuing.

Reselling rewards patience and category knowledge. Treat sample profits as data, not destiny.
