Rideshare drivers in Canada don't set fares. Uber's app shows a take-home offer for each trip, net of its service fee, and you decide whether it pays enough. Two provinces put a floor under engaged time: British Columbia pays at least $21.89 an hour plus $0.45 a kilometre from June 1, 2026, and Ontario at least the $17.95 general minimum wage from October 1, 2026. Regulated taxis show what a ride is worth to a rider: a Toronto meter starts at $4.25 and adds about $1.75 a kilometre, and Metro Vancouver's common rate is $3.75 plus $2.18 a kilometre. Employed taxi drivers earn a $19.00 median (Job Bank). A small car costs about $0.12 a kilometre in fuel alone and about $0.26 with depreciation and maintenance (CAA, Ontario). A busy 5-hour Toronto shift can net about $25 an hour before income tax.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Pay floors (2026):** BC $21.89 per engaged hour + $0.45 per engaged km; Ontario $17.95 per hour of work assignments. Waiting for a ping isn't covered; tips don't count toward either.
- **Taxi meters (riders' benchmark):** Toronto $4.25 start, $0.25 per 143 m (about $1.75/km); Metro Vancouver $3.75 flag, $2.18/km including GST.
- **Wage benchmark:** taxi drivers $19.00 median in Canada, $20.77 in BC, $18.46 in Ontario (Job Bank).
- **Car cost:** CAA's Ontario figures for a 2025 Nissan Sentra work out to $0.12/km fuel and about $0.26/km with depreciation and maintenance, before rideshare insurance.
- **Accept a trip if:** offer ≥ (minutes incl. pickup ÷ 60 × target hourly) + (km incl. pickup × cost per km).

## How does rideshare pay work in Canada?

Uber's Canadian [upfront offers](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/blog/more-transparency-with-upfront-offers-in-canada/) show your expected take-home fare and the destination before you accept. The offer is net of Uber's service fee and includes surge. Uber no longer posts fixed time and distance rates; the offer weighs time, distance, market conditions and demand at the destination. A longer pickup usually brings a higher offer and a slightly lower service fee. Tips, wait-time fees and cancellation fees are added after the trip, and tips are 100% yours. If a trip runs substantially longer or shorter than estimated, the fare is adjusted on the receipt.

Provincial rules then set a floor on engaged time:

| Rule | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BC ride-hail minimum | $21.89 per hour of engaged time from June 1, 2026, plus at least $0.45/km while engaged | [BC minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) |
| BC tips | Not counted toward the minimum; 100% of tips to the worker | [BC overview (PDF)](https://engage.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/121/2026/07/Overview-of-Minimum-Standards-and-Protections-for-App-based-Workers.pdf) |
| Ontario platform work | General minimum wage ($17.95/h from Oct 1, 2026) for each work assignment or pay period, tips excluded | [Ontario DPWRA page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers), [Ontario minimum wage](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/minimum-wage) |
| Other provinces | No platform-specific floor on these pages | — |

In BC, engaged time runs from accepting a trip to completing or cancelling it. Ontario's page says a work assignment generally starts when you accept it. Time spent waiting for a request counts in neither province.

## What does your car cost per kilometre?

Every kilometre you drive, including pickups and empty returns, costs money. Two published benchmarks:

| Benchmark | Figure | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |

## How do you work out your real hourly rate?

```text
Net per hour  = (trip earnings + tips − km driven × cost per km) ÷ logged-in hours
GST/HST       = collected on fares; owed to the CRA (less input tax credits)
Trip floor    = (minutes incl. pickup ÷ 60 × target hourly) + (km incl. pickup × cost per km)
BC top-up     = (engaged hours × $21.89 + engaged km × $0.45) − trip earnings, if positive
```

1. **Take trip earnings and tips** from the weekly statement. Keep the GST/HST line separate.
2. **Log every kilometre** while logged in, plus the drive to your starting area. The [vehicle expenses guide](/side-hustles/vehicle-expenses-delivery-rideshare-canada/) covers the logbook.
3. **Use your cost per kilometre,** or $0.26 from CAA until you have your own records.
4. **Divide by logged-in hours,** including waiting.
5. **Hold back income tax and CPP.** Self-employed drivers pay both [CPP shares](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html); see the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

## What do worked rideshare shifts look like?

**Illustrative.** Trip earnings, tips, kilometres and hours are assumed. The pay floors, HST rate and CAA cost per kilometre are published.

### Example 1: Toronto Friday evening, 5 hours logged in

- Trip earnings $150 before HST, tips $15, 140 km driven.
- Car cost 140 × $0.26 = $36.40.
- The HST Uber collected on the gross fares for these trips arrives with your weekly pay on a separate line. It isn't part of the $150 and it's owed to the CRA, less input tax credits for the HST on Uber's fees, fuel and maintenance.
- Net before income tax: $150 + $15 − $36.40 = **$128.60**, or **$25.72 per logged-in hour**, above the $18.46 Ontario taxi median.

### Example 2: Vancouver weeknight with a top-up check

- 4 hours logged in, 2.5 hours engaged, 60 engaged km. Trip earnings $74 before tips.
- BC floor: 2.5 × $21.89 = $54.73, plus 60 × $0.45 = $27.00, for $81.73.
- The $74 falls short, so the platform would owe a **$7.73** top-up for the pay period. Tips sit on top and don't reduce it.

### Example 3: A short trip with a long pickup

- Offer $9.50. Pickup 4 km, trip 6 km, 22 minutes in total. Target $25 an hour net, cost $0.26/km.
- Floor: 22 ÷ 60 × $25 = $9.17, plus 10 × $0.26 = $2.60, for **$11.77**.
- Decline: the offer is $2.27 short. A rider would pay $14.50 for the same 6 km by Toronto taxi meter.

### Example 4: Downtown Toronto to the airport, empty return

- Offer $48. Trip 30 km and 35 minutes, plus 30 km and 30 minutes driving back empty: 65 minutes, 60 km.
- Floor: 65 ÷ 60 × $25 = $27.08, plus 60 × $0.26 = $15.60, for **$42.68**.
- Accept: $5.32 above the floor, more if you catch a return trip.

## How can rideshare drivers raise earnings per hour?

You can't set the fare, so the levers are when, where, which trips and what each kilometre costs.

1. **Keep the best windows.** Two months of shift logs show which hours pay; drop the rest.
2. **Decline below your floor.** Long pickups for short trips are where hourly pay leaks; the offer card shows the destination before you accept.
3. **Cut cost per kilometre.** CAA's figures put fuel at about half the Sentra's $0.26 a kilometre; a more efficient car lowers the floor on every trip.
4. **Check BC top-ups.** Compare engaged time and kilometres with your pay each period.
5. **Claim input tax credits** on fuel and maintenance so less of the HST you collected goes to the CRA.
6. **Track promotions** separately; they're added after the trip and change week to week.

## What rideshare earnings mistakes are common?

- **Spending the GST/HST.** Outside Quebec, Uber pays it to you weekly; it belongs to the CRA.
- **Ignoring waiting time.** Neither BC nor Ontario pays for it, so your hourly rate must.
- **Counting only trip kilometres.** Pickups and empty returns cost the same $0.26.
- **Comparing gross fares with a taxi wage.** Job Bank's $19.00 median is a wage; compare it with your net after car costs, not your gross.
- **No logbook.** Without one, business-use kilometres are hard to support.

For the insurance and licence mistakes drivers make most, see the [rideshare driving mistakes guide](/side-hustles/rideshare-driving-side-hustle-canada/#what-mistakes-do-new-rideshare-drivers-make-in-canada).

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect rideshare earnings?

Rideshare income, including tips, is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) under industry code 485310, taxi service. Unlike most side hustles, ride-sharing drivers must register for GST/HST from the first fare; the CRA's [sharing economy page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/sharing-economy.html) says the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) doesn't apply. Its example: an Ontario driver with $10,000 in fares collects and remits $1,300 of HST.

Outside Quebec, Uber's [tax information page](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/drive/tax-information/) says it pays you the GST/HST collected each week and shows it on weekly statements and monthly and annual Tax Summaries; you remit and file. In Quebec, Uber collects and remits GST/QST for you, and you still file annually. The CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) and [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) cover rates by province. The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) and [platform income tax guide](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/) cover the details.

Weekly payouts mix income, tax owed and money for the car. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account option for keeping the tax and maintenance money apart from spending money.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Pay floors, taxi tariffs, Job Bank wages, CAA costs and Uber's and the CRA's pages were checked on October 9, 2026. Taxi meters are what riders pay and include no platform fee, so they're a ceiling, not a driver rate. Job Bank wages are for employed taxi drivers. CAA's figures are for one car at CAA's assumptions and exclude insurance; the CRA travel directive rate is a reimbursement rate, not a tax deduction. Uber doesn't publish fixed rates or Canadian average earnings, so trip earnings, tips, hours and kilometres in the examples are illustrative.
