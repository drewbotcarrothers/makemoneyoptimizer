## What does a side hustle blog really cost to host over four years?

The intro rate is only part of it. Hostinger's 48-month Premium price was CAN$201.12 up front, which works out to the advertised CAN$4.19 a month, but after that term it renews at CAN$15.39 a month.

### Illustrative example

Assumed situation: you prepay Hostinger Premium for 48 months, then renew month-to-month for one more year at the listed renewal rate.

- First 48 months: CAN$201.12.
- Year 5 at renewal: CAN$15.39 × 12 = CAN$184.68.
- Five-year total: CAN$201.12 + CAN$184.68 = CAN$385.80, before any domain renewal or taxes.

This is arithmetic on listed prices, not a quote; hosts change prices and terms often. Run the same sums on Namecheap's yearly price and WordPress.com's plan before you decide.

## What mistakes do new bloggers make choosing a host?

- **Comparing intro prices only.** Always compare renewal prices.
- **Prepaying four years for an untested idea.** A shorter term costs more per month but less if you quit.
- **Forgetting the domain renewal.** "Free domain for 1 year" renews at the normal price.
- **Skipping backups.** Check how often backups run and how to restore them.
- **Buying add-ons at checkout.** Many are optional; you can add them later.

## Which tax rules apply to blog hosting costs in Canada?

If your blog is a business, hosting and domains are generally deductible expenses on Form T2125, and foreign-currency charges are converted to CAD for your records.
