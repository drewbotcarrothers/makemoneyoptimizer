Pricing printables in Canada means setting a price per file or bundle, then checking what is left after platform fees. On Etsy, a Canadian seller pays a 6.5% transaction fee, 3% plus C$0.25 for payment processing on domestic and US orders, a 0.50% regulatory operating fee, and a US$0.20 listing fee, charged again when a listing renews. On Gumroad you pay 10% plus $0.50 on direct sales. Fixed per-sale fees take a big share of very cheap files, so bundles and sets often make more sense than single pages. This guide suits sellers who will work out net per sale before choosing prices.

## What fees do Canadian printables sellers pay on Etsy and Gumroad?

Canadian printables sellers on Etsy pay a listing fee, a transaction fee, payment processing, and a regulatory operating fee, according to Etsy's [fees and taxes page](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014483627-What-are-the-Fees-and-Taxes-for-Selling-on-Etsy) and related help articles. Gumroad lists its fees on its [pricing page](https://gumroad.com/pricing).

| Fee | Etsy (Canadian seller) | Gumroad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listing | $0.20 USD per listing, converted to your payment currency; a listing expires after 4 months; the same fee applies when it auto-renews, including on a sale | None |
| Transaction | 6.5% | 10% + $0.50 per sale through your profile or direct links; 30% through Discover |
| Payment processing | 3% + C$0.25 for orders from Canada and the US; 4% + C$0.25 for other international orders ([processing fees](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015628847-What-are-Payment-Processing-Fees-for-Selling-on-Etsy)) | Included in the fee above |
| Regulatory operating fee | 0.50% in Canada ([regulatory operating fee](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500011073202-What-is-a-Regulatory-Operating-Fee)) | None |
| Sales tax | Check Etsy's tax pages for your buyers' locations | Gumroad says it has been merchant of record since January 1, 2025 and handles sales tax |

Etsy also has optional advertising fees; this guide leaves them out.

## Which pricing models work for printables in Canada?

Printables pricing models for Canadian sellers are single files, themed bundles, tiered sets, editable templates priced higher than flat files, and commercial-use licences priced above personal use.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single file | One page or design | Popular one-off designs | Fixed fees eating most of a low price |
| Bundle | Several related files at less than their total | Planners, classroom packs | Bundles that undercut single sales |
| Tiered set | Basic set and an extended set | Planners with optional add-on pages | Too many confusing versions |
| Editable template | File the buyer customises (for example in Canva) | Invitations, social posts | Support questions about editing |
| Commercial licence | Higher price for business or resale use | Clip art, design assets | Unclear licence wording |

Check comparable listings' prices in your niche before choosing, rather than relying on a general "average".

## How do you calculate a printables price in Canada?

Calculate a printables price in Canada by working out net per sale after every fee at a few prices, then dividing your design time and costs by that net to see how many sales each price needs.

### Step 1: Count design time and costs

Hours to design, proof, make mockups, and write listings, plus tool subscriptions.

### Step 2: Work out net per sale

Price minus each platform fee, including fixed per-sale amounts.

### Step 3: Calculate break-even sales

Costs ÷ net per sale. Value your design hours at a rate you choose.

### Step 4: Compare with similar listings

Price relative to page count, design quality, and editability.

### Step 5: Set aside tax

Sales are self-employment income; see the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast, average, or survey. Suppose you sell a planner printable on Etsy for C$8 to a Canadian buyer, there is no shipping, and the listing auto-renews when it sells. Using the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4246 for October 2, 2026, the US$0.20 renewal is about C$0.28.

The fees are: transaction 6.5% × C$8 = C$0.52; processing 3% × C$8 + C$0.25 = C$0.49; regulatory operating fee 0.50% × C$8 = C$0.04; renewal about C$0.28. That totals C$1.33, leaving about C$6.67. This assumes processing is charged on the C$8 alone; check Etsy's page for what is included in the order total.

At C$4, the same fees total about C$0.93, nearly a quarter of the price, leaving C$3.07. A C$20 bundle pays about C$2.53 in fees and leaves C$17.47.

On Gumroad, a US$6 printable through a direct link pays $0.60 + $0.50 = $1.10, leaving US$4.90, about C$6.98. Gumroad's pricing page does not name the currency; this example assumes US dollars.

Suppose a planner took 10 hours to design and you value your time at C$35 an hour: C$350. At C$6.67 net per Etsy sale, you need 53 sales to recover that time (C$350 ÷ C$6.67 = 52.5).

## When should you raise printables prices in Canada?

Raise printables prices in Canada when you add pages or editable versions, when reviews show strong value, or when a listing sells steadily at its current price. Change one listing at a time and compare net revenue per week, not just units.

Moving single low-price files into bundles is another way to raise revenue per order.

## What printables pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common printables pricing mistakes in Canada are pricing single pages so low that fixed fees take most of the sale, forgetting the renewal fee on each sale, and selling commercial use at personal-use prices. Each lowers your real earnings.

Write clear licence terms. Bundle low-priced files. Recheck fees when Etsy updates them.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect printables pricing in Canada?

Printables sales in Canada are self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both [CPP shares](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net income. GST/HST registration follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all your side hustles.

Who collects tax on a sale depends on the platform and the buyer's location. The CRA explains platform rules in its [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html), and the [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) covers sales you make yourself. The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers registration.
