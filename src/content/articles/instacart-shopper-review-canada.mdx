Instacart is a legitimate grocery shopping and delivery gig in Canada for people with a car who are comfortable choosing items carefully and lifting heavy orders. As of October 2026, Instacart says full-service shopper earnings are batch pay plus promotions plus tips, with 100% of tips going to the shopper ([Instacart](https://company.instacart.ca/shoppers/shopper-earnings)). Ontario and British Columbia add legal pay floors for platform work. Instacart's own pages say "actual earnings may vary," and this review does not estimate hourly earnings.

## Is Instacart available to shoppers in Canada?

Yes, in many Canadian cities. Instacart's [Canadian shopper page](https://shoppers.instacart.ca/) lists the basics: be at least 18, be authorized to work in Canada, be able to lift 40 lb (18 kg), and have a car and a smartphone. You provide your driver's licence, SIN and bank details during sign-up, and Instacart issues a payment card for paying at the store. Shoppers are independent contractors.

## How does Instacart shopper pay work in Canada?

Each batch shows its earnings before you accept, and you are not penalized for declining. Instacart's [earnings page](https://company.instacart.ca/shoppers/shopper-earnings) breaks pay into:

| Component | What Instacart says |
| --- | --- |
| Batch pay | Reflects expected effort: travel to store and customer, quantity and weight of items, expected shopping time. May include boosts and heavy pay |
| Heavy pay | At least $2 on qualifying batches where items of 8+ lb total 50 lb or more |
| Boosts | Extra pay when there aren't enough shoppers nearby |
| Promotions | Extra earnings opportunities during high demand |
| Tips | 100% to the shopper; Instacart covers a zeroed-out tip up to $10 if no issue was reported |

Instacart notes that its peak earning times feature is not available in Ontario or British Columbia.

## What pay rules apply to Instacart shoppers in Ontario and B.C.?

Two provinces set minimums for platform work, whether or not workers are employees.

- **Ontario:** the [Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers) entitles delivery and courier platform workers to at least the general minimum wage, assessed per work assignment or per pay period, not counting tips. Ontario's general minimum wage is $17.95 an hour from October 1, 2026 ([Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/minimum-wage)).
- **British Columbia:** online platform workers get at least $21.89 per hour of engaged time from June 1, 2026, plus at least $0.35 per km for delivery using transport other than walking ([B.C.](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage)). Engaged time runs from accepting an order to completing it. Instacart's [B.C. update](https://company.instacart.com/shopper-community/changes-to-instacart-in-british-columbia) describes how it applied these rules from September 3, 2024.

### Illustrative example

Assumed numbers, using B.C.'s published floor: a B.C. shopper accepts a batch, and the engaged time is 45 minutes with 12 km driven.

- Wage floor: 0.75 hours × $21.89 = $16.42
- Distance allowance: 12 km × $0.35 = $4.20
- Minimum for that engaged time, before tips: $16.42 + $4.20 = $20.62

This is a floor, not a prediction of what a batch pays, and waiting time between batches is not engaged time.

## How do Instacart payouts work in Canada?

Instacart pays weekly by direct deposit, between Wednesday and Friday, for work done the previous Monday to Sunday. For faster access, instant cashout costs $0.50; Instacart says you can cash out batch earnings minutes after a delivery and full earnings including tip two hours after delivery ([Instacart](https://company.instacart.ca/shoppers/shopper-earnings)).

## What are the pros and cons of Instacart for Canadians?

**Pros**
- See batch pay and tip before accepting; no penalty for declining.
- 100% of tips, with tip protection up to $10.
- Legal pay floors in Ontario and B.C.
- Weekly deposits, or $0.50 instant cashout.

**Cons**
- Requires a car and heavy lifting.
- Fuel, wear, and parking are your costs.
- Earnings vary by batch, store and time; peak-time tools are not offered in Ontario or B.C.
- Substitutions and out-of-stock items add customer-service work.

## What mistakes do new Instacart shoppers make?

- **Accepting heavy, far batches without checking the full payout.** Look at distance, items and units together.
- **Not tracking kilometres.** Your vehicle costs are deductible only with a log.
- **Ignoring insurance.** Ask your insurer whether delivery use is covered.
- **Rushing substitutions.** Message customers; tips depend on it.
- **Paying the cashout fee every day.** Weekly deposits are free.

## How is Instacart income taxed in Canada?

Instacart shoppers are independent contractors, so earnings are self-employment income on Form T2125, with the business share of vehicle costs deductible. Delivery is subject to the normal $30,000 GST/HST small-supplier test, unlike rideshare ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)).
