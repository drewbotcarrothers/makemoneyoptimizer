Charging for freelance writing in Canada works best as a per-project price built from the hours a piece really takes (research, drafting, and revisions) at a billable rate, then expressed per word or as a monthly retainer if the client prefers. Job Bank lists employed authors and writers at $25.48 (low), $36.81 (median), and $53.00 (high) an hour nationally. A freelancer prices above that employee wage because pitching, admin, software, and both halves of CPP are unpaid. This guide suits writers who will time a few pieces before publishing a rate.

## What do writers earn per hour in Canada?

Employed writers in Canada earn a median of $36.81 an hour, according to [Job Bank's writer wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/20925/ca) (NOC 51111, authors and writers except technical, reference period 2023–2024, updated November 19, 2025). The same NOC covers copywriters. Those are employee wages; a freelance rate should sit above them.

## Which pricing models work for freelance writing in Canada?

Freelance writing in Canada is usually priced per project, per word, hourly, or on a monthly retainer for a set number of pieces. Per-project pricing protects you best because it prices the research, not the length.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per project | One price for a defined piece, with a set number of revision rounds | Blog posts, case studies, web pages | Scope creep: write the brief into the quote |
| Per word | Rate × final word count | Publications, content agencies | Research-heavy short pieces that underpay |
| Hourly | Rate × hours | Editing, interviews, unclear scope | Clients who want a fixed number |
| Monthly retainer | Set number of pieces per month at a set fee | Ongoing content clients | Unused pieces rolling over forever |
| Add-ons | Interviews, SEO briefs, uploading to the CMS, images | Real extra work | Including them by default |

Name the number of revision rounds and what counts as a new piece. Rights and bylines are part of the price too; say who owns the work and when.

## How do you calculate a freelance writing rate in Canada?

Calculate a freelance writing rate in Canada from a target hourly pay, your billable share, and the hours a piece actually takes from brief to final revision. Divide by word count only after you know the project price.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

Use the Job Bank table as a floor. A freelance target below the employee median rarely survives a year.

### Step 2: Estimate billable share

Pitching, invoicing, and learning a client's style are unpaid. Measure your split over a month.

### Step 3: Time a piece end to end

Research, outline, draft, edit, revision round, and formatting. Time three similar pieces.

### Step 4: Price the project, then convert

Project price = hours × billable rate. Per-word rate = project price ÷ words.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $45 an hour, between Job Bank's national median of $36.81 and high of $53.00. Suppose 65% of your working time is billable. The billable rate is $45 ÷ 0.65 = $69.23 an hour.

Suppose a 1,200-word researched blog post takes 4 hours, including one revision round. The project price is 4 × $69.23 = $276.92, which you might quote as $275. That works out to $275 ÷ 1,200 = about $0.23 a word for this kind of piece.

A 600-word product page that needs a 1-hour interview and takes 3 hours in total is 3 × $69.23 = $207.69, or about $0.35 a word. Same writer, different per-word figure, because research and interviews do not scale with length.

## When should you raise freelance writing prices in Canada?

Raise freelance writing prices in Canada when your calendar is full, when pieces keep taking longer than priced, or when your published samples move you into a better-paying niche. Change new-client rates first and give retainer clients notice before the next term.

A short, specific note works: the new project prices, the date, and what is included. Raising per project type is easier to explain than a percentage.

## What freelance writing pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common freelance writing pricing mistakes in Canada are quoting per word for research-heavy work, offering unlimited revisions, and starting without a deposit. Each turns a profitable piece into an unpaid one.

For the copyright, GST/HST, and tax mistakes writers make most, and how to fix them, see the [freelance writing mistakes guide](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-mistakes-canada/).

Write the brief into the quote. Charge for interviews. Take a deposit from new clients. Do not compare your rate with content mills; you are pricing a different product.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect freelance writing prices in Canada?

Freelance writing income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

When registered, the rate depends on the place of supply, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html).
