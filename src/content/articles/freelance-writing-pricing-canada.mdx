## What do Canadian publications pay freelance writers?

Canadian publications that post their rates pay $0.10 to $1 a word, with business and investigative outlets at the top. Rates were on each publication's page on October 9, 2026.

| Publication | Published rate | Notes |

Magazine work pays well per word but takes pitching, interviews and fact-checking. Time the hours before comparing it with business writing.

## Which pricing models work for freelance writing?

Price business work per project from timed hours, accept per-word rates from publications, and sell retainers to repeat clients.

| Model | How it works | Published example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per project | One price for a defined piece with set revision rounds | Sarah McKenzie: blog post from $400, web page from $350 |
| Per word | Rate × assigned words | The Logic $1; Studio $0.30; Maisonneuve $0.15–$0.20 |
| Hourly | Editing, interviews, unclear scope | Freel: $53–$90 mid-level Toronto |
| Monthly retainer | Set number of pieces a month | Exall Digital: $550 for 4 posts |
| Add-ons | Interviews, SEO briefs, CMS upload, images | Priced separately |

Name the number of revision rounds and what counts as a new piece. Rights and bylines are part of the price too: say who owns the work and when it transfers, often on payment.

## How do you calculate a freelance writing rate?

Build an hourly rate, time a piece end to end, price the project, and only then convert to a per-word figure.

```text
Hourly rate    = (target pay + CPP + software, subscriptions per hour)
                 ÷ share of hours billable
Project price  = hours from brief to final revision × hourly rate
Per-word rate  = project price ÷ words
```

**Illustrative rate build.** Replace each input with your own:

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Target pay | $45/hour (between the $36.81 median and $53.00 high) |
| CPP at 11.9% | $5.36 |
| Software and subscriptions per hour | $2.00 |
| Share of hours billable | 65% (pitching and admin are unpaid) |
| **Rate** | ($45 + $5.36 + $2) ÷ 0.65 = $80.55, **round to $80** |

$80 sits inside Freel's $53 to $90 mid-level Toronto range.

**Illustrative rate sheet** at $80 an hour, before GST/HST:

| Service | Assumed hours | Price | Per word |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blog post, about 1,200 words, 1 revision round | 4 h | $320 | $0.27 |
| Web page, about 600 words, with a short interview | 3 h | $240 | $0.40 |
| Case study, about 1,000 words, with an interview | 6 h | $480 | $0.48 |
| Newsletter issue, about 800 words | 2.5 h | $200 | $0.25 |
| Landing page | 8 h | $640 | — |
| Retainer: 4 blog posts a month (4 × $320 = $1,280, less 5%) | 16 h | $1,215 | — |
| Extra revision round | hourly | $80/hour | — |
| Rush (under 3 business days) | — | +25% | — |

Research and interviews don't scale with length, which is why the same writer's per-word figure runs from $0.25 to $0.48.

### Quote 1: one blog post for an Ontario clinic

- 1,200 words, keyword brief supplied, one revision round: 4 h × $80 = **$320**.
- With 13% HST if you're registered: $361.60.
- Market check: Exall Digital puts independent Ontario freelancers at $150 to $300 per 1,000 words; Sarah McKenzie starts at about $400 per 1,000–1,200.

### Quote 2: monthly content retainer

- Four 1,200-word posts a month: $1,280 less 5% = **$1,215 a month**, $14,580 a year.
- Unused posts expire at month end; a fifth post is $320.
- Market check: Exall Digital's four-post package is $550; Sarah McKenzie's retainers start at $1,500.

### Quote 3: magazine feature versus business post

- A 1,500-word feature for Studio Magazine at $0.30 a word pays **$450**. If pitching, two interviews and drafting take an assumed 10 hours, that's $45 an hour.
- The same 1,500 words at The Logic's $1 a word pays **$1,500**, or $150 an hour over the same 10 hours.
- Business check: the $80 rate gives $800 for 10 hours, so a lower-paying magazine is a portfolio choice, not an income one.

### Quote 4: landing page and email sequence for a B.C. startup

- Landing page 8 h ($640) + three launch emails at 1.5 h each (4.5 h × $80 = $360) = **$1,000**.
- With 5% GST if registered: $1,050. Half up front.
- Market check: Freel puts a Toronto mid-level landing page at $800 to $3,000.

## How should you write a freelance writing quote and invoice?

Write the brief into the quote: words, sources, interviews, revision rounds, delivery format and rights.

```text
WRITING QUOTE — [client]                         Date: [date]
Piece: blog post, "[working title]", about 1,200 words
Includes: research from your brief + up to 5 public sources,
  1 outline approval, 1 draft, 1 revision round, Google Doc delivery
Price: $320 (before GST/HST)
Not included: interviews ($80/hour), images, CMS upload,
  a second revision round ($80/hour), rush (+25%)
Deposit: 50% ($160) for new clients, balance on delivery
Rights: you own the final text once paid in full.
  I may show the published piece in my portfolio.
Timeline: draft within 7 business days of the deposit
Prices before GST/HST [or: + HST 13%, # ____]
```

```text
INVOICE #2026-047   Date: [date]
Content retainer, October (4 blog posts) ......... $1,215.00
Extra revision round, 1 h × $80 .................. $80.00
Subtotal ......................................... $1,295.00
HST 13% (registered only) ........................ $168.35
Total ............................................ $1,463.35
Payment by e-Transfer within 15 days.
```

## How do you raise freelance writing prices?

Raise prices when your calendar is full, when pieces keep taking longer than priced, or when published samples move you into a better-paying niche.

1. **Raise new-client rates first.** Existing retainers get notice before the next term.
2. **Raise per project type.** "$320 to $360 per post" is easier to explain than a percentage.
3. **Move into a niche.** The Article Room puts specialized writing at $0.50 to $1.00+ a word, against $0.20 to $0.50 for mid-level.
4. **Price interviews and revisions.** They're the hours that don't scale with word count.
5. **Use published ceilings.** Sarah McKenzie's $400 post and Freel's $90 mid-level rate show where the next step sits.
6. **Send the notice:**

```text
Hi [Name], from [January 1] my blog post rate moves from $[320] to
$[360], and the four-post retainer from $[1,215] to $[1,370]. Briefs
booked before then keep the current price. — [Your name]
```

For notice timing and scripts, see [how to raise your side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/), and use [the first-year price review](/side-hustles/first-year-side-hustle-price-review-canada/) to pick the new number.

## What freelance writing pricing mistakes are common?

- **Per word for research-heavy work.** A 600-word page with an interview took 3 hours above, $0.40 a word.
- **Unlimited revisions.** Name the rounds and price extras hourly.
- **No deposit.** Take 50% from new clients.
- **Comparing with content mills.** You're pricing a different product.
- **Ignoring rights.** Say when ownership transfers.

For the copyright, GST/HST and tax mistakes writers make most, see the [freelance writing mistakes guide](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada/#what-to-watch-out-for).

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect freelance writing prices?

Freelance writing income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) outside Quebec. GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

When registered, the rate depends on the place of supply, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers when voluntary registration makes sense, the [home office guide](/side-hustles/home-office-expenses-side-hustle-canada/) covers workspace deductions, and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) covers what to hold back from each payment.

This is not tax, legal, or accounting advice.
