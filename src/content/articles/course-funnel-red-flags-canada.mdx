<QuickAnswer>

- **A course can be a real product.** The funnel is the sales page that sells the course with income screenshots, a countdown, and a second purchase before you have used the first one.
- **Income claims have rules.** The Competition Bureau says a performance claim needs proof, and a testimonial is not that proof. A quote also needs the person's prior written approval.
- **Recruit-to-earn is a different rule.** If you are paid for signing other people into the same plan, multi-level marketing disclosure rules apply, and pyramid selling is a criminal offence.
- **The legal version of teaching** is [selling an online course you actually build](/side-hustles/online-course-side-hustle-canada/).

</QuickAnswer>

A side hustle course funnel in Canada is a sales sequence that sells a method, then a more expensive method, using income claims the buyer cannot check. The topics repeat: drop-servicing, a pre-built niche website, an "AI agency," or a turnkey vending route or ATM. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations-and-deceptive-marketing-practices) says any representation that is false or misleading in a material way can fall under the Competition Act. A claim is material if it could influence someone to buy. Courts look at the general impression, not only the footnote.

This is general information, not legal advice. Building a course from a skill you have is a different job, covered in [how to sell an online course](/side-hustles/online-course-side-hustle-canada/). Offers that ask you to pay before you can withdraw are [side hustle scams](/side-hustles/side-hustle-scams-canada/).

## What does a side hustle course funnel look like in Canada?

A course funnel looks like a free training, a cheap starter module, and then a high-ticket call. The free video spends most of its time on the seller's results. The checkout page adds a bonus that disappears when a timer hits zero. None of that is automatically illegal. It becomes a problem when the general impression is "buy this and the income follows," and the seller cannot show that typical buyers get that result.

| What you see | Ordinary course | Funnel to slow down on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The promise | A skill, with hours attached | A monthly income, often "guaranteed" or "done for you" |
| Proof | A syllabus and a sample lesson | Screenshots, rented cars, and student quotes with no dates |
| The next charge | You choose a second product later | The call's job is to sell a larger package before you finish the first |
| Other people | You do the work | You are paid more if you recruit buyers |

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre's [job scam page](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/scams-fraudes/job-emploi-eng.htm) is about a related script: paying to unlock earnings, or being asked to recruit. A course that never asks for a deposit can still be a misleading ad. Use both tests.

## Which income claims does the Competition Bureau treat as misleading?

The Bureau's deceptive-marketing page says not to make a performance claim unless you can prove it, and that testimonials usually do not amount to adequate proof. Its page on [tests and testimonials](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/use-tests-or-testimonials) says section 74.02 of the Competition Act restricts publishing a testimonial unless the person has already published it or has approved it in writing, and the published version has to match what they approved. A quote pulled out of context can breach that rule.

Read an income claim with three questions:

1. **Is the number the seller's revenue, or profit after ads, software, and the course fee?** A screenshot of a Stripe balance is not profit.
2. **Is it one person, or what a typical buyer made?** One outlier is not a forecast.
3. **Would the page still make sense if the dollar figures were deleted?** If the only reason to buy is the dollar figure, you are buying the claim.

"Results not typical" in small type does not fix a headline that says everyone replaces a job. The Bureau says the general impression counts.

### Illustrative example

This sketch shows how a headline and a footnote can describe different numbers. It is not a real course, and it is not a finding against any seller.

Suppose a page says students make $5,000 a month. Suppose the only document behind it is one person's sales total of $5,000 before ad spend of $3,200 and a software bill of $400. The amount left in that sketch is $5,000 − $3,200 − $400 = $1,400, and that $1,400 is still one person's month, not a class average. The $5,000 headline is the seller's sentence. It is not a wage, and it is not what you should expect. If the seller will not show how the $5,000 was counted, do not pay to find out.

## When does a course become a multi-level or pyramid pitch?

A multi-level marketing plan, in the Competition Bureau's [MLM and pyramid page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/multi-level-marketing-and-pyramid-selling), is a legal way to sell goods or services in which a participant is paid for supplying a product to another participant, who is then paid for supplying a product further along. If anyone makes a claim about what you could earn, the operator or participant has to give a fair, reasonable, and timely disclosure of what typical participants actually receive, or are likely to receive. The Bureau's [enforcement guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/publications/multi-level-marketing-plans-and-schemes-pyramid-selling) says a compensation representation includes lifestyle photos and testimonials about quitting a job, not only a dollar range.

Pyramid selling is illegal. The same Bureau page says it is a criminal offence to establish, operate, advertise, or promote a pyramid scheme. The markers it lists include compensation for recruitment, required purchases as a condition of joining (other than a starter kit sold at cost), inventory you cannot resell or use in a reasonable time, and no buy-back on reasonable commercial terms.

A course that simply teaches a skill is not an MLM. A course that pays you for bringing in the next buyer, or that makes you stock product you cannot sell, needs the MLM and pyramid reading before you pay.

## How do drop-servicing, niche-site, AI-agency, and vending pitches fail the same test?

Four labels show up in Canadian side-hustle ads. The work behind each label can be real. The funnel version skips the part that takes time.

### Drop-servicing

Drop-servicing means you sell a service and pay a freelancer to deliver it. The honest version is client-finding, contracts, and quality control. The funnel version says the clients and the freelancers are already lined up once you buy the template. You still have to find a buyer who pays more than the freelancer costs. [Overhyped side hustles](/side-hustles/overhyped-side-hustles-canada/) is the wider frame for pitches that skip the hours.

### Pre-built niche sites

A pre-built site is a domain, a theme, and a stack of articles someone else wrote. Buying it does not buy traffic. Hosting, a domain, and months of publishing are the actual costs, which is the subject of [SEO blogging](/side-hustles/seo-blogging-side-hustle-canada/) and [digital products, honestly](/side-hustles/digital-products-reality-canada/). A page that says the site is already earning should show the analytics, the expenses, and what happens to those earnings after the sale. If it will not, you are buying a story.

### AI agencies

An AI agency is freelance work: a client pays you to set up a tool and you remain responsible for the result. A funnel that says the agency runs itself, or that you can sell "AI" without a named service, is selling the label. Paid project work on existing platforms is a different comparison in [AI training vs paid studies vs freelancing](/side-hustles/ai-training-vs-studies-vs-freelancing-canada/).

### Turnkey vending and ATMs

A turnkey vending route or a white-label ATM is a machine, a location agreement, and restocking or cash handling. The red flag is an upfront fee for "guaranteed locations" you cannot visit before you pay. The operating guides are [vending machines](/side-hustles/vending-machine-side-hustle-canada/) and [ATMs](/side-hustles/atm-machine-side-hustle-canada/). [Dropshipping](/side-hustles/dropshipping-side-hustle-canada/) has the same shape online: a store is not income until someone buys.

## What should you do before you pay for a side hustle course in Canada?

Slow the purchase down until the page survives a plain reading.

### Step 1: Write the promise in one sentence

If you cannot state what you will be able to do on Monday without using the seller's income number, the product is the feeling, not a skill.

### Step 2: Ask for the typical result in writing

For an ordinary course, ask what a buyer can finish. For anything that pays you to recruit, the Bureau's MLM rules are about typical compensation, disclosed in a way that is fair, reasonable, and timely. A screen recording of the seller's own account is not that disclosure.

### Step 3: Price the second purchase before the first one

Many funnels are cheap until the sales call. Decide the most you will spend, including the "implementation" package, before the call. A timer is not a cost.

### Step 4: Check whether you are the customer or the next recruiter

If the higher tier pays you for enrolments, stop and read the pyramid markers above. The CAFC job page also flags schemes that pay you to recruit.

### Step 5: Keep the receipt and the page

Save the sales page, the checkout, and the emails. If the claim was false, those files are what the [reporting guide](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-scam-canada/) tells you to keep for the Competition Bureau and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
