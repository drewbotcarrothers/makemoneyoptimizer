Holiday gift wrapping in Canada is paid work wrapping other people's presents, either at a booth, at a pop-up, or at a kitchen table you control. The work suits people with neat hands, a short December calendar, and the patience to price paper and ribbon as supplies instead of as decorations you give away.

A booth is a rent problem. A kitchen is a zoning problem if clients come to your home. People who would rather sell a file than fold paper can look at [printables](/side-hustles/printables-side-hustle-canada/). People who want a winter route outdoors can look at the [winter side hustles roundup](/side-hustles/best-winter-side-hustles-canada/) and at [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/).

## How does a gift wrapping side hustle work in Canada?

A gift wrapping side hustle in Canada sells time and materials around a present the client already bought. You do not need a store. You need a surface, paper you can cut square, a price per gift or per session, and a plan for where the work happens. There is no national wrapping fee on this site.

| Path | Who controls the hours | What you must confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mall or store kiosk | The property manager or retailer | A written licence or lease, their insurance ask, and their hours |
| Pop-up at a market, office, or party | The organizer, then you | Table fee, power, and whether you may sell paper or only labour |
| At the client's home | You, for that appointment | Parking, pets, and how many gifts fit in the time |
| At your home | You, if the dwelling allows it | Zoning or a home-based licence where the city requires one |

Mall management does not publish one Canada-wide kiosk application. Ask the property in writing. A verbal "you can have the corner on Saturdays" is how a booth disappears on the first weekend of December. Keep the email.

## Who is holiday gift wrapping a fit for in Canada?

Holiday gift wrapping in Canada fits someone who can stand or sit for a shift, keep a queue calm, and finish a corner that does not pop open in a car. It is a poor fit if you dislike December crowds, if you cannot say no to a fragile item you do not know how to pack, or if you need income in February. The peak is the two weeks before Christmas. Practise in November on your own parcels so the first paying gift is not your first corner.

The work pairs with a [house cleaning](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-side-hustle-canada/) client who asks for help before guests arrive, if you price wrapping separately from the clean. It is not a substitute for a seasonal retail job. Those shifts are covered in [seasonal holiday jobs](/side-hustles/seasonal-holiday-jobs-canada/).

## How do mall kiosks, pop-ups, and at-home wrapping differ in Canada?

Mall kiosks, pop-ups, and at-home wrapping in Canada are three different rents. The mall charges for the spot. The pop-up charges a vendor fee or nothing, depending on the host. Your home charges you in rules: who may visit, how much of the dwelling the work may use, and whether your lease allows a business.

### Step 1: Ask the property before you buy a booth kit

Email mall or store management with the dates, the footprint, and whether you need power. Ask what insurance they require and what the fee is. Write their answer into your price. This guide does not state a mall fee, because the lease is private and it changes by property. If they require a commercial liability certificate, get the quote before you pay a deposit. The Insurance Bureau of Canada says commercial liability insurance would generally cover amounts you are legally obligated to pay for bodily injury or property damage to others, and that personal liability on a homeowner's policy would typically not cover those situations. A pair of scissors and a cord across a corridor are the risks to describe. This is not insurance advice.

### Step 2: Treat a pop-up like a short contract

A craft market, an office lobby, or a party host is the client for the space. Ask the hours, who keeps the wrapping revenue, and whether you may bring only labour while they supply paper. A company party that wants you on site for three hours is a session price, not a per-gift price you hope will add up. Get the session fee in writing, including what happens if the party ends early.

### Step 3: If the work is in your home, read that city's home-business page

Rules are municipal. They are examples for that city, not a Canadian standard.

The City of Vancouver says a home-based business may need a [home-based business licence](https://vancouver.ca/doing-business/home-based-business.aspx). Its [FAQ](https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/home-based-business-web-faq.pdf) says a business licence is required to operate a home-based business, that the business must stay within the home-based rules, and that business insurance is not required to apply for the licence. The same FAQ says you may still want insurance, especially if customers come to your home, because home insurance policies typically do not cover commercial activities. Vancouver's home-based page says the business can occupy up to 33.3 percent of the total gross floor area or 46 square metres, whichever is less, and that up to two non-resident employees are permitted at any one time. Wrapping paper stacked through a living room can fail that area test. Confirm the live pages before you invite clients over. Those limits are Vancouver's.

Toronto's [business regulations page](https://www.toronto.ca/business-economy/new-businesses-startups/business-regulations/) says someone who operates from home should confirm with Toronto Building whether zoning allows the business. Toronto does not, on that page, list gift wrapping as a licensed category with a published fee. Search before you assume you need a municipal licence, and search before you assume you do not. This guide does not copy a fee from another business type onto wrapping.

Wrapping at the client's kitchen avoids a home-based licence question in your unit. You still need a way to carry paper, and your tenant insurance or home policy may not cover supplies in the car. IBC says equipment away from home, and goods stolen from a vehicle, are not what a typical homeowner's policy or personal auto policy is for. Ask a broker.

### Step 4: Keep other people's gifts boring and documented

Write the client's name on a tag before you start. Photograph nothing that shows a card number, a cheque, or an address list. You are holding someone else's property. A lost gift is your problem to explain. Decline cash inside an envelope unless the client wraps that envelope themselves and you only add paper on the outside. Do not open a sealed box to "see what it is" if the client can tell you the shape.

### Step 5: Set a last drop-off time

December 24 is a trap if you also have family plans. Publish a last drop-off that you can finish. A mall that requires you to stay until close can override that plan. Read the lease. For gifts the client will mail, point them at Canada Post's own tools rather than promising a delivery date. Shipping cutoffs are covered in the [holiday Etsy and craft-market guide](/side-hustles/holiday-etsy-craft-market-shop-canada/).

## How should you price gift wrapping in Canada?

Price gift wrapping in Canada from minutes per gift, the paper you supply, the place you work, and the awkward items you refuse or surcharge. A flat "any gift" price loses money on a bicycle-shaped box and looks expensive on a book.

| Factor | How it changes the job |
| --- | --- |
| Size and shape | A book is one sheet. A stroller is a bag and more time |
| Your paper or theirs | Their paper removes your materials line and can add fuss |
| Ribbon and tags | Cheap to you, visible to them; put it in the price or leave it out |
| Volume | A household of twenty gifts is one trip. Twenty strangers are a queue |
| Location | Mall rent, a vendor fee, or bus fare to their kitchen |
| Fragile or high-value items | More time, and a written note that you are not an insurer of the contents |
| Rush dates | December 23 is a different product from December 10 |

Write a short menu: small, medium, awkward, and "we do not wrap." Awkward can mean a basket, a wheel, or anything that does not accept a rectangular fold. "We do not wrap" should include items you will not be responsible for, such as an unboxed television or a pet.

## What does an illustrative wrapping price in Canada look like?

An illustrative wrapping price in Canada is assumed minutes and an assumed materials cost. It is not a quote, a survey, or a mall's rate.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose you choose $36 an hour as your labour target. That number is yours to replace. Suppose a rectangular gift takes 8 minutes, which is 8/60 of an hour. Labour is (8/60) × $36 = $4.80. Suppose paper, ribbon, and a tag cost you $1.20 on that gift. The sketch price is $4.80 + $1.20 = $6.00 before tax.

Suppose one household books 24 gifts at that sketch. Revenue is 24 × $6.00 = $144. Time is 24 × 8 minutes = 192 minutes, or 3.2 hours. Labour in the sketch is 3.2 × $36 = $115.20. Materials are 24 × $1.20 = $28.80. The $144 covers both, before travel and before tax. If the bus is $6 return, the sketch is $138 after that fare, and $138 / 3.2 hours = $43.13 an hour only because the materials were marked up inside the $6. Change the $1.20 and the hour changes. These figures are assumptions.

Suppose a booth day has a space fee you assume at $250. That $250 is not a mall's published price. It is a stand-in so you can see the arithmetic. At $6 a gift, $250 / $6 = 41.67, so 42 gifts cover the assumed fee before you pay yourself. Forty-two gifts at 8 minutes each are 336 minutes, or 5.6 hours, on top of setup. If only 20 gifts arrive, 20 × $6 = $120, which is $130 under the assumed $250 fee. The sketch loses money before your time. A quiet Saturday is why you ask the property for the fee in writing before you print a sign.

Suppose an office books you for 3 hours at the same $36 target, supplies its own paper, and pays a session instead of a per-gift price. Labour is 3 × $36 = $108 before tax, before your travel. If they also ask you to bring paper, add the rolls you actually buy. Do not copy $6 or $108. Time ten of your own gifts before you believe the 8 minutes.

## What should a wrapping agreement include in Canada?

A wrapping agreement in Canada names the date, the place, who supplies paper, the price, when it is due, and what you will not wrap. For a booth, attach the property's rules. For a home visit, name the address and the maximum number of gifts in the booked time. Extra gifts are a second visit or a higher price, not a surprise hour.

Take a deposit for a party booking so a cancellation still covers paper you cut to their colours. Say whether the deposit is kept. Inventing that rule on December 20 is how the review goes badly. You are not a courier. If they need the gifts mailed, that is a separate errand with Canada Post's rates, not a promise inside the wrap price.

## What mistakes do new wrappers make in Canada?

Common gift-wrapping mistakes in Canada are buying a month of paper before a booth is confirmed, pricing every gift the same, and treating mall foot traffic as a forecast. Traffic is the mall's, not a count of people who will stop. Another mistake is wrapping at a dining table in a city that requires a home-based licence or a zoning check, then finding out when a neighbour complains.

Foil paper, huge bows, and a price copied from a United States video are the craft mistakes. Practise a square corner on kraft paper. Put the blade away from the queue. Tape a cord down or route it outside the walkway. IBC's point about liability is the reason, not a decoration tip.

Do not store clients' unwrapped gifts overnight unless the note says you will, and unless you can explain the loss. A car trunk is a poor vault. IBC says a personal auto policy is not the cover for business supplies stolen from a vehicle. Ask before you rely on it.

## Which tax rules apply to gift wrapping in Canada?

Gift wrapping fees in Canada are self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. A December booth plus other hustles can share that pot. Under the test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. If you are registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Confirm it on the [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) page and with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). If you are not registered, do not add GST/HST.

A business number is how the CRA identifies a business account. The CRA's [registering your business](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html) page is the starting point when you need one, including when you register for GST/HST. Using a trade name other than your own is a provincial registry question. This guide does not state a registry fee.

Keep paper receipts and the booth lease. A cash Saturday still belongs on the form.
