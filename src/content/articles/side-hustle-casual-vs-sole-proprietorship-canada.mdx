<QuickAnswer>

- **"Casual" isn't a tax category.** If you carry on an activity for profit, the CRA treats it as a business, you're a sole proprietor by default, and you report it on Form T2125 from the first dollar.
- **No filing creates a sole proprietorship.** Trading under your own legal name usually needs no registration. A trade name costs $60 to register in Ontario and $70 in BC ($30 name request + $40 registration), checked October 9, 2026.
- **CPP:** self-employed people pay 11.9% (both halves) on net earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026, up to $8,460.90.
- **GST/HST:** register once taxable sales pass $30,000 in one calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your businesses.
- **Selling your own used things now and then** isn't a business. Buying things to resell for profit is.

</QuickAnswer>

In Canada, there is no separate "casual income" status for a side hustle. The CRA's business guide defines a business as an activity you intend to carry on for profit, with evidence to support that intention ([CRA T4002](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002.html)). Once that's true, you're a sole proprietor, whether you earned $300 or $30,000, and you report the income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). What changes as a side hustle grows isn't your legal form. It's the list of obligations that switch on at certain points: a business name, CPP, GST/HST, tax instalments, licences and insurance. All government pages below were checked on October 9, 2026.

The step-by-step paperwork is in [registering a sole proprietorship](/side-hustles/register-sole-proprietorship-canada/) and [reporting side hustle income to the CRA](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/). For selling on marketplaces, see [tax on reselling, gig apps and platform income](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).

## What is the difference between casual side income and a sole proprietorship?

There's no tax difference. "Casual" describes how often you work, not how the income is taxed. If you're earning for profit, you're already a sole proprietor. The real comparison is between personal sales that aren't a business, a sole proprietorship under your own name, and one with a registered trade name. Incorporation is shown for comparison.

## Is a Canadian side hustle a hobby, casual income or a business?

The tax question is whether you're carrying on a business. T4002 says a business includes a profession, a calling, a trade, an undertaking of any kind and an adventure or concern in the nature of trade ([CRA T4002](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002.html)). Charging clients for dog walks, cleaning, design work or deliveries fits easily.

What sits outside it? Selling your own used belongings now and then, or a genuine pastime with no commercial intent. The line for resale is covered in [tax on reselling and platform income](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).

One more check: if a single client controls when, where and how you work and supplies your tools, you may be an employee, not a business. The CRA's [RC4110](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4110/employee-self-employed.html) looks at intent, control, tools and equipment, and the chance of profit or risk of loss. The facts of the work decide your status, not the word "contract".

## What does it cost to register a sole proprietorship in Canada?

A sole proprietorship under your own legal name usually costs nothing to set up. Registering a trade name has a provincial fee. Two examples, checked October 9, 2026:

Other provinces set their own rules and fees. A provincial name registration doesn't register you with the CRA. A business number comes when you register for GST/HST or a payroll account. See [getting a CRA business number](/side-hustles/how-to-get-cra-business-number-canada/).

## What changes as your Canadian side hustle grows?

Think of it as a checklist that fills in over time rather than one big switch.

### Step 1: Start records on day one

The CRA requires you to keep records that support your income and expenses ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records.html)). A spreadsheet and a folder of receipts is enough to begin. See [record keeping for side hustles](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).

### Step 2: Decide on a name

Trading under your exact legal name usually needs no registration. A trade name like "Northside Dog Walking" usually does, with the provincial registry: $60 in Ontario, $70 in BC.

### Step 3: Separate the money

A separate account isn't required for a sole proprietor, but it makes records and tax savings much easier. See [separating business and personal money](/side-hustles/separate-business-personal-money-canada/).

### Step 4: Watch the GST/HST threshold

Track taxable sales by calendar quarter. The CRA's [when to register page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) sets two tests, counting all your businesses:

- **Over $30,000 in a single calendar quarter:** you charge GST/HST on the sale that takes you over, and must register.
- **Over $30,000 across four consecutive quarters** (but not in one): you stop being a small supplier at the end of the month after the quarter in which you went over, and must register within 29 days.

Ride-hail drivers are the exception. They must register from their first fare.

### Step 5: Budget for CPP

Self-employed people pay both the employee and employer CPP contributions. The CRA's [2026 rates table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/payroll-deductions-contributions/canada-pension-plan-cpp/cpp-contribution-rates-maximums-exemptions.html) lists 5.95% each on pensionable earnings between the $3,500 basic exemption and the $74,600 maximum, so up to $8,460.90 in total for self-employed people. Earnings between $74,600 and $85,000 also attract a second additional contribution, at 8% for the self-employed ([CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html)). If you also have a job, the basic exemption and the maximum are shared across both, and your employment contributions count toward the same maximum.

### Step 6: Add licences and insurance as the work changes

City licences and liability insurance depend on the work, not your income. See [side hustle insurance](/side-hustles/side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

## What does the change look like with real numbers?

**Illustrative. The incomes, expenses and quarterly sales below are assumptions to show how the rules work, not typical results. Each example assumes a full calendar year in 2026. Verify the arithmetic for your own return.**

**Example 1: a small first year.** You tutor two students a week under your own name and earn $3,200, spending $400 on materials. You're already a sole proprietor: you report $3,200 of income and $400 of expenses on T2125. Tutoring is your only work, so your net of $3,200 − $400 = $2,800 is below the $3,500 CPP basic exemption, and you owe no CPP. You're nowhere near $30,000, so no GST/HST.

**Example 2: the same hustle, bigger.** In year two you call the business "Bright Path Tutoring", register the name in Ontario for $60, and earn $14,000 with $1,500 of expenses. Net is $12,500. CPP is ($12,500 − $3,500) × 11.9% = $1,071.00, on top of income tax.

**Example 3: a side hustle beside a job.** You earn $40,000 from a job and $10,000 net from freelancing. Your job already used the $3,500 basic exemption, so CPP on the side income is $10,000 × 11.9% = $1,190.00. Your employer has withheld your employee CPP on the job, but nothing is withheld on the side income.

**Example 4: crossing $30,000 over four quarters.** Your taxable sales are $6,000 (January to March), $7,500 (April to June), $8,000 (July to September) and $9,000 (October to December), for $30,500 across four quarters. No single quarter went over $30,000, so you stop being a small supplier at the end of January, the month after the quarter in which you went over. You must register and start charging GST/HST from your effective date of registration.

## What mistakes do people make with casual side income in Canada?

- **Not reporting because it's "just casual."** Business income is reportable from the first dollar.
- **Registering a name and thinking the tax side is done.** A provincial name registration doesn't register you for GST/HST or file anything with the CRA.
- **Forgetting CPP.** It's calculated on your return, so it can surprise people who only budgeted for income tax.
- **Counting only one hustle for GST/HST.** The $30,000 test covers all your businesses.
- **Watching only the yearly total.** The $30,000 test runs over any four consecutive quarters, not the calendar year.
- **Calling an employee relationship a contract.** The facts of the work decide status, not the label.

## How is a growing side hustle taxed in Canada?

Your net profit from T2125 is added to your other income and taxed at your marginal rate, plus self-employed CPP. Nothing is withheld, so set aside a share of every payment. See [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/). If you run two different hustles, the CRA asks for a separate T2125 for each. See [tracking which side hustles pay](/side-hustles/track-which-side-hustles-pay-canada/).
