A firewood side hustle in Canada buys seasoned wood from a local yard and resells it as delivered half-cords, stacked cords or small bundles, or sells delivery and stacking as a service. The rules that matter are federal: Measurement Canada sets the legal units, and the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) restricts moving firewood out of pest-regulated areas. Canadian yards listed a full cord of seasoned hardwood at about $410 to $925 on October 19, 2026, depending on the region and the species.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Legal units:** bulk firewood is sold by the stacked cubic metre, the cubic foot, or the cord (128 cubic feet) and fractions of a cord. Measurement Canada calls "face cord" and "stove cord" illegal terms ([Selling firewood](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/measurement-canada/en/selling-measured-goods/selling-firewood)).
- **Bagged wood:** bags, boxes and strapped bundles must show the product name, the responsible seller's name and address, and the quantity.
- **Pest rules:** moving firewood out of a CFIA-regulated area is prohibited without authorization, with penalties of up to $50,000 under the Plant Protection Act ([CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/plant-health/forestry/hazards-moving-firewood)).
- **What yards charge (full cord):** $410 delivered hardwood (Yarmouth, NS), $440 to $500 by zone (SAM, Halifax), $552 bush cord (Seasoned Firewood, Ontario), $600 for 3 skids (Fairwood, Ontario), $925 birch (TMH, Calgary).
- **Bundles:** SAM Firewood lists 1-cubic-foot bags at $10.

</QuickAnswer>

## What units can you legally sell firewood in, in Canada?

In Canada you can sell bulk firewood by the stacked cubic metre, the cubic foot, or the cord of 128 cubic feet, including fractions such as half or a quarter of a cord. Measurement Canada's [selling firewood](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/measurement-canada/en/selling-measured-goods/selling-firewood) page recommends the stacked cubic metre, and says terms such as "face cord" or "stove cord" are illegal because sellers use them for quantities smaller than 128 cubic feet.

That matters because many yards still use those words. Fairwood's own FAQ prices "a face cord" at $200 a skid, and describes a skid as about a third of a full cord, roughly 37 cubic feet. If you resell, write every quantity in cubic feet or as a fraction of a 128-cubic-foot cord, so the buyer can check it.

**How to measure a stack**, from Measurement Canada's [measuring page](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/measurement-canada/en/buying-and-selling-measured-goods/measuring-bulk-firewood):

- In inches: length × height × width ÷ 1,728 = cubic feet; ÷ 128 = cords. Their example: 96 × 48 × 12 inches = 55,296 cubic inches = 32 cubic feet = a quarter cord.
- In centimetres: length × height × width ÷ 1,000,000 = cubic metres; ÷ 3.6 = cords.

A buyer is told by Measurement Canada's [buying page](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/measurement-canada/en/consumers/buying-measured-goods/buying-firewood) to be present, measure the stack before using any, and ask for a receipt showing the quantity and type of wood, the seller's name, address and phone number, and the price. Give that receipt without being asked.

**Bagged, boxed or strapped wood** sold at retail needs the product name, the responsible seller's name and address, and the quantity on the package (or on a hang tag for strapped wood). The prepackaged quantity is shown in cubic decimetres, and cubic feet may be added. One cubic foot is about 28.3 dm³.

## Which CFIA pest rules apply to selling firewood?

The CFIA prohibits moving firewood out of a regulated area, and says moving firewood from places where regulated pests have been found can violate the Plant Protection Act, with penalties of up to $50,000 and/or prosecution. Its [hazards of moving firewood](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/plant-health/forestry/hazards-moving-firewood) page lists the pests by province, including emerald ash borer in Manitoba, Ontario, Quebec, New Brunswick and Nova Scotia, and says Alberta is free of federally regulated pests but prohibits moving elm wood under provincial law.

The page allows three cases:

- moving firewood within a regulated area;
- moving firewood within non-regulated areas, as long as you do not pass through a regulated area;
- moving firewood out of a regulated area with a CFIA movement certificate for heat-treated firewood, or under a compliance agreement.

For a side hustle this means: buy from a yard near your customers, ask where the wood was cut (the CFIA suggests less than 80 km from where it is sold), and do not haul a load from your cottage region to the city. Check the regulated-area map for your province on the CFIA page before your first delivery.

## What can a firewood side hustle sell?

A firewood side hustle can sell small bundles, delivered fractions of a cord, stacking, or a winter refill route. These ideas suit a one-truck or one-trailer operator:

1. **1-cubic-foot bundles for campfires and patios.** Matches SAM's $10 bag as a price anchor.
2. **Kindling bags.** SAM lists kindling at $10 per cubic foot.
3. **Half-cord deliveries to townhouses.** Many buyers have no room for a full cord.
4. **Stacking as an add-on.** The buyer's yard, your labour, measured afterwards.
5. **Monthly refill route.** The same houses every three or four weeks through February.
6. **Cottage-season pickup orders.** Sold locally only; respect the CFIA rule above.
7. **Restaurant and pizza oven supply.** Ask about species and length first.
8. **Short-length wood for small stoves.** SAM charges $30 extra per cord for 14-inch and $60 for 12-inch.
9. **Bundles for convenience stores.** Retail labels must show name, address and quantity.
10. **Seniors' delivery and carry-in.** Pair with [tech help for seniors](/side-hustles/tech-help-for-seniors-side-hustle-canada/) or snow clearing.

## Illustrative example: one cord as bundles versus a delivered half cord

This is an illustrative example. The cord price and the $10 bag price are sourced from the yards above. The bag count per cord, bag cost, delivery fee and your hours are assumptions.

**Option A: bundles.**

- Sourced: bush cord at $550 including HST (Connell's).
- Assumed: 110 one-cubic-foot bags from a 128-cubic-foot cord, because loose wood in a bag packs less tightly than a stack, and $0.75 a bag for the bag and label.
- Revenue at the $10 SAM bag price: 110 × $10 = $1,100.
- Costs: $550 + 110 × $0.75 = $550 + $82.50 = $632.50.
- Left before fuel and time: $1,100 − $632.50 = $467.50.
- Assumed 8 hours to bag, label and sell: $467.50 ÷ 8 = about $58 an hour before fuel.

**Option B: delivered half cords.**

- Assumed trade price: $250 a half cord from the yard (below its $340 retail half bush cord).
- Assumed sale: $320 delivered and stacked, under the $340 retail half bush cord above.
- Left: $320 − $250 = $70 a delivery, before fuel. At an assumed 2 hours each, $35 an hour.

| Bags per cord (assumed) | Revenue at $10 | Costs ($550 + $0.75 a bag) | Left before fuel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 90 | $900 | $617.50 | $282.50 |
| 110 | $1,100 | $632.50 | $467.50 |
| 125 | $1,250 | $643.75 | $606.25 |

Bundles pay more per hour in this sketch but sell slowly and need a label. Delivered half cords move volume but depend entirely on the trade price a yard will give you.

## What does a first season look like?

A first firewood season starts in October with a supplier, a measuring routine and a label, then sells through December and January when demand peaks.

| Weeks | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Find a yard within your area; ask for a written trade price per cord and where the wood was cut |
| 2 | Check the CFIA regulated areas for your route; buy one cord and measure it yourself |
| 3 | Print labels (product, your name and address, quantity in dm³ and cubic feet); bag a test batch |
| 4 | List bundles and half cords locally; offer stacking as an add-on |
| 5 to 8 | Deliver, measure in front of the buyer, and give a receipt every time |
| 9 to 12 | Turn repeat buyers into a refill route through February |

## Which tax rules apply to selling firewood in Canada?

Firewood sales are business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), with the wood, bags, fuel and a share of vehicle costs as expenses. GST/HST registration is required once taxable sales pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters, under the CRA's [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) rules. SAM Firewood's page, for example, quotes prices excluding HST. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Vehicle records are covered in the [mileage log guide](/side-hustles/mileage-log-cra-side-hustle-canada/).
