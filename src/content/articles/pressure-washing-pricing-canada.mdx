## How do you calculate a pressure washing rate?

Build an hourly rate from target pay, CPP and equipment costs, divided by the share of your hours that are billable, then time each surface including setup.

```text
Hourly rate   = (target pay + CPP + fuel, chemicals, machine wear, insurance per hour)
                ÷ share of hours billable
Surface price = (setup + washing hours) × hourly rate + materials (sand, soft-wash solution)
Check         = surface price ÷ square feet, against published per-sq-ft rates
```

**Illustrative rate build.** Replace each input with your own:

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Target pay | $30/hour (about 1.5 times the $19.23 Job Bank median) |
| CPP at 11.9% | $3.57 |
| Fuel, chemicals, machine wear, insurance per hour | $15.00 |
| Share of hours billable (quoting, driving, loading unpaid) | 65% |
| **Rate** | ($30 + $3.57 + $15) ÷ 0.65 = **$74.72, round to $75** |

$75 sits at the bottom of DT Cleaning's $75 to $100 budget-operator band and above TaskRabbit's $29 to $46 city averages.

## How do you raise pressure washing prices?

Raise prices at the start of each season, and use the structures companies already publish.

1. **Set a real minimum.** Beaver Wood says most GTA operators charge $200 to $300 per visit, because setup costs the same on a small job.
2. **Bundle surfaces.** Beaver Wood calls combining services on one visit the cleanest way to stay above the minimum; it raises the average ticket.
3. **Tier interlock work.** Interlock Experts sells wash, wash + re-sand, and wash + re-sand + seal at rising per-sq-ft rates.
4. **Charge for height and soft washing.** P&J Cleaners adds 20% to 30% per extra storey; soft wash costs more per sq ft than pressure washing.
5. **Price add-ons per piece.** AnyClean charges $20 per furniture piece and $7 per window.
6. **Send last year's clients a spring notice:**

```text
Hi [Name], booking spring washes now. Your driveway and deck package is
$[375] this year (from $[360]). Book before [date] to keep last year's price.
— [Your name]
```

## What pressure washing pricing mistakes are common?

- **Pricing small jobs at the per-sq-ft rate.** A 120 sq ft patio at $0.40 is $48, below the cost of showing up.
- **Pressure washing siding.** Beaver Wood warns it causes damage that shows up a year or two later; quote soft washing.
- **Leaving out sand on interlock.** Washing strips joint sand; price the re-sand in.
- **No before photos.** Cracks and loose boards get blamed on the wash.
- **Pricing at app rates.** TaskRabbit's $29 to $46 averages don't cover a gas machine, chemicals and insurance.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect pressure washing prices?

Pressure washing income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) outside Quebec. Register for GST/HST once taxable sales from all your businesses pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). Then add the rate where you work ([CRA rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)): a $360 job becomes $378 with Alberta's or B.C.'s 5% GST and $406.80 with Ontario's 13% HST. Seasonal income all lands in one tax year, so set aside tax from each job.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
