Charging for pressure washing in Canada usually means a per-job price for a driveway, deck, or patio, built from timed hours and converted to a per-square-foot figure once you have measured a few jobs, with a minimum charge for small surfaces. The closest public benchmark is Job Bank's wage data for building exterior cleaners: $15.50 (low), $19.23 (median), and $27.00 (high) an hour nationally for employees. A self-employed washer prices well above that because setup, travel, fuel, chemicals, machine wear, and both halves of CPP come out of the same rate. This guide suits someone who will measure and time surfaces before quoting.

## Which pricing models work for pressure washing in Canada?

Pressure washing in Canada is usually priced per job for common surfaces, per square foot once you know your speed, with a minimum charge, and with sealing or stain as separate quotes.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per job | One price for "double driveway" or "12 × 16 deck" | Common residential surfaces | Heavy staining or moss that doubles the time |
| Per square foot | Measured area × your rate | Larger or irregular surfaces | Quoting a rate you have not proven on real jobs |
| Minimum charge | Lowest price for any visit | Small steps, walkways | Losing money on setup |
| Bundle | Driveway + walkway + steps at one price | Raising the ticket on one visit | Discounting more than the saved setup time |
| Separate quote | Sealing, staining, siding, roofs | Specialized or riskier work | Selling work you have not trained for |

Soft-wash siding and roofs are different services with different risks. Keep them off your sheet until you have training and insurance that covers them.

## How do you calculate a pressure washing rate in Canada?

Calculate a pressure washing rate in Canada from a target hourly pay, the share of time lost to setup and travel, timed minutes per surface, and per-job fuel, chemicals, and machine wear. Then convert to per square foot from your own measured jobs.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

Read the Job Bank row as a floor. Equipment-heavy work usually needs a higher self-employed rate than the employee median.

### Step 2: Count setup and travel

Unloading, connecting water, testing a patch, rinsing, and loading again can take a large share of a short job.

### Step 3: Time a surface

Time a measured driveway or deck from first spray to final rinse. Divide the area by the minutes to get your speed.

### Step 4: Add fuel, chemicals, and wear

Gas for the machine and vehicle, detergent, nozzles, and hose wear. The machine itself is a startup cost.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $30 an hour, above Job Bank's national high of $27.00 because you own the equipment. Suppose 65% of your working time is billable once setup and travel are counted. The billable rate is $30 ÷ 0.65 = $46.15 an hour.

Suppose a 600-square-foot double driveway takes 2 hours including setup. Labour is 2 × $46.15 = $92.31. Add an assumed $15 of fuel, detergent, and wear: $107.31, which you might round to $110 before tax. That works out to $110 ÷ 600 = about $0.18 per square foot for this surface at this speed.

A 192-square-foot deck (12 × 16 feet) that takes 1.5 hours with careful wood settings is 1.5 × $46.15 + $15 = $84.23. At $0.18 per square foot it would be $34.56, far too low. Per-square-foot rates only transfer between similar surfaces, so keep separate rates for concrete and wood.

## When should you raise pressure washing prices in Canada?

Raise pressure washing prices in Canada at the start of the season, when fuel and detergent costs rise, or when your calendar fills weeks ahead in spring. Peak spring demand is the time to hold prices, not discount.

Track minutes per square foot by surface type. If decks consistently run longer than priced, raise decks, not everything.

## What pressure washing pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common pressure washing pricing mistakes in Canada are using one per-square-foot rate for every surface, skipping a minimum, and quoting from a photo without asking about stains. Each turns a short job into an unpaid afternoon.

Ask about oil stains, moss, and rust. Test a patch before quoting heavy staining. Check your city's rules on where wash water may drain; the [side hustle guide](/side-hustles/pressure-washing-side-hustle-canada/) covers that. Do not quote siding or roofs from a driveway price.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect pressure washing prices in Canada?

Pressure washing income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, counting all your taxable side hustles.

When registered, use the rate for the province where you work from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers input tax credits on the machine and supplies.
