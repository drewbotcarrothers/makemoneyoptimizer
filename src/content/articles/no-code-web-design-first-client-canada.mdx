## What offer gets a first no-code web design client in Canada?

A first no-code web design client in Canada usually buys one page with their real phone number, what they do, and what they refuse, plus one round of edits. "A web presence" is hard to purchase. "One page for a house cleaner, handed off in your name" is a purchase.

Say what is outside the first job. A store, a booking system, five extra pages, and "make it rank" are later quotes. If the page will collect names or emails, privacy law may apply. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner's [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/) page is the federal starting point. Alberta, British Columbia, and Quebec have their own private-sector statutes. You are not their lawyer.

CIRA's [Canadian Presence Requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/legal-policy-and-compliance/canadian-presence-requirements/) explain who may register a .ca name, including a Canadian citizen and a permanent resident among other categories. The client, if they qualify, should be the registrant.

## Where do Canadians find a first no-code web design client?

Canadians find a first no-code client among local businesses that still have only a social profile, and among people who have seen a sample page. A bought list is a poor start.

Businesses with a visible gap. You can open their profile and see that a customer cannot find the phone number, the area, or the service. You are offering that page.

People who already know you. A former coworker who runs a shop. Show the sample. Do not build their real site before a deposit.

A marketplace, if you use one. This guide does not state a fee. The profile should say one page, not a store.

If you email, [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies. ISED's [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page covers consent, the time limits it describes for implied consent, identification, and unsubscribe requests acted on within 10 business days. Point the client at the same law if they will email people from the new page. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first no-code note say?

A first no-code note in Canada should name the missing page, the one-page scope, the handoff, and the deposit.

**Example message.** This is a generic example you can adapt. It is not a note that produced a client.

> Hello. Your profile lists the service and does not yet have a page with the phone number, the area, and what you do not do. I built a sample one-page site for that kind of business, labelled as a sample. A first job would be one page, one revision, with the domain and the login in your name if you qualify, at a fee we agree before I build. I do not promise a search ranking. Would you like the sample link?

Do not register their name in your account to "get started." That shortcut is the dispute.

The sample should use placeholder text, not a real customer's customer list. If the buttons do not work on a phone, the page is not ready to show. ## What does a 14-day plan for a first no-code client look like?

A 14-day plan for a first no-code web design client in Canada should produce a sample page, a handoff sentence, and a short list of businesses that have no page. A paid yes is a bonus. Silence is not a reason to open a hosting plan in your name.

### Days 1 and 2: Fix the offer

One page. No store. No ranking promise. Read CIRA's presence page before you talk about a .ca name.

### Days 3 to 5: Build the sample

Use placeholder copy. Open it on a phone. Write the agreement: pages, rounds, date, price, deposit, and who owns the login.

### Days 6 to 9: Show it to real businesses

Five notes that name a real gap. If you email, follow ISED's consent page. Do not build their live site during the silence.

### Days 10 to 14: Build after the deposit, or improve the sample

If someone says yes, register nothing in your personal name that should be theirs, deliver the page, and hand off the login. If nobody says yes, fix the phone layout. Do not add a store to the offer.

## What mistakes block a first no-code web design client in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first no-code client in Canada are promising rankings, holding the domain, and building before a deposit. One page is enough.

A hosting bill you pay "for now" becomes your renewal. A contact form that stores a customer list you keep after the handoff is a privacy problem, not a feature. Emailing a scraped list skips anti-spam consent.

A free site with no cap is a gift. Call it a gift if you choose it. Do not describe it as a client, and do not keep the login.

## Which tax rules apply to a first no-code web design client in Canada?

No-code web design income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement and the e-transfers. If you pay hosting and bill it back, keep that receipt separate from your fee. One page does not by itself cross the small-supplier line. Web design is not ride-sharing unless you also carry passengers for a platform. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate where the supply is made. Confirm it on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set the tax aside.
