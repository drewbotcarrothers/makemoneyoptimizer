## Who does furniture assembly in Canada suit?

Furniture assembly in Canada suits people who already build furniture at home, can protect floors, and will stop when the instructions and the wall disagree. It suits evenings and Saturdays, because that is when the boxes are in the apartment and the client is home. It is a poor fit if you learn on a client's only bed the night before a move, if you cannot carry a panel with help, or if you will take a job that is really electrical, plumbing, or a wall you are not allowed to open.

Specialising is the useful version of this hustle. A person who does beds, office chairs, and one brand of wardrobe gets faster. A person who also mounts TVs, hangs pot lights, and "just connects the gas range" is doing licensed-trade work under a furniture label. The line is provincial. ## Which furniture is worth specialising in, and which jobs should you refuse?

Furniture assembly in Canada is worth specialising in when the piece is slow for a household and familiar to you: a bed with a mechanism, a large wardrobe, a sectional, a desk with cable management. Refuse work that is not assembly.

Leave these alone unless you hold the ticket your province requires:

- Electrical: new circuits, hardwired fixtures, and anything that is not a plug into an existing outlet.
- Gas appliances and lines.
- Structural changes, and anchors into a wall you cannot identify. If the instructions require a wall anchor, use the hardware in the box and stop if you cannot find a solid fixing point. Do not invent a fastener.
- Plumbing that opens a wall.

A tall piece that the instructions say must be anchored is part of the assembly, not an optional extra. Follow that sheet. If the client refuses the anchor, write that down and do not pretend the piece is finished as designed.

[CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/mmh/mmhintro.html) says manual handling — lifting, carrying, pushing — is a common cause of back injury, and that the risk rises when the load is heavy, awkward, unstable, or blocking your feet, or when the floor is uneven or the space is tight. A wardrobe panel in a stairwell is that description. Take a second person for the panels you cannot control, and decline the piece rather than drag it alone. ## How should you price furniture assembly in Canada?

Price furniture assembly in Canada from the minutes the piece takes, the travel, and a rate above an employee wage, because you bring the tools and you do not get the benefits on the Job Bank page. [Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/12657/ca) lists employed furniture assemblers (NOC 94210) at $17.00 (low), $22.79 (median), and $30.00 (high) an hour nationally, updated 19 November 2025, reference period 2023–2024. Ontario is $17.95, $22.00, and $30.00. British Columbia is $18.39, $23.25, and $35.00. Alberta is $23.00, $26.00, and $37.39. Quebec is $18.50, $22.00, and $26.72. The same page says 82% of workers in that occupation receive at least one non-wage benefit. Those figures describe employees, often in manufacturing and retail shops. They are not a TaskRabbit rate and not a price for a wardrobe in a condo.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey. Suppose your target is $28 an hour, between the national median of $22.79 and the high of $30.00. Suppose 70% of the visit is assembly and the rest is parking, shoes, and finding the hardware bag. The billable rate is $28 ÷ 0.70 = $40 an hour.

Suppose a desk you have built before takes 45 minutes, and the drive is 15 minutes each way that you will not bill as a separate trip because the next job is nearby. Labour on the desk is (45 ÷ 60) × $40 = $30. Suppose a wardrobe you have not built takes 2.5 hours including the anchor the instructions require: 2.5 × $40 = $100. Suppose you add an assumed $8 for a bit you broke and parking. The sketch invoice for both pieces is $30 + $100 + $8 = $138 before tax. If the client is across the city and the 15-minute assumption is wrong, the hourly result falls. Time the piece. Do not copy $30 or $100.

A flat price per piece is easier for the client to accept than an open hourly clock. Write what happens if parts are missing: you stop, you do not make a hardware-store run for free, and a second visit is a new price.

## How do you start furniture assembly in Canada?

Starting furniture assembly in Canada means naming the pieces you will build, buying the bits those pieces use, and doing one paid job before you buy a van wrap.

### Step 1: Write the yes-list and the no-list

Three pieces you have finished. The trades you will not touch. Publish both.

### Step 2: Time those pieces at home

Use a box you already own, or help someone for a written practice price of zero only if they know it is practice. Note the minutes.

### Step 3: Choose the door

If your city is on TaskRabbit's locations page, read the [review](/side-hustles/taskrabbit-review-canada/) and the [first-jobs guide](/side-hustles/taskrabbit-first-jobs-canada/) before you pay a registration fee. [Moving help](/side-hustles/moving-help-side-hustle-canada/) is the carrying; assembly is the building. Sell them as separate lines.

### Step 4: Ask an insurer the business question

Tell them you assemble furniture inside homes. A personal policy is the wrong document to guess from. This is not insurance advice. The [insurance guide](/side-hustles/side-hustle-insurance-canada/) is the question list.

### Step 5: Photograph the finished piece and the empty floor

That photo is the next client's proof. A pile of cardboard left in the hall is the review you did not want.

## What mistakes do furniture assemblers make in Canada?

Common mistakes are pricing from a US hourly screenshot, skipping the wall anchor, and accepting a "while you're here" electrical job. Another is treating Job Bank's $22.79 median as your invoice rate. That median is an employee wage for a broader occupation, and the benefits line on the same page does not follow you into a condo.

A further mistake is one price for every piece. A nightstand and a wall-anchored wardrobe are not the same visit. Missing parts are the manufacturer's problem until you agree, in writing, to source a replacement and bill for the time.

Watch for task scams that send a cheque larger than the job and ask you to wire the difference back. That pattern is covered in [task scams](/side-hustles/task-scams-canada/) and [fake cheque overpayment scams](/side-hustles/fake-cheque-overpayment-scams-canada/). A real client pays you. ## Which tax rules apply to furniture assembly in Canada?

Furniture assembly income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both shares of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) on taxable supplies. Assembly is a taxable supply. Registration is not optional once you cross the threshold in a quarter or over four consecutive quarters, and other side hustles share that total.

When registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, from the CRA's [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) page. Service income stays on T2125. [T2125 vs T776](/side-hustles/t2125-vs-t776-canada/) is the form choice. Short answers are in the [tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/). Keep a [mileage log](/side-hustles/mileage-log-cra-side-hustle-canada/) if you drive between homes.
