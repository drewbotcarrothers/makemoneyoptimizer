Charging as a virtual assistant in Canada usually means an hourly rate for new clients, then prepaid blocks of hours or a monthly retainer once the work is regular, with specialised tasks such as bookkeeping support or podcast editing priced higher. The closest public benchmark is Job Bank's administrative assistant wage data: $19.23 (low), $26.44 (median), and $36.88 (high) an hour nationally for employees. A freelance VA prices above that because software, unpaid admin, no benefits, and both halves of CPP come out of the same rate. This guide suits VAs who will track their hours before offering a retainer.

## What do administrative assistants earn per hour in Canada?

Employed administrative assistants in Canada earn a median of $26.44 an hour, according to [Job Bank's administrative assistant wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/24789/ca) (NOC 13110, reference period 2023–2024, updated November 19, 2025). Job Bank has no separate "virtual assistant" title; this is the closest benchmark we found. A freelance VA rate should sit above it.

## Which pricing models work for virtual assistants in Canada?

Virtual assistant work in Canada is usually priced hourly, as prepaid blocks of hours, as a monthly retainer for a set number of hours, or per task for repeatable jobs.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hourly | Rate × tracked time | New clients, unclear scope | Clients who micro-check timesheets |
| Prepaid hour block | 10 or 20 hours bought up front, used over a set period | Occasional clients | Expiry rules for unused hours |
| Monthly retainer | Reserved hours each month at a set fee | Regular clients | Unused hours rolling over forever |
| Per task | Fixed price for a repeatable job (inbox clean-up, travel booking) | Defined tasks you have timed | Tasks that change shape |
| Specialist rate | Higher hourly rate for skilled work | Bookkeeping support, podcast editing, automation | Pricing skilled work at admin rates |

Retainers should say how many hours are reserved, the response time, what happens to unused hours, and the overage rate.

## How do you calculate a virtual assistant rate in Canada?

Calculate a virtual assistant rate in Canada from a target hourly pay, your billable share, software costs, and how many hours a client really uses each month. Then build blocks and retainers from the hourly rate.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

The Job Bank table is your floor check.

### Step 2: Estimate billable share

Onboarding, learning a client's tools, prospecting, and invoicing are unpaid.

### Step 3: Add software and equipment

Your own subscriptions, headset, and computer wear, divided across billable hours.

### Step 4: Track a month of work

Before offering a retainer, track a client's real hours for a month.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $30 an hour, between Job Bank's national median of $26.44 and high of $36.88. Suppose 75% of your working time is billable. The billable rate is $30 ÷ 0.75 = $40 an hour.

A 10-hour prepaid block at $40 is $400. Offering it at $380 is a 5% discount for paying up front. A 20-hour monthly retainer at $38 an hour is 20 × $38 = $760, with extra hours billed at $40.

Suppose the client uses only 14 retainer hours in a month and the agreement says unused hours do not roll over. Your effective rate that month is $760 ÷ 14 = $54.29 an hour, because the client paid to reserve your time. If unused hours roll over without limit, a retainer becomes a prepaid block with no end date.

## What does a sample virtual assistant rate sheet look like in Canada?

A virtual assistant rate sheet in Canada lists the hourly rate, blocks, retainers, and specialist rates. The table is an **illustrative example** built on the $40 billable rate, not a market average.

| Service (illustrative) | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Hourly (admin, inbox, calendar, data entry) | $40 per hour |
| 10-hour block, valid 60 days | $380 |
| Monthly retainer, 20 hours, no rollover | $760 ($38 per hour) |
| Overage on retainer | $40 per hour |
| Specialist work (bookkeeping support, podcast editing, automations) | $50 per hour |
| Rush (same-day turnaround) | +25% |

Prices are before tax. Write the response time, working hours, time zone, and the rollover rule.

## When should you raise virtual assistant prices in Canada?

Raise virtual assistant prices in Canada when your hours are full, when you add a specialist skill, or at the annual review in your agreement. Change new-client rates first and give retainer clients notice before the next month.

Move clients who keep needing specialist work to the specialist rate for those tasks.

## What virtual assistant pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common virtual assistant pricing mistakes in Canada are copying the lowest rate you can find online, unlimited rollover on retainers, and pricing skilled work at admin rates. Each fills your week while lowering pay.

For the access, privacy, and tax mistakes VAs make most, and how to fix them, see the [virtual assistant mistakes guide](/side-hustles/virtual-assistant-mistakes-canada/).

Track time from day one. Write a rollover rule. Charge for rush work. Do not take on regulated work, such as tax filing for others, without the right training and coverage.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect virtual assistant prices in Canada?

Virtual assistant income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

When registered, the rate depends on the place of supply, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers registration, and the [home office guide](/side-hustles/home-office-expenses-side-hustle-canada/) covers workspace claims.

Retainers and prepaid blocks are easier with recurring invoices and time tracking. <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> is one invoicing option; check whether your plan includes time tracking. The invoice fields the CRA expects are in [how to invoice clients](/side-hustles/how-to-invoice-clients-canada/).
