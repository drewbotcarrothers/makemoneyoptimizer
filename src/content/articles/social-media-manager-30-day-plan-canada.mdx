A 30-day social media manager launch plan for Canada uses week 1 to pick a niche and build samples, week 2 to set packages, rates, and an access routine, week 3 for outreach, and week 4 to onboard a first client and report. It suits organized people who write well, understand how small businesses use social platforms, and can stick to a posting schedule.

## What happens in week 1 of a social media manager launch plan in Canada?

Week 1 is focus and proof: choose who you serve, which platforms, and build samples.

### Days 1 and 2: Choose a niche and platforms

Pick a type of business you understand, such as local restaurants, trades, or salons, and the one or two platforms their customers use.

### Days 3 to 5: Build sample calendars

Create two or three sample monthly content calendars with mock posts for imaginary or consenting real businesses. Label mock work clearly as samples. A design tool like <AffiliateLink program="canva" label="Canva" /> or any free alternative is enough.

### Days 6 and 7: Tidy your own profiles

Make sure your own profiles look current and show the kind of content you would create for clients.

## What happens in week 2 of a social media manager launch plan in Canada?

Week 2 is the offer: packages, an agreement, and a secure way to work in client accounts.

### Days 8 to 10: Set packages and rates

Use the [pricing guide](/side-hustles/social-media-manager-pricing-canada/) to design two or three monthly packages (for example, posts per week, platforms, and whether you reply to comments).

### Days 11 and 12: Write an agreement

Cover scope, approval process, posting schedule, who owns content, payment terms, and how either side ends the arrangement. This is not legal advice.

### Days 13 and 14: Plan secure access

Ask clients to add you through platform roles or business managers instead of sharing passwords. Get Cyber Safe's [MFA page](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts/multi-factor-authentication) says to enable multi-factor authentication whenever it is offered.

## What happens in week 3 of a social media manager launch plan in Canada?

Week 3 is outreach.

### Days 15 to 17: Reach your network and local businesses

Tell people you know and visit or call local businesses in your niche. If you email businesses you have no relationship with, read ISED's [consent page](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email), which lists questions such as whether they published their contact information and whether they posted "don't contact" instructions. This is not legal advice.

### Days 18 and 19: Offer a short audit

Offer a brief review of a prospect's current profiles with two or three practical suggestions. Keep it short so it shows skill without giving away the whole job.

### Days 20 and 21: Hold discovery calls

Ask about goals, audience, budget, and who approves posts. Send a proposal within a day or two.

## What happens in week 4 of a social media manager launch plan in Canada?

Week 4 is onboarding, publishing, and honest reporting.

### Days 22 to 24: Onboard

Collect brand assets, gain access through platform roles, and agree on an approval workflow and first month's calendar.

### Days 25 to 27: Publish and handle disclosures

Publish on schedule. If a client's posts promote products from partners or include paid relationships, the Competition Bureau's [influencer guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says material connections should be disclosed clearly and prominently, and that tags or discount codes alone are unlikely to be enough.

### Days 28 to 30: Report and review

Send a short report with what was posted and the platform metrics you agreed to track. Do not promise follower or sales growth. Review your outreach results and plan month two.

### Illustrative example

A teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast: suppose one client signs a monthly package at price M. If M were $400, that is $400 a month before software and tax. A second client at the same price would make $800. Your packages and client count will differ.

## What social media manager launch mistakes should you avoid in Canada?

Common launch mistakes are serving every industry and platform, asking clients for passwords, promising growth numbers, skipping a written approval process, and ignoring disclosure rules on sponsored posts.

## Which tax rules apply when launching social media management in Canada?

Social media management income as a contractor is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and the self-employed pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
