## What do ad networks and affiliate programs pay Canadian bloggers?

Ad networks and affiliate programs pay Canadian bloggers at rates they publish, not rates you negotiate. These are the two most common starting points; read the live terms before you plan around them.

| Income line | What the program says | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google AdSense display ads | Publishers receive 80% of revenue after the advertiser platform takes its fee; when advertisers buy through Google Ads, publishers keep about 68% | [AdSense revenue share](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/180195?hl=en) |
| Amazon.ca Associates | Fixed standard commission rates by category, from 0% (gift cards, grocery) to 10% (luxury beauty); 2% for "all other categories" | [Amazon.ca commission statement](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ) |
| Sponsored post | Price you set and invoice | Your rate card |

Google does not publish a fixed amount per 1,000 page views, and it would be guesswork to state one here. Your AdSense report is the only reliable number, and it changes by topic and season.

## Which pricing models work for sponsored blog posts in Canada?

Sponsored posts on a Canadian blog are usually priced as a flat fee per post, sometimes with add-ons for social shares or a newsletter mention. Some bloggers price per hour of writing; most quote a flat fee that includes the writing.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flat fee per post | One price for writing and publishing | Most sponsors | Unlimited edits from the sponsor |
| Writing fee + placement fee | Writing priced by hours, placement priced by audience | Blogs with steady traffic | Explaining two numbers clearly |
| Package | Post + newsletter mention + social share | Bloggers with a list | Each channel needs its own disclosure |
| Update to an existing post | Add a sponsor section to a ranking page | High-traffic pages | Changing a page readers trust |

## How do you calculate a sponsored post price in Canada?

Calculate a sponsored post price in Canada from the hours to research, write, and publish, plus a placement fee that reflects how many readers will see it. Then add disclosure and link terms before you quote.

### Step 1: Time the post

Research, drafting, images, sponsor review, and publishing. A sponsored post often takes longer than your own because of approval rounds.

### Step 2: Price the writing time

Use an hourly rate at or above Job Bank's blogger median for your province.

### Step 3: Add a placement fee

Base it on monthly page views for similar posts. This is where audience size enters the price.

### Step 4: Write the link and disclosure terms

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) say buying or selling links for ranking purposes is link spam, and that paid links are fine when marked rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow". The Competition Bureau's [influencer guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says to disclose payment clearly. Put both in the agreement.

### Step 5: Set tax aside and round

Self-employed income on a blog is taxable like any other business income (below).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose a sponsored post takes 5 hours including one review round, and your target is $40 an hour, a little above Job Bank's national blogger median of $36.81. Writing is 5 × $40 = $200.

Suppose similar posts on your blog get about 4,000 page views a month, and you choose a placement fee of an assumed $25 per 1,000 monthly page views: 4 × $25 = $100. This rate is a teaching assumption, not a market figure. The quote is $300, with rel="sponsored" on the sponsor's link and a disclosure at the top of the post.

For comparison, if that same post instead linked an Amazon.ca product in a 4% category and sent buyers who spent $1,000 in a month, the commission would be 4% × $1,000 = $40.

## When should you raise blog sponsorship prices in Canada?

Raise sponsored post prices in Canada when page views grow, when sponsors rebook, or when approval rounds take longer than you priced. Update your rate card every few months with current traffic screenshots.

Turning sponsors away that do not fit your readers protects the traffic that makes the placement fee worth anything.

## What SEO blog pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common SEO blog pricing mistakes in Canada are selling followed links, pricing only the writing time, and treating early ad income as a forecast. The first one can hurt the whole site's search visibility.

Never accept a deal that forbids a disclosure or a rel="sponsored" tag. Do not quote a page-view number you cannot show. Do not build a budget on a single month of ad revenue. ## How do GST/HST and income tax affect blog pricing in Canada?

Blog income in Canada, including ad revenue, commissions, and sponsored posts, is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your businesses. Whether a particular payment from a foreign ad network is taxable for GST/HST depends on the facts; ask a tax professional once you register.
