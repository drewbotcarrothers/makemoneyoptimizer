Reselling in Canada means buying items below what they are worth (at thrift stores, garage sales, estate sales, clearance racks or liquidation) and selling them for a profit on eBay.ca, Poshmark, Facebook Marketplace or Kijiji. You can start with your phone and a few items from your own closet. The margin is decided by three numbers you can check before you buy: what the item has actually sold for, the platform's fee, and what shipping will cost.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Fees (checked October 9, 2026):** eBay.ca charges 13.6% of the total sale (including shipping and sales tax) plus $0.40 per order over $10 in most categories, with 250 free listings a month. Poshmark Canada takes a flat C$3.95 on sales under C$20 and 20% on sales of C$20 or more. Kijiji is free for most private Buy & Sell listings.
- **Shipping:** Poshmark sends a prepaid Canada Post label that covers parcels up to 2.5 kg. On eBay you buy postage yourself, and the final value fee also applies to the shipping you charge.
- **CRA can see platform sales:** since 2024, platforms report sellers to CRA once they reach 30 sales of goods or more than $2,800 in a calendar year on that platform.
- **Real example:** Victoria thrifter Marcus Pollard told *The Walrus* he bought a first edition of *At Swim-Two-Birds* at Value Village for $3.99 and sold it for about $4,900. Big scores like this are rare; most profit comes from volume.
- **Tax:** occasional sales of your own used stuff are usually not business income, but buying to resell is. Report on Form T2125, pay CPP on net earnings above $3,500, and register for GST/HST after $30,000 in taxable sales.

</QuickAnswer>

## Who reselling suits

Reselling works best for people who like the hunt and can do repetitive work consistently. It suits you if:

- You already know a category: sneakers, vintage clothing, books, video games, tools, kitchenware, sports cards, outdoor gear or furniture.
- You have space at home to store inventory and a spot with good light to photograph it.
- You can visit thrift stores, garage sales or estate sales regularly, often on weekend mornings.
- You're comfortable negotiating with strangers and meeting buyers for local pickup.

It's a weaker fit if you have no storage space, dislike packing and post office trips, or need steady income each week. Sales come in bursts, and inventory that sits ties up cash.

### Read eBay's fee example before you price

eBay's own example: a phone sells for $400 with free shipping to a buyer in Ontario. Because the fee is charged on the total including 13% HST, the total is $452, the final value fee is $61.87 (13.6% of $452 plus $0.40), and with a $0.30 insertion fee the seller keeps $337.83, about 84% of the sale price before shipping costs. Price with that in mind.

eBay also charges a 3% currency conversion charge when it converts funds for Canadian sellers, rising to 3.25% from October 14, 2026, and a $20 dispute fee if you're found responsible for a chargeback.

## Real Canadian resellers and what they found

- **Marcus Pollard (Victoria, B.C.).** A long-time thrifter profiled in *The Walrus* in February 2026, Pollard found a first edition of Flann O'Brien's *At Swim-Two-Birds* at Value Village for $3.99 and sold it to a buyer in England for "$4,900 or something like that." He also said sourcing has changed: shoppers now scan book barcodes with apps, and big thrift chains sort donations and sell the best pieces online themselves.
- **Jane Mundy (Victoria, B.C.).** Mundy ran a vintage shop in the early 1980s and told *The Walrus* she once bought about eight hand-carved duck decoys at a garage sale for $8 and sold one for "about 300 or 400 bucks." She also described selling a piece for $50 and seeing it in an antique shop window a week later priced in the thousands, which is the reseller's reminder to research before you price.
- **Kristina Urquhart (The Vintage Seeker).** Urquhart curates a directory of Canadian vintage and pre-loved sellers. She told CityNews in March 2025 that some sellers had 30% to 70% of their customers in the US, which made US tariffs and the end of duty-free shipping a serious problem for them.

Those stories show both sides of the business: knowledge finds the big wins, and cross-border rules can hit your margins hard.

## The math on one flip

**Illustrative example.** You buy a branded jacket for $8 (an assumed thrift price) and it sells for $50. On eBay you charge the buyer $15 for shipping and assume postage really costs you $15. The buyer is in Ontario, so 13% HST is added to the total.

| Line | eBay.ca | Poshmark Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Item price | $50.00 | $50.00 |
| Shipping charged to buyer | $15.00 | Paid by buyer to Poshmark |
| Total eBay fee is based on ($65 + 13% HST) | $73.45 | n/a |
| Platform fee | $10.39 (13.6% × $73.45 + $0.40) | $10.00 (20% of $50) |
| Your postage cost (assumed) | $15.00 | $0 (prepaid label up to 2.5 kg) |
| Cost of item (assumed) | $8.00 | $8.00 |
| **Profit before tax** | **$31.61** | **$32.00** |

On a $50 item the two come out close. The gap widens as prices rise: on a $200 item, Poshmark's 20% is $40, while eBay's 13.6% on the item alone (before shipping and tax) is $27.60. Sold locally on Kijiji or Marketplace for cash, the same jacket would net $42 with no fee, but you need a local buyer.

**Illustrative month.** Twenty flips like this at about $32 profit each comes to $640 before tax, excluding your time sourcing, photographing, listing, packing and dropping off parcels. Track your hours; many resellers find their real hourly rate is lower than they assumed. The pricing formulas are in [how to price items for resale](/side-hustles/reselling-pricing-canada/).

## Your launch plan: the first 30 days

### Week 1: sell what you already own

- Pick 10 to 20 items from your home that you don't use.
- Search each on eBay and filter for **Sold items** to see real prices, not hopeful asking prices.
- List bulky items on Marketplace and Kijiji, clothing on Poshmark, and collectibles or electronics on eBay.
- Learn the photo routine: front, back, tags, labels, flaws, measurements.

### Week 2: set up the business side

- Open a separate account for sales and purchases. Options are compared in [KOHO vs Wealthsimple vs EQ Bank](/side-hustles/koho-vs-wealthsimple-vs-eq-bank-side-hustle-canada/).
- Start a spreadsheet with item, date bought, cost, platform, date sold, sale price, fee and postage.
- Set up storage: labelled bins and a numbered location code in each listing's notes.
- Buy mailers, tape and a scale. Hold off on a label printer until you ship a few parcels a week.

### Week 3: first sourcing trips

- Visit two or three thrift stores, a garage sale route or an estate sale. Set a budget of $50 to $100 for the week.
- Check sold prices on your phone before you buy anything. Buy only items where the sold price covers at least the fee, postage and your cost with room to spare.
- List everything within 48 hours of buying it.

### Week 4: ship, review and refine

- Ship within your stated handling time; late shipping hurts eBay seller ratings.
- Review what sold, how fast and at what profit. Drop categories that sit.
- Lower prices on anything listed for more than three weeks, or bundle it.

### Days 31 to 90

| Weeks | Focus | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | Specialize in two categories that sold fastest | Faster sourcing decisions |
| 7–8 | Set a regular sourcing route and listing schedule | Consistent weekly listings |
| 9–10 | Cross-list unsold items on a second platform | Lower dead stock |
| 11–12 | Review profit per hour; decide on a label printer and storage | A system you can repeat |

## Daily workflow and checklists

**Sourcing checklist**

- [ ] Check sold prices, not active listings
- [ ] Inspect zippers, seams, stains, smells, missing parts and batteries
- [ ] Test electronics where the store allows it
- [ ] Check Health Canada's recalls site for children's products and helmets
- [ ] Walk away from counterfeits; selling them can get your account shut down

**Listing template (copy and fill in)**

> **Title:** [Brand] [Item type] [Model/style] [Size] [Colour] [Key feature]
>
> **Condition:** [New with tags / Excellent used / Good used]. [List any flaws honestly, with photo numbers.]
>
> **Measurements:** [Pit to pit, length, inseam, or dimensions in cm and inches]
>
> **Details:** [Material, year or era, model number, what's included]
>
> **Shipping:** Ships within [1–2] business days from [province] by [carrier] with tracking.
>
> **Pickup:** [Local pickup in [neighbourhood] available / Shipping only]

**Packing checklist**

- [ ] Fold or wrap, then bag soft goods to protect against moisture
- [ ] Box fragile items with padding on all sides
- [ ] Weigh and measure, then buy the correct label
- [ ] Add the order number inside the parcel
- [ ] Drop off and keep the receipt or tracking number

Honest descriptions also keep you on the right side of the Competition Bureau's rules on false or misleading representations.

## Taxes and CRA rules for resellers

This is the part most new resellers get wrong.

- **Personal items vs a business.** Selling your own used things now and then is generally not business income. Buying items to resell for profit, regularly, is a business. Our guide on [whether selling on Kijiji is taxable](/side-hustles/is-selling-on-kijiji-taxable-canada/) walks through the difference.
- **Platforms report to CRA.** Under the Reporting Rules for Digital Platform Operators, a seller is only excluded if they had fewer than 30 sales of goods and no more than $2,800 in consideration in the calendar year on that platform. Hit either limit and the platform reports you. See [tax on reselling and platform income](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).
- **Income tax:** report revenue, cost of goods sold, fees, postage and supplies on Form T2125. [How to report side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/) covers the form.
- **CPP:** 11.9% of net self-employment earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026 (QPP in Quebec).
- **GST/HST:** you're a small supplier until your taxable sales pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Rates vary by province, for example 13% HST in Ontario and 14% in Nova Scotia since April 1, 2025. Some marketplaces collect sales tax from buyers under CRA's digital-economy rules, but you are still responsible for your own registration status.
- **Keep receipts for inventory.** Thrift stores and garage sales rarely give detailed receipts, so photograph items with the price tag and log the cost the day you buy.

## Provincial and local notes

- **Toronto and some other cities license second-hand shops.** Toronto requires a Second Hand Shop licence for businesses that buy and sell used goods from a premises. Online-only reselling from home is a different situation, so check your own municipality before you open a storefront or booth.
- **Ontario and Nova Scotia:** HST at 13% and 14% applies once you're registered.
- **Quebec:** QPP instead of CPP, and QST through Revenu Québec once registered.
- **Importing inventory:** if you bring goods in from the US to resell, CBSA's guide to importing commercial goods applies. Buying pallets or bulk lots from abroad adds duties and brokerage fees.

## What to watch out for

- **Sourcing is getting harder.** Pollard and Mundy both told *The Walrus* that thrift chains now sort donations and sell the best items online themselves, and that other resellers scan items with phone apps.
- **US buyers are harder to reach.** The US ended its de minimis exemption on August 29, 2025. CFIB reports that parcels sent by Canada Post now have US duties collected even on CUSMA-qualifying goods, while couriers can still apply CUSMA. Factor this into anything priced for American buyers.
- **Dead stock ties up cash.** Items that don't sell in 60 to 90 days are better bundled, discounted or donated than stored forever.
- **Fees stack.** eBay charges on shipping and tax, plus extra fees for below-standard sellers. Poshmark's 20% bites on mid-priced items.
- **Safety and scams.** Meet local buyers in public places, never accept overpayments, and keep all communication on the platform.
- **Counterfeits and recalled goods** can get your account suspended and expose you to liability.
