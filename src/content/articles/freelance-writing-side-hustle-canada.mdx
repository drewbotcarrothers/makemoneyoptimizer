Freelance writing in Canada means getting paid per word, per piece or per hour to write things businesses and publications need: blog posts, website pages, email newsletters, case studies, reports and articles. You can start with the computer you already own, a free word processor and two or three writing samples. Rates published by the Canadian Freelance Guild run from $0.10 a word at small community newspapers to $1 to $3 a word for business web writing, so what you earn depends far more on who you write for than on how fast you type.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Pay benchmark:** employed writers in Canada (NOC 51111) earn a median of $36.81 an hour, with a range of $25.48 to $53.00 (Job Bank, updated November 2025). Freelancers have no benefits, so they need to charge more than that for the same take-home pay.
- **Published freelance rates (Canadian Freelance Guild guidelines):** business and website writing $1 to $3 a word or $60 to $100 an hour; newsletters $0.30 to $1.50 a word; trade magazines $0.30 to $2 a word; small community newspapers $0.10 to $0.50 a word.
- **Platform cuts:** Upwork takes a 0% to 15% fee per contract. Fiverr pays sellers 80% of each order, including extras and tips.
- **AI pressure is real:** a peer-reviewed study of an online labour market found freelancers in the most affected jobs lost 2% of monthly jobs and 5.2% of monthly earnings after ChatGPT's release. Generic blog filler is the work that is shrinking.
- **Tax:** report income on Form T2125, pay 11.9% CPP on net self-employment earnings above $3,500, and register for GST/HST after $30,000 in taxable supplies.

</QuickAnswer>

## Who freelance writing suits

Freelance writing rewards people who already explain things well in writing and who can handle a slow, uneven start. It is a good fit if:

- You can write clean, organized prose without a heavy edit, and you are comfortable being edited by someone else.
- You know an industry well. Nurses, accountants, tradespeople, software workers and teachers can charge more because clients pay for accuracy, not just sentences.
- You can tolerate waiting to get paid. In a J-Source survey of Canadian publications, most outlets paid freelancers 30 to 90 days after sign-off, and one writer reported waiting 18 months on a single invoice.
- You are fine with sales. Most of the job at the start is pitching, following up and chasing invoices.

It is a poor fit if you need predictable weekly cash, or if you want to write only what interests you. Editorial journalism pays least. In the same J-Source piece, Toronto freelancer Renee Sylvestre-Williams explained that her better-paying ghostwriting, marketing and branded copy is what lets her take on editorial pieces that pay less.

If you prefer to stay off camera and off the phone, freelance writing is on our list of [side hustles for introverts](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-introverts-canada/), and it needs no car, so it's also one of the [side hustles without a car](/side-hustles/side-hustles-without-a-car-canada/).

## What freelance writers actually get paid in Canada

There is no current national survey of freelance writer income in Canada. The most recent large survey by the Professional Writers Association of Canada dates from 2006, so treat any "average freelancer income" figure you see online with caution. These are the best sources available as of October 2026.

### The employee benchmark

Job Bank's wage report for writers (NOC 51111), updated November 19, 2025 from 2023–24 Labour Force Survey data, shows:

| Region | Low | Median | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canada | $25.48 | $36.81 | $53.00 |
| Ontario | n/a | $40.38 | n/a |
| Alberta | n/a | $38.56 | n/a |
| British Columbia | n/a | $36.44 | n/a |
| Quebec | n/a | $36.00 | n/a |
| Nova Scotia | n/a | $33.00 | n/a |

The same report says 91.8% of employed writers get benefits. As a freelancer, you pay your own CPP at double the employee rate, buy your own software and take no paid vacation, so a freelance rate equal to the employee median leaves you worse off.

Two lessons jump out. Business clients pay several times what publications pay, and ghostwritten articles are usually priced at two to three times the normal rate, according to the same guidelines.

### What Canadian publications reported paying

When J-Source surveyed Canadian outlets, only six gave hard numbers. Rates ranged from 10 cents a word (Maisonneuve) to 50 cents a word (Up Here). Reader's Digest's online rate was 40 cents a word. The Deep paid 40 cents a word for the first 4,000 words and 25 cents after. Today's Parent paid $100 to $2,500 per story. That survey is from 2018, so use it as a sense of the market's shape, not current rates.

### Platform fees

- **Upwork:** a Freelancer Service Fee of 0% to 15% per contract. The fee is shown in the offer or your proposal and stays fixed once the contract starts. Upwork's own example is billing $22.22 an hour at a 10% fee to net $20. Direct Contracts carry a reduced fee.
- **Fiverr:** you earn 80% of the purchase amount on every completed order, including Gig Extras and tips. Fiverr also asks Canadian sellers to complete a tax compliance form. Early Payout costs 1% if you're eligible.

For a side-by-side of both marketplaces, see [Fiverr vs Upwork for Canadians](/side-hustles/fiverr-vs-upwork-canada/) and our [Upwork review](/side-hustles/upwork-review-canada/). ## Real Canadian freelance writers

These are people who have described their work publicly. Their results are not typical or guaranteed, but they show how the work actually builds.

- **Kern Carter (Toronto), author and ghostwriter.** In a June 2025 interview with *How I Make Money Writing*, Carter said his first paid gig was $50 to write a bio for a local Toronto rapper. Eleven years in, he reported earnings of $80,000 to $140,000 a year, mostly from ghostwriting, and said he earns up to $25,000 per ghostwriting project. He expected book advances and royalties from deals with Penguin and Scholastic to overtake ghostwriting.
- **Eva Holland (Yukon), magazine writer.** Holland told J-Source she made a point of no longer chasing $100 stories that took as much effort as bigger ones, because "a small cheque took just as long to show up in the mailbox as a big one." She said it took her 10 years to reach close to a middle-class income.
- **Renee Sylvestre-Williams, freelancer for over 10 years.** Also quoted by J-Source, she mixes editorial work with ghostwriting, marketing and branded copy, asks international clients to cover wire fees, and once waited 18 months for payment. Her line to new freelancers: "Freelancing is a business, not a hobby."
- **Lisa Coxon, the J-Source reporter.** After a 2017 layoff, Coxon freelanced full time for nearly nine months, relying on EI and part-time technical editing, before taking a salaried job. She wrote that a couple of hundred dollars arriving every few weeks might have kept her freelancing.

The pattern: the writers who last specialize, chase business and ghostwriting work that pays well, and treat invoicing and cash flow as part of the job.

## How AI changed the market

Freelance writing changed after November 2022. Researchers Xiang Hui, Oren Reshef and Luofeng Zhou studied an online freelance platform and found that freelancers in occupations most affected by ChatGPT, writing among them, saw a 2% drop in monthly jobs and a 5.2% drop in monthly earnings compared with less affected freelancers. They also found that top-rated freelancers were not protected. The peer-reviewed version appeared in *Organization Science* in 2024.

In practice, that means:

- Cheap, generic blog posts are the most exposed work. A client who used to pay $50 for a 1,000-word "10 tips" post can now draft it in seconds.
- Work that needs interviews, original reporting, subject expertise, a brand voice, accountability, or a writer who knows Canadian rules (tax, health, finance, law) holds up better.
- Clients increasingly hire writers to edit, fact-check and shape AI drafts. Price this as editing or strategy, not as "cheap writing."

## 18 freelance writing services you can sell

Pick one or two to start. Clients hire specialists, so "I write case studies for Canadian SaaS companies" lands better than "I write anything."

1. **Blog posts for a niche you know** such as accounting, trades, health or software. Charge per piece, not per word, once you know your speed.
2. **Website page copy** (home, about, service pages) for small local businesses.
3. **Case studies** built from a customer interview. These need a phone call, which AI cannot do for the client.
4. **Email newsletters** written monthly for a business. Newsletters are recurring work; the guideline range is $400 to $6,000 per issue. Pair it with our [newsletter guide](/side-hustles/newsletter-side-hustle-canada/).
5. **LinkedIn ghostwriting** for founders and consultants.
6. **Ghostwritten articles** under a client's name, usually priced at two to three times normal rates.
7. **Product descriptions** for e-commerce stores, sold in batches.
8. **Grant and funding application drafts** for non-profits and small firms (you write, they submit).
9. **Press releases** for local events, openings and launches.
10. **White papers and reports** for B2B companies; corporate writing guidelines go up to $12,000 per project.
11. **Technical documentation** such as user guides, help articles and SOPs.
12. **Speech writing** for weddings, retirements and executives.
13. **Resume and LinkedIn profile writing** for job seekers.
14. **Local-business content** such as Google Business Profile posts and service-area pages. This overlaps with [local SEO](/side-hustles/local-seo-side-hustle-canada/).
15. **French–English bilingual copy**, if you are fluent in both. See the [freelance translation guide](/side-hustles/freelance-translation-side-hustle-canada/).
16. **Magazine and trade publication articles**: lower pay, but bylines build credibility.
17. **Editing and AI-draft cleanup**, priced hourly.
18. **Book ghostwriting or memoir projects** once you have a track record. Carter reported up to $25,000 per project, and the guidelines list $10,000 to $50,000 flat fees.

## Your launch plan: the first 30 days

This is the short version. The day-by-day plan is in the [30-day freelance writing plan](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-30-day-plan-canada/).

### Week 1: pick a niche and build samples

- Choose one industry you know and one or two services from the list above.
- Write three samples aimed at that niche: one blog post of 800 to 1,200 words, one short case study or website page, and one newsletter or LinkedIn post.
- Publish them somewhere you control, such as a free Google Docs link, a one-page portfolio site, or a LinkedIn article.
- Set your floor rate using the formula in the pricing section below.

### Week 2: set up the business side

- Open a separate account for writing income. Options are compared in [KOHO vs Wealthsimple vs EQ Bank](/side-hustles/koho-vs-wealthsimple-vs-eq-bank-side-hustle-canada/).
- Create a Wave or FreshBooks account and a basic invoice template.
- Write a one-page contract with scope, revision limit, payment terms and rights (template below).
- Build a list of 50 target businesses or editors in your niche.

### Week 3: pitch every weekday

- Send 5 to 10 personalized pitches a day using the templates below. Keep each one under 150 words.
- Apply to 3 to 5 relevant Upwork jobs a day if you're using a marketplace.
- Track every pitch in a spreadsheet: name, date, offer, follow-up date, result.

### Week 4: follow up and deliver

- Follow up once on every pitch after 5 to 7 business days.
- If a client says yes, deliver early and ask for a testimonial and a referral.
- Review: which pitch got replies? Double down on that niche and angle.

### Days 31 to 90

| Weeks | Focus | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | Keep pitching; add one new sample from real client work (with permission) | 2 to 3 paid pieces total |
| 7–8 | Offer a monthly package to your best client (for example, four posts a month) | First recurring client |
| 9–10 | Raise your rate for every new client by 10% to 20% | Fewer, better clients |
| 11–12 | Drop the lowest-paying work; set aside tax money monthly | Clear view of hourly earnings |

## Pitch templates you can copy

Cold emailing businesses in Canada falls under Canada's anti-spam legislation (CASL). Commercial emails generally need consent, and there are limited exceptions. Read [cold email and CASL](/side-hustles/cold-email-lead-generation-casl-canada/) before you send bulk outreach. Always identify yourself, include contact information and offer a way to opt out.

**Pitch to a business (short email or LinkedIn message):**

> Subject: Case study idea for [Company]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I noticed [Company] has [specific thing: a new service page, no customer stories, a blog last updated in 2024]. I write [service] for [niche] companies, and I'd like to turn one happy customer into a 1,000-word case study you can use on your site and in sales emails.
>
> Here's a sample: [link]. My rate for a case study, including one customer interview and two rounds of revisions, is $[X].
>
> Would a quick call next week make sense? If this isn't relevant, reply "no thanks" and I won't follow up.
>
> [Your name] · [City, province] · [email] · [portfolio link]

**Pitch to an editor (publication):**

> Subject: Pitch: [Working headline]
>
> Hi [Editor name],
>
> [One-sentence hook with a fact or news peg.] I'd like to write a [word count]-word piece for [section] on [topic], drawing on interviews with [types of sources] and [data source].
>
> Why me: [one line on your expertise or access]. Recent work: [two links].
>
> Thanks for considering it,
> [Your name]

**Follow-up (5 to 7 business days later):**

> Hi [Name], following up on my note from [day] about [offer]. Happy to send a shorter version or a different angle if that's more useful. Thanks, [Name]

## Your client workflow

Use the same workflow for every job so nothing gets missed:

1. **Discovery:** confirm the goal, audience, word count, deadline, sources, and who approves the final draft.
2. **Quote and contract:** send a fixed price with scope, revision limit, deposit and rights.
3. **Deposit:** ask new clients for 50% up front on anything over a few hundred dollars.
4. **Outline:** send a short outline for approval before you draft. This prevents most rewrites.
5. **Draft:** write in Google Docs and share with comment or suggestion access.
6. **Revisions:** include two rounds; quote extra rounds hourly.
7. **Invoice:** send it the day the client approves, with payment terms (for example, net 15).
8. **Follow up:** ask for a testimonial and a referral, and add the piece to your portfolio if allowed.

### Contract checklist

- [ ] Scope: deliverable, word count range, number of interviews, sources
- [ ] Deadline for first draft and for client feedback
- [ ] Revisions: two rounds included, then $[X]/hour
- [ ] Price, deposit, payment terms, late fee
- [ ] Kill fee if the client cancels after work starts (for example, 50%)
- [ ] Rights: what the client gets and when (usually on full payment)
- [ ] Byline or ghostwritten, and portfolio permission
- [ ] Who supplies images, and AI-use expectations

On rights: under Canadian copyright law, the author of a work is generally its first owner unless the work is made in the course of employment, so as a freelancer you usually own what you write until you assign or license it. Spell out in writing what the client gets. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office has a plain-language overview.

## Setting your rate: a worked example

**Illustrative example.** Say you aim to earn the employee median of $36.81 an hour in take-home value, and you can bill 60% of your working hours (the rest goes to pitching, admin and invoicing; this percentage is an assumption, not a published figure).

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Target value per working hour | Job Bank median | $36.81 |
| Add CPP gap: you pay both halves (11.9% vs 5.95%) | $36.81 × 1.0595 | $39.00 |
| Adjust for 60% billable time | $39.00 ÷ 0.60 | $65.00 per billable hour |
| If you use Upwork at a 10% fee | $65.00 ÷ 0.90 | $72.22 per hour billed |
| If you sell on Fiverr (you keep 80%) | $65.00 ÷ 0.80 | $81.25 per hour of work priced in |

That $65 an hour falls within the $60 to $100 an hour guideline for business website writing. Then convert hourly to per-piece prices. If a 1,000-word blog post takes you four hours from outline to final, your floor is about $260. Raise it if the client needs interviews, subject expertise or fast turnaround.

**Illustrative side-hustle month.** If you sell eight posts a month at $260 each, that's $2,080 in revenue. On Upwork at a 10% fee, you'd keep $1,872 before income tax and CPP. Expenses like Grammarly Pro and a Wave Pro plan would reduce your taxable profit.

## Taxes for freelance writers in Canada

Freelance writing income is self-employment income. The essentials, current to October 2026:

- **Income tax:** report revenue and expenses on [Form T2125](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/). Platform payouts from Upwork or Fiverr count, even if paid in US dollars.
- **CPP:** you pay both the employee and employer share, 11.9% of net self-employment earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026. Quebec residents pay into the QPP instead.
- **GST/HST:** you're a small supplier until your worldwide taxable supplies pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. After that, you must register and charge GST/HST. Services supplied to clients outside Canada may qualify as zero-rated exports, so check CRA's rules before you invoice foreign clients.
- **Rates depend on where your client is:** for example, 13% HST in Ontario, 14% HST in Nova Scotia since April 1, 2025, and 5% GST plus provincial sales tax rules in provinces like Manitoba and B.C. Use CRA's GST/HST calculator page for current rates.
- **Deductions:** software subscriptions, a share of home office costs, internet, a portion of your phone, and platform fees. Rules and limits are explained in [home office expenses for side hustles](/side-hustles/home-office-expenses-side-hustle-canada/).

Set money aside from every payment until you know your real tax bill; [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) walks through the math.

## Provincial notes

- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and you'll deal with Revenu Québec for QST once registered. French-language work is a real niche here.
- **Ontario:** highest Job Bank median for writers ($40.38), and the largest concentration of agencies and head offices in Toronto.
- **Alberta and B.C.:** medians of $38.56 and $36.44. Energy, tech and tourism companies hire B2B writers.
- **Atlantic Canada:** Nova Scotia's median is $33.00. Most clients can be anywhere in Canada, so you aren't limited to local rates.
- **The territories:** Yukon-based Eva Holland built a national print career, which shows location matters less than the client list.

## What to watch out for

Freelance writing has real downsides you should plan for.

- **Late and slow payments.** J-Source found most Canadian outlets paid 30 to 90 days after sign-off. Ask for deposits, use clear payment terms and invoice the same day you deliver.
- **Low-paying work crowds out better work.** Like Holland, set a floor rate and stop taking jobs below it.
- **AI is shrinking the bottom of the market.** Generic content is the most exposed. Specialize, offer interviews or reporting, and sell editing and strategy.
- **Platform dependence.** Upwork and Fiverr fees, rules and algorithms can change. Build direct clients and a referral base alongside any marketplace.
- **Scope creep.** "One more round" turns a $260 post into a $20-an-hour job. Your contract's revision limit protects your rate.
- **Currency swings.** US clients pay in US dollars and wire fees can eat into payments. Holland noted a $15 flat fee for wire transfers in 2018; check your own bank's current fees.
