## Which books are worth reselling in Canada?

Textbooks in a current edition, and copies with a feature you can point to, are the books worth pricing in Canada. A mass-market paperback that sells for a few dollars is usually worth less than the mailer and the stamp once a fee comes off.

A textbook scan is a clue, not a sale. Courses change editions. If you cannot confirm which edition a campus bookstore or the listing actually wants, leave the book. A previous edition can sit until you donate it.

Collectible copies are the other pile: a stated first printing, a dust jacket you still have, or a signature you can describe honestly. "Looks old" is not a printing statement. If you cannot read the copyright page, you cannot claim a printing.

General stock, meaning ordinary novels, diet books, and last decade's bestsellers, is the pile that teaches the postage lesson. Price one of them with a real quote before you buy a box.

| Typical failure | A new edition strands the copy you bought | The mailer costs more than the book |

## How do Amazon.ca and AbeBooks charge for books?

Amazon.ca lists Media - Books, DVD, Music, Software, Video at 15% of the total sales price. That total includes the item price, shipping, and gift wrap, or a minimum referral fee if the table shows one for that row. Read the books row on the [pricing page](https://sell.amazon.ca/pricing) before you rely on 15% alone. The public page does not print a separate media closing fee. Amazon's [estimate page](https://sell.amazon.ca/pricing/estimate) says the revenue calculator includes variable closing fees. Open it on the exact book and read every line before you treat a scan as profit.

Plan fees sit on top. The Individual plan is $1.49 per item sold. The Professional plan is CAD $29.99 a month. Amazon's page says Individual sellers use Amazon's set shipping rates and have to ship at those rates even when the shipping credit is less than the actual cost. Professional sellers set their own shipping rates except for books, music, video, and DVD. On books, you receive the credit Amazon sets. Price the book so the credit can cover a real mailer to the buyer's province.

AbeBooks publishes a different schedule, and it is in US dollars. The [fee schedule](https://www.abebooks.com/docs/booksellercentral/agreement/fee-schedule-usd-en-2025-07) says selling fees on that page are USD, and that your billing currency (USD, GBP, or EUR) is set from your bank account at signup and cannot be changed. The [sell page](https://www.abebooks.com/books/sell/) describes two plans:

- **Basic:** no monthly subscription, US$1 per item sold, commission of 15% on the first US$500 of the item amount and 10% above that, and no processing fee.
- **Premium:** a monthly subscription from US$25, based on how many listings you had. The schedule's first band, 0 to 500 listings, is US$25, and the top band is US$500. Commission is 8%, with a minimum of US$0.50 and a maximum of US$40 per item. On AbeBooks.com, a processing fee of 5.5% applies to the first US$500 of the order and 3.5% above that, with a US$0.50 minimum. Other AbeBooks sites use different processing rates. Read the schedule for the site the order comes from.

A US-dollar fee on a Canadian book is easy to undercount. Convert it on the day you price, and add your own postage in Canadian dollars.

eBay.ca is a third option for a book a collector will search for. Its fee is a percentage of the total amount of the sale plus a per-order fee, and the table varies by category. Books are not the sports-card row. Use the [eBay.ca selling fees page](https://www.ebay.ca/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822) for the category you actually choose.

Paid scanner apps are optional. Some charge a monthly subscription whether you buy a book or not. Read the current price on the developer's site and subtract a month of it from your sketch. A free scan inside a seller app still has to be checked against the fee lines above. This guide does not link those apps as affiliates.

## How do you flip books in Canada without losing the postage?

Flip books in Canada by checking the edition, quoting postage, and mailing only a copy whose price covers both. Local pickup is the test that removes the stamp.

### Step 1: Read the copyright page

Note the edition, the year, and any printing line. A textbook without an edition number is a guess.

### Step 2: Scan, then open the fee breakdown

A ranked list of asking prices is not a sold price. On Amazon.ca, run the revenue calculator for that ISBN and read the referral fee, the plan fee, the shipping credit, and any closing fee the calculator shows.

### Step 3: Weigh the book

Postage follows weight and thickness. A kitchen scale is enough for a first quote. A <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="padded book mailer" search="bubble mailer for books" /> should match the book you have, not a bulk case of the wrong size. Buy a few mailers after the first copy is worth shipping.

### Step 4: Decide local or mailed

Campus-area pickup can beat a national listing for a heavy textbook. Mail a lighter copy when the shipping credit or the buyer's postage covers the quote.

### Step 5: Describe the copy you have

Highlighting, a cracked spine, and a missing disc are the listing. A buyer who needed a clean copy will return a marked one, and you pay the trip twice.

### Step 6: Keep the purchase note

Write what you paid, even at a church sale. That number is the cost of the copy when you do the books later.

## What does a textbook flip look like after Amazon.ca fees?

A textbook flip in Canada is the amount you receive after the 15% media referral fee, the plan fee, the book, and the real postage, with any closing fee added from the calculator.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical textbook.**

Suppose a current-edition textbook costs $8 at a thrift store and the total sale price Amazon uses is $40, item plus the shipping amount that sits inside the referral-fee base. Referral fee at the published 15%: 0.15 × $40 = $6. Individual plan: $1.49. Suppose the mailer and the postage you actually pay are $11. That $11 is an assumption, not a Canada Post or Amazon credit quote. Cost of the book: $8.

$40 − $6 − $1.49 − $11 − $8 = $13.51 before any variable closing fee the revenue calculator shows, before a return, and before tax. If the same arithmetic is run on a novel with a $6 total sale price, 15% is $0.90, the plan fee is still $1.49, and an $11 mailer already exceeds the book. That is why ordinary stock loses. Do not copy $40 or $11.

On AbeBooks Basic, a US$20 item would be charged 15% of US$20, which is US$3, plus the US$1 per-item fee, for US$4 in fees before your Canadian postage. The schedule is USD. Convert on the day you list.

## What mistakes are common when flipping books in Canada?

Common book-flipping mistakes in Canada are buying previous editions, ignoring the shipping credit, and photocopying a textbook instead of reselling the copy.

- **A scanner bar that ignores fees.** Subtract 15%, the plan fee, postage, and any closing fee the calculator shows.
- **Professional-plan shipping on books.** You do not set that rate. Amazon's page says the exception is books, music, video, and DVD.
- **A subscription you forgot.** A scanner app's monthly price is due in a quiet month too.
- **Copying pages to sell.** [Copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/learn-copyright-canadian-intellectual-property-office) is the sole right to produce or reproduce a work. Section 3 of the [Copyright Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-3.html) includes the right to sell a tangible copy in a case where ownership has not already been transferred with the copyright owner's authorization. Reselling a book you bought is a later sale of that copy. Reproducing the book is a different act. This is not legal advice.
- **Guessing a signature.** Say what you can see. A name written inside a cover is an inscription you can photograph, not an authentication.

## Which tax rules apply to book flipping in Canada?

Book flipping in Canada is generally business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) when you buy books to resell. Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income. Clearing a personal shelf once is a different question from an ongoing buy-and-sell. The rental form is the wrong one; see [T2125 versus T776](/side-hustles/t2125-vs-t776-canada/).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). Marketplace sales can fall under the CRA's [digital-economy rules for qualifying goods](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/sales-good.html), where the platform may collect the tax for a seller who is not registered. Keep the fee reports. Mixed-up questions are in the [side hustle tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/).
