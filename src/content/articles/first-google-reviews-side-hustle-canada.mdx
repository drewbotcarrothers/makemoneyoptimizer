- **Ask after a real job, and ask every customer the same way.** Google's review tips say you can share a link or a QR code. They also say incentives are prohibited.
- **Do not pay, discount, or gift a review.** The Maps policy bars payment, discounts, and free goods or services in exchange for a review, a change, or the removal of a negative review.
- **Do not review your own business, and do not ask only the happy customers.** Conflict-of-interest reviews and selective asks are in the same prohibited-content rules.

Your first Google reviews in Canada are notes from people who actually hired your side hustle, posted on the Google Business Profile that represents that work. They suit a cleaner, a lawn route, a dog walker, a tutor who meets in person, or any other service that shows up where the customer is. They do not suit an online-only offer that is not eligible for a profile, and they do not suit anyone willing to buy a star rating. Google's rules are the whole method. A discount for a review is not a shortcut. It is a policy violation.

## What does Google allow when you ask for a review in Canada?

Google allows a plain ask, a link, and a QR code. It does not allow a reward. The [tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) say reviews should reflect a genuine experience, and that offering incentives such as free or discounted goods or services in exchange for posting, changing, or removing a review is prohibited. The same page says you can remind customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code, that you should reply, and that a mix of positive and negative reviews can be more trustworthy than a wall of perfect ones. Customers need a Google Account to post. The account can use an email that is not Gmail.

The Maps [prohibited and restricted content](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) policy is the enforcement page. It says contributions should reflect a real experience. It bars offering incentives, including payment, discounts, or free goods or services, in exchange for any review or for revising or removing a negative one. It bars selectively soliciting positive reviews and discouraging negative ones. It also covers content posted because of an incentive, and reviews biased by a conflict of interest. Google's [incentivized or biased reviews](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/16597558?hl=en) page is the short name for that rule. A review you write about your own side hustle is a conflict of interest. A review from a friend who never hired you is not a genuine experience.

| Allowed | Not allowed |
| --- | --- |
| A link or QR code after the job, offered to customers generally | A discount, gift card, free extra, or cash for a review |
| A polite reply that does not pitch a deal | Asking only the customers who smiled |
| Flagging a review that breaks the content policy | Paying someone to remove or rewrite a negative review |
| Waiting until you have done the work | Seeding the profile with your own account or a relative's |

## Who can have a Google Business Profile for a side hustle in Canada?

A side hustle can have a profile when the business makes in-person contact with customers during its stated hours, which is the eligibility idea in Google's [Business Profile policy overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en). Online-only businesses are the example that page's readers get sent back to check. Read it before you create a listing for a service nobody can visit or that never visits them. A keyword-stuffed business name is a separate way to lose the profile. The local SEO guide states the boundary: sell an accurate listing, not a rank.

If you are not eligible, do not invent an address. Reviews on a listing you should not have are not a growth plan.

## How do you get the first reviews without breaking Google's rules?

Get them by finishing the job, asking every customer the same way, and replying without a coupon.

### Step 1: Claim the profile you are allowed to have

Use the real business name, the real service area, and hours you keep. If you are not sure you qualify, stop at the policy overview. The setup detail for people who later do this as a client service is the [Google Business Profile guide](/side-hustles/google-business-profile-side-hustle-canada/).

### Step 2: Create the review link or QR code

Google's tips page says that is the reminder. Put it on the invoice, the receipt, or a card you hand over at the end of the job. Do not put a reward next to it.

### Step 3: Ask after the work, in the same words, every time

A sentence is enough: the job is done, here is the link, a review is optional. Say it to the difficult job and the easy one. Selective asks are on the prohibited list.

### Step 4: Reply without a promotion

The tips page says replies should be professional, short, and not a place to offer deals. Thank a specific detail. On a negative review, do not post the customer's private information. If the situation is messy, invite them to phone or email. If you believe the review breaks Google's content rules, flag it. Do not offer a refund in the reply in exchange for a change. A refund you owe for a bad job is a customer-service decision you handle off the review, and it is still not payment for a new star rating.

### Step 5: Keep a count of asks, not a count of stars you bought

Ten finished jobs and ten asks can still produce two reviews. That is allowed. Buying the other eight is not. Neighbourhood apps are a different kind of comment. Nextdoor recommendations are explained in the [local client channels](/side-hustles/local-client-channels-canada/) comparison. They do not replace this profile.

### What a first month can look like

This is a sequence, not a promise of how many reviews appear.

- **Week 1:** Confirm eligibility. Claim the profile. Save the review link.
- **Week 2:** Do the jobs you already booked. Ask at the end of each one. Reply to anything that posts.
- **Week 3:** Same ask. Do not add a discount because the count is low.
- **Week 4:** Read the replies you sent. Cut any sentence that offers a deal. If a review violates the policy, flag it. If it is merely unhappy and true, answer it.

## What review mistakes get a side hustle in trouble in Canada?

The mistakes are paying for reviews, asking only fans, reviewing yourself, and stuffing the business name with search phrases. Also common: emailing a scraped list of past customers you do not have consent to email. The ask belongs at the job, or in a message the customer expects. Commercial email has its own consent rules. The client-channel guide links the anti-spam pages.

A profile manager who offers "twenty reviews in a week" as a product is offering the prohibited thing. If you later sell profile help, the boundary is already written in the [first Google Business Profile client](/side-hustles/google-business-profile-first-client-canada/) guide: no fake reviews, no incentives.

## Which tax rules apply to a side hustle that collects Google reviews in Canada?

The reviews are not income. The jobs that led to them are. Service income in Canada is reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) when you are carrying on the activity. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). A review does not change that test.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Recheck Google's review tips and the prohibited-content policy when you print a QR code. The policy text is the constraint, not a blog summary.
