An art print side hustle in Canada sells reproductions of your own paintings, drawings or digital art, printed by a fine-art lab or on your own printer, through Etsy, your own site or a market table. Canadian labs listed an 8x10 giclée print at $15 (Imagefoundry, Toronto, sample price) to $56.87 (The Lab Vancouver, Hahnemühle paper) on October 19, 2026, and Canada Post's oversize letter rate within Canada starts at $2.61. Copyright in your original work is automatic, so the business is yours to license as well as print.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Print cost, 8x10:** $15 sample price on cotton rag or baryta (Imagefoundry, Toronto); $25.41 on enhanced matte paper or $56.87 on Hahnemühle fine art paper (The Lab Vancouver), which takes 15% off extra prints of the same image.
- **Print cost, 11x14:** $30 (Imagefoundry) to $38.04 to $77.85 (The Lab Vancouver).
- **Etsy fees for a Canadian seller:** US$0.20 a listing, 6.5% transaction, 3% + $0.25 processing on Canadian and US orders, and a 0.50% Regulatory Operating fee ([Etsy](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014483627-What-are-the-Fees-and-Taxes-for-Selling-on-Etsy)).
- **Postage (oversize mail):** $2.61 up to 100 g in Canada, $4.29 to the US, $8.60 international ([Canada Post](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/personal/stamp-prices.page)).
- **Copyright:** a work is protected the moment it is created and fixed; registering with CIPO is voluntary ([CIPO](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/guide-copyright)).

## Which rules apply to selling art prints in Canada?

The rule that matters most is copyright: you can only sell prints of work you own the rights to. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office's [guide to copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/guide-copyright) says a work is protected the moment it is created and fixed in a material form, that registering with CIPO is voluntary (a registration certificate is evidence of ownership, which can be challenged in court), and that the owner has the sole right to produce or reproduce the work or a substantial part of it. Copyright generally lasts for the life of the author, the rest of that calendar year, and 70 years after; the term was extended from 50 years on December 30, 2022.

What that means for a print seller:

- **Your own originals:** you hold the reproduction right, so you can print and sell them.
- **Someone else's art, characters or logos:** you need permission from the rights holder before selling prints.
- **Commissioned work:** put in writing who keeps the copyright and whether you may sell prints of it.
- **Licensing to a business:** when a company wants your art on a product or ad, CARFAC-RAAV's [2026 reproduction royalty schedule](https://carfac-raav.ca/2026-en/2026-en-3/) (Section 3, commercial and advertising) sets minimum recommended royalties in Canadian dollars, excluding taxes. It also notes that moral rights protect a work from unapproved alteration or distortion, including overprinting and cropping, and require credit to the artist.

## Illustrative example: what one 8x10 print earns on Etsy

This is an illustrative example. The print cost, supplies, Etsy fees and postage are sourced above; your sale price, the shipping you charge and the package weight (assumed under 100 g) are assumptions.

- Assumed sale: $35 for the print + $4 shipping charged = $39 order total.
- Print: $15 (Imagefoundry sample price) or $25.41 (The Lab Vancouver, enhanced matte).
- Supplies: sleeve $0.10 + mailer $0.66 = $0.76.
- Postage within Canada, up to 100 g: $2.61.
- Etsy fees: 10% × $39 = $3.90, + $0.25 processing + about $0.28 listing (US$0.20 at 1.4154 CAD per USD, Bank of Canada, October 16, 2026) = $4.43.
- Left at the $15 print cost: $39 − $15 − $0.76 − $2.61 − $4.43 = $16.20.
- Left at the $25.41 print cost: $39 − $25.41 − $0.76 − $2.61 − $4.43 = $5.79.

| Sale price (assumed) + $4 shipping | Etsy fees | Left with a $15 print | Left with a $25.41 print |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $25 | $3.43 | $7.20 | −$3.21 |
| $35 | $4.43 | $16.20 | $5.79 |
| $45 | $5.43 | $25.20 | $14.79 |

Formula: (price + shipping) − print − supplies − postage − [10% × (price + shipping) + $0.53]. The print cost is the lever: ordering several prints of one image (15% off extras at The Lab Vancouver) or asking a lab for a quantity quote moves the right-hand column a lot. A market table removes postage and Etsy fees but adds a table fee and a card reader.

## Which print products sell for a Canadian artist?

The products that sell are the ones that turn one artwork into several price points. These ideas suit an artist starting with a handful of originals:

1. **8x10 prints in sleeves.** The entry price point at markets and online.
2. **Matted 8x10 prints (11x14 outside).** Ready to frame, a higher price for $1.95 in mat kit cost.
3. **11x14 and 16x20 prints.** Fewer sales, larger margin per sale.
4. **Small editions, numbered and signed.** Say the edition size and keep to it.
5. **Local landmark and neighbourhood scenes.** Sell to people who live there and to visitors.
6. **Pet and house portraits as commissions, with print rights in writing.** Commission once, sell prints only if the client agrees.
7. **Botanical and wildlife studies of Canadian species.** Suits markets, cottage-country shops and gift buyers.
8. **Holiday cards from your artwork.** A low-price add-on to a print sale.
9. **Calendars for the coming year.** Sold in October to December.
10. **Print bundles (three small prints).** Spreads postage and Etsy's fixed fees.
11. **Gallery-shop and café consignment.** Ask the commission rate and payment terms in writing.
12. **Licensing to local businesses.** Use the CARFAC-RAAV schedule as your minimum.
13. **Digital downloads of the same art.** No postage at all; see [printables](/side-hustles/printables-side-hustle-canada/).
14. **Print-on-demand products from the same files.** Mugs and shirts without stock; see [print-on-demand](/side-hustles/print-on-demand-side-hustle-canada/).
15. **Proof-and-test runs before a market.** Imagefoundry's $25 proof print checks colour before you order a batch.

## What does a first 30 days look like?

A first month gets three to five images scanned and proofed, a small batch printed, and the first listings live.

| Week | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Pick 3 to 5 originals you own outright; scan or photograph them at high resolution; ask a lab for a quote |
| 2 | Order one proof per image; compare colour with the original; fix files before the batch |
| 3 | Order a small batch of 8x10s; buy sleeves and mailers; weigh one finished package for postage |
| 4 | List on Etsy (one listing per image, sizes as variations); book a market table for November or December |

### A listing you can copy

```
[Title of artwork] - giclée art print, 8x10 / 11x14
Printed on [paper name] with archival pigment inks by a Canadian fine-art lab.
Signed on the back. Ships flat in a sleeve with a backing board in a rigid mailer.
Frame not included. Colours may vary slightly between screens.
Original artwork (c) [year] [your name]. Purchase does not include reproduction rights.
```

### A commission clause you can copy

```
Copyright in the artwork stays with the artist. The client receives the original
[or a print] for personal display. The artist [may / may not] sell prints of this
artwork. The client may share photos of it online with credit to the artist.
```

## Which Canadian artists sell prints, and what do they say?

Three Canadian artists described their print and merchandise shops to [CBC Arts](https://www.cbc.ca/arts/gifting-artist-made-merch-this-holiday-season-it-matters-to-these-makers-1.6279305) in December 2021. The article gives no dollar figures:

- **Ambivalently Yours (Prince Edward County, Ontario):** plans each year on earning about half her income from shirts, cards and other items inspired by her art.
- **Hilary Jane Boulé Petersen (Montreal):** her online shop sold nothing but prints at first, for fans who could not get a tattoo appointment; prints remained her top-selling item.

## Which tax rules apply to selling art prints in Canada?

Print sales are business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), with lab costs, supplies, postage, Etsy fees and market table fees as expenses. GST/HST registration is required once taxable sales pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters, under the CRA's [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) rules; the [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) explains the test. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
