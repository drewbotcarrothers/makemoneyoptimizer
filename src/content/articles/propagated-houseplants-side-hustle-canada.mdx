A propagated houseplant side hustle in Canada roots cuttings from plants you already own, pots them up, and sells them locally or online. Canadian garden centres and plant shops listed 4-inch houseplants at $4.99 to $19.99 on October 19, 2026, which sets the ceiling for a home seller, and a 4-inch nursery pot cost about $0.14 in a 100-pack. The rules to know are the CFIA's Japanese beetle soil rules in parts of British Columbia and Etsy's roughly 10% in fees if you sell online.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Shop prices for 4-inch plants:** Vermeer's (Welland, Ont.) listed pothos at $4.99 to $9.99 and Monstera 'Thai Constellation' at $19.99; Plant Gather (Kelowna, B.C.) listed 4-inch Monstera deliciosa at $12 and Tradescantia at $8 to $10.
- **Supplies:** 100 four-inch pots for $13.99, 2 quarts of perlite for $15.99 and 75 g of Doff rooting powder for $15.62 on Amazon.ca (October 19, 2026).
- **Etsy fees for a Canadian seller:** US$0.20 a listing, 6.5% transaction, 3% + $0.25 processing on Canadian and US orders, and a 0.50% Regulatory Operating fee ([Etsy](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014483627-What-are-the-Fees-and-Taxes-for-Selling-on-Etsy)).
- **Soil rule (B.C.):** potted plants with soil or growing media need a CFIA movement certificate to leave a Japanese beetle regulated area in Burnaby, Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Kamloops or Vancouver; cuttings without soil are exempt ([CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/plant-health/invasive-pests-and-plants/insects/japanese-beetle/japanese-beetle-bc/guidance-movement-plants)).
- **Business penalty:** CFIA penalties for violations in the course of business run $1,300 to $10,000.

## Which rules apply to selling houseplants in Canada?

Selling houseplants you propagated at home is mostly a matter of soil: the federal Plant Protection Act regulates moving certain plants and soil, and the CFIA restricts plants with soil leaving Japanese beetle regulated areas. Canada Post's [plants page](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/non-mailable-matter/plants.page) says the Plant Protection Act and CITES (the endangered species convention) regulate the movement of certain plants, seeds, plant parts and soil, and refers mailers to the CFIA.

### What does the CFIA's Japanese beetle rule mean for a B.C. seller?

The CFIA's [regulated areas page](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/plant-health/invasive-pests-and-plants/insects/japanese-beetle/regulated-areas) (effective June 1, 2026) lists regulated areas in Burnaby, Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam, two parts of Kamloops, and Vancouver. From inside one of those areas, a movement certificate is required year-round to move plants with soil or growing media attached, including peat moss or bark, out of the area. The CFIA's [B.C. guidance](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/plant-health/invasive-pests-and-plants/insects/japanese-beetle/japanese-beetle-bc/guidance-movement-plants) spells out what is exempt and what is not:

| Item | Needs a movement certificate to leave the area? |
| --- | --- |
| Cuttings, clippings and other above-ground plant parts | No |
| Plants shaken or washed "substantially free from soil" (no clump over 12.7 mm) | No |
| Non-commercial houseplants grown indoors and never put outdoors or on a patio | No |
| Potted plants for planting, including plants in soil-less growing media | Yes, year-round |
| Potted houseplants that were grown outdoors or used as patio plants | Yes |

The guidance says retailers in a regulated area selling potted plants to customers who may take them out of the area must use one of four options. Option 1 is the simplest for a home seller: plants kept exclusively in a closed indoor area during the June 15 to October 15 flight period can leave without a certificate. The other options involve CFIA treatments, certification programs or a CFIA-issued certificate with signage and inspection. Applications go to the CFIA at least 5 business days before the move. Penalties range from $500 to $1,300 for individuals and from $1,300 to $10,000 for violations committed in the course of business.

In practice: if you live in one of those areas, sell unrooted or bare-root cuttings to buyers outside it, keep stock plants indoors all year, and call the CFIA (604-292-5742) before selling potted stock to someone who will take it out of the area. ### Does the Japanese beetle rule affect shipping between provinces?

Yes, for potted plants. The CFIA's [directive D-96-15](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/plant-health/invasive-pests-and-plants/directives/horticulture/96-15) (16th revision, effective June 2, 2025) regulates plants with soil, soil-related matter or growing media moving within Canada. It says Japanese beetle is established in Ontario, Quebec, Prince Edward Island, New Brunswick and Nova Scotia, and it sorts every province into Categories 1 to 4. Potted plants moving from a Category 2, 3 or 4 province (or a B.C. regulated area) into a Category 1 or 2 area need a CFIA-issued movement certificate, backed by pest-free production, treatment or a certification program. Shipments into Category 3 or 4 provinces need no Japanese beetle certification. Check Appendix 1 of the directive for your destination's category before you ship.

The directive's exemptions match the B.C. guidance: plants and plant parts "substantially free" of soil, and non-commercial houseplants grown indoors. Plants grown to sell are commercial, so for a seller the practical exemption is the bare-root or unrooted cutting.

## Illustrative example: one month of selling 4-inch pots

This is an illustrative example. The shop prices, pot price and Etsy fees are sourced above; the number of plants, the mix cost, your sale prices and the shipping charge are assumptions.

**Local sales (Marketplace or a market table):**

- Assumed: 20 rooted plants a month, potted in 4-inch pots.
- Sourced: $0.14 a pot. Assumed: $0.50 a pot for potting mix and perlite.
- Cost per plant: $0.14 + $0.50 = $0.64. For 20: $12.80.
- Assumed price: $8, at or below the $8 to $10 that Plant Gather charges for 4-inch Tradescantia.
- Revenue: 20 × $8 = $160. Left before your time: $160 − $12.80 = $147.20.

| Price per 4-inch pot (assumed) | 20 pots | 40 pots | Cost of pots and mix (20 / 40) | Left (20 / 40) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $6 | $120 | $240 | $12.80 / $25.60 | $107.20 / $214.40 |
| $8 | $160 | $320 | $12.80 / $25.60 | $147.20 / $294.40 |
| $12 | $240 | $480 | $12.80 / $25.60 | $227.20 / $454.40 |

**One Etsy sale:**

- Formula: Etsy fees = 10% × (item price + shipping charged) + $0.25 processing + about $0.28 listing (US$0.20 at the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4154 CAD per USD on October 16, 2026).
- Assumed: a $12 plant with $15 shipping charged to the buyer.
- Fees: 10% × $27 = $2.70, + $0.25 + $0.28 = $3.23.
- Left: $27 − $3.23 − $0.64 (pot and mix) − the postage you actually pay − the box and padding.

The Etsy fee is charged on the shipping you collect too, which is why a cheap plant with expensive shipping earns little online. Bundles (three plants in one box) spread the shipping and the fixed fees across more revenue.

## What does a first season look like?

A first season starts by building stock plants, because you can only sell what your stock plants can spare.

| Weeks | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 | Choose 3 to 5 plants from the shop-price list above; take cuttings from your own healthy plants |
| 3 to 6 | Root in water or mix; pot the rooted cuttings into 4-inch pots; label each one |
| 7 to 8 | Photograph full pots in daylight; check for pests on every plant before listing |
| 9 to 10 | List locally at or under shop prices; try one market table or a plant swap |
| 11 to 12 | Repeat what sold; add hanging baskets or gift trios for December |

If you live in a B.C. Japanese beetle regulated area, decide before week 9 whether you will sell only within the area, sell cuttings without soil, or contact the CFIA about potted stock.

### A pest check you can copy

```
Before every sale
[ ] Leaves checked top and underside (webbing, sticky spots, white fluff)
[ ] Soil surface checked for gnats; pot drained, not soggy
[ ] New plants quarantined 2 weeks away from your stock plants
[ ] Plant was grown indoors all year (B.C. regulated areas: note this on the tag)
[ ] Name and care tag in the pot
```

```
[Plant name], 4-inch pot, rooted and established - $[__]
Grown from my own plants, indoors in [city]. Pot is [__] inches; plant is
about [__] inches tall. Rooted [month]. Pest-checked before pickup.
Pickup [area] or meet at [public place]. 3 for $[__].
```

## Who has turned propagating into a business in Canada?

Dolly Villedo in Winnipeg started by propagating plants at home and selling them on Facebook Marketplace, according to a September 2020 [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/manitoba/plant-momma-winnipeg-1.5740069) report. Her husband converted their garage into a greenhouse to keep up with demand, and she opened the PlantMomma storefront on Stella Avenue in mid-August 2020. She told CBC it had taken three years to grow the business to that point. The article does not give sales or income figures.

## Which tax rules apply to selling houseplants in Canada?

Plant sales are business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), with pots, soil, labels, grow lights, Etsy fees and postage as expenses. GST/HST registration is required once taxable sales pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters, under the CRA's [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) rules; the [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) explains the test. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
