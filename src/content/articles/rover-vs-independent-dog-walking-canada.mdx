<QuickAnswer>

- **Short answer:** start on Rover if you have no local network, because Rover brings clients, handles payment and backs bookings with the Rover Guarantee. Go independent once regulars and referrals fill your week, because you keep 100% of the price.
- **Rover's cut:** a $49 profile review fee, then 20% of each booking, so a $25 walk pays you $20. In Rover's pilot cities (Saskatoon, Regina, Québec, Nanaimo, Calgary, Vancouver, Ottawa-Gatineau) the fee is 30% on a client's first $499, 15% to $999 and 10% after.
- **Owners pay more on Rover too:** an 11% owner fee, capped at $65, turns a $25 walk into $27.75.
- **Independent costs:** liability insurance (Zensurance's example is about $100 a year for $1 million general plus $1 million professional liability), payment fees if you take cards (Square: 2.8% + 30¢ on an online invoice), and permits such as Toronto's $305.99 a year for four to six dogs.

</QuickAnswer>

## What does the Rover Guarantee cover, and what does it not?

The Rover Guarantee reimburses certain costs when the responsible person does not pay, but Rover's [Canadian guarantee terms](https://www.rover.com/ca-en/terms/guarantee/), effective 28 September 2026, state that it is not insurance and that sitters remain solely responsible for any insurance the law requires.

| Covered loss | Canadian limit per incident | Key conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vet costs for a pet injured in your care | $25,000 | $250 minimum contribution; vet documents within 14 days; treatment within 30 days |
| Damage to the owner's property | $100,000 | $250 minimum contribution; photos and proof of value within 14 days |
| Damage to your property caused by the client's pet | $25,000 | $250 minimum contribution; excludes pet-care gear and behaviour you were told about and could have prevented |
| Medical costs of a third party injured by the client's pet | $100,000 | Rover must accept it would be a valid legal claim |

Every claim needs a booking accepted before the incident and paid through Rover at more than $10 per service date, and you must notify Rover during the booking or within 48 hours. The Guarantee excludes meet-and-greets, vehicle accidents, other people's dogs walked alongside the booked pet, and pre-existing, breed-specific or orthopedic conditions. A walk for a neighbour you invoice yourself has no Guarantee at all, and Rover's help centre tells sitters to [decline requests to be paid outside Rover](https://support-ca.rover.com/hc/en-ca/articles/360036586192-What-if-a-client-wants-to-pay-me-directly-not-on-Rover-s-site).

## What does independent dog walking cost in Canada?

Independent dog walking in Canada costs an insurance premium, card fees if you accept cards, and any local permit. There is no per-booking fee.

- **Insurance.** Zensurance's [dog walker page](https://www.zensurance.com/pet-services-insurance/dog-walker) says an individual dog walker can expect to spend about $100 a year for $1 million commercial liability and $1 million professional liability. Its listed coverage includes loss of keys ($5,000 limit), care, custody and control of the animals ($5,000), and a veterinary fees extension of $1,000 per animal and $5,000 in total. APOLLO's [pet business page](https://apollocover.com/business-insurance/pet-business) says most pet professionals pay $25 to $70 a month for full coverage. Your quote depends on dogs per walk, services and claims history.
- **Card payments.** Interac e-Transfer costs nothing on many personal accounts. [Square Canada](https://squareup.com/ca/en/pricing) charges 2.8% + 30¢ on an online invoice, 2.5% on a credit card tapped in person, and 0.75% + 7¢ on debit, with 1.5% more on cards issued outside Canada.
- **Permits.** Toronto's [Commercial Dog Walker Permit](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/commercial-dog-walker-permit/) is required to walk four to six dogs commercially in City parks. From January 1, 2026 it is $305.99 for a year, $153.00 for six months or $76.50 for three months, tax not included, and you must show $2,000,000 of commercial general liability insurance naming the City as an additional insured. Calgary has a [professional dog walker permit](https://www.calgary.ca/pets/permits.html) for walking more than six dogs off leash in an off-leash park, and Metro Vancouver requires a [commercial use permit](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/commercial-use-dog-walking-permit) in its regional parks. The permit applies whether the booking came through Rover or not.

A Toronto group walker needs a $2 million policy for the permit, so the $100 example, written for $1 million, is not the right quote for that walker.

## How much do you keep on Rover versus walking independently?

On a $25 walk you keep $20 on Rover's standard fee and $25 independently, and the client pays $27.75 on Rover against $25 direct. The examples below show a month and a first quarter with one regular.

### Illustrative example: one regular client, one month

**Assumed numbers for teaching, not a market rate or a typical result.** One client books five walks a week at $25 for four weeks: $500 in bookings.

| | Rover standard (20%) | Rover pilot city, new client | Independent, e-Transfer | Independent, Square invoice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Client pays | $555.00 ($500 + 11% owner fee) | $555.00 | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| Fees you pay | $100.00 | $149.85 | $0 | $14.30 |
| You keep | $400.00 | $350.15 | $500.00 | $485.70 |

The pilot fee: the first $499 at 30% is $149.70, and the remaining $1 at 15% is $0.15, for $149.85. The Square invoice: 2.8% × $500 = $14.00, plus $0.30.

### Illustrative example: the first three months with that client

| Month | Rover standard | Rover pilot city | Independent, e-Transfer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $400.00 | $350.15 | $500.00 |
| 2 | $400.00 | $425.05 | $500.00 |
| 3 | $400.00 | $450.00 | $500.00 |
| Total kept | $1,200.00 | $1,225.20 | $1,500.00 |

Month 2 starts with $500 of history, so $499 is charged at 15% ($74.85) and $1 at 10% ($0.10). Month 3 is entirely at 10% ($50). However the bookings are split, the pilot's fees on a client's first $1,500 come to $274.80, against $300 at the standard 20%. After the first month, a long-term client in a pilot city costs less on Rover than the standard fee, but never less than nothing.

### What the fee buys

On these numbers Rover costs $1,200 a year per regular client at the standard rate. An independent walker's insurance at Zensurance's $100 example is covered by the first week. The real question is whether you can find and keep the client without Rover. If a flyer run and a building notice board fill two slots, independent wins by a wide margin. If you would sit with an empty calendar, Rover's 20% is cheaper than no clients.

The owner fee matters for pricing. To take home $25 a walk on Rover's standard fee, you list at $31.25, and the client pays $34.69 with the 11% fee. A direct client paying $28 gets a cheaper walk and you earn $3 more.

## Who should pick Rover or independent dog walking in Canada?

Pick Rover when you need clients and independent walking when you already have them. This is a decision rule from the published fees, not a ranking.

| If you are… | Lean toward | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New to the area with no pet-owner network | Rover | Search and reviews bring first bookings |
| In a pilot city with long-term regulars | Rover can work | Fees drop to 10% once a client's history passes $1,000 |
| Known in your neighbourhood or building | Independent | No 20% fee, and the owner pays less |
| Walking four to six dogs in Toronto parks | Either, with the City permit and a $2 million policy | The permit applies regardless of platform |
| Unwilling to chase payments | Rover | Payment is collected before the service |
| Worried about a vet bill you cannot cover | Rover plus your own policy | The Guarantee covers vet costs up to $25,000 after a $250 contribution, but is not insurance |

You can use Rover for the first clients and build a separate list of your own from neighbours and referrals. Keep each Rover client's bookings on Rover, as Rover's help centre asks, and market your own service separately.

## What mistakes do dog walkers make choosing between Rover and going independent?

Dog walkers most often ignore the pilot tiers, treat the Rover Guarantee as insurance, or skip local permits.

- **Assuming 20% applies everywhere.** In pilot cities, a new client's first $499 is charged at 30%.
- **Treating the Guarantee as insurance.** Rover's terms say it is not, and that claims under $250 get nothing.
- **Pricing without the fee.** To keep $20 a walk on Rover's standard fee, list at $25.
- **Walking extra dogs on a booking.** The Guarantee excludes other people's dogs walked alongside the booked pet.
- **Skipping the permit.** Toronto's permit covers four to six dogs in City parks and requires $2 million of liability insurance.

## Which tax rules apply to Rover and independent dog walkers in Canada?

Dog walking income from Rover or direct clients is generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Report the full booking amount as income, and deduct Rover's service fee, the $49 profile fee, card fees, permits and insurance as business expenses. Platforms can report earnings to the CRA, as covered in [tax on reselling and gig apps](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/). Track e-Transfers and cash with the same care as app deposits; see [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).

GST/HST registration follows the CRA's [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), counting Rover and direct income together.
