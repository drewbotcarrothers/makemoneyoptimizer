## How much does it cost to start 3D printing for profit in Canada?

3D printing for profit in Canada costs about $360 to start with an entry-level printer and one spool, about $549 with a faster printer, tools and mailers, and about $870 with an enclosed printer, or about $1,320 with a year of insurance. All figures use Amazon.ca prices checked on October 9, 2026, with 13% Ontario HST.

## Who should use a 3D printing startup budget in Canada?

This budget suits people who can design or source printable files they're allowed to sell, have a room they can ventilate, and are patient with failed prints while they learn. The lean kit suits learning and first local sales. The standard kit suits steady small orders. The pro kit suits printing materials that need an enclosure, or running the printer for long hours.

It's a poor fit if the only place for the printer is a bedroom you sleep in, or if your product ideas copy characters, logos or designs you don't have a commercial licence for. The [Copyright Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-3.html) gives the owner the sole right to reproduce a work.

## What does ventilation and safety cost?

The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety's [additive manufacturing fact sheet](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/additive_manufacturing.pdf) says heating filament can release volatile organic compounds and ultrafine particles, gives PLA and ABS as examples, and says to keep exposure as low as reasonably achievable. It lists enclosure and local exhaust ventilation as controls. Read the safety data sheet for each filament you buy.

Budget ventilation with the printer, not after. A cheap printer running in a closed bedroom isn't a saving. Resin printing needs gloves, more ventilation and a plan for handling liquid resin, so it's a separate budget.

## How many sales does it take to break even on 3D printing?

**Illustrative. Uses the $1.84 cost per print above and an assumed $22 sale price. Verify the arithmetic for your own prices.**

Selling locally with no marketplace fee, each $22 print leaves $22.00 − $1.84 = $20.16. On Etsy, a Canadian seller nets about 0.90 × price − $0.53 (from the [printables pricing guide](/side-hustles/printables-pricing-canada/)), so $22 nets $19.27, leaving $19.27 − $1.84 = $17.43.

Counts are cost divided by margin, rounded up. For example, $548.98 ÷ $20.16 = 27.23, so 28 sales. Your time isn't counted. If each print takes 20 minutes of hands-on work for removing supports, photos and packing, 28 sales is about 9 hours and 20 minutes of work, plus the time spent learning the slicer.

## Where can 3D printing startup spending stay lower in Canada?

Spending stays lower with one printer, one spool, your own designs, and local pick-up sales before you ship anything. A marketplace can wait until a design has sold in person. Skip resin, a filament dryer and a second printer until the first design has paid for its filament more than once. The tools that come with most printers are enough to start.

## Where shouldn't you cut 3D printing costs?

Don't cut ventilation. CCOHS lists enclosure and local exhaust as controls because the emissions are real. Don't sell prints of designs you don't have the rights to. Don't skip test prints of a new design before you take orders, and don't skip insurance if you sell items people use, wear or give to children.

## Which tax rules apply to a 3D printing budget in Canada?

Print sales in Canada are generally business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net self-employment income. Filament, mailers and marketplace fees are current costs. A printer that lasts for years is usually claimed through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion, not deducted in full in year one.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). If you're registered, GST/HST on the printer and filament can often be claimed as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use. If you're not registered, you don't charge GST/HST and the HST in the kits is a real cost. Once registered, charge the rate from the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). See the [side hustle tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.
