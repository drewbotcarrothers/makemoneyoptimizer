## How much does it cost to start 3D printing for profit in Canada?

A 3D printing start in Canada is the printer plus a first spool, a few mailers, and ventilation you may already have in a window and a fan. An enclosed printer, a resin setup, or a second machine is a later budget.

| How you start | Cash to verify locally | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn on one filament printer | Often a few hundred dollars for an open printer, plus one spool | Samples of a design you made. Confirm the shelf price |
| Enclosed printer | Often more, sometimes past a thousand dollars | The same work with a cover. You still need the air to leave the room |
| Resin | Printer, wash supplies, gloves, and a plan for the liquid | A different chemical setup. Read the safety data sheet first |

## What belongs in a 3D printing startup budget in Canada?

A 3D printing budget in Canada needs the printer, filament, a way to ventilate, basic hand tools, and packaging. It does not need a farm of machines.

| Item | How to budget it | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Printer | The shelf price you confirm | A used machine is cheaper and still needs a working fan and a room you can air out |
| First spool | Often about $20–$40 for a kilogram, verify locally | Weigh what you use, including failures |
| Scraper, snips, a ruler | A small hardware total | Enough to free a print from the bed |
| Ventilation | The fan or enclosure the manual expects | CCOHS lists enclosure and local exhaust ahead of hoping the room is fine |
| Mailers | A few, sized to the object | Buy after you know the object's size |
| Electricity | Your bill's rate times the hours | Usually small next to filament and the printer. See the sketch |

The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety says heating filament can release volatile organic compounds and ultrafine particles, gives PLA and ABS as examples, and says to keep exposure as low as reasonably achievable. Budget the ventilation with the printer. A cheaper open machine in a closed bedroom is not a saving.

## How do failed prints and electricity change the unit cost?

Failed prints add filament. Electricity adds a few cents if you use a household rate and a short job. Your hands, after the printer stops, are the hours.

### Step 1: Record grams, including the ones you throw away

A slicer estimate for a successful print is the starting number. Add the failures from that design.

### Step 2: Read your electricity rate

Use the cents per kilowatt-hour on your bill. Provincial rates differ and change. This guide does not publish a provincial rate.

### Step 3: Add the mailer and the marketplace fee from the live page

A sketch that stops at filament will look better than the parcel you actually send.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical print shop.**

Suppose the printer's shelf price is $400. That is an assumption. Replace it with the price in front of you. Suppose filament is $30 a kilogram, also an assumption. A successful print uses 50 grams: 0.05 × $30 = $1.50 of plastic.

Suppose 2 of every 10 attempts fail and use the same 50 grams. That is an assumption, not a measured failure rate. Waste per success: 2 × 50 g / 10 = 10 g, or 0.01 × $30 = $0.30.

Suppose the printer draws 120 watts for 3 hours, which is 0.36 kilowatt-hours, and your bill is $0.15 per kilowatt-hour. Both numbers are assumptions. Electricity: 0.36 × $0.15 = $0.054, about $0.05.

A mailer, an assumed $1. A local sale price, an assumed $22, with no marketplace fee in this sketch. Subtract the fee from the site you actually use.

$22.00 − $1.50 − $0.30 − $0.05 − $1.00 = $19.15 before your time and before tax. The printer at $400 ÷ $19.15 is about 21 sales at these assumptions before the machine is covered, and that is before a marketplace fee. Electricity in the sketch is five cents. The failures and the $400 machine are the budget. Do not copy $22 or $400.

If you spend 20 minutes of hands-on time per success, an assumption covering supports, a photo, and packing, 21 sales are 7 hours of that work plus whatever you spent learning the slicer. The printer's three hours are machine time. Count them separately so you do not pretend they were free attention.

## Where can 3D printing startup spending stay lower in Canada?

Spending stays lower with one printer, one spool, designs you already know how to draw, and sales to people who pick up. A public marketplace can wait until the object has sold once in person. Skip resin, a filament dryer, and a second machine until the first design has paid for filament more than once.

You still spend the ventilation. CCOHS puts enclosure and local exhaust in the control list because the emissions are real. Opening a window on a printer with nowhere for the air to go is the cut that is not a saving.

## Which tax rules apply to a 3D printing budget in Canada?

Print sales in Canada are generally business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income. Filament, mailers, and marketplace fees are current costs to record. A printer that lasts for years may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a full deduction in year one.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). If you are registered, tax on the printer and filament can often be an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit. Charge the provincial rate from the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) once you are registered. See the [side hustle tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/).
