Starting reselling in Canada, meaning listing goods you can describe honestly on a marketplace or for local pickup, can cost about $15–$50 when the items are already in a closet, about $50–$180 for a typical ship-from-home start with mailers, a tape, and a scale, or about $400–$1,200 when you add sourced inventory, a storage bin that is actually full, and a month of your own checkout. Those reselling figures mix typical Canadian retail ranges to verify locally with platform fees in Canadian dollars on the pages linked below. They suit people who will photograph flaws, and they do not assume a pallet.

How to list, pack, and meet a buyer safely is the [reselling side hustle guide](/side-hustles/reselling-side-hustle-canada/). This page is the cash. The first listings come from what you already own.

## How much does reselling cost to start in Canada?

Reselling in Canada starts at about $15–$50 for tape, a few mailers, and closet stock, about $50–$180 when a scale, a measuring tape, and a bin join the kit, and about $400–$1,200 when you have spent real money on inventory, you are shipping often enough to care about fees, and a Shopify month is a choice you made on purpose. The ranges are not quotes. A city's second-hand shop licence, if you open a shop, is extra and local.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What you can sell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean closet clear-out | $15–$50 | Items you already own. Local pickup needs almost no packaging. A few mailers if one item will ship. |
| Typical ship-from-home | $50–$180 | A small pack of mailers, tape, a scale, a measuring tape, and one bin. Fees come out of the sale, not out of this column, until something sells. |
| Doing it properly | $400–$1,200 | The shipping kit, inventory you paid for and wrote down, FreshBooks or a disciplined spreadsheet once the volume is a month of sales, and Shopify Basic only if marketplace listings are no longer the whole business. A shop licence is a separate civic fee. |

Platform fees below are from the sellers' own pages. Those pages change. Confirm them before you price a listing.

## Who should use a reselling startup budget in Canada?

A reselling startup budget in Canada fits people who can describe condition, measure an item, and wait for a buyer without buying a second room of stock. The lean column fits a closet. Reselling is a poor fit if you need rent from the first weekend, if you will hide a stain, or if the budget is a pallet you have not opened.

Your own checkout, later, is [Shopify store startup costs](/side-hustles/shopify-store-startup-costs-canada/). A file with no mailer is [Notion template startup costs](/side-hustles/notion-templates-startup-costs-canada/).

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for reselling in Canada?

Ongoing reselling costs in Canada are the cost of the item, the fee on the page you actually used, the mailer or the drive, and returns. Reselling break-even in the sketch is a method, not an average flip profit.

**Fee base.** eBay's page includes shipping you collected and sales tax in the amount the percentage applies to. A sketch that uses only the item price can be too low.

**Weight.** A Poshmark package over 2.5 kg is an upgrade taken from earnings. Weigh it before you list.

**Returns.** A stain you missed is a refund plus the mailer you do not get back.

**Imports.** Goods you import to sell follow the CBSA [commercial import guide](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/import/guide-eng.html), not a personal exemption story.

### Step 1: Start with owned items

Cash outlay is packaging, not inventory, until the closet is done.

### Step 2: Write the platform fee before you list

Use the page for that site. A $40 Poshmark item at C$20 or more is not a $40 payout.

### Step 3: Add the mailer or the kilometres

Local pickup has a drive. Shipping has a bag. Both are costs.

### Step 4: Hold the payout until the fee and the cost are subtracted

The deposit is not profit on the day it lands.

### Step 5: Stop sourcing when the bin is full of unsold items

A second bin is a decision after the first bin has sales, not before.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of resale profits.**

Suppose you list a jacket you already own on Poshmark at C$40. The getting-started guide says the fee at C$20 or more is 20% of the listing price, so 0.20 × $40 = $8, and you keep $32 before a label upgrade. Suppose the jacket is under 2.5 kg, so this sketch adds no upgrade. Suppose you bought one mailer at $2 even though Poshmark's fee includes a label, and you should not double-count a mailer you did not need. Owned jacket, label included: remainder = $32, minus $0 item cost. Packaging you did not need is $0 in the honest version of this sketch.

Suppose instead you thrifted the jacket for an assumed $12 and you are on eBay.ca in a most-categories listing. Simplify the fee base to the item price only: 0.136 × $40 = $5.44, plus the $0.40 per-order fee, which is $5.84. A mailer at $2. Remainder = $40 − $12 − $5.84 − $2 = $20.16 before your time and before tax set-aside. eBay's own page includes shipping collected and sales tax in the fee base, so a real fee can be higher than this simplified sketch. These totals are assumptions. Do not copy $40.

A Shopify Basic month at $37, the yearly rate, on top of a single $20 sketch does not pay for the store. That is why the store is the later column.

## Where can reselling startup spending be lower in Canada?

Reselling startup spending in Canada stays lower when the first ten listings are things you own, pickup is local, and you buy a few mailers instead of a case. Listing on Poshmark is free on the getting-started guide. eBay's first 250 listings in most categories have a zero insertion fee. Kijiji or Marketplace for a pickup can avoid a mailer. You still disclose condition.

Skip Shopify until the marketplace is the constraint. Skip a course that promises a weekend number. Skip a second-hand shop licence you do not need because you do not have a shop. Toronto's fee is for the activity that page describes. A closet is a different activity. Confirm your own city before you assume you are exempt. This is not legal advice.

A spreadsheet is enough until you cannot match a payout to an item. Then invoicing software earns its place.

## Where should reselling spending not be cut in Canada?

Reselling spending in Canada should not skip honest condition notes, a scale once you ship, or a record of what you paid. A stain you hoped nobody would see becomes a return and a fee you already lost. A guessed weight becomes a label upgrade or a dispute.

For the product safety, fee, and record mistakes resellers make most, and how to fix them, see the [reselling mistakes guide](/side-hustles/reselling-mistakes-canada/).

Do not hide the platform fee inside a "sold for $40" story. Do not import commercial goods and call them personal shopping. The CBSA guide is the path for goods you import to sell.

Insurance and a municipal licence are the lines to ask about when the activity stops being a closet. This guide does not copy one city's fee onto another, and it is not insurance or legal advice.

Keep sourcing money separate from household spending. A payout that you spend before you subtract the cost of the next item is how the bin looks profitable and the account is empty.

## Which tax rules apply to a reselling budget in Canada?

Reselling income in Canada, when you are carrying on the activity for profit, is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. The cost of goods, mailers, and platform fees are generally current expenses. A scale or a computer that lasts for years may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

A one-off clear-out of personal belongings is not the same picture as an ongoing buy-and-sell activity. Keep purchase notes either way, and ask a tax adviser if you are unsure which one you are doing. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over four consecutive quarters. Platforms may have their own collection rules. Reselling is not ride-sharing.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use. You generally need to be a registrant and you need receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, do not add GST/HST yourself and do not claim the credit. Confirm the current rules, including how a marketplace's collection affects you, with the CRA or a tax professional.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
