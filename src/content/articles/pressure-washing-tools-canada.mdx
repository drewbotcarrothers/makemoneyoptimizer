## Which pressure washing safety gear matters most in Canada?

The pressure washing safety gear that matters most in Canada is eye protection, waterproof boots and clothing, and a GFCI outlet for electric units. [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/garages/wash_clean.html) says never to direct the spray toward any person or your own body, because the water pressure may break or enter the skin and cause severe injury, and to expect kickback from the spray gun.

Stay on the ground. Second-storey siding and gutters put you on a ladder with a kicking wand, which is a different risk. If you ever do need a ladder for a different task, [CCOHS on portable ladders](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html) says falls from portable ladders are a major source of serious injury and not to carry objects in your hands while climbing.

## How does runoff change which pressure washing tools you buy in Canada?

Runoff changes the tool list because the City of Toronto says power washing with water alone still dislodges or dissolves contaminants, and that wastewater from outdoor washing must not reach any catch basin. Toronto's options are to let the water evaporate, re-route it away from a catch basin or contain it, dispose of it in an indoor drain, or have it hauled off-site by an approved hauler.

Toronto also says both the person hiring and the mobile wash business are responsible for wastewater leaving the property. Other cities use their own bylaws. In practice that means a few cheap containment items (absorbent socks or a berm, a wet vacuum if you will collect water) can matter more than extra pressure.

## What pressure washing tool mistakes are common in Canada?

Common pressure washing tool mistakes in Canada are buying a gas commercial unit before any bookings, using a narrow tip on wood or siding, running a cord through standing water, and ignoring where the water drains. Leaving water in the pump over a Canadian winter is the quiet one; follow the storage section of the manual.

## Which tax rules apply to pressure washing tools in Canada?

Pressure washing income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people make the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net business income. Cleaner and small parts are ordinary expenses. A machine that lasts years is depreciable property claimed as [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html), but a tool costing less than $500, such as every machine in the tables above, falls in Class 12 of the CRA's [CCA classes](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html) and is generally fully deductible in the year of purchase. A gas unit over $500 is usually Class 8, at 20% a year.

GST/HST registration is required once your worldwide taxable supplies, including your associates', exceed [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Add other side hustles to that total. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
