Charging for house cleaning in Canada usually means a flat price per visit set by home size and condition, built from a timed hourly rate, with deep cleans and move-outs quoted higher. Job Bank lists employed house cleaners at $15.50 (low), $19.74 (median), and $25.00 (high) an hour nationally. A self-employed cleaner prices above that employee wage because supplies, travel between homes, unpaid quoting time, and both halves of CPP come out of the same rate. ## Which pricing models work for house cleaning in Canada?

House cleaning in Canada is usually sold as a flat price per visit by bedrooms and bathrooms, with a higher first-visit or deep-clean price and a recurring rate for weekly or biweekly clients. Hourly billing suits unusual homes and add-ons.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flat per visit | Price by bedrooms, bathrooms, and condition | Recurring clients | A home that was not cleaned for months |
| Hourly | Rate × hours, with an estimate | Unusual homes, partial cleans | Clients who want one number |
| First visit / deep clean | Longer clean to reset the home, priced from timed hours | New clients, spring cleans | Pricing the deep clean like a maintenance visit |
| Recurring (weekly / biweekly) | Same home on a schedule, set price per visit | Predictable income | Monthly visits drift toward deep-clean time |
| Move-out / move-in | Empty home, inside cabinets and appliances | Tenants and landlords | Ovens and fridges add real time |

Price add-ons separately: inside oven, inside fridge, interior windows, laundry, and baseboards by hand.

## How do you calculate a house cleaning rate in Canada?

Calculate a house cleaning rate in Canada from your target hourly pay, the share of time lost between homes, the timed hours for that home, and per-visit supplies. Then round and compare with local listings.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

The Job Bank table is your floor check. Your effective pay per working hour should not sit below your province's employee low.

### Step 2: Count travel and unpaid time

Driving or transit between homes, walkthroughs, and messages. If a quarter of your day is unpaid, the billable rate has to cover it.

### Step 3: Time the home

Do a walkthrough and time a standard clean on similar homes. Bathrooms and kitchens drive the time more than bedrooms.

### Step 4: Add supplies per visit

Cleaners, cloths, vacuum bags, and gloves. Divide a month of supplies by visits that month.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP, then round

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see the [CPP contributions page](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $24 an hour, just under Job Bank's national high of $25.00. Suppose 75% of your working time is billable. The billable rate is $24 ÷ 0.75 = $32 an hour.

Suppose a standard biweekly clean of a two-bedroom, one-bathroom apartment times at 3 hours. Labour is 3 × $32 = $96. Add an assumed $6 of supplies: $102, which you might round to $105 before tax.

Suppose the first deep clean of the same home takes 1.5 times as long, 4.5 hours. Labour is 4.5 × $32 = $144, plus $8 of supplies: $152, rounded to $155. If the home later shifts to monthly visits and each takes 3.5 hours, the price should move to 3.5 × $32 + $6 = $118, rounded to $120.

## When should you raise house cleaning prices in Canada?

Raise house cleaning prices in Canada when your schedule is full, when supplies or travel costs rise, or when a home's visits run longer than the price assumed. Give recurring clients written notice, often a month, and change new-client prices first.

An annual review is common. Raising per home is more honest than a blanket percentage, because a home that now takes 3.5 hours instead of 3 needs a different price than one that still takes 3.

## What house cleaning pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common house cleaning pricing mistakes in Canada are quoting without a walkthrough, pricing a first clean like a maintenance visit, and leaving add-ons unpriced. Each one hands away hours.

For the insurance, chemical safety, and helper mistakes cleaners make most, and how to fix them, see the [house cleaning mistakes guide](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-mistakes-canada/).

Ask for photos or a short video. Separate the reset clean from the recurring price. Write what is not included (biohazards, mould remediation, heavy furniture moving). Charge a lockout or late-cancellation fee you can enforce. Do not copy the lowest flyer price; you do not know what it includes.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect house cleaning prices in Canada?

House cleaning income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Add GST/HST only after you register, which is required once your taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

When registered, use the rate for the province where you clean from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers voluntary registration and input tax credits on supplies.
