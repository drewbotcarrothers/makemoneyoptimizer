## Should you charge by the hour or by the visit?

Quote flat prices by bedrooms and bathrooms for standard and recurring cleans, and use an hourly rate with a minimum for custom work. NeatNow says the flat quote is what clients pay "however long the visit runs," while SNAM charges $50 an hour with a 2.5-hour minimum. Most clients prefer a flat price; the hourly rate is how you build it.

| Model | Use it for | Published example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat by size | Standard and recurring cleans | NeatNow: $165 for a 2-bed, 1-bath one-time clean |
| Hourly + minimum | Custom tasks, unclear scope | SNAM: $50/hour, $125 minimum |
| First clean at a higher rate | New clients | NeatNow: first-time cleans from $265; recurring price starts on visit two |
| Frequency discount | Weekly, bi-weekly, monthly | Maidless: 20% / 15% / 12.5% / 10% |

## How do you calculate a house cleaning rate?

Build an hourly rate from your target pay, CPP, supplies and travel, divided by the share of hours you can bill. Then time each home size and multiply.

```text
Hourly rate     = (target pay + CPP + supplies and travel per hour) ÷ share of hours billable
Flat price      = timed hours × hourly rate, rounded to the nearest $5
Recurring price = flat price × (1 − 10% to 20%)
First clean     = flat price × 1.5, or quote deep-clean hours
```

**Illustrative rate build.** Replace each input with your own:

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Target pay | $30/hour (about 1.5 times the $19.74 Job Bank median) |
| CPP at 11.9% | $3.57 |
| Supplies, travel and insurance per billable hour | $5.00 |
| Share of hours billable | 80% |
| **Rate** | ($30 + $3.57 + $5) ÷ 0.80 = **$48.21, round to $48** |

$48 sits inside Clean 613's $30 to $55 Ottawa range and next to the Toronto survey's $49 average, and below SNAM's $50 Montreal rate.

## How should you write a house cleaning quote and recurring agreement?

Do a walkthrough or video call, then send a written quote listing rooms, tasks, frequency and what's excluded. The [quote walkthrough guide](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-quote-walkthrough-canada/) covers what to look at.

```text
CLEANING QUOTE — [client, address]   Date: [date]
Home: 2 bedrooms, 1 bathroom, ~850 sq ft, one cat
Every visit: kitchen counters, sink, stovetop, outside appliances; bathroom
  (toilet, tub, sink, mirror); dust reachable surfaces; vacuum and mop floors;
  empty bins; make beds
Not included: inside oven/fridge/cabinets (add-ons), windows outside, walls,
  biohazards, moving heavy furniture
First clean: $215 (about 4.5 hours)
Then: $145 every two weeks, starting [date]

Payment: e-Transfer on the day of each clean
```

## How do you raise house cleaning prices?

Raise prices once a year with 30 days' notice, and use the structures companies already publish: a higher first clean, frequency tiers and paid add-ons.

1. **Charge for the first clean.** NeatNow bills the first visit at the one-time rate and starts the recurring discount on visit two. First-time cleans start at $265 there.
2. **Tier the discount by frequency.** Maidless gives 20% off weekly but only 10% off monthly. Moving a monthly client to bi-weekly raises your monthly revenue even at a bigger discount.
3. **Price add-ons instead of absorbing them.** An inside oven at $40 every few visits adds up.
4. **Price by bathroom.** NeatNow adds $25 per extra bathroom; bathrooms take the most time.
5. **Send a notice:**

```text
Hi [Name], thank you for a great year. Starting [date, 30+ days out],
your bi-weekly clean will be $[155] (from $[145]). Add-on prices stay the same.
If you'd like to keep the current rate, switching to weekly locks in $[140]/visit
for 2027. — [Your name]
```

## What house cleaning pricing mistakes are common?

- **Charging app rates once you're established.** TaskRabbit's $18 to $28 city averages are a starting point.
- **No first-clean price.** The first visit takes far longer; every company above charges more for it.
- **Unpaid add-ons.** Ovens, fridges and cabinets are listed separately everywhere.
- **No cancellation policy.** A lost slot is lost income.
- **Ignoring bathrooms.** A 2-bedroom with two bathrooms isn't the same job as one with one.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect house cleaning prices?

Cleaning income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution outside Quebec. Register for GST/HST once your taxable sales from all businesses pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). Then add the rate where you clean ([CRA rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)): NeatNow's prices are before Alberta's 5% GST, and Maidless shows its Vancouver prices with 5% GST included. A $145 clean becomes $163.85 with Ontario's 13% HST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

See [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) and the [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).
