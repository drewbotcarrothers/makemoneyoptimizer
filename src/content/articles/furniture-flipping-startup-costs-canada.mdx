## How much does furniture flipping cost to start in Canada?

Furniture flipping in Canada starts at about $40–$120 when the first piece is free or cheap and only needs cleaning, about $120–$350 when you buy a piece and repaint it, and about $350–$1,000 with a sander, extra tools, and paid transport. A local-pickup listing usually has no platform fee in this guide's scope, but your time and fuel are real.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean and sell | $40–$120 | A free or low-cost piece, soap, cloths, a surface cleaner, gloves, and a measuring tape |
| Paint refresh | $120–$350 | A paid piece, primer and paint for one small item, a brush or foam roller, a drop cloth, and fine sandpaper for finishes you know are safe to scuff |
| Equipped | $350–$1,000 | The paint kit plus an orbital sander, clamps, wood filler, new hardware, and a rented van or trailer for larger pieces |

## Who should use a furniture flipping startup budget in Canada?

A furniture flipping startup budget in Canada fits people with a place to work, a way to move a piece, and patience to wait for a local buyer. The clean-and-sell column fits a first test in a garage or balcony. It is a poor fit if you live where paint fumes or dust reach people who sleep there, or if you have no storage for a dresser that does not sell in a week.

Smaller items that ship are covered by [reselling startup costs](/side-hustles/reselling-startup-costs-canada/). Repair work in a client's home is [handyman startup costs](/side-hustles/handyman-startup-costs-canada/).

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for furniture flipping in Canada?

Furniture flipping break-even in Canada is the sale price minus the piece, supplies used, and transport, then divided by the hours you spent. The sketch below is a method, not an average flip margin.

### Step 1: Decide if the piece is worth moving

Check for a solid frame, working drawers, and no smell or pests. A piece you cannot carry up your stairs is a cost before it is a flip.

### Step 2: Check the finish before you sand

If the paint is old and you cannot confirm it is lead-free, do not sand it. Health Canada's [lead-based paint](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/lead-based-paint.html) page says not to use sanders, heat guns, or blowlamps to remove lead-based paint. Sell it as-is or pass.

### Step 3: Check recalls and regulated items

Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says dealers have the primary responsibility for the safety of second-hand products and to check the [recall site](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before selling. Cribs, bassinets, and some other children's products have specific regulations.

### Step 4: Price the supplies you actually used

A quart of paint across two tables is half a quart each.

### Step 5: Add transport both ways

The drive to buy it and, if you deliver, the drive to sell it.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical flip.**

Suppose a solid side table costs $20 on a local listing, an assumption. Supplies used: soap and cloths $5, half a quart of primer $12, half a quart of paint $14, a brush $10, for $41. Fuel for the pickup, an assumed $6. Total in: $20 + $41 + $6 = $67. Suppose it sells for $110 with buyer pickup, also an assumption. Remainder: $110 − $67 = $43 before a tax set-aside. Suppose the work took 5 hours, including cleaning, two coats, photos, and messages: $43 ÷ 5 = $8.60 an hour inside these assumptions. The brush is reusable, so a second table with the same brush improves the sketch. These totals are assumptions. Do not copy $110.

## Where can furniture flipping startup spending be lower in Canada?

Furniture flipping startup spending in Canada stays lower when the first piece only needs cleaning and tightening, when you borrow a vehicle instead of renting, and when you buy a quart, not a gallon. A clean, well-photographed piece with honest measurements can sell without paint. Skip the sander until you have a finish you know is safe to sand.

## Where should furniture flipping spending not be cut in Canada?

Furniture flipping spending in Canada should not skip gloves, a mask where labels call for one, a drop cloth, or the recall check. Do not cut the lead-paint caution to save a stripper product. Do not cut honest measurements and flaw photos to save time, because a buyer who drives across town for a wobbling dresser will not pay for it.

## Which tax rules apply to a furniture flipping budget in Canada?

Furniture flipping income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase notes, supply receipts, fuel notes, and the sale record for each piece. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Furniture flipping is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

A one-off sale of your own furniture is a different question from buying pieces to resell. If you are unsure which one you are doing, ask a tax adviser.
