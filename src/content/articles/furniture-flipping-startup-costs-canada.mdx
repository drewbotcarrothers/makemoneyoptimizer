## How much does furniture flipping cost to start in Canada?

Furniture flipping in Canada costs the price of the first piece if you own basic tools, $80.07 for a clean-and-relist kit, $228.89 for a paint-refresh kit, or $433.11 for an equipped kit with a sander, moving blankets, straps and a hand truck. Add the piece: the pillar guide's examples use an assumed $25 side table and an assumed $60 dresser. Goods include 13% Ontario HST. Use your province's rate.

| Kit | What's in it | Gear with HST |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tools you own | Soap, cloths, a screwdriver and a tape measure you already have | $0 + the piece |
| Lean, clean and relist | Mr. Clean Magic Eraser Extra Durable ($12.97) + COOLJOB work gloves, 3 pairs ($18.99) + JOREST 25-in-1 screwdriver ($15.99) + X-PROTECTOR felt sliders, 16 pieces ($22.91) | $80.07 |

| Pro, equipped | Standard + BLACK+DECKER BDERO100 5-inch random orbit sander ($39.98) + Amazon Basics moving blankets, 6-pack ($53.76) + Jack Boss ratchet straps ($26.99) + JUXANOS 500 lb folding hand truck ($59.99) | $433.11 |

The arithmetic: lean is ($12.97 + $18.99 + $15.99 + $22.91) = $70.86, × 1.13 = $80.07. Standard adds $36.99 + $32.99 + $13.94 + $47.78 = $131.70, so $202.56 × 1.13 = $228.89. Pro adds $39.98 + $53.76 + $26.99 + $59.99 = $180.72, so $383.28 × 1.13 = $433.11.

One 500 ml can of paint and one of topcoat go across more than one piece. The pillar guide's dresser example uses about half the paint and a third of the topcoat. So the standard kit is not a per-piece cost.

## Who should use a furniture flipping startup budget in Canada?

A furniture flipping startup budget in Canada fits people with a place to work, a way to move a piece, and patience to wait for a local buyer. The lean kit fits a first test in a garage or on a balcony: clean, tighten, photograph, relist. The standard kit fits someone ready to repaint a small table or nightstand. The pro kit fits dressers and sideboards, where a sander saves hours on modern finishes and a hand truck saves your back.

It's a poor fit if you live where paint fumes or dust reach people who sleep there, or if you have no storage for a dresser that doesn't sell in a week.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for furniture flipping in Canada?

**Insurance.** No insurance is legally required to sell used furniture. Ask your own insurer whether your home or tenant policy covers pieces you hold for resale and paint work in a garage. This guide does not state a premium. This is not insurance advice.

**Licence.** Some municipalities licence second-hand dealers. Search your city's bylaws if you plan to sell at volume or from a fixed location. This guide does not state a licence fee.

**Regulated products.** Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says dealers have the primary responsibility for the safety of second-hand products and should check the recall site before selling. Cribs, bassinets, and some other children's products have specific regulations. If you're not sure, don't flip it.

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Paint and topcoat,** replaced as cans run out, a fraction per piece.
- **Hardware,** only when the old pulls are broken or mismatched.
- **Fuel or rentals** for every piece, both ways if you deliver.
- **Price cuts** on pieces that sit. Storage space has a cost even when it's your garage.
- **Respirator cartridges and sanding sponges,** replaced when worn.

## Where can furniture flipping startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Start with clean-and-relist pieces.** A clean, well-photographed piece with honest measurements can sell without paint.
- **Buy one 500 ml can,** not a gallon, and one topcoat.
- **Borrow a vehicle** instead of renting, and pick pieces that fit what you can borrow.
- **Skip the sander** until you have a finish you know is safe to sand.
- **Skip courses** until your log shows which pieces sell.

## Where should furniture flipping spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't skip gloves, a respirator where labels call for one, a drop cloth, or the recall check. Don't cut the lead-paint caution to save a stripper product. Health Canada's [lead-based paint](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/lead-based-paint.html) page says not to use sanders, heat guns, or blowlamps to remove lead-based paint. If you can't confirm the finish is safe, sell the piece as-is or pass.

Don't cut honest measurements and flaw photos to save time. A buyer who drives across town for a wobbling dresser won't pay for it.

## Which tax rules apply to a furniture flipping budget in Canada?

Furniture flipping income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase notes, supply receipts, fuel notes, and the sale record for each piece. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Furniture flipping is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and small tools used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

A one-off sale of your own furniture is a different question from buying pieces to resell. If you are unsure which one you are doing, ask a tax adviser.
