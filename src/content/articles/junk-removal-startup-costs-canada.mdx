## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for junk removal in Canada?

Ongoing junk removal costs in Canada are the tipping fee, fuel, gloves, and insurance that does not shrink between loads, and the break-even sketch further down keeps this guide's assumed labour fee. Retail ranges for gear are typical Canadian retail prices to verify locally, not a study. The sketch does not invent a dump price. Junk removal break-even is a method, not a load price.

**Tipping fee.** The number on the facility's page that day, including a minimum or a commercial rate. Pass it through as its own line. A fee you absorb without reading the page is a donation.

**Diversion stops.** Paint, batteries, propane, electronics, tires, and appliances with refrigerant often cannot ride in the same pile. Product Care's paint page is one program, in the provinces it lists. A second stop is time.

**Fuel and parking.** Real on every load that is not walking distance. Log kilometres if you claim the vehicle.

**Unpaid scope.** "It's all trash" that turns out to include paint, and a basement you did not see before you priced the hour, are how a carload becomes a loss.

### Step 1: Add the one-time gear

One-time gear means gloves, straps, and a tarp, plus a dolly only if you bought one.

### Step 2: Add the fixed costs

Fixed costs mean insurance you accepted. The tipping fee is not fixed. It changes with the load and the day.

### Step 3: List the variable cost of the loads you did

Variable cost means the posted tipping fee, fuel, parking, a diversion stop, and the hours, including stairs.

Contribution equals the labour fee you kept, after the tipping fee is paid, minus other variable costs. Gear is earned back when that contribution covers the startup. Do not treat the client's dump money as wages.

## Which tax rules apply to a junk removal budget in Canada?

Junk removal income in Canada is self-employment income, including a labour deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. A tipping fee you paid and were repaid is a record on both sides. Gloves and fuel are current expenses, while a dolly or trailer that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion. GST/HST for junk removal uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the load notes, the facility receipts, and the e-transfers. A tipping fee you paid for the client is not spending money to forget. Report the income on Form T2125 with the T1. If you use a vehicle, keep a kilometre log and claim the business portion only.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add moving help and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Junk removal is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. This guide does not explain input tax credits. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work happens. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set any tax you must collect aside. The client's reimbursement of a tipping fee is not automatically your profit. Keep the receipt so the two amounts stay visible.

Search your city for a business licence and use that city's fee if there is one. This guide does not state a licence fee. A disposal site's rules are a separate check.
