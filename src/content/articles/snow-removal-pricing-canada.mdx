Pricing snow removal in Canada means choosing per visit, a seasonal contract, or a hybrid, then timing the driveway in front of you instead of copying a fee from another city. Snow removal here is paid clearing of nearby driveways and walks. The pricing job suits someone who can measure, write a trigger depth, and say no to an unlimited winter.

There is no national snow removal rate on this site. A Prairie double driveway and a mild coastal walk are different products. How to start the route is in the [snow removal side hustle guide](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada). What the shovel and blower cost is in [snow removal startup costs](/side-hustles/snow-removal-startup-costs-canada).

## How should you price snow removal in Canada?

Price snow removal in Canada from the minutes the property takes, the extras you promised, and a light, typical, and heavy winter under those rules. The quote names the address, the surfaces, the trigger depth, the windrow, salt, the done-by time, and whether tax is extra.

| Decision | What to write | What goes wrong if you skip it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per visit | You come when accumulation hits a depth you both use, and each clear has a price | A mild winter means fewer cheques. A client who must call you is easy to forget at 5 a.m. |
| Seasonal | One price for the written season, with a trigger and a cap or a fair-use note | Unlimited clears in a harsh year can pay less per hour than a hard shift at a day job |
| Hybrid | A price for a set number of visits, then a per-visit rate | Still needs a sentence for a winter that never reaches the visit count |
| Driveway size | Length, width, steps, walk, sidewalk, and slope, timed by you | A corner lot priced like a single-car pad loses the morning |
| Tax | Before tax, plus the provincial rate only if you are registered | Adding GST/HST when you are not registered, or forgetting it when you are |

Sidewalk duty is municipal. Toronto's [sidewalk clearing page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-maintenance/winter-maintenance/clearing-snow-and-ice-from-your-property/) says residents and businesses clear sidewalks next to their property within 12 hours of the end of a snowfall when accumulation is less than 2 cm, and that city crews clear public sidewalks at 2 cm or more. The same page says private driveways, parking spaces, steps, ramps, and landings are a 24-hour owner job, and that snow must not be pushed onto sidewalks, roads, or bike lanes. Ottawa's [Use and Care of Roads By-law](https://ottawa.ca/en/living-ottawa/laws-licences-and-permits/laws/laws-z/use-and-care-roads-law-no-2003-498) says no person shall throw, push, plow, dump, or otherwise deposit snow or ice on a highway, with an exception for a city employee or agent moving snow or ice. Read your own city's winter page before you include a sidewalk or a windrow. Those two pages are examples of different wording, not a Canadian standard, and Toronto's program has already changed once. Recheck the live page before you quote a fine or a deadline.

## How do per-visit and seasonal snow removal prices differ in Canada?

Per-visit snow removal in Canada pays each clear, and a seasonal price pays for a written set of rules across the winter. A hybrid snow removal price in Canada covers a set number of visits, then uses the per-visit rate. Pick one product and use it on every house so the morning decision is fast.

| | Per visit | Seasonal | Hybrid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| When money arrives | After each clear, or on a short invoice | Often a fall deposit, then instalments | Deposit for the included visits, then extras as they happen |
| Mild winter | You earn less because you came less | The client paid to have you on call | Fine if the note says what happens below the visit count |
| Harsh winter | You earn more if you can finish the route | Your hourly result falls if you priced a light year with no cap | Extras start after the written count |
| Client memory | They must know to expect you at the trigger | They budget one number | Easier to explain than unlimited service |
| Your risk | Empty January | Unlimited February | A cap you forget to enforce |

A trigger depth belongs on all three. Two centimetres and twelve centimetres are different jobs. The [beginner guide](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada) uses a written example somewhere in the 2 to 5 centimetre range. That range is an example to write down, not a survey of what operators charge. Use the same depth on every driveway.

## How do you size a driveway for a snow removal quote in Canada?

Size a snow removal driveway in Canada by measuring it and timing a clear, then adding steps, the sidewalk if it is in the job, salt, and the plough windrow. Square metres explain the shape, and minutes explain the price. A copied price per square foot from another country is not a Canadian quote.

### Step 1: Measure in metres

Walk the property in daylight. Record driveway length and width, the front walk, the number of steps, and the sidewalk length if the city page and the client both put that sidewalk in the job. Note gravel, a hill, a narrow gate, and where snow can legally sit. Gravel changes the tool, which changes the time. The [tools guide](/side-hustles/snow-removal-tools-canada) is the equipment half of that choice.

### Step 2: Time a similar clear

Time your own shovel pass on a dry run or on the first small snowfall you are willing to treat as practice. Include walking back to the sidewalk and the place you stack snow. A windrow, the ridge a city plough leaves at the end of the driveway, is a second visit unless you write it into the same price. Clients feel that ridge as your problem even when the plough came after you left.

### Step 3: Separate salt from labour

Scrape first. Melt on uncleared snow wastes the bag. Pet-safer and concrete-safer labels usually cost more, and the label is not a promise the product is harmless. Read the bag rate. An icy week can use more than a snow week. Put salt in the price as included, extra, or never.

### Step 4: Add the route, not just the pad

The minutes between houses matter. A walking loop can be a zero travel line. A drive across town is fuel, time, and a reason to decline. The done-by time is a capacity limit. If five driveways at the timed minutes do not fit before the hour you promised, the route is too big at any fee.

### Step 5: Sketch three winters before you say the number

Count visits at your trigger for a light, typical, and heavy picture you are willing to survive. The counts are your assumptions. Environment Canada does not owe you a normal year. If the heavy picture pays less per hour than you will accept, raise the price, cap the visits, or shrink the surfaces.

### Step 6: Write the quote before the snow

One page: address, surfaces, trigger, who measures, done-by time, season dates, price, when it is due, extra-visit rate, windrow, salt, what you will not do, and how either of you can cancel. The line-by-line list is the [snow removal contract checklist](/side-hustles/snow-removal-contract-checklist-canada). That checklist is not a lawyer's form.

## What does an illustrative snow removal price in Canada look like?

An illustrative snow removal price in Canada is the arithmetic below, built from assumed minutes and an assumed hourly target. It is not a quote, a survey, a recommended fee, or a forecast of snowfall.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose you choose $40 an hour as your own target for time on the property. That target is a number you replace. Suppose you timed a paved double driveway, with the front walk and four steps, at 35 minutes, and a windrow return at 10 minutes, and you include the windrow in the same visit. The visit is 45 minutes, or 0.75 hour. Labour in the sketch is 0.75 × $40 = $30. Suppose salt on that visit costs you $3. That $3 is an assumption about your bag, not a measured application rate. The visit in the sketch is $30 + $3 = $33 before tax.

Suppose a smaller paved driveway times at 15 minutes with no separate windrow. Labour is 0.25 × $40 = $10, plus the same assumed $3 of salt, so $13 before tax. A 10-minute windrow on that short driveway would be a large share of the visit. Price the windrow on purpose or leave it out in writing.

Suppose you use 6, 12, and 18 qualifying visits as your light, typical, and heavy sketches. Those counts are assumptions, not climate normals.

| Sketch | Visits | Double driveway at $33 | Smaller driveway at $13 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light | 6 | 6 × $33 = $198 | 6 × $13 = $78 |
| Typical | 12 | 12 × $33 = $396 | 12 × $13 = $156 |
| Heavy | 18 | 18 × $33 = $594 | 18 × $13 = $234 |

Per visit, the double driveway pays $198 in the light sketch and $594 in the heavy sketch. A seasonal price set on the typical column is $396 for that double driveway. The light year is 6 × 0.75 = 4.5 hours, and $396 ÷ 4.5 = $88 an hour in this sketch, because the client paid for you to be on call. The heavy year is 18 × 0.75 = 13.5 hours, and $396 ÷ 13.5 = $29.33 an hour in this sketch. If $29.33 is below the hour you will work on ice, do not sell unlimited service at $396.

A hybrid in the same sketch can price the first 10 visits at 10 × $33 = $330, then $33 each. Eighteen visits cost $330 + (8 × $33) = $594, and $594 ÷ 13.5 hours = $44 an hour. Six visits still cost $330 unless you wrote a credit, which is $330 ÷ 4.5 hours = $73.33 an hour in the sketch. The hybrid does not remove the need for a mild-winter sentence.

The route in this sketch is walked, so fuel is $0. Insurance is a quote, not a line here. Do not copy $13, $33, or $396. Replace every minute, every visit count, and the hourly target with your own. A local invoice is the sanity check. The highest number on social media is not.

## What should you say no to when pricing snow removal in Canada?

Say no, in the snow removal price, to roofs, to the neighbour's half, and to any place the city's winter page says snow must not go. Roofs and ice dams injure people, and a casual policy will not treat them as a shovel job. Most side hustles should leave them off the menu.

Do not price a public street. Toronto's sidewalk page says shovelled snow must stay on the property, and it lists a fine of $500 plus a $110 victim surcharge for pushing snow onto sidewalks, roads, or bike lanes. Confirm that figure on the live page. It is Toronto's number, and it can change. It is not a fine you can quote in another city.

If you are registered for GST/HST, the $33 in the sketch is before the rate for the province where the work happens. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate on the [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) page and with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). If you are not registered, do not add GST/HST.

## What snow removal pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common snow removal pricing mistakes in Canada are copying another city's fee, selling unlimited clears, and forgetting the windrow and the salt. A price that wins the door in October and fails the heavy-year hour test is how people quit in February.

Quoting before you have timed a clear produces a confident wrong number. Including salt, then using three bags in an ice storm, means the bag ate the visit. Treating the seasonal deposit as November wages leaves no money for melt in February. Promising a sidewalk the city already clears, or a sidewalk the bylaw assigns to the owner and you left out, starts the first argument. Discounting every house "to get started" trains the street. ## Which tax rules apply to snow removal pricing in Canada?

Snow removal income in Canada is self-employment income, including a seasonal lump sum, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for snow removal follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

A deposit in November is still income to track. Keep the agreements, the e-transfers, and the receipts for salt. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add lawn, cleaning, and handyman revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Snow removal is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. Set any tax you must collect aside. The seasonal fee is not spending money until that tax is separated.

Your price does not erase negligence. Carry liability insurance that matches driveways, walks, blowers, and ice melt, and say that you do not clear roofs. This is not insurance advice. Search your city for a business licence. This guide does not state a licence fee.
