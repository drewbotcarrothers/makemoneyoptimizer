A 30-day lawn care launch plan for Canada uses week 1 for equipment, safety, and route, week 2 for pricing and outreach, week 3 for first cuts, and week 4 for a recurring weekly route and a review of your numbers. Start in late winter or early spring so clients are booked before the first mow. It suits people who like steady outdoor work and can store and transport a mower safely.

## What happens in week 1 of a lawn care launch plan in Canada?

Week 1 is equipment and safety: get a mower that starts reliably, the protective gear CCOHS describes, an insurance quote, and a tight route.

### Days 1 and 2: Map your route

Choose a few streets you can walk a push mower between or drive with a mower secured. A tight route keeps unpaid travel low and lets you fit more lawns into one morning.

### Days 3 and 4: Get a mower and safety gear

Buy a mower you have seen start and cut. [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/landscaping/rotary.html) says to wear long pants, non-slip safety-toe footwear, and hearing and eye protection, and to check that guards and blade-stopping controls work. The [startup costs guide](/side-hustles/lawn-care-startup-costs-canada/) has the ranges and the upgrades to wait on. The [lawn care tools guide](/side-hustles/lawn-care-tools-canada/) breaks down starter mower and safety gear choices before you buy.

### Days 5 to 7: Get an insurance quote and practise

Ask a broker for a liability quote that covers thrown rocks and property damage. Practise on your own or a friend's lawn: CCOHS says to clear debris first, mow across slopes with a push mower, and never point the discharge at people, pets, or windows.

## What happens in week 2 of a lawn care launch plan in Canada?

Week 2 is pricing and outreach: set prices, decide what you will not do, and start telling people.

### Days 8 to 10: Set prices

Use the [pricing guide](/side-hustles/lawn-care-pricing-canada/) to choose per-cut or seasonal pricing and set add-on prices for edging, cleanup, or bagging clippings.

### Days 11 and 12: Decide your service limits

Write down what you will not offer. Ontario's [pesticides page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/pesticides-home-lawns-and-gardens) says certain pesticides, including 2,4-D and glyphosate, cannot be used for cosmetic purposes on lawns and gardens. Other provinces and cities set their own rules, so confirm before offering any treatment.

### Days 13 and 14: Start outreach

Drop flyers on your route, tell neighbours, and consider a free Google Business Profile; Google says you can [add or claim a profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778?hl=en) at no charge if eligible.

## What happens in week 3 of a lawn care launch plan in Canada?

Week 3 is quotes and first cuts: see each lawn, quote it, and deliver a clean first visit.

### Days 15 to 17: Quote each lawn

Visit each property, note gates, slopes, and obstacles, and quote in writing. Confirm where clippings go and whether the client wants edges trimmed.

### Days 18 and 19: Do first cuts

Walk the lawn for rocks and debris before every cut, as CCOHS advises. Sweep walks and driveways before you leave.

### Days 20 and 21: Follow up and fix

Message clients after the first cut. Note anything to change, such as cutting height or a missed edge.

## What happens in week 4 of a lawn care launch plan in Canada?

Week 4 builds the route: fixed weekly or biweekly slots, a rain plan, add-ons, and records.

### Days 22 to 24: Lock in recurring slots

Offer each client a fixed day in a weekly or biweekly rotation, grouped by street.

### Days 25 and 26: Write a rain and heat policy

CCOHS says not to mow wet grass and to take frequent breaks on hot days. Tell clients how rain days shift the schedule.

### Days 27 and 28: Plan seasonal add-ons

List spring cleanup and fall leaf work as add-ons. [Fall leaf cleanup](/side-hustles/fall-leaf-cleanup-side-hustle-canada/) covers the autumn version.

### Days 29 and 30: Review your numbers

Total income, fuel, and repairs. If you drive between lawns, the CRA's [motor vehicle records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/motor-vehicle-records.html) page says to log the date, destination, purpose, and kilometres of each business trip.

### Illustrative example

A teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast: suppose by day 30 you have five weekly lawns at a per-cut price C. Weekly revenue is 5C. If C were $40, that is $200 a week before fuel, repairs, insurance, and tax. Your price and lawn count will differ.

## What lawn care launch mistakes should you avoid in Canada?

Common launch mistakes are buying a mower you have not seen run, scattering clients across town, mowing wet grass, offering pesticide treatments without checking the rules, and skipping a written quote. More are in [lawn care mistakes](/side-hustles/lawn-care-mistakes-canada/).

## Which tax rules apply when launching lawn care in Canada?

Lawn care income is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and the self-employed pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
