<QuickAnswer>

- **T2125** is the CRA form for business or professional income: freelancing, delivery, reselling, services. Gross income goes on line 13499 of the return and net income on line 13500.
- **T776** is the form for rental income from real estate when you rent space and provide only basic services such as heat, light, parking and laundry. Gross rent goes on line 12599 and net rent on line 12600.
- **Extra services change the form.** Cleaning, security and meals can make a rental a business. The CRA's own examples put an Airbnb with meals and cleaning on T2125 and one with only heat, hydro and parking on T776.
- **CPP is the big difference.** Self-employed people pay 11.9% CPP on net business income between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026. Rental income from property isn't subject to CPP.
- **Deadlines differ too.** Rental-only filers file by April 30. If you or your spouse are self-employed, filing is due June 15, but any balance is still due April 30.

</QuickAnswer>

T2125 and T776 are different CRA forms because business income and rental income from property are taxed differently. [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), Statement of Business or Professional Activities, is for a side hustle you carry on as a business. [Form T776](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/rental-income/completing-form-t776-statement-real-estate-rentals.html), Statement of Real Estate Rentals, is for rental income from real estate that counts as income from property. Using the wrong one puts income on the wrong lines and can get your CPP wrong. All CRA pages below were checked on October 9, 2026.

This is general information, not tax advice. [Guide T4002](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002.html) goes with T2125, and [Guide T4036](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4036/rental-income.html) goes with T776. For a step-by-step on T2125, see [how to report side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/). Other common mix-ups are in the [side hustle tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/).

## What is the difference between T2125 and T776?

T2125 reports business income, which is subject to self-employed CPP and gets the June 15 filing deadline. T776 reports rental income from real estate, which isn't subject to CPP and has CCA limits that stop you creating a rental loss. The deciding question is how many services you provide with the space.

## Which side income goes on Form T2125 in Canada?

Side income goes on T2125 when it is business or professional income. The CRA's [self-employment income page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/self-employment-income-lines-13499-14299-gross-income-lines-13500-14300-net-income.html) says business income generally includes income from any activity you carry out for profit or with a reasonable expectation of profit. The CRA's [completing Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125.html) page says to fill in a separate T2125 for each business. Gross business income from line 8299 of the form goes on line 13499, and net business income on line 13500. Professional, commission, farming and fishing income use their own lines in the same group.

Freelancing, delivery, reselling items you bought to resell, and services you sell for profit all follow the T2125 pattern. Clearing out your own closet usually isn't a business. That's covered in [is selling on Kijiji taxable](/side-hustles/is-selling-on-kijiji-taxable-canada/).

**CPP applies on T2125 income.** The government's [CPP contributions page](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) says self-employed people pay the full 11.9% on pensionable earnings, based on net business income after expenses, and don't contribute on other income such as investment earnings. For 2026 the earnings ceiling is $74,600, the basic exemption is $3,500, and the self-employed maximum is $8,460.90. Earnings between $74,600 and $85,000 also attract the second additional contribution, at 8% for the self-employed. [How much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) covers this in more detail.

## Which side income goes on Form T776 in Canada?

Side income goes on T776 when it's rental income from real estate and counts as income from property. The CRA's [rental income or business income](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/rental-income/you-have-rental-income-business-income.html) page says to look at the number and types of services you provide. In most cases, renting space with basic services only is income from property. Basic services are heat, light, parking and laundry facilities. Guide T4036 is only for rental income from property. If the rental is a business, it sends you to Guide T4002 and T2125.

The CRA's [completing T776](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/rental-income/completing-form-t776-statement-real-estate-rentals.html) page sets out the lines:

- **Line 8141, gross rents** for all units, including short-term rentals.
- **Line 8230, other income**, including the fair market value of rent paid in goods or services. The CRA's example is a tenant who pays part of the rent by clearing snow.
- **Line 8299, total gross rental income**, which goes on line 12599 of the return. Net rental income goes on line 12600.
- **Co-owners** enter the gross rental income for the whole property on line 12599, not their share. Your share comes in later, in the net calculation.

T776 is only for real estate. Renting out a car, tools or other movable property isn't what it's for. If that activity is a business, it goes on T2125.

## When do extra services move a rental from T776 to T2125?

Extra services move a rental to T2125 when they go beyond basic services and the operation starts to look like a business. The rental-or-business page says that if you provide additional services such as cleaning, security and meals, you may be carrying on a business, and the more services you provide, the greater the chance.

The CRA's [sharing economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/sharing-economy.html) page gives two examples:

- **Charles** rents his property on Airbnb, and the price includes meals, cleaning and reservation services for tour packages. Those extras are generally a business, so he reports self-employment income on lines 13499 and 13500 and files T2125.
- **Renée** also rents on Airbnb, but her price covers only accommodation, with heat, hydro, parking and similar services. She is generally earning rental income from property and files T776.

The same page says that if you provide other services to tenants, that portion of your income is self-employment income on T2125. Don't report the same dollars on both forms.

## What rules apply only to T776 rental income?

Three T776 rules catch side-income renters out: CCA can't create a rental loss, short-term rental expenses can be cut off if the rental isn't compliant, and CCA on your own home can cost you part of the principal residence exemption.

**CCA can't create or increase a rental loss.** [Guide T4036](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4036/rental-income.html) says this directly. In its own example, Salvador has an overall net rental loss of $500 across three properties, so he can't claim any CCA on the buildings or appliances. Most buildings bought after 1987 are Class 1, at 4%.

**Rental losses have limits.** T4036 says you can deduct a rental loss against other income if you incurred the expenses to earn income. You can't claim a loss if you rent to someone you know for less than you'd charge a stranger, or if the arrangement is cost-sharing rather than a business aiming at profit.

**Non-compliant short-term rentals.** T4036 defines a short-term rental as residential property rented, or offered for rent, for less than 90 consecutive days. If the province or municipality doesn't allow short-term rentals at that location, or requires registration, a licence or a permit you don't have, the non-compliant share of expenses isn't deductible. The formula is A × B ÷ C: deductible short-term rental expenses, times non-compliant days, divided by short-term rental days. The same rule applies on T2125. The CRA's [T2125 expenses page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125/expenses-section-form-t2125.html) says you can't deduct the non-compliant amount, including CCA.

**Your own home.** T4036 has a chapter on the principal residence and changes in use. The [home office guide](/side-hustles/home-office-expenses-side-hustle-canada/) explains why many people renting part of their home claim operating costs and leave CCA on the house at zero. Read the guide or ask a professional before claiming CCA on a home you live in.

## How do deadlines, instalments and GST/HST differ?

**Filing deadlines.** The CRA's [due dates page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/important-dates-individuals.html) lists April 30 as the filing deadline for most people and June 15 if you or your spouse or common-law partner are self-employed. Any balance owing is due April 30 either way. A T776-only filer doesn't get the June date.

**Instalments.** The CRA's [instalments page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/payments/payments-cra/individual-payments/income-tax-instalments.html) says you may have to pay instalments if your net tax owing is more than $3,000 ($1,800 in Quebec) for 2026 and in either 2025 or 2024. That applies to both forms, because neither business nor rental income has tax withheld. Instalments are due March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.

**GST/HST.** This is a separate question from which income form to use. Under the CRA's [GST/HST memorandum on residential rentals](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/19-2-2/residential-real-property-rentals.html), renting residential accommodation to the same person for a month or more is generally exempt. Short-term accommodation of less than a month is taxable if you're a registrant, unless it costs $20 a day or less. See [the $30,000 GST/HST myth](/side-hustles/gst-hst-30000-threshold-myth-canada/) and [when to register](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).

## What do T2125 and T776 look like with real numbers?

**Illustrative. The rents, incomes and expenses below are assumptions to show how the rules work. They aren't CRA examples, local rents or typical results. Verify the arithmetic, and get advice for your own return.**

**Example 1: a basement suite with basic services (T776).** You rent a suite for an assumed $1,200 a month, including heat, light and laundry, to the same tenant all year. Gross rent is 12 × $1,200 = $14,400, on line 8141 and then line 12599. Expenses allowed by T4036 come off to give net rent on line 12600. There's no CPP on the net rent. The rent is a long-term residential rental, so it's generally exempt from GST/HST.

**Example 2: freelance writing (T2125).** You earn an assumed $10,000 net from freelance writing after expenses. CPP is ($10,000 − $3,500) × 11.9% = $773.50, on top of income tax. Filing is due June 15, and any balance is due April 30.

**Example 3: the same $12,000 net on each form.** On T776, CPP is $0. On T2125, CPP is ($12,000 − $3,500) × 11.9% = $1,011.50. The form you use depends on the facts, not on which result is cheaper, but the difference shows why the choice matters.

**Example 4: a short-term rental that isn't compliant.** An assumed $6,000 of deductible short-term rental expenses, 200 days rented short-term, and 50 of those days without a required municipal licence. The non-compliant amount is $6,000 × 50 ÷ 200 = $1,500, which you can't deduct. If the price also included daily cleaning and breakfast, the services part would go on T2125, and the same rule would apply there.

## Which mistakes should you avoid with T2125 and T776?

- **Putting a rental with meals and cleaning on T776** because it's "Airbnb income". The CRA's Charles example puts it on T2125.
- **Splitting gross rent by ownership share.** Co-owners enter the whole property's gross rent on line 12599.
- **Claiming CCA to create a rental loss.** T4036 doesn't allow it.
- **Deducting short-term rental expenses for days you didn't have a required licence.**
- **Assuming no CPP because the income is small.** CPP applies to net business income over $3,500.
- **Filing a rental-only return on June 15.** That date applies only if you or your spouse are self-employed.
- **Claiming the personal part of shared costs** on either form.
