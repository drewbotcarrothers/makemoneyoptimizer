A client acquisition system for a Canadian side hustle is a small routine you repeat every week: show up in two channels where your customers already look, record every enquiry in one tracker, reply and follow up on a fixed schedule, and ask happy clients for a referral or review. It doesn't need software. It needs consistency, and a few legal guardrails: Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) governs follow-up emails and texts ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)), and the Competition Bureau expects reviews and testimonials to be based on actual experience ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act)).

For channel-by-channel tactics, see [Google Business Profile](/side-hustles/google-business-profile-side-hustle-canada/), [Upwork vs LinkedIn for finding clients](/side-hustles/upwork-vs-linkedin-finding-clients-canada/) and [TaskRabbit vs Kijiji and Facebook for handyman work](/side-hustles/taskrabbit-vs-kijiji-facebook-handyman-canada/). Hustle-specific first-client guides, such as [car detailing first client](/side-hustles/car-detailing-first-client-canada/) or [local SEO first client](/side-hustles/local-seo-first-client-canada/), cover the first one or two.

| Two channels | e.g., Google Business Profile plus flyers, or Upwork plus LinkedIn | 1–3 hours |
| Lead tracker | A spreadsheet row per enquiry | 10 minutes |
| Reply rule | Reply the same day where you can | As leads come in |
| Follow-up rule | One follow-up after a few days, then stop | 15 minutes |
| Referral and review ask | A short message after each good job | 5 minutes per client |

## How do you build a client acquisition system for a Canadian side hustle?

Set it up once, then run the same loop every week.

### Step 1: Pick two channels

Choose where your customers already look. Local services can use a [Google Business Profile](/side-hustles/google-business-profile-side-hustle-canada/), community groups and flyers; online freelancers can use a marketplace profile and direct outreach. Two channels done consistently are easier to judge than six done occasionally.

### Step 2: Make one clear offer

Every channel should say the same thing: what you do, where, for whom, and how to book. [The one-page business plan](/side-hustles/side-hustle-business-plan-canada/) is where you write it.

### Step 3: Track every lead

A spreadsheet with date, channel, name, request, quote, follow-up date and outcome. After a month, you can see which channel actually produces paying clients.

### Step 4: Reply fast, quote clearly

A quick, specific reply with a price or a clear next step. Use [the pricing worksheet](/side-hustles/side-hustle-pricing-worksheet-canada/) so your quotes are consistent.

### Step 5: Follow up once

If someone goes quiet after a quote, one polite follow-up a few days later is reasonable. CASL says implied consent from an inquiry lasts six months ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)); for ongoing promotional emails, ask for express consent and include an unsubscribe.

### Step 6: Ask for referrals and reviews

After a job goes well, ask: "If you know anyone who needs this, I'd be grateful for a referral," and send your review link. Never write reviews yourself or ask friends to review work they didn't receive.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers. A new house cleaner picks two channels: a Google Business Profile and a neighbourhood Facebook group. Over six weeks they log 18 enquiries in a spreadsheet: 11 from the group, 7 from the profile. Five group leads book; four profile leads book. They follow up once on every unanswered quote, which wins two more. After each first clean, they send a review link. By week six, they know the profile converts better per enquiry, so they spend more time on photos and reviews there.

## What legal rules apply to finding clients in Canada?

Three sets of rules apply.

- **CASL** covers commercial emails and texts: consent, identifying yourself, and an unsubscribe that's actioned within 10 business days ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)). See [email marketing for side hustles](/side-hustles/email-marketing-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Competition Act** rules on reviews and claims: base reviews and testimonials on actual experience ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act)), and don't use fake urgency or scarcity, such as countdowns that reset or untrue claims about limited spots ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations)).
- **Privacy:** PIPEDA, or a similar provincial law in Alberta, B.C. and Quebec, applies to personal information you collect in commercial activity ([Office of the Privacy Commissioner](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)). Keep your lead tracker private and collect only what you need.

## What client acquisition mistakes do side hustlers make in Canada?

- **Trying every channel at once** and judging none of them.
- **No tracker,** so you can't tell what works.
- **Slow replies** to enquiries.
- **Following up forever,** or adding enquiries to a newsletter without consent.
- **Fake reviews or fake scarcity.**

## How does client acquisition affect side hustle taxes in Canada?

Advertising costs, such as flyers, paid listings and a portion of your phone, are generally deductible business expenses on Form T2125; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).
