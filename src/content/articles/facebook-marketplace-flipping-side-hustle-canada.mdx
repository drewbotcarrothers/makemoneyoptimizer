## Who does Facebook Marketplace flipping in Canada suit?

Facebook Marketplace flipping in Canada suits people who know what one category of item is worth, can test it before paying, have a vehicle or a way to move items, and have some storage. Tools, bikes, small appliances, and kids' outdoor gear are common categories because condition can be checked in person.

It is a poor fit if you cannot spare evenings for messages and meetups, have nowhere to store items, or are uncomfortable meeting strangers. Every flip involves two meetups, one to buy and one to sell.

## What are Meta's rules for selling on Marketplace in Canada?

Meta's [commerce policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce) apply to every Marketplace listing, including free items. They describe Marketplace as intended for consumer-to-consumer sales and prohibit, among other things, recalled or banned products, stolen goods or items acquired without authorization, animals, alcohol, medical and healthcare products, and counterfeits. Listings that break the policies can be removed, and repeated violations can lose you access to commerce features.

For a flipper, two rules matter most. Do not buy an item that looks stolen, such as a new tool with the serial number removed or a bike sold far below value with no story. And check recalls before reselling anything, especially children's products.

Meta's help page on [getting paid for selling with shipping](https://www.facebook.com/help/449101635835192) says some shipping features are not available to all sellers. This guide does not state a Canada-wide Marketplace fee for local pickup; check the help centre from your own account.

## How do you start Facebook Marketplace flipping in Canada?

Starting Marketplace flipping in Canada means learning prices by selling your own things, then buying a few underpriced items in one category you can test. The steps below follow that order.

### Step 1: Read Meta's commerce policies and safety pages

Read the [commerce policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce), the [meeting-in-person tips](https://www.facebook.com/help/2329750133711372?locale=en_GB), and the [scams page](https://www.facebook.com/help/721562085854101/). Write down what you will not sell.

### Step 2: Sell five things you already own

List five unused items with honest photos. Note how fast each sells and at what price.

### Step 3: Pick one category you can test

Choose tools, bikes, or another category where you can check function in person. Watch sold prices for two weeks.

### Step 4: Check recalls and safety rules

Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says dealers have primary responsibility, that the law does not separate new and used products, and that recalled items that have not been corrected should not be sold. Search [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before you buy or resell. Skip cribs, car seats, and hockey helmets as first flips.

### Step 5: Buy only what you have tested

Meet in public, test the item, and pay only when satisfied. Walk away from anything that looks stolen.

### Step 6: Clean, photograph, and list honestly

Clean the item, photograph it in daylight, and describe any flaw. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) covers claims made to sell a product.

### Step 7: Sell at a safe meetup and confirm payment

Meet in a public, well-lit place. Confirm cash or an e-transfer that has landed in your bank app before handing over the item. The scams page warns against payment screenshots and overpayment offers.

### Step 8: Log every flip

Record purchase price, supplies, fuel, sale price, and time. Stop buying if the log shows losses.

## What should the first week of Marketplace flipping include?

The first week of Marketplace flipping in Canada should produce five listings of your own items and a list of sold prices in one category, with no purchases yet.

- **Day 1:** Read the commerce policies, meetup tips, and scams page.
- **Day 2:** Photograph five unused items in daylight.
- **Day 3:** List them with honest descriptions and a pickup area, not your address.
- **Day 4:** Pick one category and start a sheet of asking and sold prices.
- **Day 5:** Answer messages. Book one public meetup if someone is serious.
- **Day 6:** Complete any sales with payment confirmed first.
- **Day 7:** Review prices and speed. Decide on a small flip float only if own-item sales went smoothly.

## What Marketplace flipping mistakes are common in Canada?

Common Marketplace flipping mistakes in Canada are buying before learning prices, skipping tests at pickup, and trusting payment screenshots. Each one loses money or exposes you to a scam.

Buying items that look stolen breaks Meta's commerce policies and can cost you the item. Reselling recalled children's products shifts a safety risk onto a buyer. Giving out your home address for meetups is a safety risk Facebook's tips advise against. If you are targeted by a scam, the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/index-eng.htm) takes reports.

## Which tax rules apply to Facebook Marketplace flipping in Canada?

Marketplace flipping income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase records or screenshots, sale records, supply receipts, and a mileage log. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Flipping items on Marketplace is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
