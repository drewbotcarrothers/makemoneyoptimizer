## How much does profile management cost to start in Canada?

Google Business Profile management in Canada costs $0 with a computer, phone and free Google Account, $48.68 with a phone tripod and a profile-protection tool, $365.76 adding a business email, Canva Pro and a password manager, $715.76 with a starting liability policy, or $1,848.74 with a local SEO suite and invoicing software. Goods and software include 13% Ontario HST. Insurance is added without HST. Use your province's rate.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for profile management in Canada?

There's no licence to manage a Business Profile in Canada. Three things matter more.

**Insurance.** Zensurance's [freelancer insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/freelancer-insurance) covers freelancers of many kinds. The figure in the kits, freelancer professional liability generally from $350 to $500 a year with a $1 million limit, is the estimate that page gave on October 9, 2026, as cited in the [graphic design startup costs](/side-hustles/graphic-design-startup-costs-canada/) guide. That's an estimate, not a quote. A wrong holiday-hours update can cost a client a weekend of sales. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance or legal advice.

**Review rules.** Google's [review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) say to ask for reviews with a link or QR code and prohibit incentives. A gift card for a five-star review is not a cheaper tactic. It's a policy breach that can cost the client their reviews.

**Business licence.** Search your city for a home-based business licence if you present yourself as a local business. This guide does not state a licence fee.

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Subscriptions you kept:** Whitespark Local Platform US$1 per location, Workspace $9.20 a month, Canva Pro $142 a year, BrightLocal from US$41 a month. Cancel what last quarter's clients didn't use.
- **Insurance,** from about $350 a year once you carry it.
- **Mileage** for photo visits, which grows with each client.
- **Unpaid scope.** "Can you also do our Instagram" is a second service. Price it or decline it.
- **Owner delays.** You can't fix what the owner hasn't verified or added you to. Waiting is unpaid unless the agreement says otherwise.

## Where can profile management startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Start with what you own.** A computer, a phone and a free Google Account cover the first client.
- **Use a free Gmail address** until a client asks about your domain.
- **Use Canva Free** until saved brand kits for several clients would save real time.
- **Add tools when a client needs them.** Buy the tripod before the first photo visit, not before the first client.
- **Spread subscriptions across clients.** A $9.20 email costs less per client as you add clients.

## Where should profile management spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't skip manager access through your own Google Account. Asking for the owner's password is a security problem and a trust problem. Don't skip the agreement on how access ends.

Don't buy reviews or offer incentives for them. Don't use stock photos of another business. Real photos of the real place are the point.

Insurance is a quote when the contract asks for it. A client's wrong hours on a long weekend is a real loss.

## Which tax rules apply to a profile management budget in Canada?

Profile management income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. Subscriptions and a tripod used for the work are generally current expenses. A computer or phone that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement, the monthly reports, and the payments. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over four consecutive quarters. Add other side hustles.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, if you have the receipt. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST and do not claim the credit. Confirm the current rules with the CRA or a tax professional.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set the tax aside.
