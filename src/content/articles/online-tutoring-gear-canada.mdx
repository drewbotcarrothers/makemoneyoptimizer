<QuickAnswer>

- **Short answer:** a USB headset with a boom mic, a 1080p webcam, a soft light and a wired internet connection cover most online tutoring; add a writing tablet for math and science.
- **Sound first:** students forgive an average picture, not echo or dropouts.
- **Ergonomics:** CCOHS recommends a monitor at roughly arm's length with the top at or slightly below eye level.
- **Prices:** see current price; this is a what-to-look-for guide.

</QuickAnswer>

## What should you look for in online tutoring gear?

| Item | What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headset or mic | USB connection, boom mic with noise rejection | Clear voice without echo |
| Webcam | 1080p, decent low-light, mount that fits your screen | Sharper picture than many built-in laptop cameras |
| Writing tablet | Pressure-sensitive pen, works with your whiteboard app | Natural math and diagram writing |
| Light | Soft, dimmable, adjustable colour | Even light on your face for evening lessons |
| Connection | Wired Ethernet or strong Wi-Fi | Fewer freezes and dropped calls |
| Ergonomics | Laptop stand, external keyboard | Comfortable posture over back-to-back sessions |

## What should Canadian online tutors know?

- **Ergonomics.** CCOHS's [office ergonomics](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/office/) guidance and [monitor positioning](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/office/monitor_positioning.html) apply to a home tutoring desk.
- **Headphone volume.** Keep it moderate over long days; see CCOHS on [noise](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/noise/noise_basic.html).
- **Booking and payment.** Compare [Calendly vs Square Appointments vs Acuity](/side-hustles/calendly-vs-square-appointments-vs-acuity-canada/).
- **Deductions.** Gear used for tutoring is generally a [business expense](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses.html), with personal-use portions excluded.
