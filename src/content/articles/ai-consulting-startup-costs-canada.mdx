## How much does AI consulting cost to start in Canada?

AI consulting in Canada starts at $0 on free tiers, about $30–$90 a month with one paid AI plan and business email, and about $90–$250 a month with more tools and invoicing. Most costs are monthly subscriptions, several priced in US dollars, so the Canadian figure moves with the exchange rate.

| How you start | Monthly cash, about | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0 | Free tiers of AI tools, a personal email you already use, and a computer you own |
| Typical | $30–$90 | One paid AI subscription (Claude Pro at about $28.49 CAD a month billed monthly, as an example) and a business email address |
| Fuller | $90–$250 | A second AI or automation tool, invoicing software, a headset, and a scheduling tool |

## Who should use an AI consulting startup budget in Canada?

An AI consulting startup budget in Canada fits people who can show a small business owner how to do one named task faster and safer, and leave written steps behind. The lean column fits someone testing with free tiers. It is a poor fit if the offer is replacing staff, or if the plan needs a client's customer data pasted into a chat on day one.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for AI consulting in Canada?

AI consulting break-even in Canada is your project fees minus subscriptions and gear, divided by hours including setup, training, and follow-up. Subscriptions run whether or not you have a client that month.

### Step 1: Use free tiers until you have a client task

Test the exact task you will sell, on your own sample data.

### Step 2: Pay for one tool you can explain

If you cannot say what the paid tier adds for the client's task, stay on the free tier.

### Step 3: Write down the data you will not use

The OPC's [generative AI principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/) say to know your legal authority for using personal information and to limit collection, use, and disclosure to what the purpose needs. [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) covers personal information handled in commercial activity.

### Step 4: Price the written handover

The steps you leave behind are part of what the client buys.

### Step 5: Avoid claims you cannot support

A promise to cut costs by a set percentage can fall under the Competition Bureau's guidance on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations).

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical consulting rate.**

Suppose a local shop pays an assumed $400 for a one-task project: drafting product descriptions with an AI tool, a written prompt guide, and a one-hour training call. Monthly tools: Claude Pro at about $28.49 CAD and business email at $7, about $35.49. A headset at $35, charged to this first month. Remainder: $400 − $35.49 − $35 = $329.51 before a tax set-aside. Suppose the work takes 8 hours: $329.51 ÷ 8 = about $41.19 an hour inside these assumptions. In a month with no client, the subscriptions are a $35.49 loss. These totals are assumptions. Do not copy $400.

## Where can AI consulting startup spending be lower in Canada?

AI consulting startup spending in Canada stays lower when you use free tiers until a client task needs more, pay annually only after a few months of steady use, and use the client's own approved tool accounts for their work. Skip paid AI certificates that a small business owner will not ask about.

## Where should AI consulting spending not be cut in Canada?

AI consulting spending in Canada should not cut the time to check outputs, a written privacy boundary, or a scope that says what the tool cannot do. An output you did not read becomes the client's error. A client list pasted into a tool with no authority to use it is a privacy problem, not a shortcut.

## Which tax rules apply to an AI consulting budget in Canada?

AI consulting income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep signed scopes, invoices, and subscription receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. AI consulting is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
