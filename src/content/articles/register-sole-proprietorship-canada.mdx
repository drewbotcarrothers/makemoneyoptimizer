<QuickAnswer>

- **Own name, no registration (usually):** in most provinces a sole proprietor who trades under their exact legal name does not have to register a business name. Adding a word like "Consulting" changes that.
- **Trade name, register with the province:** for example $60 for five years in Ontario, $30 name request plus $40 registration in B.C., and $68.55 in Nova Scotia.
- **Registering is not tax registration.** You still report income on Form T2125, and GST/HST is a separate test covered in [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).

</QuickAnswer>

A sole proprietorship in Canada is simply you, doing business on your own account. You do not create one by filing anything: the moment you sell a service or product regularly, you are a sole proprietor. What you may have to register is the **business name**. In most provinces and territories, if you trade under your exact legal name ("Priya Shah") you do not need to register, but if you add anything ("Priya Shah Design", "Northside Dog Walking") you register that name with the provincial or territorial registry, usually for roughly $60 to $130 in government fees, including any name reservation, depending on where you live. The Government of Canada's [sole proprietorship page](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/business/start/register-with-gov/register-sole-prop-partner.html) lists the registries and notes that Newfoundland and Labrador has no provincial registration for sole proprietorships.

This guide is the first step of the admin set. After it come [getting a CRA business number](/side-hustles/how-to-get-cra-business-number-canada/) (only if you need one), [opening a business bank account](/side-hustles/business-bank-account-side-hustle-canada/), and [reporting the income to the CRA](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/).

## Do you have to register a sole proprietorship in Canada?

Usually only if you use a business name other than your own legal name. Federal law does not require a sole proprietor to register; the requirement comes from each province or territory, and the [federal overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/business/start/register-with-gov/register-sole-prop-partner.html) says that "in some cases, sole proprietorships operating under the name of the business owner do not need to register."

Ontario is a clear example. Section 2(2) of the [Business Names Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90b17) says no individual shall carry on business or identify the business to the public under a name other than their own name unless the name is registered. The Northwest Territories registry page puts it more bluntly: you are only exempt if you use your "own, exact legal name", so "John Smith Consulting" needs registration because "Consulting" is not part of the legal name ([NWT business names](https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-names/)).

Registering is still worth considering when you trade under your own name if a bank asks for registration documents before opening a business account. That is covered in [how to open a business bank account](/side-hustles/business-bank-account-side-hustle-canada/).

## How do you register a business name step by step?

The order is the same almost everywhere: pick a name, check it, reserve it if your province requires that, register, then deal with tax accounts.

### Step 1: Decide whether you need a name at all

If your clients will pay "Priya Shah" and your invoices, website, and social accounts use your legal name only, many provinces do not require registration. If you want a brand name, plan to register it. The rule is about how you identify the business to the public, so a logo, sign, or Instagram handle using a brand name counts.

### Step 2: Search and reserve the name

Several provinces require a name reservation first: Saskatchewan, Manitoba (for a "named" business), Nova Scotia, PEI, and Yukon list reservation fees in the table above. B.C. calls it a name request. Ontario lets you search the registry for free before you register. A registered business name is not a trademark, and Alberta's page warns that registering a name "does not grant any right of ownership of the name."

### Step 3: Register online with your province

Most registries take online filings with a credit card. Have your legal name, home and business addresses, a short description of the activity, and the name reservation number if you have one.

### Step 4: Save the certificate and note the renewal date

Ontario and New Brunswick registrations last five years, PEI and Yukon use three-year cycles, and Saskatchewan lists a renewal fee. Put the renewal date in your calendar now. In Ontario, if you do not renew within sixty days after the registration expires, you must file a new registration and get a new Business Identification Number ([Ontario notice of expiry](https://forms.mgcs.gov.on.ca/dataset/6073def4-7470-47a9-bf13-4bc22cec9bda/resource/48361552-0263-4e5b-b4d6-43bbfac83fe1/download/on00227e.pdf)).

### Step 5: Check whether you now have a CRA business number

The CRA says you receive a BN automatically when you register or incorporate with Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, or Saskatchewan, and that you register separately in Newfoundland and Labrador, the Northwest Territories, Nunavut, Quebec, and Yukon ([When you need a BN](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/business-registration/business-number-program-account/need-bn.html)). The older federal sole-proprietorship page lists a shorter set of provinces, so if you are unsure, follow the CRA page and the steps in [how to get a CRA business number](/side-hustles/how-to-get-cra-business-number-canada/).

## What does registering a sole proprietorship not do in Canada?

Registering a business name does not limit your liability, does not register you for GST/HST, and does not change how you pay income tax.

- **Liability:** Alberta's registry says a business name "does not have a legal existence in its own right," so the sole proprietor is personally responsible for the debts and obligations of the business ([Alberta](https://www.alberta.ca/register-business-name)). Insurance, not registration, is what protects you from a claim; see [side hustle insurance](/side-hustles/side-hustle-insurance-canada/).
- **Income tax:** you still report the profit on Form T2125 with your personal return. [How to report side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/) walks through it.
- **GST/HST:** registration depends on the small supplier test, not on your business name. See [when you need to register for GST/HST](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Permits and licences:** the federal page points to BizPaL for municipal licences. Dog walkers, food sellers, and contractors in particular should check their city.

## What mistakes do new sole proprietors make with registration?

The common ones are registering when they did not need to, and not registering when they did.

- **Adding a word to your name and assuming it is still "your name."** "Smith Consulting" is a business name in Ontario and the NWT.
- **Thinking the registration is a trademark.** It is proof of use on a provincial register, not exclusive rights.
- **Missing the renewal.** Ontario, New Brunswick, PEI, Yukon, and Saskatchewan all have renewal cycles.
- **Registering a GST/HST account early by accident.** Business Registration Online lets you add a GST/HST account in the same session. Only do that if you intend to register; read the [GST/HST guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) first.
- **Mixing money.** Once you have a name, open a separate account; [separating business and personal money](/side-hustles/separate-business-personal-money-canada/) covers the habits.

## Which tax rules apply after you register a sole proprietorship?

None change because of the registration itself. A sole proprietor reports business income and expenses on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), pays both portions of CPP on net self-employment income, and registers for GST/HST once taxable sales pass $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters (rideshare drivers register from the first fare).
