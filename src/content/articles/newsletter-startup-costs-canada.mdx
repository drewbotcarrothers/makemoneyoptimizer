## How much does a newsletter cost to start in Canada?

A newsletter in Canada costs $0 on a free plan, $17.72 with a .ca domain, $230.15 with a domain, Canva Pro and a USB mic, or $785.51 adding a year of Kit's Creator plan. Totals include 13% Ontario HST on the domain, Canva and the mic. Use your own province's rate.

| Kit | What's in it | Before tax | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0 start | Computer and internet you have, a free Substack, Kit or beehiiv account, Canva Free | $0 | $0 |
| Lean | .ca domain from NamesPro.ca ($15.68/year) | $15.68 | $17.72 with HST |
| Standard | Lean + Canva Pro ($142/year) + FIFINE K669B USB mic ($45.99) | $203.67 | $230.15 with HST |
| Pro | Standard + Kit Creator billed yearly (US$390, about C$555.36) | — | $785.51 |
| Pro with insurance | Pro + writer's insurance at about $50/month (about $600/year) | — | about $1,385.51 |

Kit's plan and the insurance estimate are added without HST. Kit bills in US dollars, so your card statement depends on the rate on the day and your card's foreign-exchange fee. Swap Kit Creator for beehiiv Lite billed yearly (US$588, about C$837.31) and the pro kit becomes $1,067.46.

## Who should use a newsletter startup budget in Canada?

A newsletter budget fits someone with one narrow subject and a day of the week they can keep for months: local news for one town, a trade, a hobby with a buying cycle, a tax or money topic you can source properly. The $0 start fits almost everyone. The standard kit fits someone who wants their own address and a consistent look from issue one. The pro kit only makes sense once you've named the feature you need, like Kit's automations or beehiiv's ad network.

It's a poor fit if the plan is to buy an email list, or if the newsletter only exists to sell something you haven't made. ## What do Substack, beehiiv and Kit take from paid subscriptions?

These are the published rates, checked October 9, 2026. Platform prices are in US dollars unless they say CAD.

| Platform | Plan cost | Platform cut | Card processing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Substack | Free at any list size | 10% of each subscription payment | Stripe fees, plus a 0.7% Stripe Billing fee on recurring payments, per Substack's cost page |
| beehiiv | Free up to 2,500 subscribers, but paid subscriptions need Lite (US$49/month yearly at 1,000 subscribers) | 0% of subscription and digital product revenue | Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 |
| Kit | Free up to 10,000 subscribers, including paid subscriptions and digital products | Included in Kit's commerce fee | 3.5% + C$0.30 per CAD transaction, processing included |

Sources: [Substack's cost page](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037607131-How-much-does-Substack-cost), [beehiiv pricing](https://www.beehiiv.com/pricing), [Kit pricing](https://kit.com/pricing), [Stripe Canada pricing](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing).

**Correcting an earlier version of this page.** This guide used to show a paid-subscription sketch on beehiiv's free plan. beehiiv's pricing page puts paid subscriptions on Lite and up, so a beehiiv paid tier costs at least the Lite plan. Kit's free plan is the $0 option that can sell subscriptions.

A few rules that change the math:

- **Stripe in Canada** charges 2.9% + C$0.30 for domestic cards. Its pricing page adds 0.8% for international cards and 2% if currency conversion is needed. A reader paying in US dollars into a CAD account costs more than the headline rate.
- **Substack on iOS.** Substack's help pages say that outside the US, readers on its iPhone app only see Apple's in-app payment, and Apple's fee is deducted along with Substack's. Expect a lower take on those readers.
- **Sales tax on Substack.** Substack says that from December 1, 2026 it will calculate, collect and remit sales tax on subscriptions, added at checkout for US and Canadian publishers. That doesn't change your income tax.
- **Kit's managed Stripe.** Kit says Canadian creators can sell in CAD through a new Kit-managed Stripe account, with weekly payouts on Fridays after a first payout of 7 to 14 days.

<AffiliateLink program="kit" label="Kit" /> is the cheapest way to charge from day one at small list sizes. <AffiliateLink program="beehiiv" label="beehiiv" /> makes more sense once paid revenue is large enough to cover Lite.

### A one-year sketch

**Illustrative example. A teaching sketch, not a forecast.** Suppose you reach 30 paid readers at C$8 and hold them for 12 months. On Kit Free: 30 × 7.42 × 12 = C$2,671.20 kept after fees. ## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Platform cut and card fees,** on every payment, at the rates above.
- **Domain renewal,** $15.68 a year at NamesPro.ca's listed price before tax.
- **Canva Pro renewal,** $142 a year before tax, if you keep it.
- **A paid plan,** if you choose one. Kit and beehiiv plans rise with list size, so a growing free list can push you into a higher tier even before anyone pays.
- **Insurance,** about $50 a month if you buy the Zensurance-style policy.
- **Refunds and chargebacks,** which come out of your payout.

## Do you need a licence or registration to run a newsletter in Canada?

Usually not a newsletter-specific licence. Two things are worth checking:

- **A business name.** If you publish under a name that isn't your own legal name, most provinces require you to register it. In Ontario, registering a sole-proprietor business name costs $60 online, per the province's [fee page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit). Using your own name needs no registration. Check your province's registry.
- **A local business licence.** Some cities licence home-based businesses. Search your city's site before you assume one doesn't apply. This guide doesn't state a licence fee.

## What does CASL mean for your newsletter budget?

Canada's anti-spam law is free to follow and expensive to ignore. ISED's [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page says commercial email needs consent, your identification, and an unsubscribe mechanism. Budget the time to set up a signup form that says what people are joining, and keep the record of when and how each person signed up. An imported list without consent is a risk, not an asset. Start with ISED's page on [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en).

## Where can newsletter startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Start on Kit Free or Substack** if you want paid subscriptions from day one with no monthly plan.
- **Use the platform's default address** until the newsletter has a reason for a domain.
- **Use Canva Free** for the header.
- **Write text issues before audio.** The mic waits until an issue needs it.
- **Skip paid growth tools and list purchases.** A paid plan is a choice after you know which feature is missing.

## Where shouldn't you cut newsletter spending?

Don't cut consent records, a working unsubscribe link, or the time to check facts. A fast issue with a wrong number costs readers you can't buy back. ## Which tax rules apply to a newsletter budget in Canada?

Newsletter income in Canada is self-employment income when you're carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same total. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Platform fees, the domain and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, like a computer, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. If you're registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html), with receipts that show the tax. If you're not registered, you don't charge GST/HST and you don't claim the credit. For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.
