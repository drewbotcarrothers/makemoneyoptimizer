## How much does a newsletter cost to start in Canada?

A newsletter in Canada starts at $0 on a free tier, about $20–$120 with a custom domain and a design tool, and about $120–$500 over the first months with a paid plan and extra gear. Most newsletter costs are optional, and the free tiers are not trials that run out on the platforms named here.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0 | A free Substack or beehiiv publication, a computer and internet you already have |
| Typical | $20–$120 | A custom domain, a free or low-cost design tool for a header, and a separate account for payouts |
| Fuller | $120–$500 over the first months | A paid platform plan chosen for a feature you need, a USB microphone if issues include audio, and bookkeeping software |

## Who should use a newsletter startup budget in Canada?

A newsletter startup budget in Canada fits people with one narrow subject and a writing day they can keep for months. The lean column fits almost everyone. It is a poor fit if the plan is to buy an email list, or if the newsletter only exists to sell something you have not made.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for a newsletter in Canada?

Newsletter break-even in Canada is paid subscription revenue minus the platform's cut and processing, then minus any monthly plan you chose. A free tier means break-even can be the first paid reader, but your writing hours are still real.

### Step 1: Start on a free tier

Pick the platform by the features you need now, not the plan you might want later.

### Step 2: Write down the platform's cut

Substack's 10%, or beehiiv's 0% plus processing, from the live pages.

### Step 3: Find your real processing fee

Open your Stripe dashboard after the first payment and read the actual fee lines.

### Step 4: Add a paid plan only for a named feature

If the feature does not change what you send, the plan is a cost with no return.

### Step 5: Count consent before you count readers

ISED's [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page says commercial email needs consent, your identification, and an unsubscribe mechanism. An imported list without consent is a risk, not an asset.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical newsletter.**

Suppose you price a paid tier at $8 a month, an assumption, and 5 readers pay in a month: 5 × $8 = $40. On Substack, 10% is $4, leaving $36 before Stripe and tax set-aside. On a free beehiiv plan, the platform cut is 0%, leaving $40 before Stripe. Suppose you bought a $50 custom domain in the same month: the Substack sketch is $36 − $50 = −$14 for the month. The domain is a one-time cost, so month two looks different. These totals are assumptions. Do not copy $8.

## Which tax rules apply to a newsletter budget in Canada?

Newsletter income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep platform payout reports, Stripe statements, and receipts for any domain or tool. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. A newsletter is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
