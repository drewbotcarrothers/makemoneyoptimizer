## How do eBay.ca fees work on sports cards in Canada?

eBay.ca charges a final value fee on sports trading cards of 13.25% of the total amount of the sale up to $7,500, calculated per item, and 2.35% on the portion above $7,500, plus a per-order fee. For orders of $10.00 or less the per-order fee is $0.30. Over $10.00 it is $0.40. [eBay.ca's selling fees page](https://www.ebay.ca/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822) is the live table, including lower store rates if you subscribe.

The total amount of the sale is not always the number on your listing. eBay's own trading-card example sells a card for $9,500 with free shipping and treats $10,735 as the total amount of the sale because that figure includes 13% HST. A second example on the same page, a phone sold to a buyer in Ontario, does the same. Read the page's rules for shipping: if you offer international or 1-day shipping and also a cheaper domestic option, eBay says the total amount is calculated from the cheapest domestic option.

You also get up to 250 zero-insertion-fee listings a month on the standard schedule, then $0.30 a listing. eBay's trading-card example charges that insertion fee once the free listings are used. A Good 'Til Cancelled listing can renew and count again. Fees on the eBay.ca page do not apply if you list on eBay.com. Confirm the site before you price a card in US dollars and pay a US fee schedule by accident.

Amazon.ca is a different table. The public [pricing page](https://sell.amazon.ca/pricing) lists Toys and Games, and Toys and Games - Collectible Cards, at 15%. The Sports Collectibles row does not print a percentage; it points sellers to Category Requirements in Seller Central. Confirm that row before you list a card there.

## What does grading cost for a sports card sent from Canada?

Grading a sports card from Canada costs the grader's posted US-dollar fee plus postage, insurance, and any duty or tax on the way back. PSA's [fees page](https://www.psacard.com/services/fees) lists service levels by price, max insured value, and estimated turnaround. A [February 2026 PSA update](https://www.psacard.com/info/submission-updates) said new card submissions from 10 February 2026 would pay more at several levels, including Value Bulk from US$21.99 to US$24.99, Value from US$27.99 to US$32.99, and Regular from US$74.99 to US$79.99. Open the fees page before you ship. Those figures are the notice, not a promise that today's table is identical.

The Bank of Canada [daily exchange rate](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) for the US dollar was 1.4254 Canadian dollars on 5 October 2026 (Valet series FXUSDCAD). At that rate, a US$32.99 Value fee is about CAD $47.02 before a single stamp. A US$79.99 Regular fee is about CAD $114.02 on the same rate. The rate moves. Convert on the day you pay.

The return trip is a separate cost. [CBSA's mail page](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/import/personal-personnel/postal-postale-eng.html) says duties and taxes apply to international goods imported by mail that exceed the $20 exemption, and that Canada Post charges a handling fee when duty or tax is collected. A graded card coming back from the United States can sit in that process. Ask the grader how Canadian return shipping and declared value work, and price that answer into the card before you submit it.

A raw card that would sell for less than the grading fee plus two shipments is a raw sale, or a pass.

## How do you source sports cards worth flipping in Canada?

Source sports cards in Canada from places where you can see the card, or from sellers who will show front and back in daylight, and buy only what you can identify. Garage sales, local listings, and card shows are the usual start. A title that says "lot of hockey cards" is a pile, not a price.

### Step 1: Learn one sport and a few sets

Pick a league you can recognize. A small set of recent base cards teaches you names, parallels, and what a common card looks like. Branch out after you have sold a few, not before.

### Step 2: Photograph front and back before you pay

Soft corners, print lines, and a scratched surface are the condition. If the seller will not show the back, leave it. Describe the same flaws in your own listing. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) prohibits claims that are false or misleading in a material way.

### Step 3: Price the fee and the postage before you buy

Write the sale price you have actually seen, subtract the marketplace fee, and subtract a real shipping quote. A sold listing from last year is a clue, not a bid you will receive.

### Step 4: Protect the card you keep

A penny sleeve and a top-loader, or a card saver if a grader requires that style, stop the next bend. Buy a small pack once you have cards worth protecting. A <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="sleeve and top-loader pack" search="penny sleeves top loaders trading cards" /> is enough to start. A rigid mailer matters more than a new storage box. <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="Rigid card mailers" search="rigid photo mailers trading cards" /> are the piece that keeps a corner intact in a mailbox. Skip bulk supplies until sales repeat.

### Step 5: Sell locally before you ship

Pickup removes the stamp from the sketch. Use shipping when the card's price can carry the quote you just looked up, including tracking.

### Step 6: Grade only a card that can repay the trip

Submit when the raw price, the posted USD fee, both shipments, and a possible CBSA charge still leave a gap you would accept if the grade comes back lower than you hoped. Turnaround on PSA's table is measured in business days and varies by service level. The card is away for that whole period.

## What does a sports card flip look like after fees and postage?

A sports card flip in Canada is the sale price minus the card, the fee on the total amount of the sale, and the postage. The sketch below is a teaching example.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical card.**

Suppose you buy a raw card for $6 and list it on eBay.ca at $20 with free shipping. Suppose the total amount of the sale eBay uses is $22.60, which is $20 plus an assumed 13% sales tax, following the structure of eBay's own Ontario example. It is an assumption about the tax in the fee base, not a calculation from your listing. Final value fee: 13.25% × $22.60 = $2.99, plus the $0.40 per-order fee, for $3.39. Suppose tracked shipping to the buyer is $14. That $14 is an assumption, not a Canada Post quote. Look up the live rate. Sleeves, an assumed $0.50.

Money out: $6 + $14 + $3.39 + $0.50 = $23.89. Against a $20 item price, this sketch loses $3.89 before a tax set-aside. The marketplace fee was $3.39. The postage was $14. The same card sold for $20 by local pickup, with no eBay fee in the sketch, is $20 − $6 − $0.50 = $13.50 before your time. Do not copy $20 or $14.

A grading add-on changes the sketch again. A US$32.99 Value fee at the 5 October 2026 rate of 1.4254 is about CAD $47.02 before postage both ways. A card bought for $6 cannot carry that trip.

## What mistakes are common when flipping sports cards in Canada?

Common sports card mistakes in Canada are pricing off the fee percentage, grading a cheap card, and shipping without a rigid mailer.

- **Quoting 13.25% and ignoring the stamp.** The fee is on the total amount of the sale, and the parcel is extra.
- **Listing on the wrong eBay site.** eBay.com fees are not the eBay.ca table.
- **Grading to "make" a card valuable.** The fee is due at the grade you receive, including a low one.
- **Calling a card mint because the front looks clean.** Show the back and the corners you actually have.
- **Buying a repack or a lot because the headline names a star.** You are buying the cards in the photos, not the title.

## Which tax rules apply to sports card flipping in Canada?

Sports card flipping in Canada is generally business income on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) when you buy cards to resell. Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income. A one-off sale of cards from your own childhood collection is a different question. Product sales belong on T2125. [T2125 versus T776](/side-hustles/t2125-vs-t776-canada/) is the rental form, which is the wrong page for a stack of cards.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

GST/HST registration follows the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across your taxable revenue. Under the CRA's [digital-economy rules for qualifying goods](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/sales-good.html), a platform can be the one that charges GST/HST for a seller who is not registered.
