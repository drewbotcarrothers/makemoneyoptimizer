- **Short answer:** start on Kit's free plan if you plan to charge readers early, because it sells paid subscriptions at 3.5% + $0.30 a payment with no monthly fee up to 10,000 subscribers. Pick Substack if you want the simplest free start and its reader network, and accept a 10% cut. Pick beehiiv once paid revenue passes about $652 CAD a month, or when you want its ad network.
- **Free tiers:** Substack is free at any list size; beehiiv's free plan covers up to 2,500 subscribers but does not include paid subscriptions; Kit's free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers and includes selling subscriptions.
- **Paid plans (1,000 subscribers, billed yearly):** beehiiv Lite 49 USD a month (about $69.78 CAD); Kit Creator 390 USD a year (about $555.36 CAD).

All three platforms let a Canadian writer charge readers through Stripe. They differ in how they get paid: Substack takes a share of every paid subscription and never charges a plan fee; beehiiv charges a plan fee and takes 0%; Kit charges a per-payment fee that already includes card processing, and its free plan can sell. The cheapest choice depends on how many readers pay you, not on the headline plan price.

Prices below were checked on October 9, 2026. All three price their plans in USD; conversions use the Bank of Canada [daily rate](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) of 1.4240 CAD per USD for October 8, 2026, and a card issuer's rate will differ slightly.

## Which fees matter when Canadians charge readers?

The platform's cut and card processing come out of every payment. A Canadian writer selling to Canadian readers in CAD pays these rates:

| Platform | Platform fee | Card processing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Substack | 10% | Stripe, plus 0.7% Stripe Billing on recurring payments | Substack's help page lists 2.9% + 0.30 USD for U.S. cards; [Stripe Canada](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing) lists 2.9% + CA$0.30 for domestic cards |
| beehiiv (Lite and up) | 0% | Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 | beehiiv's pricing FAQ |
| Kit (any plan) | 3.5% + $0.30, all-in | Included | Kit's CAD fee is 3.5% + $0.30 |

Stripe Canada adds 0.8% for international cards and 2% when currency conversion is required. A Canadian writer whose readers are mostly American pays more on Substack and beehiiv, where Stripe's surcharges apply, than the Canadian domestic rate suggests. Kit quotes one fee per currency and says the 3.5% + $0.30 is the only cost of selling.

Substack's iOS app is a separate case: outside the U.S., readers who upgrade in the iOS app pay through Apple's in-app purchase system, and Apple's fee comes out of your payout along with Substack's 10%. You can raise the in-app price to cover it.

## What does a Canadian newsletter keep at 20 and 100 paid readers?

### Illustrative example: C$8 a month on a 1,000-subscriber list

**Assumed inputs:** a 1,000-subscriber list, a price of C$8 a month, every paid reader on a Canadian card paying monthly, and no in-app purchases. **Sourced inputs:** each platform's fees above, beehiiv Lite at 49 USD a month billed yearly (C$69.78 a month), and Kit's free plan, which sells subscriptions at this list size.

**20 paid readers, C$160 a month:**

- **Substack:** 10% (16.00) + Stripe 2.9% (4.64) + 20 × $0.30 (6.00) + 0.7% Billing (1.12) = **$27.76 in fees, keeping $132.24**
- **beehiiv Lite:** plan 69.78 + Stripe 4.64 + 6.00 = **$80.42, keeping $79.58**
- **Kit Free:** 3.5% (5.60) + 20 × $0.30 (6.00) = **$11.60, keeping $148.40**

**100 paid readers, C$800 a month:**

- **Substack:** 80.00 + 23.20 + 30.00 + 5.60 = **$138.80, keeping $661.20**
- **beehiiv Lite:** 69.78 + 23.20 + 30.00 = **$122.98, keeping $677.02**
- **Kit Free:** 28.00 + 30.00 = **$58.00, keeping $742.00**

### Where do the lines cross?

- **beehiiv Lite vs Substack:** both pay Stripe's card fee and $0.30 a payment, so beehiiv saves Substack's 10% plus the 0.7% Billing fee, 10.7% of revenue, and costs C$69.78 a month. It wins above **$652.15 CAD a month** of paid revenue ($69.78 ÷ 0.107), which is about 82 readers at C$8.
- **beehiiv Lite vs Kit Free:** Kit's 3.5% is 0.6 points above Stripe's 2.9%, so beehiiv only beats Kit on fees above about $11,630 CAD a month at the same list size. Before that, beehiiv's case is its ad network, growth tools and design, not fees.
- **Kit Free vs Substack:** Kit takes 10.1 points less on every payment at any revenue level, as long as the list stays under 10,000 subscribers.

Fees are not the whole decision. Substack's network and app can bring readers who would never find a standalone newsletter, and a larger audience at 10% beats a smaller one at 3.5%. Annual subscriptions also change the maths: one yearly payment instead of 12 monthly ones pays the $0.30 once.

## What mistakes do Canadian newsletter writers make?

- **Importing a contact list without consent.** Canada's [anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) governs commercial electronic messages; use opt-in sign-ups and include an unsubscribe link.
- **Paying for a plan before anyone pays you.** Every platform has a free starting point, and Kit's free plan can already sell.
- **Comparing plan prices without the take rate.** At 20 paid readers, beehiiv's plan fee costs more than Substack's 10%; at 100 it costs less.
- **Pricing in USD by default.** beehiiv and Kit both let you price in CAD, which saves Canadian readers conversion fees and keeps your books in one currency.
- **Moving platforms without exporting.** Export your subscriber list regularly, including which readers pay.

## What could change this newsletter comparison?

List size, the USD exchange rate and promotions move these numbers. beehiiv and Kit price plans by subscriber count, so a list of 5,000 pays more than the 1,000-subscriber prices here, while Substack's percentage stays the same. Each cent of movement in USD/CAD changes beehiiv Lite's yearly cost by about $5.88. Kit's Pro discount is labelled limited-time. Substack's tax collection starts December 1, 2026, and what it means for your own GST/HST filing depends on your registration status. This is not tax or legal advice.

## Which tax rules apply to newsletter income in Canada?

Paid subscriptions and sponsorships are business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), converted to Canadian dollars, and platform plans and processing fees used for the business are deductible expenses. Once your worldwide taxable supplies pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, you must [register for GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html); see [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/). Kit says it collects VAT only and does not charge Canadian taxes such as QST for you, so on Kit and beehiiv that work is yours. For where to keep payouts, see [Wealthsimple vs a bank business account](/side-hustles/wealthsimple-vs-bank-business-account-side-hustle-canada/).
