After this budget you can buy the least mower that does the job, keep a trailer off the shopping list until the route is real, and see how many cuts it takes to earn the machine back in a season that is not twelve months long.

How the season works by region, and how to sign the first neighbours, is the [lawn care side hustle guide](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada). This page is the equipment and the fuel. Used equipment from someone who will let you see it start beats a shiny fleet.

## The quick answer

Ranges mix typical Canadian retail and used-market prices. Check them locally this spring. They are not a bid. They assume you have somewhere legal to store a mower and a fuel can. They do not include a truck payment.

| How you start | One-time cash, about | What you can cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $250–$550 | A used push mower that starts, a rake, a broom, a fuel can, oil, and basic eye and ear protection. You trim edges with the mower and a string trimmer you do not own yet, or you borrow one for the first yards. |
| Typical | $500–$1,100 | A dependable used or entry-level new push mower, a string trimmer, trimmer line, gloves, glasses, hearing protection, and boots if you do not have any with a toe. |
| Doing it properly | $1,000–$2,200 before a trailer | A mower you trust for a weekly route, a trimmer, safety gear, a small repair kit, and a liability quote. A trailer, a riding mower, and a sprayer are upgrades you earn with more lawns than you can push. |

Grass is a season. Southern Ontario and southern Quebec give you a long enough run to matter. The Prairies are shorter and drought can cut the route. Atlantic schedules slip in the rain. The B.C. south coast stays green longer and deals with moss. The North is a short season. Do not finance southern-Ontario equipment debt for a six-week cut. The startup number has to be earned inside the weeks your climate actually grows grass.

## Who this budget fits

**It fits** if you can work in the heat, store equipment, and live where people have lawns rather than only balconies. A push mower on one street is the test. A riding mower is not step one.

**It is a poor fit** if you have nowhere to keep fuel, if your body will not push a mower, or if the “market” is a high-rise whose superintendent already has a contract. It is a poor fit if you need income in January from grass. Pair the year with [snow removal startup costs](/side-hustles/snow-removal-startup-costs-canada) or indoor work, and price that as its own budget.

The same streets produce gate repairs and spring fixes: [handyman startup costs](/side-hustles/handyman-startup-costs-canada). Recurring indoor work is [house cleaning startup costs](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-startup-costs-canada).

## Ongoing costs, and a simple break-even

**Fuel.** A push mower sips compared with a truck, and the truck is the part people forget. Track mower fuel and, separately, every kilometre you drive between lawns. Use the price you paid. A “tank a week” note is enough for the mower if you write down what the tank cost.

**Maintenance.** Blades, oil, filters, trimmer line, belts. A small route might spend $20–$60 a month in season and a larger repair the week a pull cord snaps. Used mowers spend this money sooner. That is already in the lean price if you budget a repair, and it is a nasty surprise if you do not.

**Disposal.** Bagged clippings and leaves may mean paper yard bags the city specifies, or a dump rule you must follow. Know whether you can leave clippings. Illegal dumping is not a saving.

**Insurance.** The seasonal premium, paid when the broker says. A quiet July still has the policy if you bought a season-long one.

**Noise and schedule.** Not a dollar, but a constraint. Many cities limit early Sunday machines. A route you cannot mow inside the bylaw is a route you will rush.

There is no honest national price per cut. Compare three local flyers, then check hours and fuel. If a competitor’s price only works with unpaid help, it is not your price.

### How to calculate break-even

1. One-time equipment and safety gear.
2. Monthly fixed in season: insurance slice, software, a baseline of line and oil.
3. Variable per cut: mower fuel, drive fuel, wear set-aside, bags.

Contribution per cut = fee minus variable cost.

Cuts to earn back the mower = equipment cost ÷ contribution per cut.

Then ask a season question: how many of those cuts fit in your climate before the grass stops? A mower that needs 40 cuts to pay for itself, on a street that only needs 12 cuts a year, is a hobby with expenses.

### Illustrative example

**Illustration only. Not a quote for a lawn in your city.**

Used mower $300. Rake, can, glasses, earmuffs $80. Startup = $380. You delay the trimmer. Insurance is $30 a month for a five-month season in this sketch, so $150 for the year, which you can treat as $30 a month while you are cutting. Oil and line are $15 a month. Monthly fixed while cutting = $45.

One small, flat lawn pays $40. Mower fuel is about $1.50. You walked, so drive fuel is $0. Contribution = $38.50.

Mower payback = $380 ÷ $38.50, or about 10 cuts. That could be ten lawns once, or two lawns for five weeks. Monthly fixed takes about two cuts ($45 ÷ $38.50).

Add a 8 km round trip in a car that uses 8 L/100 km at $1.60/L: fuel is 8 × 0.08 × 1.60 = $1.02, plus wear at $0.12/km = $0.96. Contribution falls to about $36.50 and the time gets worse. Four far lawns can pay less per hour than two on your block. A dry July that skips two weeks does not refund the insurance. This is why the season sketch matters more than a single Saturday. Use your prices.

## Where you can spend less

Buy used, and start it cold if you can. New is for when the used market is tired. Push mower, not rider, and no trailer for a walkable street. Secure the mower in a car. A loose mower is a crash.

Leave clippings when the client allows it. Do not buy a pesticide program. Several provinces restrict cosmetic pesticides. Mow, trim, and rake until you have a legal product. A notebook is enough until the route is annoying.

## Where not to cut corners

Eye and ear protection, and boots with a toe. A string trimmer throws debris. Your hearing does not grow back. This is the safety spend inside an otherwise lean kit.

Liability insurance before you are on a dozen properties. A window or a sprinkler head is an ordinary accident. Home insurance often excludes business use. This is not insurance advice.

Blade condition. A dull blade tears grass and makes you look sloppy, and a loose blade is dangerous. Shut the machine off and pull the boot or the plug before you clear a clog. The same habit shows up again in snow season.

Fuel storage. Follow the can and the municipality. A balcony full of gasoline is not a shop.

The season itself. Do not promise twelve equal months. If you need winter income, sell a winter service with its own price, not a discount that pretends July happens in February.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Lawn fees are self-employment income. Keep clients, dates, and receipts for fuel and repairs. Report the business on form T2125 with your return. Both shares of CPP generally apply to the net.

**Vehicle log and CCA.** A car or truck used to haul the mower needs a kilometre log if you claim it: date, destination, kilometres, purpose. Business use is business kilometres divided by all kilometres. Apply that percentage to fuel, insurance, and maintenance. The mower itself is equipment. You generally cannot deduct a lasting machine in full in the year you buy it. Capital cost allowance (CCA) spreads the cost on the business-use portion, in the class the CRA assigns. A simple first year with modest used gear is easier to explain than a trailer you financed for four lawns. If the amounts get large, ask an accountant. Do not claim the family car as 100 percent business.

**GST/HST.** Small-supplier test: $30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, including associates, in one calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Under that, you usually do not have to register. Over it, you do. Timing, including one quarter versus four, is on the CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page. Lawn care is not ride-sharing. Do not apply the taxi first-dollar rule here. Add snow and handyman revenue to the same test if those are also you.

**Input tax credits.** If you are registered, GST/HST paid on fuel, repairs, and equipment for the business can often be claimed as an input tax credit to the extent of commercial use. Keep receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST on the cut and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, use the rate for the province. Quebec charges QST as well as GST. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025. Confirm on the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Noise bylaws, yard-waste rules, and cosmetic pesticide rules are provincial or municipal. Search the ones on your street before you buy a sprayer. A business licence, if your city wants one, belongs in the budget at the fee they publish.
