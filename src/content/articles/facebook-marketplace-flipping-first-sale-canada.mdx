## What should you flip first on Facebook Marketplace in Canada?

Flip one small item you can test on the spot for a first Marketplace flip in Canada: a cordless tool with its battery, a bike, a small kitchen appliance, or outdoor gear. It should fit in your vehicle and work, or fail, in front of you.

Set expectations from a tracked test: in March 2026, Moose Money's Winston Sih spent about 20 hours flipping in Toronto and made $97 profit, roughly $4.85 an hour ([Moose Money](https://www.moosemoney.com/best-items-to-flip-on-marketplace/)). A first flip is tuition in your local prices; margin comes from knowing one category.

Before you list anything bought, sell five things you already own. That teaches you how fast items move and what buyers in your area actually pay. Skip car seats, cribs, and hockey helmets as first flips. Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) lists them among regulated products and says recalled items that have not been corrected should not be sold. Search [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) for any children's product.

## Where do Canadians find a first item to flip?

Canadians find a first item to flip in Marketplace listings priced below recent sold prices, often from people moving, clearing a garage, or upgrading. Search the category daily for a week and note which listings disappear quickly.

Walk away from deals that look too good with no story, missing serial numbers, or sellers who will not meet in public. Meta's [commerce policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce) prohibit items that were stolen or otherwise acquired unlawfully or without authorization.

## What should a first Marketplace flip listing say?

A first Marketplace flip listing in Canada should state the item, model, what you tested, what is worn or missing, and the pickup area. Honest detail cuts down on lowball messages and no-shows.

**Example listing text to copy and adapt:**

> Cordless drill kit, 20 V, with one battery and charger. Battery charged and tested; drill runs at both speeds. Case has a cracked latch (photo 4). Cleaned. Pickup at a public parking lot near [area]. Cash or e-transfer received before handover.

Do not call an item "barely used" if it was not. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) covers claims made to sell a product.

## How do you build trust on Marketplace without fake reviews?

You build trust on Marketplace in Canada with a complete profile, daylight photos from several angles, a photo of the item working, and quick, consistent answers. Ratings come from real transactions; asking friends to rate you for sales that never happened is a false signal.

Offer to let the buyer test the item at the meetup. That one line in the listing removes most doubt.

## How should you price the first Marketplace flip in Canada?

Price the first Marketplace flip in Canada from recent sold prices for the same model and condition, then check that the price covers what you paid, supplies, and fuel for both trips. Search the model, then scroll to listings marked "Sold" or watch which ones disappear within a day; asking prices that linger are not the market. Use the 50% rule as a starting point: if a model sells for $100, pay no more than about $50 so cleaning, fuel and a lower offer still leave a margin. ## How do you hand over a first Marketplace flip safely?

Hand over a first Marketplace flip safely by meeting in a public, well-lit place (a police buy-and-sell exchange zone where your region has one), telling someone your plan, and confirming payment before the item leaves your hands. Facebook's [meeting-in-person tips](https://www.facebook.com/help/2329750133711372?locale=en_GB) also say to keep your home address private and carry minimal cash.

Facebook's [scams page](https://www.facebook.com/help/721562085854101/) warns against trusting payment screenshots, overpayment offers, and sending deposits, and says Purchase Protection covers checkout purchases only, not cash or person-to-person payments. Check that an e-transfer has landed in your bank app. If you are targeted, report it to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/index-eng.htm).

## What does a 14-day plan for a first Marketplace flip look like?

A 14-day plan for a first Marketplace flip in Canada should produce five own-item sales or listings, one tested purchase, and one honest flip listing. The own-item sales in week one are the cheapest price research you will ever do.

### Days 1 and 2: Read the rules

Read Meta's commerce policies, the meetup tips, and the scams page. Write down what you will not buy or sell.

### Days 3 to 5: Sell your own items

List five unused things. Note which sell and at what price.

### Days 6 to 9: Watch one category and buy one item

Track sold prices for one category. Buy one item you can test in person, after a recall search.

### Days 10 to 14: Clean, list, and sell

Clean and photograph the item. List it honestly. Meet in public and confirm payment first.

## What mistakes block a first Marketplace flip in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first Marketplace flip in Canada are buying without testing, pricing from asking prices instead of sold ones, and releasing items on a payment screenshot. Each one costs money.

Buying items that look stolen breaks Meta's policies and risks losing the item. Forgetting fuel for two trips makes small flips unprofitable. Sharing your home address for meetups is a safety risk.

## Which tax rules apply to a first Marketplace flip in Canada?

Marketplace flipping income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

Keep purchase records, sale records, supply receipts, and a mileage log. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Flipping items on Marketplace is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST.
