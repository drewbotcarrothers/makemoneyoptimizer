App-based delivery and similar gigs are visible side options in many Canadian cities. They can add cash in busy weeks — and they can also hide costs. This sample guide helps you evaluate fit without sugarcoating.

## Fit check

**Might fit if:** you want flexible short shifts, know your city, and already have reliable transport.

**Might not fit if:** your vehicle is expensive to run, you need stable weekly income, or long hours on the road affect your health or insurance.

## Count the real costs

Gross payouts aren’t take-home. Account for:

- Fuel or charging
- Maintenance, tires, depreciation
- Phone data and bags/equipment
- Insurance implications for commercial use (confirm with your insurer)
- Unpaid time waiting between orders

A simple log for two weeks beats any viral earnings screenshot.

## Timing and boundaries

- Peak meal times and bad weather can raise demand — and stress.
- Set a hard stop for late-night shifts if safety or sleep suffers.
- Don’t chase every ping; fatigue causes expensive mistakes.

For lower vehicle wear, explore [online tutoring](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-canada) or [freelance writing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-canada).

## Safety and professionalism

- Follow platform safety features; share trip status with someone you trust when useful.
- Park legally; tickets erase a night’s work.
- Keep interactions courteous; ratings affect access to orders.
- Never claim “guaranteed hourly” outcomes to others — platforms change incentives.

## Admin notes for Canadians

Gig income is usually self-employment from a tax perspective. Keep downloadable earnings summaries and expense receipts. GST/HST and provincial rules can apply depending on thresholds and activity — verify with CRA or a professional. This site doesn’t provide tax advice.

## Try-before-you-commit plan

1. Run a short pilot (a few shifts) with a cost spreadsheet open.
2. Compare net hourly to other options you could do with the same time.
3. Reassess insurance, parking, and physical strain.
4. Continue only if the trade-offs still make sense for *your* life.

Gig delivery is a tool, not a personality. Use data, protect your safety, and keep exit options open.
