## How much does online tutoring cost to start in Canada?

Online tutoring in Canada starts at about $25–$80 when you already have a computer, a clear laptop camera, and internet, about $90–$220 when a webcam or one month of a paid meeting plan is added, and about $250–$650 when accounting software at its list price, a provincial screening fee you confirmed, and an insurance conversation are included. The ranges are not quotes. A tutoring platform's commission is not in the table. Read that platform's own page.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What you can teach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $25–$80 | A headset, the computer you have, and a free meeting tool with the time limit that tool publishes. A lamp you already own. |
| Typical | $90–$220 | The headset, a webcam if the built-in camera failed a one-minute test, and either Zoom's paid plan or a notebook for two families. |
| Doing it properly | $250–$650 | The typical kit, FreshBooks once more than a couple of families are on the calendar, a screening check your province or the parent actually requires, a short course only if you priced that course, and a broker's quote. |

USD prices in this guide use the Bank of Canada's daily rate of 1.4188 Canadian dollars per US dollar on 29 September 2026, from the [daily exchange rates](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) table. The Canadian figure is approximate. Zoom and FreshBooks may bill in US dollars or convert at checkout, and the rate will move.

## Who should use an online tutoring startup budget in Canada?

An online tutoring startup budget in Canada fits people who can explain one subject slowly, keep a quiet room, and start on time after other work ends. The lean column fits a direct booking with someone who already knows you can teach. Online tutoring is a poor fit if you need income this week, if your evening job cannot move, or if the budget is a semester of software before the first paid hour.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for online tutoring in Canada?

Ongoing online tutoring costs in Canada are the meeting plan if you kept it, insurance if you bound it, no-shows you did not write a rule for, and prep you forgot to count. Online tutoring break-even in the sketch is a method, not a Canadian hourly rate.

**Cancellations.** A written window, for example 24 hours, is part of the price. An empty chair still burned the Zoom month if you paid for Zoom.

**Platform cuts.** If a platform introduces the student, the fee is on that platform's page. It is not in the sketch below.

**Summer.** The calendar goes quiet. A yearly Zoom plan does not.

**Screening.** A cheque you paid for a check is a one-time cost. It is not a reason to teach a subject outside your sentence.

### Step 1: Add the headset and any camera you actually needed

Gear is the one-time line. A lamp you already own stays at $0.

### Step 2: Add the meeting plan at the price you will pay after the trial

Free Zoom covers a short trial lesson. A longer session needs the paid plan or a different tool whose limit you have read.

### Step 3: Add invoicing only after the second family

One e-transfer can live in a note. A four-pack needs a ledger.

### Step 4: Count prep, the call, and the note

The session log is the hours. Five lines after the call is part of the job, not a gift.

### Step 5: Apply the no-show rule you wrote first

The rule is the same for every family. Inventing it after a missed hour makes the price look optional.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of tutoring rates.**

Suppose the headset is $40, inside the $25–$80 retail range, and the laptop camera was clear, so the webcam stays at $0. Suppose lessons are 50 minutes, so the free 40-minute Zoom tier does not fit, and you use the annual Pro figure of about $20 CAD for the month. Suppose a parent books three sessions at an assumed $40 each. That $40 is an assumption for the arithmetic, not a market rate. Gross billings = 3 × $40 = $120. Cash out = $40 + $20 = $60. Remainder = $60. Suppose each session takes 30 minutes of prep and a short note, so three sessions are about three hours of work plus the calls, roughly 5.5 hours. This sketch is about $60 ÷ 5.5, or about $11 an hour, before a platform fee and before tax set-aside.

Later weeks in this sketch do not subtract the headset again. Four sessions at the same assumed $40, with Zoom still about $20, would be $160 − $20 = $140 before prep. The first week is the expensive one because of the gear. Do not copy $40. If you are registered, tax on the invoice is not extra profit.

## Where can online tutoring startup spending be lower in Canada?

Online tutoring startup spending in Canada stays lower when the first lessons fit a free meeting limit, the webcam waits for a failed recording, and the course about finding students waits until someone has paid for a real hour. Direct bookings from people who already know you skip a platform fee. A window is a light. A shared document is a whiteboard.

Use Zoom's annual price only if you will still be teaching when the year is half over. Otherwise the monthly card is the honest one, even though the per-month figure is higher. FreshBooks' introductory year is a checkout fact to confirm, and the list price belongs in the note beside it. Skip a website until a parent asks for one.

## Where should online tutoring spending not be cut in Canada?

Online tutoring spending in Canada should not skip the headset, the cancellation rule, or the screening step your province or the parent requires. A student who cannot hear you will not book the second hour. A no-show rule you invent later trains families to treat the price as optional.

Do not promise a grade, and do not buy software that implies a certificate you do not hold. Private tutoring is not the same thing as employment as a certified teacher. This guide does not find a national tutoring licence.

Insurance is a conversation with a broker before you decide a home policy covers paid teaching. This is not insurance advice. A minor's lesson is a position of trust. The adult who is paying should know the time, the price, and how to reach you.

Keep prepaid packs in an account you can reconcile. A deposit is pay for a future hour. It is not spending money on the day it arrives.

## Which tax rules apply to an online tutoring budget in Canada?

Online tutoring income in Canada is self-employment income, including a prepaid pack, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. A headset and a month of Zoom are generally current expenses. A computer or a tablet that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. GST/HST for online tutoring uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the booking messages, the e-transfers, and the session log. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing and any other side hustle to the same pot. Online tutoring is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

If you are registered, GST/HST paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use. You generally need to be a registrant when the tax was paid or became payable, and you need receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, do not add GST/HST to the session fee and do not claim the credit. Confirm the current rules with the CRA or a tax professional.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set the tax aside. A platform payout report does not replace that record.
