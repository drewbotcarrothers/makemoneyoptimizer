## How should you choose freelance writing software in Canada?

Choose freelance writing software in Canada by what clients ask you to deliver (Google Docs links, Word files, or CMS drafts), what you would otherwise do by hand, and the monthly cost against your current client count.

| Criterion | What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client file format | Do clients want .docx, Google Docs links, or CMS access? | The word processor has to match their workflow |
| Free tier limits | What the free plan leaves out | Many writers never need the paid tier |
| Currency and billing | Price in USD or CAD, monthly or yearly | A USD price moves with the exchange rate |
| Security | Multi-factor authentication support | Client drafts and payment details live in these accounts |
| Data location and privacy | Where files are stored, who can access them | Some clients set rules on where their material goes |
| Export | Can you get your data out? | Switching later should not trap your archive |

## How should freelance writers in Canada secure their tools?

Freelance writers in Canada should use unique passphrases and multi-factor authentication on every account that holds client work or payment details. Get Cyber Safe's [passphrase guidance](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts/passphrases-passwords-and-pins) recommends unique, complex passphrases or passwords for every account and device, and warns that phishing messages often pose as reputable companies to get login details. Its [multi-factor authentication page](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts/multi-factor-authentication) says to enable MFA whenever a site, app, or device allows it.

## Which tax rules apply to freelance writing software in Canada?

Freelance writing income is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Software subscriptions used for the business are generally expenses. A computer or monitor that lasts years is depreciable property. Computer hardware is generally Class 50 at 55% a year, while general-purpose software is Class 12, per the CRA's [CCA classes](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html); subscriptions are ordinary expenses. Claim only the business-use portion of anything you also use personally.

GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
