<QuickAnswer>

- **Hockey:** complete Hockey Canada's Intro to Officiating online course, then register with your provincial Member and attend its clinic. [Hockey Canada](https://www.hockeycanada.ca/en-ca/hockey-programs/officiating/how-to-get-started) says to ask the local association first whether it is accepting officials.
- **Soccer:** Canada Soccer points certification to your provincial or territorial referee program. Soccer Nova Scotia says Canada Soccer's entry-level course is for participants who are 14, and the practical session is run by the province. Confirm the age and the fee with your own association.
- **Pay is not one national game fee.** Job Bank's median for "referee, sports" in Canada is $19.00 an hour (low $15.00, high $30.00), reference period 2023–2024, updated 19 November 2025. Associations publish their own game fees.
- **Recertify when the body says so.** Hockey Canada's pathway document says officials register and re-certify annually.

</QuickAnswer>

Becoming a sports referee as a side hustle in Canada means getting certified by the governing body for that sport, then accepting games your local association assigns. Hockey and soccer are the two pathways this guide can tie to national pages. It suits people who can make weeknights and weekends, stay calm with coaches, and treat the clinic as the start rather than a formality. It is a poor fit if you only want games that never conflict with a Saturday job you already cannot move.

Evening work in a different room is [bartending and serving](/side-hustles/bartending-serving-side-hustle-canada/). Coaching fitness for hire, which is a different certification, is [personal training](/side-hustles/personal-training-side-hustle-canada/). The winter menu of outdoor work is [best winter side hustles](/side-hustles/best-winter-side-hustles-canada/).

## Who does sports officiating in Canada suit?

Sports officiating in Canada suits people who already understand the sport, can be at a rink or a field on the association's schedule, and will do the clinic and the yearly renewal. Hockey season and outdoor soccer season do not occupy the same months in most of the country, which is why some people do one and not both. Travel between rinks is unpaid unless the fee schedule says otherwise.

Job Bank's [occupation summary](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/16333/ca) describes sports officials and referees as people who observe and enforce rules and who are employed by national, provincial, and local sporting commissions, organizations, and leagues. A local assignor may still pay you per game as casual employment or on another basis. The CRA's page on [determining employment status](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/canada-pension-plan-cpp-employment-insurance-ei-rulings/employee-self-employed/determine-employment-status.html) is the test. The association's label is not.

## How do you become a hockey official in Canada?

You become a hockey official in Canada by finishing Hockey Canada's Intro to Officiating course and then the clinic your provincial Member requires. [Hockey Canada's getting-started page](https://www.hockeycanada.ca/en-ca/hockey-programs/officiating/how-to-get-started) lists two steps: the online Intro to Officiating course on the Hockey Canada Learning Lab, then registering and attending a clinic hosted by your provincial Member. It says to contact the local hockey association before you register, to confirm they are accepting officials and to confirm the Member's certification steps. Member certification may include classroom learning, on-ice instruction, and online learning.

The [officiating pathway PDF](https://cdn.hockeycanada.ca/hockey-canada/Hockey-Programs/Officiating/Downloads/hc-officiating-pathway-e.pdf) says officials must register and re-certify annually with Hockey Canada, including a Member-hosted clinic and a Hockey Canada curriculum assessment. It also says officials will be required to purchase basic officiating equipment as laid out in the program. Buy that list, not a generic kit from a video.

Age is a Member rule, not a number this guide will nationalize. [Hockey Saskatchewan](https://hockeysask.ca/officials/getting-started-officiating) says it and Hockey Canada recommend that officials be 12 by December 31 of the current season, and that a 10-year-old can ask the local minor hockey association whether games and support exist. Read your own Member's page. Adults have extra screening in some branches. The [Northern Ontario Hockey Association](https://www.noha-hockey.ca/entry-level-officials-clinics) says officials who are 18 or older must submit a valid vulnerable sector check before registering for its entry-level program. That is one Ontario branch's rule for its clinic. Ask yours.

## How do you become a soccer referee in Canada?

You become a soccer referee in Canada through your provincial or territorial association, using Canada Soccer's entry pathway. [Canada Soccer's referee page](https://www.canadasoccer.com/referee/) is the national resource and tells you to connect with your provincial or territorial Referee Development Officer for certification, training, and match-official development.

[Soccer Nova Scotia](https://soccerns.ca/referees/entry-level-referee-course-information/) describes an entry-level course in two blocks: Canada Soccer's online theory modules, then an in-person practical session the province runs. It says Canada Soccer states that participants must be 14 for the entry-level referee course. That age is stated on the Nova Scotia page. Confirm it, and the registration fee, with the association where you will actually referee. This guide does not publish a national soccer registration fee because the provinces set those numbers on their own registration pages.

Other sports, including basketball, baseball, softball, and volleyball, use their own national and provincial bodies. Start on that sport's official "become an official" page. Do not assume a hockey clinic qualifies you for a soccer field.

## What do sports referees earn in Canada?

Employed sports referees in Canada have a national median of $19.00 an hour, according to [Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/16333/ca) (updated 19 November 2025, reference period 2023–2024, Labour Force Survey). The same page lists a low of $15.00 and a high of $30.00. Those are prevailing hourly wages for the occupation. They are not a minor-hockey game fee and not a promise for a Saturday you have not been assigned.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a pay scale.

Suppose an association's fee card, which you have read, pays an assumed $35 for one game you can legally officiate at your level. Suppose you are assigned an assumed 2 games on a Sunday, and travel is an assumed 40 minutes each way that the card does not pay. Cash fees in the sketch are 2 × $35 = $70. The afternoon is longer than the games. If your target for the whole outing, including travel, is the Job Bank median of $19 an hour, then $70 covers $70 ÷ $19 = about 3.7 hours before you are under that target. The sketch does not know your fee card, your gas, or whether the association treats you as an employee. Replace $35 and the travel with the real card.

## What does officiating cost to start in Canada?

Officiating costs in Canada are the clinic or registration fee the Member publishes, a yearly renewal, and the uniform and protective equipment that body lists. This guide does not copy a fee that was not on the national page. Hockey Canada's getting-started page does not state a dollar fee for Intro to Officiating. The provincial clinic page does. Read it in the season you register.

A whistle is a small piece of that kit, and only after the association names the model. A <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="referee whistle" search="referee whistle" /> is a category search, not the approved crest, jersey, or helmet. Stripes and crests are often sold through the association so the uniform matches. A black helmet from a general listing is not automatically the CSA-rated piece a branch requires. Read the equipment list.

Screening fees, where a vulnerable sector check is required, are set by the police service that issues it. Budget the time as well as the fee. A clinic you miss because the check is late is a season you do not start.

## How do you get your first games in Canada?

You get your first games in Canada by finishing the level the assignor requires and telling that person which nights you can actually stand at the rink or the field.

### Step 1: Contact the local association before you pay

Hockey Canada says to confirm they are accepting officials. Soccer associations post course dates that fill. A payment to the wrong branch does not move you onto a game sheet.

### Step 2: Finish every part of the clinic

Online modules alone are not the certification when the Member also requires a classroom or on-ice session. The NOHA entry-level page says registering for the online introduction does not automatically register you for the in-person clinic. Treat that as a pattern to check, not as every province's form.

### Step 3: Buy only the listed equipment

The pathway PDF says basic equipment is laid out in the program. A second jersey can wait until you are being assigned.

### Step 4: Give the assignor a true calendar

Weeknights are how many minor-hockey schedules work. If you can only do one Sunday, say so. A referee who no-shows is harder to rebook than a new referee with a short honest window.

### Step 5: Recertify before the next season opens

Hockey Canada's document says annual re-certification. Soccer associations typically require a yearly registration too. Confirm the date on your association's page so a lapsed card is not a surprise in September or October.

## What mistakes are common for new officials in Canada?

Common mistakes for new officials in Canada are registering with the wrong body, buying a full uniform before the equipment list, and treating a Job Bank hourly median as the game fee. Also common: skipping the vulnerable sector check a branch requires at 18, and accepting a level of game the certification does not allow.

Show up for the games you accepted. The assignor is the client. A second sport can wait until the first season's renewal is a habit. [Personal training](/side-hustles/personal-training-side-hustle-canada/) is a different certification if what you want is coaching rather than enforcing rules.

## Which tax rules apply to referee pay in Canada?

Referee pay in Canada is taxed according to the actual relationship. If the association is your employer, expect deductions and a slip. If you are genuinely self-employed, you report net income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both shares of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution. Use the CRA's [employment status page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/canada-pension-plan-cpp-employment-insurance-ei-rulings/employee-self-employed/determine-employment-status.html) rather than a guess.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

GST/HST on self-employed officiating follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all of your side hustles. Employees do not charge GST/HST on wages. Keep the fee statements and the clinic receipts. The [record-keeping guide](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) covers what to store. Ask the association whether its insurance covers you while you are assigned. This page does not state a coverage limit.
