A home baking side hustle means selling low-risk baked goods, such as cookies, bread, brownies and cakes with shelf-stable icing, from your own kitchen. Whether you can sell from home, only at markets, or only after a permit depends on your province: Alberta and Saskatchewan allow sales from home without a permit, British Columbia and Manitoba limit home-baked sales to markets and similar events, Ontario asks you to notify your public health unit, and Quebec requires a permit and training. The Canadian home bakers in the news grew through farmers' markets, pre-orders and holiday products, one batch at a time.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Rules vary by province:** Alberta has allowed low-risk home-prepared food sales from home and at special events without a permit since June 1, 2020; Quebec requires a MAPAQ permit, hygiene training and a 100 kg-a-month cap; Manitoba prohibits home food businesses except at farmers' markets, flea markets, craft sales and bake sales.
- **Labels:** Alberta and Saskatchewan require a "made in a home kitchen" statement, your name and contact details, the food's name and the date it was made. Saskatchewan's label must be at least 12-point type.
- **Real examples:** Aurora, Ont. baker Reem Karkoutlian went from an expected 20 loaves to about 100 a week at farmers' markets (AuroraToday, August 4, 2026); Okotoks, Alta. baker Chloe Feihle brings about 50 sourdough loaves to a market day (OkotoksOnline, 2026).
- **Wage benchmark:** Job Bank lists bakers at $15.00 low, $17.50 median and $24.81 high per hour (2023 to 2024 data, updated November 19, 2025).
- **Tax:** business income on T2125 with both CPP shares; GST/HST registration past $30,000, and sweet baked goods sold in quantities under six single servings are generally taxable once you're registered.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does home baking suit?

It suits people who already bake reliably, enjoy early mornings before a market, and can follow food-safety routines in a family kitchen. You'll also need patience with paperwork, because every province handles it differently.

Good fits:

- **Stay-at-home parents** who can bake during school hours. Melanie Chenier in North Bay started on maternity leave. See [side hustles for stay-at-home parents](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-stay-at-home-parents-canada/).
- **Sourdough hobbyists** whose friends already ask to buy loaves.
- **Holiday bakers** who want a seasonal income in November and December. See [the best winter side hustles in Canada](/side-hustles/best-winter-side-hustles-canada/).
- **Crafters** who already sell at markets and can add a baked line.
- **Retirees** with time for weekly markets.

It's a poor fit if you can't keep pets out of the kitchen while baking, your specialty is cheesecake or cream pastries, or your province only allows market sales and you can't do weekends.

## Can you sell baked goods from home in your province?

Each province sets its own rules for low-risk home-prepared food. The summaries below come from official provincial pages; municipal zoning and business-licence rules can apply on top.

| Province | Where you can sell | Permit or notice | Labels and training |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alberta | From home and at special events such as craft fairs and festivals | No permit, commercial kitchen or routine inspection | Label with a home-kitchen statement, "not for resale", name, contact, food name and date; free AHS food safety courses encouraged |
| Saskatchewan | Directly to the public, to retail stores and to restaurants; not to food processors or care facilities | No routine inspection | 12-point label with "Made in a Home Kitchen That is Not Inspected by a Government Agency", name, address, phone, food name and date; a trained person must be present while preparing |
| Ontario | Home-based food businesses allowed under the Food Premises Regulation | Contact your local public health unit before selling; periodic inspection | Low-risk-only businesses are exempt from food handler certification and commercial dishwashing rules |
| Quebec | Retail sales; no wholesale or resale through an intermediary | MAPAQ permit and inspection | Mandatory hygiene and food safety training; 100 kg of food a month cap; kitchen can't be used for personal cooking at the same time |
| British Columbia | Only at temporary food markets, prepackaged; not from home or in stores | No application for lower-risk foods | Sign stating the food was prepared in an uninspected kitchen; MarketSafe or FOODSAFE Level 1 strongly recommended |
| Manitoba | Only at farmers' markets, flea markets, craft sales and bake sales | Follows the province's farmers' market guidelines | Home-based food businesses are otherwise prohibited |

Sources: Alberta's [low-risk home-prepared foods](https://www.alberta.ca/low-risk-home-prepared-foods) page and [fact sheet](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/7981d37f-185e-496f-81ae-a935831635ca/resource/c462be1f-77ab-4409-bced-b29f3e5a37b7/download/health-low-risk-home-prepared-food-fact-sheet.pdf); Saskatchewan's [Home Food Processing fact sheet](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/91663/formats/108565/download); Ontario's [Guide to Starting a Home-Based Food Business](https://www.ontario.ca/files/2024-03/moh-guide-to-starting-home-based-food-business-en-2021-11-01.pdf) (November 2021); Quebec's page on [selling home-cooked food](https://www.quebec.ca/agriculture-environnement-et-ressources-naturelles/agriculture/agriculture-urbaine/reglementation/don-vente-aliments-frais-ou-cuisines-a-la-maison) (in French); the BCCDC [temporary food market guideline](https://www.bccdc.ca/resource-gallery/Documents/Guidelines%20and%20Forms/Guidelines%20and%20Manuals/EH/FPS/Food/Guidelines%20-%20Sale%20of%20Foods%20at%20Temporary%20Food%20Markets_current.pdf) (revised August 2020); and Manitoba's [notice to home-based food businesses](https://www.gov.mb.ca/health/publichealth/environmentalhealth/protection/home.html). For any other province or territory, contact your provincial health authority or local public health unit before you sell.

A few details matter in practice:

- **Alberta:** only you and members of your household can prepare the food; others may sell on your behalf at special events. No on-site dining at home, though free samples are allowed. Inspections can still follow a complaint.
- **Saskatchewan:** "low risk" means a water activity of 0.85 or less or a pH of 4.6 or below. Foods not on the province's list may need lab testing at your expense.
- **Ontario:** the 2020 changes to the Food Premises Regulation made low-risk home-prepared foods easier to sell. Check zoning by-laws and municipal licences before calling the health unit.
- **Manitoba:** the province says it is actively seeking home-based food businesses operating outside the exception.

## Which baked goods count as low risk?

Low-risk foods don't need refrigeration to stay safe. Ontario's guide lists most breads and buns (without meat or cream filling), most baked goods without custard, cakes with icing that doesn't need refrigeration, brownies, muffins, cookies, granola, fudge and toffee. BC's list adds butter tarts and cinnamon buns with sugar icing.

**Usually allowed:** cookies, brownies, sourdough and yeast breads, bagels, muffins, scones, focaccia, butter tarts, fruit pies, shortbread, biscotti, granola, fudge, cakes with sugar-based icing.

**Usually not allowed from a home kitchen:** cheesecake, cream or custard pies, whipped-cream or cream-cheese frostings, meat pies, quiche, anything that must be refrigerated.

## What should a home-kitchen label say?

Write labels to the strictest rule that applies to you, and add allergens. Alberta and Saskatchewan spell out the required statements; BC requires a sign at the point of sale; and CFIA rules on prepackaged food can apply everywhere.

**Label template (adapt to your province):**

```text
[PRODUCT NAME] – [weight or count]
Made in a home kitchen that is not inspected by a government agency.
Not for resale.
Ingredients: [list in descending order by weight]
Contains: [wheat, eggs, milk, soy, tree nuts, etc.]
May contain: [peanuts / tree nuts, if your kitchen handles them]
Made on: [date]   Best before: [date]
[Your name / business name] · [phone or email] · [address if Saskatchewan]
```

On nutrition labelling, the Canadian Food Inspection Agency says a prepackaged food is exempt from the Nutrition Facts table if it's sold only at a road-side stand, craft show, flea market, fair, farmers' market or sugar bush by the person who prepared it ([CFIA exemptions](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/nutrition-labelling/exemptions)). The exemption is lost if you add vitamins or minerals or make nutrient or health claims. Sales through stores or online fall outside that exemption.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

**Week 1: rules and recipes.**
- Read your province's page above and call your local public health unit (required in Ontario; useful everywhere). Ask about zoning and municipal licences too.
- Take a food safety course: AHS offers free online courses in Alberta; MarketSafe or FOODSAFE Level 1 in BC; an approved course is required in Saskatchewan and training is mandatory in Quebec.
- Pick two low-risk products and write recipes in grams.

**Week 2: test and cost.**
- Bake each recipe twice at full batch size. Weigh dough pieces and record yields.
- Cost every ingredient per unit, plus packaging and labels.
- Set prices that leave at least the Job Bank baker median for your hours.

**Week 3: packaging, labels and first sales.**
- Buy boxes or bags and print labels.
- Take pre-orders from friends, neighbours and a local community group for one pickup day.
- Apply to a farmers' or craft market; many fill their vendor lists months ahead.

**Week 4: sell, record and adjust.**
- Run your first pickup day or market table. Track units baked, sold and wasted.
- Ask buyers which product they'd reorder.
- Drop the weaker product or change the price.

## What mistakes are common?

- **Selling before checking the rules.** In BC and Manitoba, selling from your front door isn't allowed.
- **Cream cheese frosting and custard.** They turn a low-risk product into a high-risk one.
- **Missing allergens on labels.** Wheat, eggs, milk, nuts and sesame are in most baking.
- **Pricing by feel.** Weigh, cost and time every batch.
- **Baking for a market without pre-orders.** Unsold bread is a loss.
- **Calling it gluten-free or nut-free** when your kitchen can't guarantee it.

## Which tax rules apply to home baking?

Selling baked goods regularly is business income. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Ingredients, packaging, labels, market fees, card fees and a share of home costs can be deductible; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/) and [side hustle tax prep before January](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-prep-before-january-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Once registered, food isn't all treated the same. The CRA's [basic groceries memorandum](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/4-3/basic-groceries.html) treats sweetened baked goods such as cakes, muffins, cookies, pies, tarts and brownies as taxable when sold in quantities of fewer than six single servings and zero-rated in packages of six or more; unsweetened bread products are generally zero-rated. That affects both pricing and whether a sale counts toward the threshold. Use <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> or a spreadsheet to track sales by product.

This is not tax, legal or food-safety advice.

### Provincial tax notes

- **Ontario:** 13% HST on taxable sales once registered.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP; QST applies alongside GST once registered.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** GST plus a separate PST; check how PST treats food you sell.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Baker volumes and prices are as reported by local news outlets in 2025 and 2026, not audited profits, and two of the bakers use licensed or certified kitchens.
