To plan dog walking routes in Canada, group clients who live close together, sell time windows instead of fixed times, and size each walk to your city's rules. A route of five dogs on three nearby streets earns more per hour than five dogs spread across town. City rules shape the route too: where dogs can be off leash, how many you can walk, and what permit you need. This guide is for walkers moving from a few clients to a real weekday route.

## How do you build a dog walking route in Canada?

### Step 1: Map your clients

Put every client on a map and draw a circle you can cover between pickups without losing much time. Google Maps can [measure distance between points](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/1628031?hl=en), so you can check the walk or drive between homes. New clients inside the circle get your normal rate. Clients outside it get a higher rate or a referral.

### Step 2: Sell windows, not exact times

Offer midday windows, such as late morning to early afternoon, so traffic, a slow eater or a late key doesn't wreck the day. Put the window in your service agreement.

### Step 3: Decide solo, pairs or groups

Solo walks fit reactive, senior or new dogs. Small groups fit dogs that have met and walk well together. Do a trial walk before adding any dog to a group, and keep a written note of who can't walk with whom.

### Step 4: Fit groups to your city's limits

Toronto's [commercial dog walker permit](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/commercial-dog-walker-permit/) applies when you walk four to six dogs. It costs $305.99 a year and requires $2,000,000 in liability insurance. Calgary's [professional dog walker permit](https://www.calgary.ca/pets/permits.html) applies when you walk more than six dogs off leash in an off-leash park. Metro Vancouver requires a [commercial use permit](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/commercial-use-dog-walking-permit) for walkers in regional parks. Check your own city before you grow a group.

## What leash and park rules shape dog walking routes in Canada?

Toronto's [responsible dog ownership page](https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/animals-pets/pets-in-the-city/dogs-in-the-city/responsible-dog-ownership/) says dogs must be leashed in public, including parks, school yards, beaches, sidewalks and roadways, unless in a designated off-leash area. Leashes should be no more than two metres long, and you can't tie a dog to a pole or bike rack. The City's [off-leash page](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/public-notices-bylaws/bylaw-enforcement/dogs-off-leash/) sets a $500 charge plus surcharges, up to $615, for a dog off leash outside a designated area. It also says dogs in off-leash areas must be vaccinated and licensed, and dogs can't be left unattended. Pick up waste every time, and carry more bags than you think you need. Other cities have similar rules, so read yours before choosing parks.

### Illustrative example

Suppose you have six clients: four within a few blocks of each other and two across town. You'd build a midday route from the four, with two calm dogs walked together and two walked solo. The two far clients would move to a fixed day with a travel surcharge or be referred out. In Toronto, you'd stay at three dogs or fewer at once unless you held the commercial permit and its insurance.

### What clustering does to your hourly rate

Using Rover's Toronto average of $20.73 for a 30-minute walk as the price, and assumed travel times (labelled **Illustrative**):

| Route shape | Walks | Walking time | Travel between homes (assumed) | Revenue | Revenue per hour on the road |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clustered (4 homes within a few blocks) | 4 solo walks | 2.0 h | 0.4 h | 4 × $20.73 = $82.92 | $82.92 ÷ 2.4 h ≈ $34.55 |
| Spread (4 homes across town) | 4 solo walks | 2.0 h | 1.6 h | $82.92 | $82.92 ÷ 3.6 h ≈ $23.03 |
| Clustered pair walk (2 compatible dogs together) | 2 dogs, 1 walk + 2 solo | 1.5 h | 0.4 h | $82.92 | $82.92 ÷ 1.9 h ≈ $43.64 |

The spread route is close to Job Bank's $20.00 Toronto-region median for animal care workers before any car costs; the clustered route is well above it. That gap is why the first rule of routing is to say no to far-off addresses or charge for the travel.

## What templates can you copy for dog walking routes?

**Route sheet (one per weekday)**

```text
Route: [Mon] midday window 11:00–14:00   Zone: [streets/postal codes]
#  Client / dog      Address (no key label)  Key method    Walk   Group?  Notes
1  [name]/[dog]      [street]                lockbox [code ref] 30 min  solo   reactive to bikes
2  [name]/[dog]      [street]                owner home    30 min  pair w/3  no treats (allergy)
3  ...
Vet / emergency contacts in phone under "[Client] VET"
End-of-route: text each owner "Walked [time], pee/poo, water topped up, door locked."
```

**Message to a client outside your zone**

```text
Thanks for reaching out! My regular route covers [area] on weekdays between
11 and 2. [Your street] is outside it, so I can either add a $[x] travel
fee per visit on [day], or refer you to [walker/app] who covers your area.
```

**Window clause for your service agreement**

```text
Walks happen within the agreed window ([11:00–14:00]), not at a fixed time.
I'll text if I expect to be more than 30 minutes outside the window.
```

## What mistakes do dog walkers make with routes in Canada?

- **Saying yes to every address.** Spread-out clients eat your hourly rate.
- **Promising exact times.** One delay pushes every later dog.
- **Growing a group past your permit.** Toronto's permit starts at four dogs.
- **Letting dogs off leash outside designated areas.** Fines apply, and so does liability.
