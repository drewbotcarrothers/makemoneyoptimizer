## What does merchant of record mean for a Canadian Gumroad seller?

Merchant of record, in Gumroad's help article, means Gumroad is the seller of record to the buyer for sales tax. The article says Gumroad handles sales-tax collection and remittance, and that you do not manage tax settings for that. The pricing page's own FAQ is narrower than its headline: it says Gumroad collects taxes in regions where it has obligations as a merchant of record, and that this varies by location. Read that line, not the slogan "all your tax obligations."

That job is the tax on the buyer's purchase. It is not your Canadian income tax, and it is not a decision about whether you must register for GST/HST. The sales-tax article also says it is not legal or tax advice, and it points some creators who already file indirect-tax returns to a reseller certificate. Digital products are the subject of this guide. The same article has a separate rule for physical goods shipped into the EU. If you ever ship a physical parcel, read that section before you assume VAT was collected.

Your payout is still income. Report it. The form choice between business income and rental income is [T2125 versus T776](/side-hustles/t2125-vs-t776-canada/). A digital file is the business-income side of that page.

## How do Gumroad fees work on a single sale?

Gumroad's fee article splits direct sales from Discover sales. Direct means the buyer used your profile or your link. Discover means a new customer found the product through Gumroad's marketplace.

| Path | What the fee article says | Processing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your link or profile | 10% + $0.50 | Card processing listed separately at 2.9% + $0.30. PayPal fees are also extra |
| Discover | 30% flat | Included in the 30% |
| After $20,000 paid sales in the calendar month | New direct sales that month: 5% + $0.50 | The $0.50 stays. Discover stays at 30%. A refund can drop you back under $20,000 |

There is no monthly plan on these pages. A $2 product still pays the $0.50, which is a large share of a very cheap file. Price with that fixed piece in view.

When a sale is refunded, the fee article says Gumroad's own fee on the refunded portion comes back to you, and the processing portion stays with the processor. Sales through your own connected Stripe or PayPal account follow a different refund note on that page. Read it before you connect one.

## What would one Gumroad sale leave you in Canada?

The sketch uses an assumed US$20 price because the payout article speaks in USD for the minimum, and the fee lines themselves do not name a currency. Confirm your dashboard. The sketch is a teaching example, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose one direct sale at $20.

- Gumroad's percentage: 10% × $20 = $2.00, plus $0.50, so $2.50.
- Card processing, if the fee article's card rate applies: 2.9% × $20 = $0.58, plus $0.30, so $0.88.
- Combined: $2.50 + $0.88 = $3.38. You keep $16.62 before any sales tax sitting on the buyer's total and before a later conversion into CAD.

At the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4254 on October 5, 2026, $16.62 USD is about $23.69 CAD if those fee dollars were USD. Suppose the same $20 sale comes through Discover: 30% × $20 = $6.00, processing included, and you keep $14.00, about $19.96 CAD at the same rate. One sale does not describe a month. ## How do Canadian creators get paid by Gumroad?

Canadian creators get paid by Gumroad to a local bank account in CAD, on the getting-paid article's country table. The article says Gumroad pays the local currency for your country and does not pay USD into a Canadian payout if USD is not that country's listed currency. Conversion uses the rate at the time of sale, not the payout date.

Practical rules from that article:

- Standard schedules need a balance of $100 USD. Some countries have a higher local minimum. Canada's row does not show one of those higher minimums.
- You can choose weekly, monthly, or quarterly. Those schedules include sales through the previous Friday (UTC), with a minimum 7-day hold. Daily and instant payouts, on that page, are for eligible US creators.
- Canadian bank accounts are in the Wednesday payout group. The bank can then take more business days to post it.
- You need a government-issued photo ID and proof of residence in the country, or a business registered there. A P.O. box is not accepted as the account address.
- Changing payout country, or switching method in a way that needs a new connected account, can forfeit unpaid amounts that sit on the old country's connected account. The article says Gumroad will name the amount and ask you to confirm. Wait until a payout in progress has finished. Read that note before you edit the country.
- Buying your own product to "test" or to speed review can get the account suspended. The article says to use the test purchase feature.

PayPal payouts, on the same page, are the path when bank deposits are not offered. Canada is on the bank list, so the Canadian path described there is the bank, in CAD. PayPal payouts are processed in USD with a 2% fee when that is the mode you are on.

## How do you start selling on Gumroad from Canada?

Starting on Gumroad from Canada means finishing one file people can use, putting it on a link you can send, and only then worrying about Discover.

### Step 1: Finish one specific file

A checklist for one job, a short ebook, a template with instructions. The [first ebook sale](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-first-sale-canada/) and the [first printables sale](/side-hustles/printables-first-sale-canada/) are the making-and-telling steps. A pile of thin files is the pattern in [overhyped side hustles](/side-hustles/overhyped-side-hustles-canada/).

### Step 2: Open the account and read the prohibited list

Create the <AffiliateLink program="gumroad" label="Gumroad" /> account, then read the pricing page's note on what you cannot sell. It points to a list that includes illegal goods, securities, gambling, and adult content, among other categories. If your file is in that list, pick another product. Do not look for a workaround.

### Step 3: Price above the fixed fee

A $2 file pays $0.50 to Gumroad before the percentage and before processing. Use the illustrative arithmetic above with your real price. Design the cover in a tool whose licence you have read. <AffiliateLink program="canva" label="Canva" /> is one editor. The [Canva Creators](/side-hustles/canva-creator-side-hustle-canada/) program is a different contract from "I designed a cover."

### Step 4: Set CAD bank payout and identity checks before you announce the link

Payments settings, photo ID, proof of address, Canada as the payout country. Do this before the first buyer, so a sale is not stuck on a missing document. The review note on the getting-paid page says reviews vary and that you should not buy your own product to speed them up.

### Step 5: Send the link to people who asked

Your own audience, a client who wanted the checklist, a newsletter people joined. If you email, follow [Canada's anti-spam legislation](/side-hustles/cold-email-lead-generation-casl-canada/). A purchased list is not a launch plan.

### Step 6: Export the CSV and record the deposit

The fee article says the sales CSV shows the fees. Match it to the Wednesday payout. Record the CAD that landed, not the sticker price on the product page. [Record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) is the habit. [USD platform income](/side-hustles/usd-platform-income-tax-canada/) helps when a line is still in US dollars.

### Step 7: Judge Discover after you have direct sales

Discover at 30% can be worth it if a buyer you would not have reached shows up. It is an expensive way to learn that nobody wanted the file. Turn it on with your eyes open, and read the fee on the next CSV.

## What Gumroad mistakes do Canadian sellers make?

Canadian Gumroad sellers most often treat the pricing-page headline as the whole fee, ignore the $100 USD minimum, or treat merchant of record as a reason to skip a tax return.

- **Quoting 10% and stopping.** The help article adds card processing on direct sales. The pricing page's "no hidden fees" line and the help article's processing line should be read together. The CSV is the tie-breaker.
- **A $2 product.** The $0.50 fixed fee is a quarter of it before the percentage.
- **Changing payout country with a balance sitting there.** The getting-paid article describes forfeiture of amounts that cannot move. Read the confirmation.
- **"Gumroad does my taxes."** It describes sales tax on the buyer's transaction where it has obligations. You still report income.
- **Uploading before you have anyone to tell.** Distribution is the work. The checkout is the easy part.

## Which tax rules apply to Gumroad sales in Canada?

Gumroad payouts to a seller in Canada are generally business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), in Canadian dollars. Platform fees you actually bear are generally expenses. Self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST registration depends on the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) and on how supplies through a merchant of record are treated in your situation. See [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/). Do not decide that question from Gumroad's marketing line.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the CSV, the payout notice, and the exchange detail. The [side hustle tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/) is the short list of mix-ups, including the idea that $30,000 of sales is tax-free. It is not.
