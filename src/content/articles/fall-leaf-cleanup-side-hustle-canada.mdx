A fall leaf cleanup side hustle in Canada means raking or blowing leaves, cutting back perennials and shrubs, and bagging everything so the city will collect it, in the weeks between leaf drop and the end of yard waste pickup. The disposal rules decide how you work. In Toronto, for example, yard waste is collected every other week from around mid-March to mid-November, only in kraft paper bags or rigid open-top containers, never plastic bags or cardboard, and the City says residents who hire landscaping contractors are responsible for making sure contractors follow the rules. Book clients for October and early November, learn your city's set-out rules, and sell the job as a finished, collection-ready yard.

## What does a fall yard cleanup service include?

A fall yard cleanup usually combines leaf removal with tidy-up tasks that prepare a yard for winter. Agree on the list in writing before you start, because "cleanup" means different things to different clients.

| Task | What it involves | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leaf removal | Raking or blowing leaves off lawns, beds, and walkways | The core job; may need two visits in heavy-tree areas |
| Bagging for collection | Filling kraft paper bags or rigid containers to city rules | Plastic bags are refused in Toronto |
| Bed and perennial cutback | Cutting back spent plants on request | Toronto suggests leaving some plant material for pollinators |
| Brush and branch bundling | Tying small branches into bundles | Toronto caps bundles at 1.2 m long, 0.6 m wide, 20 kg |
| Pumpkin and gourd removal | Adding post-Halloween pumpkins to yard waste | Toronto accepts pumpkins, gourds, and corn stalks |
| Gutter clearing | Only if you are equipped and insured for ladders | Separate risk; price separately |

## What yard waste rules apply to a leaf cleanup business?

Yard waste rules are set by each municipality, and they bind you when you work for a homeowner. On Toronto's [yard waste page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/recycling-organics-garbage/houses/yard-waste/), the City says:

- Yard waste goes in **kraft paper yard waste bags or rigid open-top containers** 55–95 cm tall; yard waste in plastic bags or cardboard boxes is not collected.
- Set it out **0.5 m from the garbage bin**, no later than 7 a.m. (and no earlier than 8 p.m. the night before) for daytime collection, or between 6 p.m. and 9 p.m. for night-time collection.
- **Brush bundles** must be tied with natural fibre rope, no longer than 1.2 m, no wider than 0.6 m, and no heavier than 20 kg; wood thicker than 7.5 cm is not accepted.
- **Grass clippings are not accepted** as yard waste, and **invasive plants** such as buckthorn, garlic mustard, and dog-strangling vine go in tied plastic bags as garbage.
- **Residents who hire landscaping contractors are responsible** for making sure the contractors follow the rules.

Other cities set different seasons, bag types, and limits, so confirm locally before quoting. If a client's yard produces more bags than collection takes, ask whether they want you to haul extra to a drop-off depot; Toronto notes fees may apply at depots.

## Should you remove every leaf?

Not necessarily. The City of Toronto suggests leaving some fallen leaves and branches on lawns in fall because they provide habitat for native pollinating insects over winter and add nutrients to the soil. Offer clients a choice between a full clear-out and a "lawn and walkways clear, beds left natural" option, and price each.

## How should you price fall leaf cleanup?

Price fall cleanup by the job after seeing the yard, based on your estimated hours plus bags and disposal. The labelled example shows the arithmetic.

### Illustrative example

The rates, hours, and bag cost below are assumptions to show the method, not Canadian market prices.

- **Target rate:** an assumed $45 an hour for your time.
- **Estimated time:** a mid-size yard with mature trees, 2.5 hours to rake, blow, and bag.
- **Bags:** an assumed 12 kraft bags at $1 each = $12.
- **Quote:** 2.5 × $45 + $12 = $112.50 + $12 = $124.50, rounded to $125.
- **Second visit:** if leaves are still falling, quote a follow-up visit separately, or offer a two-visit package at the time you book.

Track your actual hours on the first few yards and adjust the estimate.

## What mistakes do people make with a leaf cleanup side hustle in Canada?

The common mistakes are bagging wrong, booking too late, and ignoring the cold.

- **Using plastic bags where the city refuses them.** In Toronto, plastic-bagged yard waste is not collected.
- **Leaving bags on city property.** Toronto asks for yard waste to be stored on the owner's property between collections.
- **Missing the last pickup.** Toronto's collection ends around mid-November; check the schedule.
- **Putting invasive plants in yard waste.** Toronto requires them in garbage.
- **Working wet hands in near-freezing weather.** CCOHS's [cold work guidance](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/cold/cold_working.html) recommends gloves below 4°C for light work.
- **Climbing to gutters without the right ladder and coverage.** Price and insure it as a separate service, or decline it.

## Which tax rules apply to a leaf cleanup side hustle in Canada?

Fall cleanup income from your own clients is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and bags, tools, fuel, and the business share of vehicle use are generally deductible. Add it to your lawn and snow income for the GST/HST [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html).
