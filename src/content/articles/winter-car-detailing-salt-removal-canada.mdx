A winter car detailing side hustle in Canada is mostly interior work and careful, low-water exterior cleaning: lifting salt stains out of carpets and mats, cleaning salty door jambs and sills, doing rinseless or bucket washes in a garage, and protecting paint before the worst of the season. It exists because Canadian roads get a lot of salt. Environment and Climate Change Canada's road salt code of practice says about 5 million tonnes of road salts are used as de-icers on Canadian roadways in an average year. Two rules shape the work. Wash water has to follow your city's sewer bylaw (in Toronto, only rain and melted snow may enter a catch basin, and both the mobile washer and the person who hires them are responsible), and cold-weather work needs a safety plan.

## Which winter car detailing services can you offer in Canada?

The winter services that fit a side hustle focus on salt residue inside the car and low-water exterior care. The table is a planning aid, not a price list.

| Service | What it involves | Where it can be done | Water rules matter? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interior salt stain removal | Vacuuming, treating and extracting white salt lines from carpets and mats | Driveway, garage, or client's parking spot | Little; contain any extraction water |
| Floor mat swap and cleaning | Installing winter mats; cleaning and drying the originals | Anywhere | Dispose of rinse water properly |
| Door jamb and sill cleaning | Wiping salt and grime from jambs, sills, and trunk edges | Anywhere | Low-water |

| Underbody rinse | Pressure rinsing the underside | Commercial wash bay, not a driveway | Yes; use a facility |

## Where can winter wash water go in Canada?

Wash water follows the municipal sewer bylaw, and salty, soapy water is not rain. On Toronto's [washing cars and outdoor surfaces page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/water-environment/water-sewer-related-permits-and-bylaws/sewers-by-law/washing-cars-outdoors-surfaces/), the City says nothing other than rain and melted snow can flow into an outdoor catch basin. Its options for washing are a commercial car wash, washing on a permeable surface such as gravel, or using a pail with a small amount of water and disposing of it in the sanitary sewer through a laundry sink or toilet. The City adds that both the person hiring and the mobile wash business are responsible for wastewater discharged from the property. Other cities have their own bylaws; read yours before you quote an exterior.

## How do you stay safe detailing cars in Canadian winter?

Plan for cold the way outdoor trades do. The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety's [working in the cold guidance](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/cold/cold_working.html) says:

- Gloves should be used **below 4°C for light work** and below −7°C for moderate work when fine dexterity is not needed.
- A **work-warming regimen** is suggested for continuous work when the wind chill is at or below −7°C.
- At or below **−12°C**, work should include constant observation (a buddy system), a pace that avoids heavy sweating, and training on recognizing cold stress and frostbite.

Wet hands and wet clothing make cold worse, so schedule exterior work for the warmest part of the day, keep dry gloves on hand, and move interiors into a garage when you can.

The rates, times, and supply costs below are assumptions to show the method, not Canadian market prices.

## What mistakes do people make with winter car detailing in Canada?

The common mistakes are letting wash water reach the street, ignoring cold, and promising what winter will undo.

- **Hosing a car on a driveway** so salty, soapy water runs into a catch basin.
- **Leaving carpets wet** in a cold car, where they dry slowly and can smell.
- **Working without gloves** in near-freezing weather.
- **Promising a car will stay clean.** The next salty road will coat it again; sell maintenance visits instead.
- **Skipping a pre-inspection.** Note existing stains, rust, and chips with photos before you start.

## Which tax rules apply to winter car detailing in Canada?

Detailing income from your own clients is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and supplies, equipment, and the business share of vehicle costs are generally deductible. Add it to all your other side business income for the GST/HST [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html).
