<QuickAnswer>

- **Business name:** the ISC Corporate Registry lists $50 for a name reservation, $65 to register and $60 to renew.
- **PST is its own step:** Saskatchewan says all businesses operating in the province must be licensed or registered for PST (6%). GST (5%) follows the federal $30,000 small-supplier test.
- **Cities:** Saskatoon issues a Home-Based Business Licence ($135 new, $95 renewal) and Regina requires a residential business licence ($195 a year) for any business run from a home, including online businesses.
- **Minimum wage:** $15.70 an hour from October 1, 2026, for employees. No app-worker minimum.

</QuickAnswer>

In Saskatchewan, a side hustle has more registration steps than in most provinces. Beyond the usual business name question (ISC lists $65 to register, plus a $50 reservation), the province says every business operating in Saskatchewan must be licensed or registered for its 6% PST, and both Saskatoon and Regina license businesses run from home, including online ones. GST is separate and only required after $30,000 in taxable sales. This guide suits anyone in Saskatchewan starting a service, selling goods, or picking up gig work.

## Do you need to register a side hustle business name in Saskatchewan?

If you use a business name, yes, through the ISC Corporate Registry. The [ISC fee table](https://www.saskregistries.ca/hubfs/Corporate-Registry-Fees-Table-04-2026.pdf) effective April 15, 2026 lists $50 for a name reservation, $65 to register a business name and $60 to renew. The federal [sole proprietorship page](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/business/start/register-with-gov/register-sole-prop-partner.html) notes that sole proprietors using their own name sometimes do not need to register; Regina says that before it issues a licence under a business name, the name must be registered in Saskatchewan, so check with ISC and your city before you skip it.

### Step 1: Reserve the name

Search and reserve the name with ISC so you know it is available.

### Step 2: Register and save the documents

The CRA lists Saskatchewan among the provinces where a business number is issued automatically when you register ([When you need a BN](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/business-registration/business-number-program-account/need-bn.html)).

### Step 3: Apply for PST and your city licence

These are separate applications, covered below.

## Do Saskatchewan side hustlers need a PST licence?

Saskatchewan's position is broad. The province's [PST page](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/taxes-licensing-and-reporting/provincial-taxes-policies-and-bulletins/provincial-sales-tax) says all businesses operating in Saskatchewan must be licensed or registered with the Ministry of Finance for PST purposes, receiving either a vendor's licence number or a registered consumer number depending on the business. You apply online through SETS ([apply for a PST number](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/taxes-licensing-and-reporting/provincial-taxes-policies-and-bulletins/provincial-sales-tax/apply-for-a-pst-number)).

A vendor's licence is for businesses that sell taxable goods or provide taxable services. The province's [service enterprises bulletin](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/7978/formats/11533/download) lists taxable services, including commercial building cleaning and repair and installation services for goods, and describes a registered consumer number for businesses that do not provide taxable services or make taxable sales. If you are unsure which applies, ask the Ministry of Finance before you start invoicing.

## When do Saskatchewan side hustlers charge GST?

GST is federal and separate from PST. The CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) shows Saskatchewan at 5% GST plus 6% PST. You do not need to register for GST until worldwide taxable sales pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)), except rideshare drivers, who register from the first fare.

### Illustrative example

A teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote. A Regina handyperson repairs appliances, a service the bulletin lists as taxable, and bills $1,200 a month. At $14,400 a year they are under the GST threshold, so a $100 repair carries 6% PST ($6.00) once they hold a vendor's licence, but no GST. If they later pass $30,000, the same repair would carry $6.00 PST plus $5.00 GST.

## Which city licences do side hustles need in Saskatoon and Regina?

Both cities license home-based work.

- **Saskatoon:** businesses operated from the owner's principal residence receive a [Home-Based Business Licence](https://www.saskatoon.ca/business-development/business-licenses/business-licence-categories/home-based-business-licence), $135 new and $95 a year to renew. The rules cap client visits (three at a time, eight a day) and ban signs on the property. Animal boarding, grooming or care is on the city's list of prohibited home-based businesses, which matters for pet sitters who host dogs.
- **Regina:** anyone operating a business, including an online business, from a residence needs a [residential business licence](https://www.regina.ca/bylaws-permits-licences/licences/business-licences/), $195 a year. A business based outside Regina that works in the city needs a non-resident licence ($450).

For food, Saskatchewan's [home food processing fact sheet](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/91663/formats/108565/download) requires an approved food safety course for low-risk foods; see the [home baking guide](/side-hustles/home-baking-side-hustle-canada/).

## Is there a minimum wage for gig app workers in Saskatchewan?

Not a platform-specific one. The province's [minimum wage page](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/employment-standards/payment-of-wages-payroll-administration-benefits-and-time-sheets/minimum-wage-and-reporting-for-duty-pay) lists $15.70 an hour from October 1, 2026, which applies to employees. Independent contractors on delivery apps are paid on the app's terms, so measure your hourly pay after costs; [food delivery pay in Canada](/side-hustles/food-delivery-pricing-canada/) shows how.

## What are the common Saskatchewan side hustle mistakes?

- **Assuming small means no PST account.** The province says all businesses must be licensed or registered.
- **Running from home without a city licence.** Saskatoon and Regina both require one.
- **Hosting dogs in Saskatoon.** Animal boarding and care is a prohibited home-based business there.
- **Charging GST before registering.** Only add GST once you hold a GST number.

## Which tax rules apply to side hustles in Saskatchewan?

You report profit on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with your T1, pay both halves of CPP on net earnings, and Saskatchewan income tax is calculated on the same return. Keep PST collected out of your income. [Side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/) covers what you can claim, including licence fees, and <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> is one way to track two tax rates on invoices.
