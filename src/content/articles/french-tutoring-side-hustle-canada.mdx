## Who does French tutoring in Canada suit?

French tutoring in Canada suits people who can teach one level of French on purpose: early immersion reading, core French grammar, or adult conversation aimed at a test or a job. It suits bilinguals who can explain a mistake in the student's stronger language and then return to French. It is a poor fit if you are fluent and impatient, if you will teach every grade, or if you describe yourself as a certified translator. Certified translator titles are reserved. The bilingual guide cites the Ontario and Quebec regulators for that title. Tutoring is not that title.

Say which French you teach. School French in an English-language board, everyday Quebec French, and a federal language test are three products. A parent in an immersion program is buying the school course. An adult is often buying confidence for a poster that lists a language level. Read the poster with them. Do not promise the level.

## Where does French tutoring demand come from in Canada?

French tutoring demand in Canada comes from three places that are easy to blur: French immersion, core French, and adults.

[The Daily](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/251028/dq251028d-eng.htm) for 2023/2024, released 28 October 2025, says enrolments in French immersion as a share of total elementary and secondary enrolments fell to 11.6%, after a peak of 12.3% in 2020/2021. Almost every province and territory in that release was still declining. The Northwest Territories moved the other way, from 11.8% in 2022/2023 to 12.4% in 2023/2024. The release says detailed comparisons across provinces need caution because reporting practices differ. A falling share is not "no students." It is a reason to sell a specific grade in a specific city, not a national gold rush.

Immersion and core French are different programs. Statistics Canada publishes them on separate rows of [table 37-10-0009-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3710000901). Immersion is French as the language of instruction for students in English-language schools. Core French, in the table's regular second-language category, is French taught as a subject. A tutor who only drills vocabulary will frustrate an immersion family whose child takes math in French. A tutor who only speaks immersion-speed French will lose a core French student who needs the textbook chapter. Ask which program, which grade, and which province's curriculum.

Outside Quebec, minority official-language programs (French first-language schools) were 4.3% of enrolments in 2023/2024 on that Daily release, down from 4.4% at the 2020/2021 peak. Those students are not immersion students. If a parent from a French first-language school wants tutoring, the subject may be math or English, not "French as a second language." Ask.

In Quebec, the same Daily says the share of students in minority official-language programs fell to 8.2% in 2023/2024. French is the language of the majority system there. A French-tutoring offer in Montreal is often for newcomers, for allophone students, or for English speakers who need French at work, not a copy of an Ontario immersion ad. City context is in [best side hustles in Montreal](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-montreal-canada/) and, for the federal capital, [Ottawa](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-ottawa-canada/). Provincial paperwork is in [side hustle rules in Quebec](/side-hustles/side-hustle-rules-quebec-canada/).

Adults are the third pool. The preamble to the [Official Languages Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/O-3.01/page-1.html) records that the Constitution of Canada provides that English and French are the official languages of Canada. Some federal jobs ask for a language level. The poster states the level. A conversation hour can help. It is not a guarantee of the test. Newcomers who need French for a province's immigration stream should read that province's current criteria themselves. Do not invent a passing score.

## How should French tutoring be offered in person and online in Canada?

French tutoring in Canada can be in person or on a video call, and the product should say which. In person, the police-check rules in the [in-person tutoring guide](/side-hustles/in-person-tutoring-side-hustle-canada/) apply whenever the student is a child: the [RCMP](https://rcmp.ca/en/criminal-records/criminal-record-checks/vulnerable-sector-checks) says the hiring organization requests a vulnerable sector check, Ontario's [police record checks](https://www.ontario.ca/page/police-record-checks) page separates that check from a standard criminal record check, and British Columbia routes vulnerable-sector checks for covered organizations through the [Criminal Records Review Program](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/crime-prevention/criminal-record-check/law-policy), where a child is under 19. Online sessions with a child still involve a family's trust. Offer the check they ask for.

### Step 1: Pick one learner

Immersion student, core French student, or adult. One sentence.

### Step 2: Match the materials

Use the chapter they have, not a random worksheet from another province. Provincial course names differ. Ontario, British Columbia, and Quebec do not share a single Grade 9 French label.

### Step 3: Write what a session includes

A spoken warm-up, the school task, and a three-line note to the parent who was not in the room. For an adult, the note goes to the learner.

### Step 4: Price the travel or the platform, not a fantasy hourly

There is no official French-tutor wage on this page. Read three local offers. The [online tutoring pricing guide](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-pricing-canada/) shows how to build a sheet without pretending the numbers are a survey.

### Step 5: Find students where the program already exists

Immersion and core French families cluster at particular schools. A school may allow a community posting and may forbid staff referrals. Ask. Parent networks, alliances that support French second-language education, libraries, and newcomers' programs are the ordinary doors. Do not buy a class list.

## What mistakes do French tutors make in Canada?

Common mistakes are advertising "French" with no program attached, using France-only vocabulary for a Canadian school course without saying so, and promising a federal language level. Another is ignoring Quebec's different market. Another is treating Statistics Canada's 11.6% as a count of families who will hire you. It is a share of enrolments, randomly rounded, and the Daily says the figures can be revised.

A further mistake is calling yourself a certified translator, or offering certified translation of official documents. That work has its own regulators, named in the [bilingual guide](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-bilingual-french-english-speakers-canada/).

## Which tax rules apply to French tutoring in Canada?

French tutoring fees in Canada are income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed tutors pay both shares of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. Quebec self-employed contributors use the Quebec Pension Plan. Confirm that with Revenu Québec.

[Memorandum 20-6](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-6/tutoring-equivalent-services.html) says tutoring an individual in an elementary or secondary course approved for credit by a school authority, or that follows a curriculum designated by a school authority, can be exempt from GST/HST. French that is part of that curriculum can fall in the section when the connection is direct. The memorandum says post-secondary courses are outside the exemption. A general conversation package with no direct tie to a school course is the kind of broad supply the memorandum says is not exempt. Adult job-poster coaching is unlikely to be a school-authority curriculum. Treat it as taxable unless you have a ruling.

The [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) looks at taxable supplies. Exempt school tutoring may not count. Taxable adult hours do, together with your other taxable side hustles. This is not tax advice.
