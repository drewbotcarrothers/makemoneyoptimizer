Starting UGC creation in Canada, meaning short videos or photos a business pays for and then uses on its own channels, costs very little because the brand is buying the file and the right to use it, not your audience. What you're budgeting for is clear audio, even light, a steady frame, storage for raw files and, once contracts get bigger, insurance. Amazon.ca prices below were checked on October 9, 2026; storage, marketplace and insurance figures are from each company's own page the same day.

## How much does UGC creation cost to start in Canada?

UGC creation in Canada costs $0 with a phone and items you own, $53.86 with a wired lav mic and a tripod, $196.57 with a wireless mic, a softbox and cloud storage, $607.88 for a two-light, two-mic setup with design software, or $1,172.88 with a starting insurance policy. Goods and subscriptions include 13% Ontario HST; insurance is added without HST. Use your province's rate.

| $0 start | Phone you own, window light, the phone's editor, three sample clips of products you already bought | $0 |
| Lean | BOYA BY-M1 wired lavalier ($21.67) + UBeesize phone tripod with remote ($25.99) | $53.86 with HST |
| Standard | DJI Mic Mini 1 TX + 1 RX ($59.99) + UBeesize tripod ($25.99) + EMART 16" softbox kit ($59.99) + Google One 100 GB for a year ($27.99) | $196.57 with HST |

## Who should use a UGC startup budget in Canada?

A UGC startup budget fits people who can deliver clean, on-brief files and negotiate written usage terms. The lean kit fits a first portfolio. The standard kit fits someone filming a few briefs a month, often in the evening when window light is gone. The pro kit fits someone doing two-person or two-angle briefs, keeping months of raw footage, and designing their own thumbnails and rate cards.

It's a poor fit if you'll accept a job that asks you to claim a result you didn't get, or hand over files with no terms on where they may run.

## What do UGC marketplaces and platforms cost creators in Canada?

Joining is free on the main marketplaces. The cost is a cut of each job. As listed on each company's page on October 9, 2026:

- **Collabstr.** Its [pricing page](https://collabstr.com/pricing) says "Creators pay a separate 15% fee on their payout." Brands pay their own 10% hiring fee on Free and Pro plans (5% on Premium), added on top of the price you set. So a $150 package nets you $127.50, and the brand pays $165.
- **Billo.** Its [creator payment page](https://help.billo.app/en/articles/5830098-for-creators-how-and-when-you-ll-get-paid) says creators in Canada are paid weekly through Stripe, with Billo covering the transfer fee. The payout per task is set by the brand's order.
- **Direct deals.** A brand you pitched yourself pays your invoice, with no marketplace cut. You do your own chasing.

**Correcting an outdated line on this page.** An earlier version of this guide said no platform fee applies to UGC deals in its scope. That's true of a direct invoice, but Collabstr takes 15% of the creator's payout, so price marketplace packages with that cut in mind.

Collabstr's [2026 report](https://collabstr.com/2026-influencer-marketing-report) put the average UGC payout at $154 in 2025, with 80% of collaborations under $300. That's a marketplace average, not what a new creator will earn.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for UGC in Canada?

There's no licence to film product videos. Two rules matter more.

**Honest claims.** The Competition Bureau's guidance on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) applies to advertising claims. Its [influencer marketing page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says reviews and testimonials should reflect actual experience. A brief that asks you to say a product did something it didn't do is one to decline. If you also post the clip on your own account, disclose the relationship clearly. This is not legal advice.

**Insurance.** [Zensurance's videographer page](https://www.zensurance.com/videographer-insurance), read October 9, 2026, says videography business insurance starts at around $565 a year for commercial general liability and professional liability with a $1.5 million combined limit. That's an estimate, not a quote. A home policy may exclude business activity; ask a broker. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance advice.

**Business name.** If you invoice under a name that isn't your own, most provinces require a registration; in Ontario that's $60 online, per the province's [fee page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit).

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Storage.** Google One 100 GB is CA$2.79 a month; 2 TB is CA$13.99. Raws pile up fast.
- **Marketplace cuts,** 15% of each Collabstr payout.
- **Insurance,** from about $565 a year once you carry it.
- **Props and products.** Who pays for the product and who keeps it belongs in writing.
- **Re-shoots.** Unlimited revisions in a quote are an unlimited cost. Write the number of rounds.
- **Software,** such as Canva Pro at $142 a year, if you pay for it.

## Where can UGC startup spending be lower in Canada?

UGC spending stays lower when samples use products you already own, light comes from a window, and the portfolio is a free shared folder or simple page. Buy the wired lav before the wireless kit. Skip paid UGC courses and platforms that charge to be seen until you've pitched brands directly.

## Where shouldn't you cut UGC spending in Canada?

Don't cut clear audio, a written agreement with usage terms, or backups of delivered files. A brand that can't hear the hook won't rebook. A file with no usage terms can run in ads for years with no extra fee.

Keep brand payments apart from household money. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account that can hold a fee until the tax set-aside is moved.

## Which tax rules apply to a UGC budget in Canada?

UGC income in Canada is self-employment income when you're carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same total. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you've read says the platform collects. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Supplies, marketplace fees and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a camera or a computer, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. If you're registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html). Products a brand sends you may have tax consequences depending on the arrangement; ask a tax adviser if you receive products as payment. For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
