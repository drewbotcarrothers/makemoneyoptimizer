## What ladder should a Christmas light installer buy?

Buy a fibreglass extension ladder long enough to reach the eavestrough with 1 m to spare, and a multi-position ladder for porches, peaks and trees. CCOHS recommends a fibreglass ladder where electricity is a hazard and advises considering strength, type, length and CSA approval ([CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html)). Most Amazon.ca listings below state US "Type IA" or "Type 1A" ratings; check the ladder's own labels for a CSA marking if your client or insurer asks for one.

## What Canadian safety rules apply?

- **GFCI outdoors.** Use GFCIs for outdoor connections and never remove the grounding prong ([ESA](https://esasafe.com/safety/home-electrical-safety/holiday-safety/)).
- **Three strings.** Follow the manufacturer's instructions and connect no more than three strings together, even if a listing says "connectable".
- **Power lines.** Check for overhead lines before raising a ladder and stay at least 3 metres away. Use fibreglass where electricity is a hazard ([CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/portable.html)).
- **Ladder set-up.** Use the 4-to-1 angle, extend at least 1 m above the support, and tie off extension ladders at the top. CCOHS tells you to check your jurisdiction's rules when working at heights near or above 3 metres ([CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/ladders/climbing_safely.html)).
- **Ice and wind.** Frost on rungs and shingles is a fall risk; reschedule rather than climb in freezing rain or high wind.
- **Insurance.** Working on other people's roofs calls for liability coverage; see [side hustle insurance](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

## How do taxes treat installation gear?

Clips and other consumables are supplies you generally expense in the year you buy them. CRA's [classes of depreciable property](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html) puts tools under $500 in Class 12 (100%) and tools of $500 or more in Class 8 (20%), so a $563.85 ladder and a $56.99 stabilizer can land in different classes. Strings you leave on a client's house as part of the sale are cost of goods, not equipment. Prices on Amazon.ca are before tax; HST rates differ by province ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)). This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
