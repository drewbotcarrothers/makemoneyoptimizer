## Who does a faceless Instagram page in Canada suit?

A faceless Instagram page in Canada suits people who can post consistently for months, enjoy making graphics or editing short videos, and want to build an audience without being on camera. It's a poor fit if you want income soon, plan to rely on reposting other people's content, or don't have a niche you can create for week after week.

## Can you repost content on a faceless Instagram page in Canada?

Reposting other people's photos, videos, or music without permission is risky, and giving credit isn't the same as permission. CIPO's [guide to copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/guide-copyright) says original literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works are protected the moment they're created and fixed in a material form, and that a copyright owner can prevent others from reproducing the work or a substantial part of it. Ideas and facts aren't protected, so the safer model is to create your own graphics, footage, and voiceovers about ideas in your niche, use properly licensed stock and music, and ask creators in writing before featuring their work.

## What disclosure rules apply to faceless Instagram pages in Canada?

Faceless pages that earn money must follow both Meta's rules and Canadian advertising law. Instagram's [branded content guide](https://help.instagram.com/616901995832907/) says Meta's Branded Content Policies require the paid partnership label when a brand pays you to post, when you post about a gifted or loaned product, and when you post with affiliate links that earn commissions. Posts promoting your own products don't use the label, but Instagram notes you're still responsible for any legal disclosure obligations.

The Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says the Competition Act applies to anyone promoting a product, and lists payment, commissions, free products, and discounts as possible material connections. It recommends prominent, plain-language disclosures that are visible without expanding a caption, on every platform where you post, and in both audio and visual form where appropriate, and says linking to an affiliate page alone is unlikely to be enough. Being faceless doesn't change any of this.

## How do you start a faceless Instagram page in Canada?

Starting a faceless Instagram page in Canada means choosing a narrow niche, building a repeatable content format, and planning how the page will earn before you're big.

### Step 1: Pick a niche you can sustain

Specific beats broad: "budget meals for Canadian students" over "food." A Canadian angle helps you stand out.

### Step 2: Choose two or three repeatable formats

For example: a weekly tip carousel, a voiceover Reel with b-roll, and a quote graphic.

### Step 3: Create original assets

Your own footage, graphics, and voiceovers, plus properly licensed stock and music.

### Step 4: Post on a schedule you can keep

Consistency matters more than volume. Batch-create content on one day.

### Step 5: Plan monetisation early

Affiliate links to products you've researched, a simple digital product, or brand partnerships. See [Pinterest affiliate marketing](/side-hustles/pinterest-affiliate-side-hustle-canada/) for a similar evergreen approach.

### Step 6: Label and disclose

Use the paid partnership label where Meta requires it and a plain-language disclosure in each sponsored or affiliate post.

### Step 7: Review insights monthly

Keep what earns saves, shares, and clicks; drop formats that don't.

## What mistakes are common with faceless Instagram pages in Canada?

Common faceless Instagram mistakes in Canada are reposting without permission, buying followers, and skipping disclosure.

- **Reposting others' content.** Credit isn't permission under copyright law.
- **Buying followers or engagement.** It distorts your insights and can break platform rules.
- **No disclosure.** Meta and the Competition Bureau both expect clear labels.
- **Too broad a niche.** Generic pages compete with millions of accounts.
- **No plan to earn.** Followers alone don't pay; decide on a product or partnership path early.

## Which tax rules apply to faceless Instagram income in Canada?

Income from a faceless Instagram page in Canada (affiliate commissions, brand payments, and product sales) is generally business income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html).
