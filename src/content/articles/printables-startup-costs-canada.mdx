## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for selling printables in Canada?

Ongoing printable costs in Canada are the shop fee on the live page, refunds when a PDF will not print, and any subscription you kept. Printables break-even in the sketch is a method, not a shop income. The fee is not in the arithmetic below, because copying a percentage from memory is how a payout looks like profit.

**Shop fee.** Read it the day you price the file. A fee that changed is still the fee. Your sketch should use the page, not this guide.

**Refunds.** A file that clips, or a calendar that pretends every province has the same school dates, comes back. Provincial calendars are not identical. Point the buyer at their province's published calendar if the page mentions school dates.

**Copyright complaints.** A character you traced is not a supply cost. It is a file you have to take down. The CIPO copyright page is the background reading.

**Ads.** Optional, and a poor first dollar. One page that prints is the business.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical shop result.**

Suppose the computer and the layout tool are already yours. Suppose paper and ink for two test prints cost $12. Suppose you price the file at an assumed $8. That $8 is an assumption for the arithmetic, not a market rate. The shop fee is whatever the vendor's live page says, so this sketch does not invent a percentage. Suppose three people buy the file and none refund.

Gross before the fee = 3 × $8 = $24. Cash out for paper = $12. The amount after the fee is $24 minus that fee, minus any tax set-aside. This sketch cannot finish that subtraction without the live page. The $12 paper is covered only if the amount after the fee is at least $12.

The hours you spent drawing the page are not in the $24. A second template is not in it. Do not copy $8. If you are registered for GST/HST, tax may sit on the supply. That tax is not extra profit. Do not describe the file as tax-free because a printed book can qualify for a rebate.

## Where should printables spending not be cut in Canada?

Printables spending in Canada should not skip the test print, the copyright check, or the fee page. A PDF that looks fine on a phone and clips on letter paper is a refund. A character, a photo, or a layout you copied is not "inspiration" if the original is still recognizable.

Do not label a file as a Canadian school planner and then copy a calendar from another country. Do not invent buyer counts or reviews. Photograph your own test print, or describe the file without a picture you do not own.

GI-065, dated August 2014, lists agendas and electronic and digital books among goods that do not qualify for the point-of-sale book rebate it describes. The rate table in that old sheet is not the current HST table. A PDF planner is not a reason to tell buyers the file is tax-free.

## Which tax rules apply to a printables budget in Canada?

Printable sales in Canada are self-employment income, including a shop payout, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST for these digital supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table. The CRA's [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) is the further reading when buyers and platforms sit outside a simple local invoice.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the shop reports and the bank deposits. Report the income on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add ebook, template, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Selling files is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. This guide does not explain input tax credits. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate that applies to the supply. Digital products and buyers outside your province make the place-of-supply question harder than a local driveway. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Quebec charges GST and QST. Set the tax aside. Do not apply a printed-book rebate story to a download. [GI-065](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-065/point-sale-rebate-on-books.html) lists electronic and digital books, and agendas, among non-qualifying goods, and its August 2014 rate table is not today's HST table.

Search your city for a business licence if you present the shop as a local business. This guide does not state a licence fee.
