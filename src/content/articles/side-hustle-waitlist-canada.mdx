A side hustle waitlist in Canada is a simple list of people who want your service when a spot opens. Build one when you're consistently full: collect a name, contact details, the service they want and their timing, ask for clear permission to contact them, and fill openings in a fair, stated order. If you'll send them emails or texts that promote your business, Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) applies. ISED says express consent is someone agreeing, verbally or in writing, to receive messages from you, with no time limit unless they withdraw it, and every unsubscribe request must be actioned within 10 business days at no cost ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)).

A waitlist is also the clearest sign that it may be time to [raise your rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/) or [hire help](/side-hustles/hiring-help-side-hustle-canada/). For a broader email list, see [email marketing for side hustles](/side-hustles/email-marketing-side-hustle-canada/) and [the newsletter side hustle guide](/side-hustles/newsletter-side-hustle-canada/).

| Field | Why you need it | Keep it optional? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | To address them | No |
| Email or phone | To offer the spot | No |
| Service wanted | To match openings | No |
| Area or time zone | To check route or schedule fit | No |

## When should you start a side hustle waitlist in Canada?

Start one the second or third time in a month that you turn down work you'd have liked. Typical moments are the first snowfall for [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/), spring for [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada/), and the weeks before holidays for [pet sitting](/side-hustles/winter-pet-sitting-house-sitting-canada/). A waitlist also helps freelancers whose calendar is full for the next month.

## How do you set up a waitlist for a Canadian side hustle?

Keep it simple enough to run from a phone.

### Step 1: Pick one place to collect names

A free form tool, a spreadsheet, or the signup form in an email service. One list beats scattered texts and DMs.

### Step 2: Ask for consent clearly

Use an unticked checkbox with plain words, such as: "Yes, email me when a spot opens and send occasional updates from Northside Dog Walking. You can unsubscribe any time." ISED's guidance says express consent means the person said yes to being contacted, and that you should clearly identify yourself and your business ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)).

### Step 3: Say how the list works

State the order (first come, first served, or by route), roughly how often spots open if you know, and that joining doesn't guarantee service.

### Step 4: Contact people in order and give a deadline

"A Tuesday morning spot opened. Reply by Friday if you'd like it." If they pass, move to the next name.

### Step 5: Clean the list

Remove people who found someone else, who asked to be removed, or whose consent has run out.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers. A house cleaner books eight regular clients and has no room for more. Instead of saying no, they reply to each new enquiry with a waitlist link. The form asks for name, email, neighbourhood and preferred day, plus an unticked consent box. Over the next two months, a regular client moves away. The cleaner emails the first person on the list in the same neighbourhood, gives them three days to accept, and fills the spot without advertising. They also note that the list keeps growing, which feeds their next [price review](/side-hustles/first-year-side-hustle-price-review-canada/).

## What does CASL require for a side hustle waitlist in Canada?

CASL covers commercial electronic messages, such as emails and texts that promote your services ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en)). The practical rules for a small waitlist:

- **Consent:** express consent has no time limit unless withdrawn. Implied consent from an existing business relationship can last up to two years, but only six months after an inquiry or application ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)).
- **Identification:** say who you are and how to reach you in every message.
- **Unsubscribe:** include a clear, easy unsubscribe and act on requests within 10 business days.
- **Records:** keep a note of when and how each person gave consent.

## What privacy rules apply to a waitlist in Canada?

PIPEDA sets the ground rules for how private-sector organizations collect, use and disclose personal information in commercial activity. Alberta, B.C. and Quebec have their own substantially similar private-sector privacy laws ([Office of the Privacy Commissioner](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)). For a side hustle, that means collect only what you need, say what you'll use it for, keep it secure, and don't share or sell the list.

## What mistakes do side hustlers make with waitlists?

- **A pre-ticked consent box.** Consent should be an active yes.
- **Adding enquiry emails to a newsletter.** Implied consent from an inquiry is short and doesn't cover every kind of message.
- **No order or deadline.** People feel skipped and spots sit empty.
- **Collecting addresses or details you don't need yet.**
- **Never raising prices.** A long, growing list is a pricing signal.

## Do waitlists affect side hustle taxes in Canada?

Not directly: a waitlist has no tax treatment of its own. Deposits are different. If you take a deposit to hold a spot, record it in your [business records](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) and treat it according to your agreement. Form and email tools you pay for are generally business expenses; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).
