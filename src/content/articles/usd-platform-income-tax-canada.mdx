Canadians who get paid by Prolific, Outlier, UserTesting, Respondent, or a US freelance site are usually paid in US dollars or British pounds, into PayPal. The Canadian return still wants Canadian dollars. This guide is the bookkeeping path: which rate to use, which form the income usually lands on, and what the US W-8BEN is doing on the same laptop. It is general information from CRA and IRS pages, checked on October 6, 2026. It is not a ruling on your account.

The platforms themselves are [Prolific](/side-hustles/prolific-review-canada/), [Outlier](/side-hustles/outlier-ai-review-canada/), [DataAnnotation](/side-hustles/dataannotation-review-canada/), and [user testing sites](/side-hustles/user-testing-sites-canada/). The broader return is [how to report side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/).

## How do you convert USD platform income to Canadian dollars?

Report foreign income in Canadian dollars. The CRA's Income Tax Folio S5-F4-C1 says that where one of the currencies is Canadian, the relevant spot rate for a day after February 28, 2017 is the rate quoted by the Bank of Canada for that day. If the Bank of Canada quotes the rate but not on that exact day, you use the closest preceding day that has a rate ([folio S5-F4-C1](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-5-international-residency/series-5-international-residency-folio-4-foreign-currency/income-tax-folio-s5-f4-c1-income-tax-reporting-currency.html)).

The 2025 income tax package says the same thing in plainer language: in general, convert using the Bank of Canada rate in effect on the day the amount arises. In some cases the CRA accepts another rate if it is widely available, verifiable, published by an independent provider on an ongoing basis, recognized by the market, used in line with well-accepted business principles, used in your financial statements if you have them, and used consistently year to year. Bloomberg, Thomson Reuters, and OANDA are named as sources the CRA generally accepts. An average of rates over the period may be used in some cases. The folio adds that if rates fluctuate significantly, an average for the period will not generally be accepted ([5000-G, 2025 package](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pub/5000-g/5000-g-25e.pdf)).

For a handful of PayPal deposits, converting each deposit on its date is the clean version. For many small study rewards, an annual average can be the practical version, if you use it for the whole year and the folio's warning about large swings does not apply. Pick one method and keep it. Do not convert January at the daily rate and December at whatever PayPal felt like, unless you are deliberately using the daily rate every time.

The Bank of Canada daily average for the US dollar was 1.4254 CAD on October 5, 2026 ([daily rates](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/)). That rate is an example for the sketches below. Your payout uses the rate on its own date.

PayPal may show a different CAD amount because it prices a conversion spread. You can keep USD in a PayPal balance and convert later. For the tax return, the folio is about when the income arises, not when you happen to click "convert" in the app. If you are unsure which date applies to a held balance, write down both the payout date and the conversion date and ask a tax preparer before you file. This article does not choose that date for you.

## Which form does AI-training and study income go on?

When you are doing the work as a self-employed contractor, the income and the expenses go on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), filed with your T1. Outlier's own pages call the work an independent-contractor engagement. DataAnnotation calls the people doing it contractors. UserTesting says it does not withhold taxes. None of that wording files the form. You do.

Report the gross in CAD and deduct the costs that belong to the work: a headset used for tests, PayPal fees that were actually charged, and the platform fee Respondent deducts before it pays you. The set-aside for income tax and CPP is [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/). Outside Quebec, self-employment CPP is calculated on Schedule 8 and the amount payable is [line 42100](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/line-42100-cpp-contributions-payable-on-self-employment-other-earnings.html). Quebec uses the Quebec Pension Plan on the provincial return. Do not copy a percentage from a blog when the schedule exists.

A platform might also be a [reporting platform operator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/reporting-rules-digital-platforms.html) that sends seller information to the CRA. Many US research tools are not in that Canadian regime. Either way, you report the income. A missing slip is not a zero.

### Illustrative example

Assumed mix of published rules and made-up hours. Suppose, in one week, you complete two Prolific studies that each pay the US$8 hourly floor for 30 estimated minutes: 2 × US$8 × 0.5 = US$8. You also redeem a Respondent incentive advertised at US$40. Respondent's published fee is 5% with a US$1 minimum, so 5% × 40 = US$2, and you receive US$38. The week's USD receipts are US$8 + US$38 = US$46. At 1.4254, US$46 is about $65.57 CAD. That CAD figure is what a daily-rate method would start from if October 5, 2026 were the day each amount arose. It is not a typical week, and the hours are assumptions. Prolific's floor and Respondent's fee are the only published inputs.

## What is Form W-8BEN doing on a US platform?

[Form W-8BEN](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-8-ben) is the IRS certificate of foreign status for an individual who is the beneficial owner of a payment that can be subject to US withholding. The IRS says you give it to the withholding agent or payer, whether or not you are claiming a reduced treaty rate. Outlier's role pages use US "1099" language even on a role that lists Canada. Fiverr and Upwork ask Canadians for their own tax forms. The pattern is the same: the US payer wants to know you are not a US person.

Completing W-8BEN does not remove the income from your Canadian return. If US tax is actually withheld, keep the statement. A foreign tax credit on [Form T2209](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2209.html) may reduce Canadian tax on the same income. This guide does not tell you which treaty article applies to a given platform. That is a question for the form instructions and, if the amount is large, for a tax preparer. The [Upwork review](/side-hustles/upwork-review-canada/) shows how one marketplace walks Canadians through the same form.

## Does a PayPal balance mean you file Form T1135?

Not by itself. [Form T1135](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/foreign-reporting/foreign-income-verification-statement.html) is an information return for specified foreign property that cost more than $100,000 at any time in the year. The CRA's questions-and-answers page says the test is cost amount, not market value, and that you still report the income from foreign property even when the cost is under $100,000. A year of study payouts that you spend as they arrive is a different fact pattern from holding foreign investments over that threshold. This guide does not decide whether a particular PayPal wallet is specified foreign property. If the USD sitting in payment apps plus other foreign property is anywhere near $100,000, read the T1135 page before you assume you are under it.

## What about GST/HST on USD platform income?

The small-supplier threshold is $30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters, or in a single quarter. It is not an income-tax bracket and it is not a hobby exemption. The CRA page is [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). The short correction is [the $30,000 GST/HST myth](/side-hustles/gst-hst-30000-threshold-myth-canada/). Add this USD work to your other side hustles before you decide you are under the line. Services to a non-resident can have their own place-of-supply and zero-rating rules. Do not charge or refrain from charging GST/HST based on this paragraph alone.

## How do you keep the records?

### Step 1: Download the payout list monthly

PayPal, plus the platform's own history. Note the date, the currency, the gross, and any fee.

### Step 2: Convert with one method

Daily Bank of Canada rate on each row, or one average for the year if you are using the average method the folio allows. Write the rate in the sheet. A tool such as <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can store the CAD amount next to the receipt. <AffiliateLink program="wealthsimple-tax" label="Wealthsimple Tax" /> is one way to prepare the T2125 at filing time. The software does not know your rate method unless you enter the CAD figures.

### Step 3: Separate the spending

A PayPal balance used for groceries is harder to untangle in April. The account-separation guide is [separating business and personal money](/side-hustles/separate-business-personal-money-canada/).

### Step 4: File the income even if no slip arrives

T2125 does not require the platform to have mailed you a Canadian form. Keep the sheet for six years with your other records, as [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) describes.

## What do people get wrong about USD side income?

- **Reporting the PayPal CAD conversion as the tax figure without checking the date.** The folio points at the Bank of Canada rate for the day the amount arises.
- **Waiting for a T4A.** Many of these payers will never send one. The income is still income.
- **Treating W-8BEN as a Canadian filing.** It goes to the US payer.
- **Treating $100,000 as a tax-free cap.** T1135 is a foreign-property report. The income is taxable underneath it.
- **Treating $30,000 as an income-tax threshold.** That figure is the GST/HST small-supplier test.
- **Forgetting pounds.** Prolific pays GBP and USD. Convert each currency. Do not run pounds through the USD rate.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Rates and forms change. Re-read the folio in the year you file.
