To raise your side hustle rates in Canada, set the new price, use it for every new client right away, and give existing clients written notice of the new rate and the date it starts. Keep any price you already promised in a signed quote or contract. Ontario's consumer rules, for example, say the final price can't be more than 10% above a written estimate in a contract unless the client agrees to a new price and signs a change ([Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-when-signing-or-cancelling-contract)). For the size of the raise, start from your own cost growth: Canadian consumer prices rose 3.0% in the year to August 2026, and fuel far more.

This guide is about the number, the conversation and the paperwork. For the market rate in your hustle, start with its pricing guide, such as [house cleaning pricing](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-pricing-canada/), [dog walking pricing](/side-hustles/dog-walking-pricing-canada/) or [freelance writing pricing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-pricing-canada/). | Situation | What to do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New enquiry | Quote the new rate | Nothing to change, nothing to explain |
| Regular client, no contract | Written notice with a start date | Gives them time to decide |
| Signed quote or contract | Keep the agreed price until the job or term ends | The contract sets the price |
| Ontario consumer with written estimate | Stay within 10% unless they sign a change | [Ontario contract rules](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-when-signing-or-cancelling-contract) |
| Newly registered for GST/HST | Add tax to your price and say so | Sales tax can be added to an advertised price |

## When should you raise your side hustle rates in Canada?

Raise rates when the numbers or your calendar tell you to. Common signals:

- **You're fully booked** and turning work away, or your waitlist is growing. See [building a side hustle waitlist](/side-hustles/side-hustle-waitlist-canada/).
- **Your costs went up.** Supplies, fuel, insurance and platform fees all change. The CRA's [T2125 guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125.html) lists the expense lines; if yours grew, your old price earns less.
- **Your effective hourly fell.** Travel, admin and unpaid quoting time eat into a rate that looked fine on paper. [Tracking which side hustles pay](/side-hustles/track-which-side-hustles-pay-canada/) shows how to measure it.
- **You've added skill or proof.** Reviews, a portfolio, a certification or faster work justify a higher price.
- **A year has passed.** An annual review is a natural, expected moment to adjust.

## How much should you raise your rates?

Cover your cost growth first, then add a step for demand or skill. These are the published cost signals for 2026.

| Cost signal | Change | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All-items CPI, Aug 2025 to Aug 2026 | +3.0% (164.8 to 169.8) | [Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260914/dq260914a-eng.htm), released Sept 14, 2026 |
| Services | +2.8% | Statistics Canada |
| Transportation | +7.5% | Statistics Canada |
| Gasoline | +22.8% | Statistics Canada |
| CPI excluding food and energy | +2.1% | Statistics Canada |
| CRA tax-exempt car allowance, 2026 | 73¢/km for the first 5,000 km, up one cent; 67¢ after | [Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/01/government-announces-the-2026-automobile-deduction-limits-and-expense-benefit-rates-for-businesses.html), Jan 14, 2026 |

A desk-based freelancer whose costs track general inflation can use the 3.0% headline as a floor. A driving-heavy hustle (snow removal, mobile detailing, dog walking routes) has seen fuel rise far faster, so a 3% raise won't keep up if fuel is a big share of your costs.

### The rate formula

```text
Cost-floor rate   = current rate × (1 + your cost growth %)
New rate          = cost-floor rate + demand/skill step, rounded to a clean number
Break-even churn  = 1 − (current rate ÷ new rate)
   (the share of clients you could lose and still earn the same revenue)
```

Break-even churn is the number that makes a raise feel less risky. Going from $40 to $45 means you could lose 1 − 40/45 = 11.1% of clients and still bring in the same revenue, while working fewer hours.

## How much notice should you give?

There's no single legal notice period for a casual, no-contract arrangement. Match the notice to how much the client relies on you.

| Client type | Suggested notice | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New enquiry, one-off job | None; quote the new rate | Quote |
| Occasional repeat client | Mention it with the next booking | Email or text |
| Weekly or biweekly regular | 30 days or more | Email or text, plus a reminder a week before |
| Monthly retainer | 1 to 2 months, timed to a billing cycle | Email, then confirm on a call |
| Signed season agreement or contract | Next season or next term | New agreement |
| Prepaid package | Honour the remaining visits at the old price | Note on the receipt |

## How do you raise rates with existing clients in Canada?

Tell them early, keep it short, and put it in writing.

### Step 1: Pick the new rate and the date

Choose one number per service and one start date, using the formula above. A month's notice for regular clients is a courteous default; Jobber's guide suggests 1 to 3 months.

### Step 2: Check what you've already promised

Look for signed quotes, contracts, prepaid packages and season agreements. A snow contract signed in October is a season price; change it next season. For contract wording, see [the freelance contract guide](/side-hustles/freelance-contract-canada/) and the [snow removal contract checklist](/side-hustles/snow-removal-contract-checklist-canada/).

### Step 3: Send a dated written notice

Use email or text so you both have a record. The templates below cover the usual cases.

### Step 4: Decide whether to grandfather anyone

Grandfathering means keeping some long-time clients at the old rate for a set time. It can work for your first loyal clients, but put an end date on it so you don't end up with a calendar full of below-market work.

### Step 5: Update everything public

Change your price list, listings, booking page and [invoice template](/side-hustles/how-to-invoice-clients-canada/) on the same day. Mismatched prices across listings cause awkward conversations.

## What do worked rate increases look like?

**Illustrative.** The rates, client counts and hours below are assumptions. The CPI figure, Ontario's 10% rule and tax rates are the sourced ones. Tax lines apply only once you're registered for GST/HST.

### Example 1: lawn care, $40 to $45 per weekly cut

- 12 weekly clients at $40 = $480 a week. New rate $45: break-even churn = 1 − 40/45 = 11.1%.
- If one client leaves (8.3% of 12), 11 × $45 = $495 a week, $15 more than before, for one fewer cut.
- If all 12 stay over a 24-cut season: 12 × 24 × $5 = $1,440 more revenue.
- Timing: text returning clients in February that the 2027 rate is $45 from the first spring cut. Anyone who signs a season agreement at $45 keeps that price until it ends.

### Example 2: dog walker, inflation-only raise

- Current 30-minute walk: $23. Cost floor at 3.0% CPI: $23 × 1.03 = $23.69, rounded to **$24**.
- A five-walk weekly client at $110 (5 × $23 less 5%) moves to 5 × $24 = $120 less 5% = **$114**.
- With 13% HST in Ontario: $114 × 1.13 = $128.82.
- Because this walker drives between dogs and gasoline rose 22.8%, a plain 3% may not cover fuel; a $25 walk (+8.7%) would also be defensible with the cost table above.

### Example 3: freelance writer or VA on a monthly retainer

- Retainer: 20 hours a month at $45 = $900. New rate for new clients from today: $55.
- Existing retainer client: two months' notice, timed to the billing cycle. New retainer: 20 × $55 = **$1,100**.
- Grandfather option: $50 for three months (20 × $50 = $1,000), then $55.
- With 13% HST once registered: $1,100 × 1.13 = $1,243.

### Example 4: Ontario handyman job where the scope grows

- Written estimate in the contract: $800 before HST. Ontario's rule caps the final price at $800 × 1.10 = **$880** unless the client agrees to a new price and signs a change.
- The client adds a second shelf unit worth $250. Without a signed change, you can't bill $1,050.
- With a signed change order to $1,050: $1,050 × 1.13 HST = $1,186.50 (if registered).

## What should your rate increase messages say?

State the new rate, the start date and what stays the same, in a few sentences.

### Weekly or biweekly client

```text
Hi [Name], thanks for having me [clean every other Friday] this year.
Starting [date, 30+ days out], my rate for your [home/dog/lawn] will be
$[new] per visit (currently $[old]). Everything else stays the same:
same day, same checklist. If you have any questions, just reply here.
```

### Monthly retainer client

```text
Subject: [Your business] rates from [month]

Hi [Name],
My hourly rate for new clients is now $[55]. For your retainer, the new
rate starts with the [month] invoice, two billing cycles from now:
[20] hours at $[55] = $[1,100] a month (currently $[900]).
Scope, turnaround and reporting stay the same. Happy to talk it through
on our next call.
[Your name]
```

### Grandfathered client

```text
Hi [Name], you've been with me since [year], so I'm keeping you at
$[old] until [date, 3 months out]. From [date], your rate will be $[new],
the same as my current price list. Thanks for being one of my first clients.
```

### Reply to pushback

```text
I understand. The new rate reflects [higher fuel and supply costs /
my full schedule]. If it doesn't work for you, I can [move you to every
other week / offer the basic package at $[X]] instead, or I'm happy to
recommend someone else.
```

### Price list line

```text
Rates effective [date]. Prices before GST/HST. Regular clients receive
at least 30 days' written notice of any change. Signed quotes are
honoured for the job quoted.
```

### Invoice note for the first bill at the new rate

```text
INVOICE #2027-014   [Client]   Week of [date]
5 × 30-minute walk at $24.00, less 5% weekly rate ..... $114.00
HST 13% (registered only) ............................. $14.82
Total .................................................. $128.82
Note: new rate effective [date], as advised by text on [date].
```

## How do sales tax and advertising rules affect a rate increase in Canada?

Two federal rules matter. First, the Competition Bureau warns that advertising a price that can't be attained because of mandatory added fees is against the law; the only charges that can be added on top are government ones such as sales tax ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/drip-pricing)). So raise the price itself instead of adding a "service fee" or "fuel surcharge" that every client must pay. Optional extras a client chooses, like a weekend slot, are different from a fee nobody can avoid.

Second, if a rate increase pushes your total taxable sales over $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, you must register for GST/HST and start charging it ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). The threshold counts all your businesses together, not just this one. Tell clients when tax starts appearing on invoices; see [GST/HST registration for side hustles](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).

## What mistakes do side hustlers make when raising rates?

- **Apologizing for pages.** One or two sentences of context are plenty.
- **Surprise increases on the invoice.** Clients should hear about the new rate before they're billed.
- **Breaking a signed price.** Wait until the term ends or get a signed change.
- **Adding a mandatory surcharge instead of a higher price.** That risks a drip pricing problem.
- **Raising less than your costs rose.** A 3% raise on a route where fuel is a large cost still leaves you earning less per hour.
- **Raising once, then never again.** A yearly review keeps changes small and expected.
- **Forgetting the tax set-aside.** More revenue means more tax; check [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

## How do higher side hustle rates affect taxes in Canada?

Higher rates raise your business income, which you report on Form T2125 with your personal return. Net profit is taxed at your marginal rate, and self-employed CPP contributions also rise with it. If your income grows enough, the CRA may ask you to pay [tax instalments](/side-hustles/cra-tax-instalments-side-hustle-canada/). Keep invoices showing the old and new rates in your [business records](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).
