A professional organizer side hustle in Canada sells hours of decluttering, closet and pantry set-up, and move packing to households, and January is when people book it after the holidays. Canadian organizers that publish rates charge $65 to $125 an hour for one person and $110 to $200 an hour for a team of two, usually with a four-hour minimum. There is no official certification in Canada, according to Professional Organizers in Canada (POC). Rates below were read on each company's own page on October 19, 2026.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Solo rates:** $65 an hour for a junior organizer (Harmony, Vancouver and Victoria), $90 for one organizer (Cut The Clutter), $125 for an estate or downsizing specialist (Cut The Clutter).
- **Team rates:** $110 an hour for two (Neat Freakss, Calgary), $118 plus 5% GST for two (Simply Organized, South Surrey), $160 for two (Harmony), $200 for two specialists (Cut The Clutter).
- **Minimums:** four hours is common (Harmony, Cut The Clutter).
- **No official credential:** POC says no Canadian non-profit organizing association offers certification, and its training program does not let members call themselves "certified".
- **Association cost:** POC membership is $270 a year plus a one-time $50 administrative fee.

</QuickAnswer>

## What do Canadian professional organizers charge?

Canadian professional organizers charge by the hour per organizer, with packages for larger projects and add-ons for travel, consultations and hauling. These are the published rates on October 19, 2026:

| Company (area) | Rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Harmony Home Organizing](https://harmonyhomeorganizing.ca/services-rates/rates-and-gift-cards/) (Vancouver, Victoria) | One junior organizer $65/hour; team of 2 $160/hour | 4-hour minimum; in-home consultation $50; handyman $80/hour, 2-hour minimum |
| [Neat Freakss](https://www.neatfreakss.ca/servicesprices) (Calgary) | $110/hour, which includes 2 organizers | Bronze package 6 hours from $625; Silver 18 hours from $1,875; senior and student pricing on request |
| [Cut The Clutter](https://cuttheclutter.ca/rates/) | One organizer $90/hour; experienced specialist $125/hour; 2-person team $200/hour; 2 packers $170/hour | Minimum booking 4 hours; hoarding recovery packages from $5,000 |
| [Simply Organized For You](https://simplyorganizedforyou.ca/rates-service-packages) (South Surrey) | $118/hour + 5% GST for 2 organizers | Travel fees by zone from $40; home staging flat rate $750 + GST for up to 5 hours |

Notice that team rates are not double the solo rate. Neat Freakss' $110 for two is $55 per organizer-hour. A solo side hustler at $65 to $90 an hour sits in the middle of this table.

## Do you need a certification to be a professional organizer in Canada?

No certification is required to work as a professional organizer in Canada. POC's [membership questions page](https://organizersincanada.com/join/how-to-join-poc/membership-questions/) says there are no official credentials, that no Canadian non-profit organizing association offers a certification program, and that its Trained Professional Organizer program is training, not accreditation. POC says that training does not entitle anyone to use words such as certified, accredited or qualified in marketing, or the initials TPO.

What POC does offer:

- **Membership:** $270 a year plus a one-time $50 administrative fee.
- **Directory listing:** new members appear in the "Find an Organizer" directory after three classes: Introduction to Professional Organizing, Ethical Standards for POC Members, and Safety Practices for Professional Organizers.
- **Insurance:** a business insurance program available to members only.
- **A pricing course:** POC offers a Fees and Billing course, and lists expertise, experience, location, travel distance and local competition as the factors in setting a rate.

POC also points to the Board of Certified Professional Organizers for the CPO certification, and to the Institute for Challenging Disorganization for work with chronically disorganized clients.

## Which organizing services sell in January?

The organizing services that sell in January are the ones tied to holiday gifts, new-year resolutions and tax-season paperwork. These 15 services fit a part-time organizer, each with the reason it sells:

1. **Post-holiday toy and kids' room reset.** New gifts, no room for them.
2. **Closet edit with donation drop-off.** Winter clothes are out; summer clothes can be stored.

5. **Home office set-up for tax season.** Receipts and slips arrive in February.

7. **Garage shelving plan.** Measured in January, installed when it warms up.
8. **Downsizing for seniors.** Slow, careful work; specialists charge more (Cut The Clutter's $125).
9. **Move prep for spring listings.** Agents list in spring; decluttering starts now.
10. **Rental and condo storage locker sort.** Small space, fixed time.
11. **Craft and hobby room set-up.** Supplies bought over the holidays.
12. **Laundry room and linen closet.** A good four-hour minimum job.
13. **Kitchen cabinet reset.** Duplicate gadgets go to donation.
14. **Basement playroom to guest room.** Holiday visitors showed the need.
15. **Virtual organizing sessions.** Video calls for clients outside your area.

## Illustrative example: one January of weekend organizing

This is an illustrative example. The hourly rates come from the published pages above; the number of bookings, your supply costs and travel are assumptions.

- Assumed rate: $75 an hour solo, between Harmony's $65 junior rate and Cut The Clutter's $90.
- Assumed bookings: one 4-hour minimum job each Saturday and Sunday for 4 weekends = 8 jobs = 32 hours.
- Revenue: 32 × $75 = $2,400.
- Assumed costs: $15 a job in labels, bags and bins you bring = $120; $10 a job in fuel and parking = $80.
- Left before tax: $2,400 − $120 − $80 = $2,200, or $2,200 ÷ 32 = $68.75 an hour.
- POC membership, if you join in January: $270 + $50 = $320, so $2,200 − $320 = $1,880 for the month.

| Jobs in January (4 hours each) | Revenue at $75 | Left after $25 a job in supplies and travel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | $1,200 | $1,100 |
| 8 | $2,400 | $2,200 |
| 12 | $3,600 | $3,300 |

Supplies the client keeps (bins, drawer dividers, shelving) should be bought with the client's approval and invoiced at cost, or bought by the client. POC lists "the purchase of supplies" as one of the policies to put in your service agreement.

## What does a first-client plan look like?

A first-client plan starts in November with a portfolio of your own home and one or two friends', then books January dates before the holidays end.

| Weeks | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Organize your own pantry and one closet; take before and after photos with no personal items visible |
| 2 | Do two free or discounted sessions for friends in exchange for photos and a review |
| 3 | Write a service agreement: rate, 4-hour minimum, cancellation, supplies, donation runs |
| 4 | Post a "January reset" offer locally; offer a gift card for holiday shoppers |
| 5 to 6 | Book January weekends; confirm each job in writing |
| 7 to 10 | Deliver the jobs; ask each client for a review and one referral |

### A post you can copy

```
January reset: closets, pantries and toy rooms

I'm a local home organizer in [area]. I help you sort, donate and set up
a system you can keep. $[rate]/hour, 4-hour minimum, and I bring bags,
labels and a donation drop-off plan.

Booking January weekends now. Gift cards available for the holidays.
Message me with the room and a photo for a time estimate.
```

### A job-day checklist

```
[ ] Confirm the room, the goal and the time window the day before
[ ] Bring bags, labels, a marker, measuring tape and gloves
[ ] Sort: keep / donate / recycle / garbage / elsewhere in the house
[ ] Client decides on every item; you never discard without a yes
[ ] Measure before suggesting any bins or shelving
[ ] Donation run or junk pickup booked before you leave
[ ] Photos (with permission), invoice, and a review request
```

## Which tax rules apply to a professional organizer side hustle in Canada?

Organizing income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), with supplies, travel, POC dues and insurance as expenses. GST/HST registration is required once taxable sales pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters, under the CRA's [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) rules. Simply Organized's page quotes its rate "+ 5% GST", which is how a registered B.C. business shows it. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
