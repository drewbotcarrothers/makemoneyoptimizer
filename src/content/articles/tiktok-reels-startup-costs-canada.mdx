## How much does a TikTok and Reels side hustle cost to start in Canada?

A TikTok and Reels side hustle in Canada starts at $0 with a phone you own, about $35–$120 with a tripod and microphone, and about $120–$400 with light, a backdrop, and a paid app. The apps themselves are free to post on, so gear is the only startup line most people need.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for TikTok and Reels in Canada?

TikTok and Reels break-even in Canada is the fee a business pays, or the product revenue a clip drives, minus gear, travel, and any app, then divided by your hours. Views alone do not pay a bill in this guide.

### Step 1: Film four test clips with what you own

Watch them on another phone. Write down the one thing that looks or sounds worst.

### Step 2: Buy the fix for that one problem

A tripod if the frame shakes. A microphone if the voice is lost. Light if the face is dark.

### Step 3: Write the disclosure before the first paid clip

The Competition Bureau's page on [influencer marketing](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says to disclose material connections, such as payment, free products, or discounts, and that a tag or discount code alone is unlikely to be enough.

### Step 4: Add travel to client jobs

A clip filmed at a café across town has transit or fuel.

### Step 5: Spread gear over several jobs

A $40 microphone on one job is a large cost. On ten jobs it is small.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical rate.**

Suppose a local bakery agrees in writing to pay $120 for three short clips filmed on site, an assumption, not a market rate. You already own the phone. You buy a tripod for $20 and a clip-on microphone for $25, $45 total, and charge both against this first job. Transit is $7. Remainder: $120 − $45 − $7 = $68 before a tax set-aside. Suppose the job takes 5 hours including filming, editing, and a revision: $68 ÷ 5 = $13.60 an hour inside these assumptions. On a second job with the same gear, the remainder is $113 before transit. These totals are assumptions. Do not copy $120.

## Which tax rules apply to a TikTok and Reels budget in Canada?

Short-form video income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep written agreements, e-transfer records, and gear receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Short-form video is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
