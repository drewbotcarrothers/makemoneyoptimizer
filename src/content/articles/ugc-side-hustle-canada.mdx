UGC (user-generated content) as a side hustle means filming short, natural-looking product videos and photos that brands pay for and then post on their own accounts, websites and ads. You don't need followers, because the brand is buying the content and the right to use it, not your audience. Most UGC deals are small: on Collabstr's marketplace the average UGC payout was $154 in 2025, and 80% of all creator collaborations cost brands under $300.

## Who does UGC suit?

It suits people who are comfortable on camera, can talk about a product naturally in 15 to 60 seconds, and can follow a written brief. You need decent light, steady hands (or a tripod) and the patience to reshoot a hook ten times.

Good fits:

- **Parents**, who are a target customer for kids', home and food brands.
- **Fitness, beauty and skincare users**, who already know how to demonstrate products on themselves.
- **Tradespeople and DIYers**: skilled trades was the most expensive niche on Collabstr in 2025 ($309 average) and the second fastest-growing (+103% in searches).
- **Pet owners**, for pet food, toys and accessories.
- **Students and newcomers**, who can reach brands looking for diverse creators and languages.

It's a weaker fit if you want passive income (every video is new work), can't film in daylight hours, or don't want your face in brand ads.

## How does UGC pay?

You charge for the content and for how the brand may use it. The pieces you'll price:

1. **Base video:** one 15- to 60-second video with a hook, body and call to action.
2. **Extra hooks:** alternate first three seconds so the brand can test ads.
3. **Photos:** product or lifestyle stills. Billo, for example, adds $40 when a task includes four photos.
4. **Usage rights:** how long and where the brand can use your content, especially in paid ads.
5. **Whitelisting:** the brand runs ads through your social account; this usually costs extra.
6. **Exclusivity:** you agree not to work with competitors for a period, which should cost the brand much more.
7. **Raw footage:** unedited clips; charge for them rather than giving them away.

Payment comes three ways: through a marketplace that holds the brand's money until the content is approved (Collabstr, Billo), through a platform's creator marketplace, or directly from a brand you pitched, usually by invoice.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

### Week 1: Pick niches and film three samples

- Choose two niches from the list where you're a real customer.
- Film three spec videos with products you own: a problem-solution video, an unboxing and a "3 reasons I use this" testimonial.
- Shoot in 4K or at least 1080p (Salha Aziz said filming in HD for two years left her videos looking washed out after compression), vertical, in window light.

### Week 2: Build a portfolio and rate card

- Put the three videos on a simple portfolio page (Canva, Google Drive folder, or a link-in-bio page).
- Write your rate card: base video, extra hooks, photos, usage periods and raw footage.
- Create profiles on Collabstr and Billo.

### Week 3: Pitch ten brands

- Choose ten small brands you already buy from, especially Canadian ones.
- Send the pitch below with one relevant sample.
- Apply to marketplace tasks daily.

### Week 4: Deliver and ask for a testimonial

- Deliver on time with a short delivery note listing files and usage terms.
- Ask for a one-line testimonial and permission to show the video in your portfolio.
- Log hours per video so you know your real hourly rate.

### Days 31–90: Raise rates and add retainers

- After five paid briefs, raise your base rate by $25 to $50.
- Offer a monthly package (for example, four videos a month) to brands that rehire you.
- Add paid-usage and whitelisting fees to every quote.

## Templates you can copy

### Pitch email to a brand

```
Subject: UGC videos for [brand] — [product] demo idea

Hi [name],
I'm a Canadian UGC creator who [uses / is the customer for] [product].
I make short, natural videos for brands to use in ads and on product
pages. Here's a sample in a similar category: [link].

Idea for you: a 30-second "[hook idea]" video showing [benefit].
My rate starts at $[base] per video, with extra hooks and ad usage
available. Happy to send a full rate card.
[First name] | [portfolio link]
```

### Video script structure

```
0–3 s    Hook: a problem, a surprising result, or "I didn't expect..."
3–20 s   Body: show the product solving it; 2–3 specific benefits
20–30 s  Call to action from the brief ("Try it with code..." / "Link below")
Alt hooks: film 3 different first lines for ad testing
```

### Usage terms checklist (put it in every quote)

- Deliverables: number of videos, length, hooks, photos, raw footage.
- Where it can be used: organic social, website, email, paid ads.
- How long: for example, 30, 90 or 180 days of paid-ad usage.
- Whitelisting or Spark Ads through your account: yes or no, and the fee.
- Exclusivity: none, or the category and length, and the fee.
- Revisions: how many rounds are included.
- Payment: amount in CAD or USD, deposit, due date.

### Delivery note

```
Files: [list with file names]. Resolution: 4K, 9:16.
Usage: [scope] for [period] from [date]. Whitelisting: [yes/no].
Invoice #[x] attached, due [date]. Thanks for working with me!
```

## What do Canada's advertising rules require?

The brand is responsible for the ads it runs, but you share responsibility for what you post and say. The Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says material connections (payment, free products, discounts) must be clearly disclosed when you endorse something, and its [2019 notice](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2019/12/influencer-marketing-businesses-and-influencers-must-be-transparent-when-advertising-on-social-media.html) told businesses and influencers to be transparent.

- If you post a brand video on your own account, disclose it clearly (for example, "#ad" or the platform's paid-partnership label) at the start.
- Only say what you have actually experienced; the Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) covers performance claims.
- Don't read health or efficacy claims the brand can't back up.
- Get permission before filming anyone else, and don't show other people's personal information.

## What mistakes do new UGC creators make?

- **Giving unlimited usage for a one-time fee.** Price paid-ad usage by time period.
- **Handing over raw footage free.** Aziz calls this out specifically.
- **Filming in low resolution.** Platform compression makes it worse.
- **One take for voiceovers.** Record in short sections.
- **Pitching every brand.** Pitch products you'd genuinely use; it shows on camera.
- **No written terms.** Even a one-paragraph email confirming deliverables and usage protects you.
- **Ignoring gifted-product value.** Free products can be taxable income.

## Which tax rules apply to UGC in Canada?

UGC income is self-employment income. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Products you receive in exchange for content generally have value that counts as income; keep a log of what you received and its value. Deductible costs include gear (larger items through capital cost allowance), props, editing software, marketplace fees and a share of your phone plan. USD payouts are converted to CAD.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count other side-hustle revenue too. UGC isn't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Services to brands outside Canada can be treated differently; check the CRA's rules for your situation. For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered; check Revenu Québec. French-language content for Quebec brands is a strong niche, and Quebec has its own rules on advertising to children.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** provincial sales tax rules are separate from GST; check whether your services are taxable provincially.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered.

Fees for Collabstr and Billo were read on October 9, 2026. ## Which side hustles sit near UGC?

UGC sits next to other camera and content work.
