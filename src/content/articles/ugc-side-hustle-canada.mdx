UGC in Canada is user-generated content: a short video or a set of photos you film of a product, which a business may use in its own ads or on its own accounts, under a written note that says where the file may go and for how long. UGC suits people who can speak to a phone, follow a brief, and refuse a job that asks them to invent a result. It is a poor fit if you need a follower count first, or if you will hand over a file with no usage terms.

This guide does not publish a national UGC rate. There is no official Canadian price list for a 15-second clip. A number from a creator's screenshot is not your invoice. The Competition Bureau's rules on disclosure and on false claims still apply when the brand posts the file, and they apply to you when you post it.

## Who does UGC in Canada suit?

UGC in Canada suits people who can demonstrate a product they have actually handled: a kitchen tool, a skincare step you use, a piece of software on a screen you are allowed to show. A phone and a clean background are enough for sample clips. The work is a poor fit if you will not say "I have not tried that," if you want the brand to pay for your lifestyle rather than a file, or if the only portfolio is reposts of other creators.

A sample clip you made of a product you bought is a portfolio piece. A sample clip of a product you do not own, filmed from someone else's ad, is not. Brands hire the person who can follow a brief. They do not hire a follower count this guide refuses to invent.

## How much can UGC earn in Canada?

UGC earnings in Canada cannot be promised as a rate per clip. What you keep depends on the list below. This guide does not publish a day rate.

- How many clips, and how many revision rounds the note includes
- Whether the brand may run the file as an ad, and for how long
- Whether they may use your name and face on their ad account
- Product you had to buy and did not get to keep
- Hours to film and to reshoot
- A claim you refused because you had not experienced it
- Other side-hustle revenue in the same GST/HST pot

A brief that says "usage in perpetuity, all media, whitelisting" is a different product from "three clips on your phone, posted once by the brand." Price the sentence that is actually in the note. If the note is silent, ask before you film.

## How do you start UGC in Canada?

Starting UGC in Canada means making two honest sample clips, writing a usage note, and sending that note before you accept a product. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Pick a product type you have used

Kitchen, desk, outdoor, software you may show. Stay inside what you can talk about without reading a script you do not believe.

### Step 2: Film two samples on your phone

Show the product, say what you actually noticed, and stop. Do not claim a health result, a savings result, or a "before and after" you did not record. The Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) is the standard for claims.

### Step 3: Write the usage note you will require

Clips, rounds of edits, where the file may be posted, whether it may be an ad, whether your face may be used on the brand's ad account, the end date, and the fee. "Organic only" and "paid ads" are different lines. Leave whitelisting off until you know what it means for that brand: they post from an account that uses your identity. If you do not want that, say so.

### Step 4: Read the disclosure guidance

The Competition Bureau's [influencer page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says you should disclose material connections, including payment, free product, or commissions, in a way viewers will see. A 2019 [Bureau news release](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2019/12/influencer-marketing-businesses-and-influencers-must-be-transparent-when-advertising-on-social-media.html) says reviews should be based on actual experience, and that businesses can be liable for influencer content too. If the brand posts your file as an ad, the disclosure still has to be clear. Put a line in the brief that says who adds it.

### Step 5: Do not film other people without a reason

The Privacy Commissioner's [PIPEDA brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) explains that private-sector organizations have duties when they collect personal information in commercial activity. A customer's face in a shop, or a child's face, is not set dressing. Alberta, British Columbia, and Quebec have their own private-sector privacy laws for activity inside those provinces. This is not legal advice. If the clip needs a person who is not you, get a written yes or leave them out of frame.

### Step 6: Send the note to five businesses that already sell to people like you

A local maker, a shop whose product you bought, a service you use. Attach the samples. Name the fee for the narrow usage. Do not say "open to collab" with no price.

### Step 7: If they send a contract, read the usage against your note

If their paper is wider than your note, reply with the difference. Do not film "while you think about it." Product that arrives unsolicited is not an agreement. You can decline.

### Step 8: Deliver the files and the invoice the same week

Name the files. Keep a copy until the end date in the note, then delete customer data you do not need. Do not post the brand's clip on your own account unless the note allows it.

### Step 9: After two paid briefs, keep or cut the offer

If every client asked for ads and you only priced a social post, the next note has two prices. Honour the written fee for the client who already booked.

## What should the first week of UGC in Canada include?

The first week should produce two sample clips, a usage note, and five sends. A paid brief is a bonus.

- **Day 1:** Choose the product type. List claims you will not make.
- **Day 2:** Film two samples in daylight. Watch them on a phone.
- **Day 3:** Write the usage note with two prices: post on their accounts, and paid ads, if you are willing to do both.
- **Day 4:** Read the Competition Bureau influencer page. Add a disclosure line to the note.
- **Day 5:** List five businesses. Send the note and the samples.
- **Day 6:** If someone replies with a wider contract, answer the usage question only. Do not start filming.
- **Day 7:** If nobody replies, improve the first five seconds of the sample. Do not lower the fee to fill the silence until you know the usage.

## What UGC mistakes are common in Canada?

Common UGC mistakes in Canada are sending a file with no usage limit, inventing a result, and pricing every job as if it were a single organic post. The Bureau's guidance is that a material connection should be obvious, and that a testimonial should reflect real experience. A script that says the product "cleared your skin in three days," when it did not, is a claim problem even if the brand wrote the line. You can refuse the line.

Another mistake is filming in a workplace using an employer's customers or data. That can be a privacy problem and an employment problem. Use your own kitchen.

A follower-count requirement on a job post is the brand's preference. It is not a Canadian licence. A clear sample still matters more than a rented audience. Do not buy followers. A brand that asks you to hide that a post is paid is a brand to decline.

## Which tax rules apply to UGC in Canada?

UGC income in Canada is self-employment income, including a fee for a brief, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your own taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the briefs, the e-transfers, and the receipts for a tripod or a product you bought for a job. Report the work on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. UGC is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside. Free product is not automatically outside income. This guide does not tell you the dollar value of a gift. Ask before you ignore it.

Search your city for a business licence and use that city's fee if there is one. This guide does not state a licence fee from another city.
