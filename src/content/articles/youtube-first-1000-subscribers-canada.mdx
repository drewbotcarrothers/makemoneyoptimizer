## What does the first 1,000 subscribers mean for a channel in Canada?

The first 1,000 subscribers on YouTube is an eligibility input, not a paycheque. YouTube's [Partner Program overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en) says a channel can become eligible with 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. Qualified watch hours from Shorts in the Shorts feed do not count toward the 4,000 watch-hour threshold.

The same page says you also need to follow the channel monetization policies, live where the program is available, have no active Community Guidelines strikes, turn on 2-Step Verification, have advanced features access, and link an AdSense for YouTube account. Meeting a number is not acceptance. YouTube reviews the channel. The page says YouTube is introducing Partner Program updates effective 1 February 2027, and tells creators to review and accept updated terms in YouTube Studio by 31 January 2027. Read that notice before you rely on this paragraph.

Canada is on the country list for the [expanded Partner Program](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en). That page says creators in eligible countries can apply at 500 subscribers with 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. That tier is earlier access to fan funding and Shopping. Ad revenue and YouTube Premium revenue sharing on that page still use 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours, or 1,000 subscribers with 10 million qualified public Shorts views in 90 days. Confirm both help pages in YouTube Studio. This guide does not publish a per-view rate.

## Where do Canadians find their first YouTube subscribers?

Canadians find first subscribers by publishing a topic a specific viewer can use, and by telling people who already asked what they are making. Buying subscribers, trading empty subs, and emailing a scraped list are ways to fail a later review and, for the email, a legal problem.

Pick viewers you can name. A provincial process, a winter chore, a trade skill, a hobby with real steps. Say which province you mean when the rule is provincial. A video that could have been filmed anywhere still has to be accurate. Do not invent statistics to sound authoritative.

People who know you are a legitimate early audience when you tell them once, in a place they expect to hear from you. A community you belong to, if its rules allow a link. A friend who asked for the explanation. That is not the same as buying a shout-out or a subscriber pack.

Search is the path that can continue after your own circle is tired of the link. Titles that describe the task, a description without fake numbers, and another video on the same topic are the mechanics you control. YouTube decides what to recommend. This guide does not claim a title formula produces 1,000 people.

If you email a list about a new video, [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies to commercial messages. ISED's [getting consent to send email](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page says you need consent before commercial emails or texts. Express consent means the person agreed. Implied consent is limited, and that page says it may last up to two years from a relationship, or six months after an inquiry. The message needs your name, a mailing address, contact details valid for at least 60 days, and an unsubscribe mechanism acted on within 10 business days at no cost. A purchased list of "people interested in side hustles" is the wrong start. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first video description and note say?

A first YouTube description in Canada should say what the viewer will be able to do, which place the steps apply to, and what you left out. A note to a person should only go to someone who asked, or to a list that already meets the consent rules above.

**Example description.** This is a generic example. It is not a description that produced subscribers.

> How to [do one task] in [province], with the official page linked in the first lines. I show the steps I used and the point where the form asks for [a fact]. This is not a government video, and it does not promise an outcome from a program. Next video: the mistake I had to undo.

**Example note.** Generic, for someone who asked what you are publishing.

> You asked how I handle [task]. I posted a short video with the steps and the official link. Here it is. If you would rather not get these, say so and I will not send another.

Do not add "smash subscribe" language that promises income. YouTube's eligibility page says channels can lose monetization when they violate the monetization policies, regardless of subscriber count.

## How do you build proof without fake subscribers or reviews?

You build proof for a Canadian channel with public videos you actually made, on one topic, that a stranger can follow. Bought subscribers, comment pods presented as an audience, and a fake comment praising the channel are not proof. They are also a poor way to approach a review that looks at policy.

Four honest videos teach you the hours. A hundred private drafts teach you to hide. Reused clips you do not have rights to are how a later review goes badly. Use your own footage, your own voice, and links to official pages when you state a rule.

A community post that says "we hit 1,000" before you have is a false claim. Wait until YouTube Studio shows the number. Even then, say what the help page says: the count is not payment.

## What does a 14-day plan for the first subscribers look like?

A 14-day plan toward a first 1,000 subscribers in Canada should publish a small set of videos on one topic and tell people who already asked. It should not be read as a schedule that produces 1,000 people. Subscriber pace is not something this guide can promise.

### Days 1 and 2: Lock the topic

Write one sentence: who the video helps, in which province if the topic is local, and what they can do at the end. Draft four titles that name the task. Read the Partner Program pages once so the numbers are YouTube's, not a thumbnail's.

### Days 3 to 5: Publish the first two

Record with the phone. Fix audio if the room is louder than the words. Publish one video with an official link where you state a rule. Publish a second, or record it, before you buy more gear.

### Days 6 to 9: Stay on the same topic

Publish a third video that answers a question the first two left open. Tell people who asked, once. In Studio, you can select Get notified in the Earn area if you are not eligible. Do not buy subscribers to move the count.

### Days 10 to 14: Publish again and log the hours

Publish a fourth video or a scheduled one. Write down how long each upload took. Stop if the topic sentence is no longer true. Do not open a second channel to escape a quiet first one. Check the 1 February 2027 terms note on the eligibility page so you know a review of terms is coming for monetizing channels.

## What mistakes block the path to 1,000 subscribers in Canada?

Mistakes that block a real path to 1,000 subscribers in Canada are buying the count, copying footage you do not own, and planning a budget around ads in month one. The phone is enough until the audio is the problem.

Titles that promise income, and reused clips, are how a channel fails a later review. Quitting after one video teaches you nothing about a schedule. Emailing a purchased list creates an anti-spam problem and an audience that did not ask.

Switching topics every upload makes it unclear what the Subscribe button is for. Four videos on one task are a clearer test than ten unrelated shorts.

## Which tax rules apply to a YouTube channel in Canada?

YouTube income in Canada, when it arrives, is still income to record. Self-employment income is reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your own taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep payout records if you ever receive them. A quiet year is still worth a note that the amount was zero or small. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. A channel is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.

If you also sell digital products or merchandise to people in Canada, platform tax gets more complicated. The CRA's [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) describes rules, in effect since 1 July 2021, for certain non-resident suppliers and distribution platform operators. A checkout tax line is not your whole registration answer.
