## Can newcomers have a side hustle in Canada?

Yes, if your status allows it. Permanent residents and citizens can work for anyone and be self-employed. Temporary residents can only work within the conditions on their permit, and IRCC says on its [unauthorized work page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/admissibility-enforcement/consequences-unauthorized-work.html) that a visitor visa or eTA does not give you the right to work. The same page tells workers to read their permit carefully, because it sets out all the conditions for working in Canada, and breaking them can make you inadmissible.

IRCC's [work permit page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/permit/temporary/need-permit.html) describes two types:

- **Employer-specific work permit:** the most common; you need a job offer, and your employer may need a labour market impact assessment (LMIA). Because the permit is tied to that employer, a side gig for someone else may fall outside your conditions.
- **Open work permit:** you don't need a job offer and can work for most compliant employers in Canada, but you're only eligible in certain situations.

If you're unsure whether a gig app or freelance client fits your permit, read the conditions printed on it and ask IRCC or a licensed immigration consultant or lawyer before starting. IRCC lists the possible consequences of unauthorized work as removal from Canada, a permanent fraud record with IRCC, a five-year ban on returning, and harm to future applications, including permanent residence. This is not immigration or legal advice.

## How do newcomers get a SIN in Canada?

Newcomers get a SIN from Service Canada online, by mail, or in person. The [SIN page](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/sin.html) says a SIN is a 9-digit number you need to work in Canada or access government programs, and that it's illegal for anyone else to use it. For temporary residents, Service Canada's [temporary residents page](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/sin/temporary-residents.html) says:

- You need a valid work permit, study permit, or visitor record from IRCC that authorizes you to work; IRCC email messages are not accepted.
- You also need a secondary identity document such as a passport.
- Your SIN starts with "9" and expires on the same date as your permit, so update it whenever you get a new permit or your status changes.

Permanent residents and citizens get a SIN that doesn't expire.

## How do newcomers avoid job scams in Canada?

Newcomers avoid job scams in Canada by never paying for a job, a job offer, or a work permit. IRCC's [unauthorized work page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/admissibility-enforcement/consequences-unauthorized-work.html) says you may be getting scammed if someone promises a work permit or job offer if you pay them, says they'll help you become a permanent resident if you work for them, or asks for documents or payment through social media.

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre's [job scam page](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/scams-fraudes/job-emploi-eng.htm) adds these warning signs:

- A job offer you didn't apply for, often from a web-based email address.
- Upfront deposits, "upgrade fees," or "unlock payments" to keep working.
- A balance shown on a website or app that you can't withdraw.
- Requests to receive money into your bank account and forward it, which can make you a money mule.

The CAFC says never send deposits or pay fees for a job, and to report fraud to local police and the [Report Cybercrime and Fraud](https://reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca/) website.

## What mistakes do newcomers make with side hustles in Canada?

The common newcomer side hustle mistakes in Canada are working outside permit conditions, sharing a SIN carelessly, and assuming gig income is taxed automatically.

- **Not reading the permit.** Your conditions decide what's allowed, not the app's sign-up screen.
- **Letting a SIN expire.** Renew it with your permit.
- **Oversharing ID.** Only give your SIN to employers and payers who need it for tax reporting.
- **Assuming tax is withheld.** Gig and freelance payments usually have no tax deducted.
- **Skipping records.** Keep every payout statement and receipt; see [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).

## Which tax rules apply to newcomers' side hustles in Canada?

Once you're a Canadian tax resident, side hustle income is generally taxable in Canada. Self-employment income goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable sales pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), except for ride-sharing, which has its own rule (see [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/)). Start with [how to report side hustle income to the CRA](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/) and [taxes on gig app income](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/). Most newcomers can start under their own name without registering anything; [registering a sole proprietorship](/side-hustles/register-sole-proprietorship-canada/) explains when a business name needs provincial registration.

A tax tool such as <AffiliateLink program="turbotax-canada" label="TurboTax Canada" /> can walk you through a first Canadian return; check that the version you pick supports self-employment income, and note that free CRA-certified options exist.
