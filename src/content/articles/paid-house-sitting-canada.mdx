House sitting in Canada is two different deals that share a key. On TrustedHousesitters, a sitter cares for a home and pets in exchange for somewhere to stay, and the company's help centre says sitters do not charge the owner. On Rover, and with a direct client, the owner pays for the stay or the visits. Paid sitting suits people who want a fee and can be in that house on those nights. The exchange suits people who were already going to travel and can absorb the membership and the trip. It is a poor fit if you need rent money this month, because the exchange does not pay it.

The winter version of paid and exchange stays is [winter pet sitting and house sitting](/side-hustles/winter-pet-sitting-house-sitting-canada/). Holiday pricing is [Christmas week pet sitting and house sitting](/side-hustles/christmas-week-pet-sitting-house-sitting-pricing-canada/). Daytime walks are [dog walking](/side-hustles/dog-walking-side-hustle-canada/).

## Is house sitting paid in Canada?

House sitting is paid in Canada when the owner pays you a fee. It is not paid when the deal is a stay in exchange for care and the platform's own rule is that no money changes hands between members. Headlines that promise you will "get paid to house sit" are usually describing the second model, or they are describing a US gig the article did not check in Canada. Read the page that would actually pay you.

TrustedHousesitters' [help article](https://support.trustedhousesitters.com/hc/en-gb/articles/213537169-How-much-will-sitters-charge-to-look-after-my-home-and-pets) says sitters do not charge, and that no money is exchanged between the sitter and the owner. The [sitter join page](https://www.trustedhousesitters.com/join-now/house-sitters/) says both pet parents and sitters pay an annual membership for access, and that no money changes hands between members. The [travel article](https://support.trustedhousesitters.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360000086169-Who-pays-for-travel-to-a-sit) says you pay your own way to the sit. The [booking-fee article](https://support.trustedhousesitters.com/hc/en-gb/articles/31682057259421-Does-everyone-pay-a-booking-fee) says that, alongside the annual membership, a confirmed sit can include a booking fee paid by both the owner and the sitter, and that Premium members do not pay that booking fee. That fee is paid to the platform. It is not wages from the homeowner. Amounts change. Read them on the day you join. This guide does not restate a price that the page can edit.

Rover is the paid model. The [Rover review](/side-hustles/rover-review-canada/) cites Rover's Canadian help centre: a profile review fee, a service fee on bookings, and a tiered pilot in named cities. House sitting is one of the services sitters can list. Confirm the live [service-fee article](https://support-ca.rover.com/hc/en-ca/articles/360037446911-What-are-the-service-fees) before you subtract a percentage from a job you have not booked. Direct clients pay the fee you wrote down, with no platform cut, and with no platform guarantee either.

## How do TrustedHousesitters and paid sitting compare in Canada?

They compare as an exchange versus a fee. Use the row that matches the problem you actually have.

| | TrustedHousesitters | Rover or a direct paid sit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you receive | A place to stay while you provide the care the listing describes | A fee for the nights or visits in the booking |
| What you pay | Membership, your travel, and a booking fee if your plan charges one | Rover's published sitter fees, or your own costs on a direct sit |
| What the owner pays you | Nothing, under the help-centre rule | The booking total, minus a platform fee if you used one |
| Fits | Travel you were going to do, with dates that match a listing | Income, in a city where you can sleep in that house |
| Fails | When you needed cash, or when the flights cost more than the hotel you avoided | When you double-book the night, or when you counted on a fee the exchange never offered |

A sit that is "free accommodation" still has a cost: the membership, the trip, food the listing does not cover, and nights you cannot sell to a paying client. Add those before you call it income.

## What should a paid house sit include in Canada?

A paid house sit in Canada should say, in writing, where you sleep, which animals you care for, and what you will not do. Snow clearing, a deep clean, and plant watering are easy to absorb for free and expensive in time. Snow is [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/). A clean before the owner returns is [house cleaning](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-side-hustle-canada/). A walk in the afternoon is [dog walking](/side-hustles/dog-walking-side-hustle-canada/). Put extra tasks on their own line or leave them out.

Children in the house are not a sit. [Babysitting](/side-hustles/babysitting-side-hustle-canada/) has training and provincial child-care limits. Do not fold childcare into a pet sit because you are already staying over.

Hosting the pet at your home is boarding, not house sitting. Leases, condo rules, and municipal animal limits apply. The winter sitting guide separates drop-ins, overnights in the client's home, and hosting at yours.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote or a survey. Suppose a direct client pays an assumed $60 a night for three nights of pet care in their home. Fees are 3 × $60 = $180. Suppose groceries you were not told to use from their kitchen cost $40. Amount left is $140 before tax. Suppose the same three nights are a TrustedHousesitters listing in another city. Cash from the owner is $0. Suppose the train is an assumed $160 and a membership slice for those dates is an assumed $40. You are down $200 on cash, and you did not pay for a hotel. The hotel you avoided is the point of that model, and it is not $180 of income. Do not copy $60. Price a paid sit from nights you will not double-book. ## What are the risks of house sitting in Canada?

The risks are the house, the animal, and the nights you promised twice. Ask a broker whether a personal home policy covers paid care in someone else's home, and whether an exchange sit is still "business" if you are not paid cash. The Insurance Bureau of Canada's [business coverage page](https://www.ibc.ca/insurance-basics/business/types-of-business-insurance-coverage) is the overview of commercial policies. It is not a quote. Rover's guarantee has limits the [Rover review](/side-hustles/rover-review-canada/) tells you to read on Rover's own page. TrustedHousesitters' membership pages describe their own protections. Read them. Do not assume a platform replaces a policy.

Keys, alarm codes, and vet permission belong in one note the owner has agreed, not in a group chat. The [scams guide](/side-hustles/side-hustle-scams-canada/) is the wider habit of not handing a stranger a way into someone's routine. A sit that asks you to pay a deposit to the "owner" before you see the house is a reason to stop.

Municipal business licences and home-based rules, if clients or pets come to your place, are local. Confirm them. This is not legal or insurance advice.

## Which tax rules apply to house sitting in Canada?

A fee for house sitting or pet sitting in Canada is generally self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both shares of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) on taxable supplies. Dog walking and sitting share that pot. The [tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/) is the short version. Service income stays off the rental form. [T2125 vs T776](/side-hustles/t2125-vs-t776-canada/) explains why.

An exchange with no fee is not the same record. You may still have membership and travel receipts. Whether a free stay is a taxable benefit depends on facts this guide will not guess. If the stay is valuable and tied to services, ask the CRA or a tax preparer before you treat it as nothing. This is not tax advice.
