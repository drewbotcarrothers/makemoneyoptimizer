## Which product safety mistakes do resellers make in Canada?

The main product safety mistake resellers make in Canada is assuming second-hand items are exempt from safety law. They are not.

### Mistake 1: Selling recalled or banned products

Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says the Canada Consumer Product Safety Act prohibits selling products that are a danger to human health or safety. It lists banned products such as baby walkers and lawn darts. Search the [recalls site](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) by brand and model before listing.

### Mistake 2: Buying regulated children's items you cannot verify

The guide says traditional drop-side cribs have been prohibited since December 29, 2016, and that products such as car seats, cribs, and toys have specific requirements. If you cannot confirm an item's history and compliance, do not buy it to resell.

## Which pricing and honesty mistakes do resellers make in Canada?

The main pricing mistake resellers make in Canada is calculating profit from the sale price instead of from what they keep after fees and shipping.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring fees in your margin

Poshmark's [getting started guide](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/get_started_on_poshmark) says sales under C$20 pay a flat C$3.95 and sales of C$20 or more pay 20%. On a C$10 item, that flat fee takes 39.5%. Check each platform's fee page and work out what you keep before you buy.

### Mistake 4: Overstating condition

The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) applies to how products are described. Photograph flaws, state them plainly, and never call an item "new" or "authentic" without being sure.

## Which scam and record mistakes do resellers make in Canada?

The main scam mistake resellers make in Canada is shipping or handing over an item before payment has actually arrived.

### Mistake 5: Falling for payment scams

Common tricks include fake payment confirmation emails, overpayment with a request to refund the difference, and requests to ship before paying. Confirm the money is in your account first. Report fraud to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/index-eng.htm).

### Mistake 6: Not counting dead stock

Items that never sell still cost money. Track them and include them in your profit calculation.

### Mistake 7: No purchase records

The CRA's [keeping records page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records.html) explains what to keep. For resellers, that means buy receipts, sales records, fee statements, and shipping costs.

## How do you set up reselling to avoid these mistakes in Canada?

Set up reselling in Canada to avoid these mistakes by building safety checks, a margin calculation, and a record system into how you buy.

### Step 1: Make a do-not-buy list

Banned products, recalled models, and regulated children's items you cannot verify.

### Step 2: Check recalls before listing

Brand and model on the recalls site.

### Step 3: Calculate what you keep before buying

Sale price minus fees and shipping, minus cost.

### Step 4: Photograph and describe flaws

Plain language, no guesses about authenticity.

### Step 5: Log every buy and sale

Date, item, cost, sale price, fees, and shipping.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not an average. Suppose you buy 20 items for $5 each ($100). Fifteen sell on Poshmark.ca at $25 each: fees are 15 × $25 × 20% = $75, so you keep 15 × $25 − $75 = $300. Five never sell. Profit is $300 − $100 = $200. If you count only the cost of the 15 sold items (15 × $5 = $75), you would think profit was $300 − $75 = $225, overstating it by the $25 tied up in unsold stock.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect reselling in Canada?

Buying items to resell for profit in Canada is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html); occasionally selling your own used belongings is generally not. GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters.
