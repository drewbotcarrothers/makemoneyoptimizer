A house cleaning side hustle in Canada means cleaning homes for a flat fee per visit, usually on a weekly, bi-weekly or monthly schedule, plus one-time deep cleans and move-out cleans. You need basic supplies, a way to get to clients, a clear checklist and, ideally, liability insurance. Published 2026 price lists put a bi-weekly clean of a two-bedroom home at roughly $120 to $170 in Ottawa and $140 in Calgary, which works out to much more per hour than the $19.74 median wage for employed cleaners.

- **Employee benchmark:** light duty cleaners (NOC 65310) earn a median of $19.74 an hour in Canada (Job Bank, updated November 2025). Self-employed cleaners set their own rates but pay their own supplies, travel and CPP.

- **Startup:** basic supplies, an online booking or invoicing tool, and liability insurance. Many clients have their own vacuum.

## Who house cleaning suits

House cleaning is physical, repeatable work with strong repeat demand. It suits you if:

- You're thorough and like visible results.
- You can be on your feet, kneeling and lifting for three or four hours at a time.
- You're reliable. Clients hand you their keys and alarm codes; trust is the product.
- You have evenings, weekends or weekdays free. Many clients want cleaning while they're at work, so weekday daytime slots are valuable.

It's a harder fit if you have back, knee or respiratory problems, or no reliable transportation. A car makes carrying supplies far easier, although some cleaners use clients' own supplies and equipment to make transit work.

## Real Canadian cleaning business owners

- **Melissa Maker, Clean My Space (Toronto).** Maker started a Toronto cleaning service in 2006 knowing, in her words, nothing about cleaning beyond disliking it. She told *The Globe and Mail* in 2014 that her friends didn't know which cleaning companies they could trust, and that most cleaning companies "lacked good websites, they lacked good marketing, they lacked standards." After five years in the business she started YouTube videos to get attention for the service. By early 2017 the channel had over 600,000 subscribers, and her video on cleaning a mattress had almost 11 million views (*Toronto Guardian*).
- **Jacqui Jepson, The Pink Wand (Airdrie, Alberta).** According to Community Futures Centre West, Jepson grew a part-time cleaning gig into a full business in 2008, hired a business coach, and built her own franchise system. The Pink Wand had four franchisees, with Jepson running eleven other locations, at the time of the profile.

Both started with the same gap: clients who wanted someone trustworthy, consistent and easy to book.

## Pricing and the math on one client

Most cleaning businesses price per visit based on home size and bathrooms, not hourly. Clients like knowing the price; you benefit from getting faster.

**Illustrative example.** You take on a two-bedroom, one-bathroom home on a bi-weekly schedule and charge $140 a visit, matching NeatNow's published Calgary bi-weekly price and inside Clean 613's Ottawa range of $120 to $170. Assume the clean takes three hours and supplies cost about $5 a visit (both assumptions; time your own cleans).

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Price per visit | $140.00 |
| Supplies (assumed) | –$5.00 |
| Gross profit per visit | $135.00 |
| Per hour at 3 hours | $45.00 |
| Job Bank employee median for comparison | $19.74 |

**Illustrative side-hustle month.** Six bi-weekly clients like this means about three cleans a week. At 52 ÷ 12 = 4.33 weeks a month, that's roughly 13 cleans and about $1,755 in gross profit a month, before travel, insurance, CPP and income tax. Annual revenue would be about $21,840, below the $30,000 GST/HST threshold.

Remember to subtract travel time. Two clients on the same street are worth more than two clients across town. The full pricing method is in [how to price house cleaning](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-pricing-canada/).

## Your launch plan: the first 30 days

### Week 1: define your service

- Write your standard checklist (template below) and decide what's not included.
- Set prices for 1-bed, 2-bed, 3-bed and 4-bed homes using the price tables above as a reference.
- Time yourself cleaning your own home and a friend's or relative's home with the checklist.
- Decide your service area, ideally one or two neighbourhoods.
- Read every product label before the first job. [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/chemicals/cleaning-products-working-safely.html) says never to mix different cleaning products, and never to mix bleach and ammonia.

### Week 2: get set up

- Get liability insurance quotes. Options are compared in [APOLLO vs Zensurance](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/).
- Buy the core kit, set up a booking or invoicing tool, and open a separate account.
- Create a simple one-page website or Google Business Profile with your prices, service area and booking link.
- Write a client agreement (checklist below).
- If clients or an agency ask for a police check, ask which type they need. Ontario's [police record checks page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/police-record-checks) describes three types and says vulnerable sector checks must be completed by your local police service.

### Week 3: find your first clients

- Tell friends, family, neighbours and coworkers. Ask for introductions, not favours.
- Post in local community groups and on neighbourhood apps with a clear offer and price.
- Drop flyers in a few streets near your home. If you email people who aren't already contacts, read the CASL [consent rules](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) first.
- Offer a first deep clean, then move happy clients to a recurring schedule.
- Marketing scripts are in [how to get your first house cleaning clients](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-first-clients-canada/).

### Week 4: deliver and ask for reviews

- Send a confirmation the day before each clean.
- After each first clean, send a short message asking how it went, then ask for a review.
- Offer recurring clients a referral reward, like $20 off their next clean for each new client they send.

### Days 31 to 90

| Weeks | Focus | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | Convert first cleans into recurring clients | 3 to 4 recurring clients |
| 7–8 | Cluster clients by neighbourhood; add add-ons | Less driving per dollar |
| 9–10 | Raise prices for new clients if you're booked | Higher hourly earnings |
| 11–12 | Review profit per hour; decide whether to add move-out cleans | A full, steady schedule |

## The first-visit walkthrough

Before the first clean, visit the home or do a video call for 10 minutes. It prevents most disputes later.

1. **Walk every room** with the client and note anything fragile, valuable or off-limits.
2. **Ask about products:** allergies, fragrance-free requests, natural stone counters, hardwood floor finishes and any surfaces that need a specific cleaner.
3. **Confirm access:** key handover or lockbox, alarm code, parking and where to leave the key afterwards.
4. **Ask about pets** and where they'll be while you work.
5. **Agree on priorities.** If time runs short, which rooms matter most?
6. **Photograph existing damage** (with permission) such as chipped tiles or scratched floors, so nobody blames you later.
7. **Confirm the price and schedule in writing** the same day, using the agreement checklist below.

## Checklists and templates

**Standard clean checklist (copy and adapt)**

*Kitchen*
- [ ] Wipe counters, backsplash and outside of appliances
- [ ] Clean sink and faucet
- [ ] Wipe stovetop and microwave inside and out
- [ ] Wipe cabinet fronts and handles
- [ ] Empty garbage and recycling

*Bathrooms*
- [ ] Clean and disinfect toilet, including base
- [ ] Scrub tub/shower and fixtures
- [ ] Clean sink, counter and mirror
- [ ] Wipe cabinet fronts
- [ ] Replace towels if left out

*All rooms*
- [ ] Dust reachable surfaces, light switches and door handles
- [ ] Vacuum carpets and rugs
- [ ] Vacuum and wash hard floors
- [ ] Make beds (linen change if requested)
- [ ] Empty small bins

**Not included unless booked:** inside oven, inside fridge, inside cabinets, interior windows, laundry, walls, and anything above reach without a stepladder.

**Client agreement checklist**

- [ ] Service, frequency and price per visit
- [ ] What's included and add-on prices
- [ ] Access: key, lockbox or code, and alarm instructions
- [ ] Pets: where they'll be during the clean
- [ ] Cancellation policy (for example, 24 hours' notice or a fee)
- [ ] Breakage policy and your insurance
- [ ] Payment method and due date
- [ ] Re-clean guarantee window (for example, 24 hours to report a missed spot)

**Booking reply template**

> Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out! For a [number]-bedroom, [number]-bathroom home, a standard clean is $[price], and bi-weekly cleans are $[price] per visit after the first clean. The first visit is a deep clean at $[price] because it takes longer. I bring all supplies [or: I can use your supplies if you prefer]. I have openings on [days]. Want me to book you in?

## Insurance and safety

Liability insurance matters in other people's homes. A broken heirloom, a flooded floor or a damaged countertop can cost more than months of profit. NeatNow, for example, advertises that every clean is covered by $2 million of liability insurance, and clients increasingly ask. Get quotes for general liability and ask about coverage for damage to clients' property and lost keys. See [APOLLO vs Zensurance](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

For your own safety: use gloves, ventilate bathrooms, never mix bleach with ammonia or acid-based cleaners, and lift with your legs.

## Taxes for house cleaners

- **Income tax:** house cleaning fees are self-employment income. Report revenue and expenses (supplies, insurance, software, vehicle use for business, phone share) on Form T2125, even if no client gives you a slip. See [how to report side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/).
- **CPP:** 11.9% of net earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026 (QPP in Quebec).
- **GST/HST:** you're a small supplier until your worldwide taxable supplies pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. The first-dollar rule for taxi and rideshare drivers does not apply to cleaning. Once registered, charge the rate where the cleaning happens, such as 5% GST in Alberta, 13% HST in Ontario or 14% HST in Nova Scotia.
- Keep supply receipts and a mileage log from day one.

## Provincial notes

- **Alberta:** only 5% GST once registered, and no provincial sales tax. NeatNow's Calgary prices are quoted before GST.
- **Ontario:** 13% HST once registered. Toronto's dense condo neighbourhoods let you fit more cleans into a day.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP and QST applies once you register; the Job Bank median for cleaners is highest here at $20.47.
- **B.C.:** Job Bank's low end for cleaners is the highest in the country at $18.25.
- **Atlantic Canada:** Nova Scotia charges 14% HST once registered; medians are lower, so price against local companies.

## What to watch out for

- **Your body is the business.** Repetitive bending and scrubbing add up. Build in rest days and use long-handled tools.
- **Travel eats margin.** Cluster clients and charge a trip fee outside your core area.
- **Cancellations and lockouts.** A written 24-hour cancellation policy protects your income.
- **Scope creep.** "Could you just do the oven?" should trigger your add-on price.
- **Trust and liability.** Without insurance, one accident can wipe out a season's profit. The FCAC's [home insurance page](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/insurance/home.html) says home insurance isn't business insurance and generally won't cover claims related to a home-based business.
- **Mixing products.** Health Canada's [household chemical safety page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/household-chemical-safety.html) says never to mix household chemical products, because some mixtures produce harmful gases, and to keep rooms well ventilated. Keep every product in its labelled container.
- **Keys and codes.** Alarm codes, lockbox codes and addresses are personal information under [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/). Label keys with a client code, never an address.
- **Paying a helper without checking their status.** The CRA's [RC4110 guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4110/employee-self-employed.html) weighs control, who supplies tools, financial risk and chance of profit. A helper you schedule, supply and supervise may be an employee, with payroll obligations.
- **Underpricing.** If you're booked solid within weeks, your prices are probably too low.
