After this guide you can offer a clear cleaning service in your area, price a first home without guessing, and run a one-week trial that aims at a recurring booking instead of a single exhausted deep-clean.

House cleaning is unglamorous and unusually durable. People who trust you will keep you through recessions that wipe out gig-app bonuses. The trust is the product. The mop is just the tool. If you want outdoor seasons instead of indoor routines, look at [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada) and [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada). If you would rather assemble furniture than scrub showers, start with [odd-job apps](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-side-hustle-canada) or [handyman work](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada).

## Who it fits

**This can fit if** you are reliable, you do not mind other people’s mess, you can work on your feet for a few hours, and you can get to the same homes on a repeating day. It fits people who like a visible finish and a quiet house. It fits newcomers and students who can build a route near transit or a car they already have.

**This is a poor fit if** strong cleaners, dust, or pet hair make you sick and you will not wear protection. It is a poor fit if you cannot commit to a weekday window, if you hate being in a stranger’s space, or if you expect salon-level income from one Saturday deep-clean. It is a poor fit if you will not set boundaries about hoarding, biohazards, or unsafe homes. You are allowed to decline a unit.

Employees of a cleaning company are a different path: hourly, less admin, less control. This guide is for you as the person clients pay directly.

## What you are actually selling

Sell a maintained home, not a miracle. A practical starter offer:

- A recurring clean every week or every two weeks
- A written list: kitchens, bathrooms, floors, dusting the reachable surfaces, emptying the bins they set out
- A longer first clean, priced higher, because the first visit is always worse
- Add-ons only if you want them: inside oven, inside fridge, interior windows, laundry

Leave these off the list until you have a reason and a price: mould remediation, pest infestations, hoarding, post-construction dust at scale, and anything that needs hazardous-material training. Those are other businesses.

Clients in Canada find cleaners through neighbours, building chats, local Facebook groups, and sometimes apps. Apps take a fee and supply a stream. Direct clients pay you the whole fee and expect you to be the scheduler. Many cleaners use an app to fill the first month, then keep the relationship direct only if the platform’s rules allow it. Read those rules. Poaching against them can get you banned. A cleaner introduction through a neighbour never had that problem.

## How to price without copying a stranger’s rate

Cleaning prices move with city, home size, pets, and condition. A one-bedroom maintained every two weeks is not a five-bedroom first visit with two dogs. Charge the first clean more. Then charge the maintenance visit less, and protect that lower rate by keeping the schedule. If they cancel for two months, the next visit is a first clean again.

Include your time to get there. A $120 clean that takes 90 minutes of transit is a weak hourly rate. Tighten the map before you discount the price.

Ask to be paid by e-transfer on the day, or weekly for a set route. Cash with no record feels simple and becomes a tax and dispute problem. Do not work a month “on trial” for free. A paid first clean is the trial.

You will not be full in week one. A side hustle of two recurring homes is a success if the hourly net, after supplies and travel, beats your alternatives and your body agrees.

## Step-by-step

### 1. Write a one-page scope

List what is included, what costs extra, and what you do not do. Add how you handle pets, how you enter, and how much notice you need to cancel. This page is your script when someone asks you to “just do whatever.”

### 2. Decide the product: maintenance, not rescue

Book recurring slots. Use the first visit to bring the home to a level you can keep. If a home needs ten hours before it can be maintained, either price those ten hours or decline. Heroic free hours teach the client the wrong price.

### 3. Gather a small kit and a preference list

Cloths, bathroom cleaner, kitchen cleaner, glass, mop, gloves, shoe covers. Ask each client about scents, products they refuse, and floors that cannot be wet. Write it on the page you keep for that home. Memory fails by the fourth client.

### 4. Get the insurance question answered

Tell your home or tenant insurer if you are unsure whether a side business is excluded. Then price a small liability policy aimed at cleaning. The quote is the decision. If you are in a client’s home weekly, you want a policy that knows that.

### 5. Find two people, not two hundred

Tell neighbours, parents at a school, and one local group. Offer the scope and the price range for a typical apartment or townhouse, and offer a paid first clean. Avoid “DM me for rates” with no numbers. People who are ready to book want a range.

### 6. Do the first clean like a checklist

Top to bottom in each room. Bathroom cloths stay in the bathroom. Move knick-knacks back to the same spot. Do not throw away anything that is not in a bin. Photograph nothing personal. Send a short “done, I locked up” message.

### 7. Book the next visit before you leave

“Same time in two weeks?” is the whole sales pitch. If they hesitate, you still get paid for today. If they say yes, put it in a calendar you will not forget, and send the date in the same thread.

### 8. Add homes only along the route

The third client should be near the first two. A pretty rate across the city is how cleaners burn out. Transit pass or fuel is a real line in the cost table. Treat it that way.

### 9. Raise or refine after a month

If every visit runs long, the home is not a maintenance clean or the price is wrong. Adjust the scope or the price before you resent the client. A calm conversation beats a ghosted Thursday.

## Your first week

- **Day 1:** Write the scope and a price range for a small home and a larger one. Check whether your city wants a business licence for cleaning services.
- **Day 2:** Price insurance. Buy cloths and cleaners only.
- **Day 3:** Tell ten specific people and post once locally.
- **Day 4:** Book one paid first clean. Ask the product and pet questions in advance.
- **Day 5:** Do the clean. Time each area so you learn.
- **Day 6:** Message to book the recurring slot. Rest your hands.
- **Day 7:** Review the hourly net including travel. If it works, ask that client for one introduction. If it does not, change the map or the offer before you add homes.

## Tips that are easy to miss

Colour-coded cloths are the cheapest quality system you will ever use. Bathroom, kitchen, glass, floors. Clients who care about hygiene notice, even if they never say the word microfibre.

The first clean sets the clock. If you skip baseboards and then include them forever, you trained yourself into unpaid work. Put “reachable surfaces” in the scope and stick to it.

Keys need the same care as in pet sitting. Code them. Do not tag the address. Confirm alarm codes out loud once, then store them somewhere that is not a contact named with the street.

Fragrance-free is a common Canadian request in smaller condos and among people with asthma. Carry one unscented option so you are not stuck.

Pets and cleaners do not mix in a bucket on the floor. Keep products closed, and do not let a dog drink from the mop bucket. If a home is unsafe because of an animal, leave. [Dog walking](/side-hustles/dog-walking-side-hustle-canada) is a separate skill.

## Common mistakes

Deep-cleaning for a maintenance price to “win the client.” You will win a client who expects that forever.

Using a product that damages stone, hardwood, or a special tub because it worked on your laminate. Ask, test a corner if you are unsure, or skip that surface.

No shows and last-minute cancellations without a policy. Write one: illness happens, and so does a fee or a forfeit of the slot if they cancel the morning of.

Working alone in a way that ignores safety. Tell someone which address you are at. Leave if a situation feels wrong. No clean is worth that.

Mixing cash tips into memory and forgetting the supplies. If you cannot see profit, you do not have profit yet.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Cleaning fees are self-employment income. Keep invoices and supply receipts. Report the income even without a T4A. Use the T2125 with your T1 for the business details, and plan for both the employee and employer portions of CPP on the net.

The small-supplier test decides GST/HST registration. The CRA describes a small supplier as a person whose worldwide taxable supplies, with associates, are $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Stay under it and you generally need not register. Cross it and you must, on the schedule the CRA explains on its [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page. Cleaning is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar taxi rule does not apply. Once registered, charge the provincial rate. HST provinces differ from GST-plus-PST provinces. Quebec has GST and QST. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025. The CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) is the list to trust. Set the tax aside in the separate account.

Some cities require a business licence for home services. Condos may require the owner, not you, to register visitors. Ask. Provincial employment rules matter if you later hire a helper: a “contractor” who uses your supplies, your schedule, and your clients may be an employee in the CRA’s eyes. Get advice before you build a crew. Workers’ compensation boards differ by province if you have workers.

Products: follow label directions. Do not mix bleach and ammonia. That is not a style preference. It is how people hurt their lungs.
