## Who is house cleaning a good fit for in Canada?

House cleaning in Canada fits people who like a visible finish and a quiet house, including newcomers and students who can build a route near transit or a car they already have. House cleaning is a poor fit when strong cleaners, dust, or pet hair make someone sick and they will not wear protection, when a weekday window will not hold, or when the expectation is high income from one Saturday deep-clean. House cleaning is also a poor fit when the cleaner will not decline hoarding, biohazards, or an unsafe home.

Employees of a cleaning company follow a different path: hourly pay, less admin, and less control over the route. This guide is for the person the client pays directly.

## What does a house cleaning visit include in Canada?

House cleaning in Canada sells a maintained home on a repeating visit, and the first visit is priced higher because that visit takes longer. The written list covers kitchens, bathrooms, floors, dusting reachable surfaces, and bins the client sets out. Mould remediation, pest infestations, hoarding, large post-construction dust, and hazardous-material work are different businesses and stay off the starter list.

| Offer | What the client gets |
| --- | --- |
| Recurring maintenance | A repeating visit on the written list, priced below the first clean |
| First clean | A longer visit that brings the home to a level the maintenance visit can keep, priced higher |
| Optional add-ons | Inside the oven, inside the fridge, interior windows, or laundry, only if you want that work |
| Not in the starter offer | Mould remediation, pest infestations, hoarding, large post-construction dust, hazardous-material cleanup |

Clients in Canada find house cleaning through neighbours, building chats, local Facebook groups, and sometimes apps. An app can supply bookings and takes a fee under that app’s own terms. Direct clients pay the full fee and expect the cleaner to handle scheduling. Read the platform rules before moving a booking off an app. A neighbour introduction has no platform rule attached.

## How do you start house cleaning in Canada?

Starting house cleaning in Canada means writing a scope, gathering a small kit once a booking is real, doing one paid clean, and booking the next visit before leaving. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Write a one-page scope

List what is included, what costs extra, and what you do not do. Add how you handle pets, how you enter, and how much notice a cancellation needs. This page is the script when someone asks for “whatever needs doing.”

### Step 2: Sell maintenance, and price a rescue separately

Book recurring slots. Use the first visit to bring the home to a level you can keep. If a home needs many extra hours before it can be maintained, price those hours or decline. Unpaid extra hours teach the client the wrong price.

### Step 3: Gather a small kit and a preference list

Cloths, bathroom cleaner, kitchen cleaner, glass cleaner, a mop, gloves, and shoe covers are enough to start. Ask each client about scents, products they refuse, and floors that cannot be wet. Write the answers on the page you keep for that home.

### Step 4: Get an insurance quote

Ask your home or tenant insurer whether a side business is covered. Then price a small liability policy aimed at cleaning. The quote is the decision. A weekly visit in a client’s home should be a visit the policy knows about. This is not insurance advice.

### Step 5: Find two people near the route

Tell neighbours, parents at a school, and one local group. Offer the scope and a price range for a typical apartment or townhouse, and offer a paid first clean. People who are ready to book want a range.

### Step 6: Do the first clean from a checklist

Work top to bottom in each room. Bathroom cloths stay in the bathroom. Move knick-knacks back to the same spot. Do not throw away anything that is not in a bin. Photograph nothing personal. Send a short message that the clean is done and the door is locked.

### Step 7: Book the next visit before you leave

Ask whether the same time in two weeks works. If the client hesitates, you still get paid for today. If the client says yes, put the date in a calendar and send it in the same thread.

### Step 8: Add homes only along the route

The third client should be near the first two. A strong rate across the city is how cleaners burn out. Transit or fuel is a real line in the cost table.

### Step 9: Adjust the scope or the price after a month

If every visit runs long, the home is not a maintenance clean or the price is wrong. Adjust the scope or the price before the Thursday becomes something you resent.

## What is easy to miss on a house cleaning route in Canada?

House cleaning in Canada goes more smoothly when cloths stay colour-coded, the first clean does not quietly add unpaid tasks, and keys are stored without an address label. Some homes ask for fragrance-free products, so one unscented option is worth carrying. Open cleaner bottles and pets do not belong in the same bucket.

Colour-code bathroom, kitchen, glass, and floor cloths. Clients who care about hygiene notice, even if they never say the word microfibre.

The first clean sets the clock. If you skip baseboards and then include them forever, those baseboards became unpaid work. Put “reachable surfaces” in the scope and stick to it.

Keys need a code, not an address tag. Confirm alarm codes once, then store them somewhere that is not a contact named with the street.

If a home is unsafe because of an animal, leave. [Dog walking](/side-hustles/dog-walking-side-hustle-canada/) is a separate skill. Keep products closed, and do not let a dog drink from the mop bucket.

A written scope, a spot test, and a cancellation fee or a forfeited slot are the fixes. Someone should know the address, and the cleaner leaves if a home feels unsafe.

## Which tax rules apply to house cleaning in Canada?

House cleaning fees in Canada are self-employment income: keep invoices and supply receipts, report the income even without a T4A, and file [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with the T1. Self-employed people pay the whole Canada Pension Plan contribution on that net, which is both the employee and employer portions, as described under [Contributions to the Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). GST/HST registration for house cleaning follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

The Canada Revenue Agency describes a small supplier as a person whose worldwide taxable supplies, with associates, are $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Stay at or under that test and registration is generally optional. Cross it and registration follows the schedule on the CRA’s when-to-register page. House cleaning is not taxi or ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi and ride-sharing page does not apply. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the clean happens. Confirm that rate, including for Quebec, on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set the tax aside in the separate account.

Search the city’s website for a business-licence requirement. This guide does not state a Canada-wide fee. Condos may ask the resident to register a visitor. Ask the client. If you later pay a helper, get advice before you treat that person as a contractor. This guide does not decide employment status. If you have workers, check that province’s workers’ compensation rules.

Follow label directions. Do not mix bleach and ammonia.
