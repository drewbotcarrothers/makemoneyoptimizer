They suit people who already run the workflow, and they do not assume a catalogue of forty databases.

Selling Notion templates in Canada starts at about $0 when the free Notion plan can hand the buyer a duplicate and the checkout you choose has no monthly fee, about $15–$40 when you add Notion Plus for a month, and about $80–$200 when Shopify Basic at the yearly rate, a domain you priced, and a month of support time you actually track are in the fuller start. The ranges are not quotes. Gumroad's percentage is a cost of the sale, not a cost of the empty month.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for Notion templates in Canada?

Ongoing Notion template costs in Canada are the Notion seat if you kept it, the checkout percentage on each sale, refunds when the setup page was thin, and support hours. Notion template break-even in the sketch is a method, not a catalogue income.

**Discover versus direct.** Thirty percent and ten percent plus fifty cents are different products. Know which link you sent.

**Refunds.** A buyer who cannot duplicate the file is a cost you can prevent with a clearer page.

**Updates.** When Notion changes a feature you used, the file needs a pass. That hour is part of the price.

**A store subscription with no traffic.** Shopify's month arrives whether or not anyone buys.

### Step 1: Build one file on the plan that can share it

If Free cannot hand off the feature, Plus is the upgrade. Business is not the default.

### Step 2: Pick a checkout and write its fee on the price

Gumroad's page has no monthly charge. Shopify's page does. Use the one you will still want in month four.

### Step 3: Record a three-minute setup

The recording is the support you do not have to repeat. Film fake data.

### Step 4: Count support hours on the first sales

A file that generates an hour of email per sale is priced too thin, or the page is unclear.

### Step 5: Add a second template only after a stranger finishes the first

Unsold duplicates are not an asset. They are hours.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of template income.**

Suppose the file is built on Notion Free, so the seat is $0. Suppose you sell through a direct Gumroad link, not Discover. Suppose the price is an assumed $29. That $29 is an assumption, not a market rate. Gumroad's direct fee is 10% + $0.50 USD. Ten percent of $29 is $2.90. The $0.50 USD is about $0.71 CAD at the rate in this guide, and the platform may not convert it that way. Fee sketch = $2.90 + $0.71 = $3.61. Net on one sale = $29 − $3.61 = $25.39, before your hours and before tax set-aside.

Suppose three people buy in a test month: 3 × $25.39 = $76.17. Suppose you spent twelve hours building and answering questions. The hour sketch is about $76.17 ÷ 12 = $6.35.

Suppose you also open Shopify Basic at the yearly figure, $37 CAD for the month, and you use it only for this file. Three sales at the Gumroad net do not cover a Shopify month, and you would not pay both checkouts on the same sale. One Shopify sale at the same assumed $29, with Basic card rates of 2.8% + 30¢, is a fee of about $1.11, so $29 − $1.11 − $37 = a loss of about $9 before your hours. The store does not pay for itself in this assumption. These totals are assumptions. Do not copy $29.

## Which tax rules apply to a Notion template budget in Canada?

Notion template income in Canada is income to record. When you are carrying on the activity for profit, self-employment income goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. A Notion subscription and platform fees are generally current expenses. A computer that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. GST/HST on your own taxable supplies uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Digital sales can also sit under the CRA's [digital-economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) pages when a platform is involved. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Gumroad's pricing page says it handles sales-tax collection as merchant of record. That sentence is Gumroad's description of its role. It does not, by itself, tell you whether your supplies count toward the small-supplier test. Keep payout reports. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over four consecutive quarters. Add other side hustles. Template sales are not ride-sharing.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, if you were a registrant and the receipt shows the tax. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST yourself and do not claim the credit. Confirm how a platform's collection interacts with your registration before you add tax a second time.

Once you must charge, use the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
