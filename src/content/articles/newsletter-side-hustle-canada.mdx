A newsletter side hustle in Canada is a regular email on one subject that earns from paid subscriptions, sponsorships, affiliate links or your own products. You can start free on Substack, beehiiv or Kit, Substack keeps 10% of paid subscriptions, and Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) requires opt-in consent before you send. Ottawa journalist Paul Wells says his $8-a-month Substack now covers two salaries; a smaller stock-analysis newsletter reported 18 paid subscribers and $1,982.83 CAD for 2025.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Platform costs (checked October 9, 2026):** Substack is free to publish and keeps 10% of paid subscriptions, plus Stripe fees. beehiiv has a free plan, then Lite at US$49 and Pro at US$95 a month billed yearly, with a 0% take on paid subscriptions. Kit is free up to 10,000 subscribers, and Creator is US$33 a month billed yearly.
- **Payment fees:** Stripe Canada charges 2.9% + CA$0.30 per domestic card payment, and Substack's page lists a 0.7% Stripe Billing fee on recurring payments.
- **Real numbers:** Paul Wells reported 39,054 total subscribers in April 2025, with paid up 30% year on year. A year later he had 40,446 total, with paid down 10%.
- **Law:** CASL requires consent, sender identification and a working unsubscribe in every commercial email.
- **Tax:** report income on T2125. GST/HST registration is required once you pass $30,000, and digital subscriptions sold to Canadians are generally taxable.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does a newsletter suit?

A newsletter suits people who can explain one subject well, week after week, and are patient enough to build a list over months. It fits around a day job because you choose the schedule.

It suits:

- **Professionals with niche knowledge:** trades, HR, real estate, nursing, software, policy.
- **Local experts:** a city's food scene, events, municipal politics or real estate.
- **Hobbyists with real depth:** fishing spots, board games, gardening zones, cycling routes.
- **Writers and journalists**; see [side hustles for writers](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-writers-canada/).
- **Creators** who want an audience no algorithm controls, alongside [YouTube](/side-hustles/youtube-side-hustle-canada/) or [TikTok and Reels](/side-hustles/tiktok-reels-side-hustle-canada/).

It doesn't suit anyone who needs income in the first few months or can't commit to a regular send. If you'd rather write for clients, see [freelance writing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada/) or [email marketing](/side-hustles/email-marketing-side-hustle-canada/) for businesses.

## How do newsletters make money?

| Model | How it pays | Best for | Typical starting point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid subscriptions | Readers pay monthly or yearly | Analysis, insider or local news, expertise | Free issues first, paid tier once there's an archive |
| Sponsorships | Brands pay per placement | Focused audiences brands want | A few thousand engaged subscribers |
| Ad networks | Platform sells ads into your issues | beehiiv users who want hands-off ads | Varies by platform and audience |
| Affiliate links | Commission on sales | Product-heavy niches (tools, gear, finance) | Any size, with disclosure |
| Own products | Templates, guides, courses, ebooks | Practical niches | A small engaged list |
| Services | Consulting, coaching, freelance work | Professional niches | The newsletter is the lead source |

Most successful small newsletters stack two or three models. Paid subscriptions give predictable income; sponsorships and products add spikes.

## Real Canadian newsletter numbers

These are publicly documented. Their results aren't typical.

**Paul Wells (Ottawa political journalist, Substack).** In his April 19, 2025 subscriber report, three years after his first post, Wells reported 39,054 subscribers (paid and free combined), up 69% in a year, with paid subscriptions up 30% year on year. He charged $8 a month or $80 a year, kept about half his work free, offered a "Next-Level Support" tier at up to $250 a year, and wrote that paid subscriptions covered two salaries, travel and equipment, and some commissioned freelance work. In his April 17, 2026 update he reported 40,446 total subscribers, up about 4%, with paid subscribers down 10% year on year. **Lesson:** growth can plateau even for a well-known writer, so add products (he added a podcast) and keep the free tier strong.

**The J. Nicholas (stock analysis, Substack, revenue in CAD).** In a January 1, 2026 annual update, writer Jacob B reported 1,508 subscribers, 18 paid subscribers and **$1,982.83 CAD** in 2025 revenue after shifting from sponsorships to a paid-subscription model. A related book brought in about $300 more. **Lesson:** a small, specific list can earn real money, but early paid conversion is a small percentage of the list.

## Worked math: what does a newsletter earn?

**Paid subscriptions on Substack**

Formula: paid subscribers × price − Substack 10% − Stripe fees.

**Illustrative.** Subscriber counts and the conversion rate are assumptions; the $8 price matches Wells's published price and the fees are Substack's and Stripe Canada's published rates.

| List size | Paid (assumed 3%) | Gross at $8/mo | Substack 10% | Stripe (2.9% + $0.30 + 0.7%) | Net per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 | 15 | $120 | $12.00 | $8.82 | **$99.18** |
| 2,000 | 60 | $480 | $48.00 | $35.28 | **$396.72** |
| 10,000 | 300 | $2,400 | $240.00 | $176.40 | **$1,983.60** |

Stripe is calculated per payment: each $8 charge costs $8 × 3.6% + $0.30 = $0.588. Annual plans reduce the per-payment $0.30.

**The same 2,000-subscriber list on beehiiv Lite.** No revenue share, so $480 − $35.28 Stripe = $444.72, minus the plan's US$49 × 1.4240 (Bank of Canada rate, October 8, 2026) = about $69.78 → **about $375 a month**. At this size Substack and beehiiv come out close; the gap widens in beehiiv's favour as paid revenue grows.

**Sponsorship (Illustrative).** If a sponsor pays an assumed $30 per 1,000 subscribers for one placement, a 5,000-subscriber weekly newsletter selling two placements a month earns 5 × $30 × 2 = $300 a month. Sponsors look at open and click rates, not just list size, so track both.

## How should you price a paid tier?

Substack's help page says readers pay "the monthly, annual, or founding member price the writer sets", and most platforms support the same three levels. Wells's published structure is a useful Canadian reference point: $8 a month, $80 a year (two months free), and a higher supporter tier of up to $250 a year.

| Tier | What it's for | Pricing approach |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Growth, trust, sponsors' reach | Keep your best evergreen pieces here |
| Monthly | Low-commitment trial | Your headline price |
| Annual | Better retention and fewer payment fees | About 10 × the monthly price |
| Founding or supporter | Superfans and businesses | 2–3 × the annual price |
| Group or team | Workplaces that expense subscriptions | Discount for 4+ seats |

What paid subscribers get should be obvious in one line: an extra weekly issue, full archive access, data tables, a community thread, or local listings. Avoid putting everything behind the paywall; free issues are your marketing.

## How do you grow a newsletter list?

| Channel | How it works | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Your existing network | Personal messages asking people to subscribe themselves | Low, one-time |
| Social clips | Short posts or videos that link to the signup | Ongoing |
| Recommendations and swaps | Newsletters of a similar size recommend each other | Low per swap |
| Referral rewards | Readers earn a perk for referring friends | Setup once |
| Guest issues and podcasts | Appear in front of another audience | Medium |
| Lead magnet | A free checklist, template or local guide for signing up | One-time build |
| Search | Publish issues on the web so they rank for specific questions | Long-term |
| Local partners | Coffee shops, gyms and community groups share your signup | Medium |

Pick two channels and do them every week rather than trying all eight at once.

## What should you measure?

| Metric | What it tells you | What to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| Subscribers added per week | Whether growth channels work | Double down on the channel that adds the most |
| Open rate | Subject lines and trust; inflated by some mail apps' privacy features | Compare against your own average, not others' |
| Click rate | Whether readers act; sponsors care about this most | Put one clear link near the top |
| Replies | Engagement and content ideas | Ask one question per issue |
| Paid conversion | Share of the list paying | Test what's behind the paywall before changing price |
| Churn | Paid subscribers who cancel each month | Survey cancellations; improve the paid-only issue |

Platform dashboards in Substack, beehiiv and Kit show all of these. Write your numbers in a simple spreadsheet each month so you can see trends.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

### Week 1: Define and set up

- **Day 1:** Write one sentence: "A weekly email for [reader] about [subject] so they can [outcome]."
- **Day 2:** Pick a platform (Substack for zero cost and discovery; beehiiv or Kit for growth tools and products).
- **Day 3:** Write the welcome email and an "About" page that says who it's for and how often you send.
- **Days 4–7:** Write three issues before you announce anything.

### Week 2: Launch to people who opted in

- Email or message friends and colleagues asking them to subscribe themselves (don't import contacts who didn't opt in).
- Post the first issue on LinkedIn and one community you belong to, following its rules.
- Add a signup link to your email signature and social bios.

### Week 3: Find the growth loop

- Ask every new subscriber one question in the welcome email ("What's your biggest question about X?").
- Turn the answers into issues.
- Swap recommendations with one newsletter of a similar size.

### Week 4: Review

- Check open and click rates on four issues.
- Keep the formats people click and reply to.
- Decide your first monetization test: an affiliate link, a $10 product or a paid tier after 12 free issues.

### Days 31–90

- **Weeks 5–8:** Send on the same day and time every week. Add a referral reward if your platform supports it.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Launch the paid tier or a small product; see [your first newsletter sale](/side-hustles/newsletter-first-sale-canada/).
- **Weeks 11–12:** Build a one-page sponsor kit (audience, open rate, rates) and pitch three relevant Canadian businesses.

## Workflow and templates

**Weekly issue workflow**

1. Collect ideas all week in one note.
2. Outline on day 1 (30 minutes): one main point, three supporting items, one call to action.
3. Draft on day 2 (2 hours).
4. Edit, add links and a subject line on day 3 (1 hour).
5. Schedule for the same time each week.
6. Share a snippet on one social platform.

**Issue template**

```
Subject: [Specific result or question] – [Newsletter name] #[N]

Hi [first name],
[One-paragraph hook: what changed this week and why it matters to you.]

1. The main thing: [300–600 words]
2. Quick hits: [3 links with one line each]
3. One question: [ask readers to reply]

[Sponsor or product mention, clearly labelled]
[Your name, mailing address, unsubscribe link]
```

**Sponsor pitch**

> Subject: Reaching [audience] in [city/province]
> Hi [name], I write [newsletter], a weekly email for [audience]. [X] subscribers, [Y]% average open rate, [Z]% in [province]. I'd like to offer [brand] a sponsored spot in [month]: one 80-word placement with a link, clearly labelled as sponsored. Rate: $[ ]. Recent issues: [link].

**One-page sponsor kit outline**

```
[Newsletter name]: a weekly email for [audience] in [region]
Subscribers: [X] | Average open rate: [Y]% | Average click rate: [Z]%
Readers: [top 3 job titles or interests], [share in Canada / your province]
Placements: Primary (top, 80–100 words + logo) $[ ] | Classified (bottom, 30 words) $[ ]
Package: 4 issues for the price of 3
Policy: all sponsored content is labelled "Sponsored"; we only accept products relevant to readers
Contact: [email] | Recent issues: [link]
```

**CASL checklist**

- [ ] Every subscriber opted in themselves (checkbox or signup form, not pre-checked)
- [ ] You can prove when and how each person consented
- [ ] Every email shows your name and a mailing address
- [ ] Unsubscribe link works and is processed within 10 business days
- [ ] Sponsored content is labelled

## What does CASL require?

Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation applies to commercial electronic messages, which covers most newsletters that sell, promote or include ads. The CRTC's [CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm) sums up the three requirements: **consent**, **identification** of the sender, and an **unsubscribe mechanism**. Express consent means the person took a positive action, such as checking an empty box or submitting a signup form, and the sender must be able to prove it. Asking for consent by email is itself a commercial message, so you can't email a bought or scraped list to "ask permission". ISED's [guidance on getting consent](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) and [section 6 of the Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/section-6.html) set out the details. Unsubscribe requests must be honoured within 10 business days.

This is not legal advice.

## Which tax rules apply to newsletter income?

Subscription, sponsorship, affiliate and product income is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net profit (QPP in Quebec).

This is not tax advice.

- **GST/HST:** registration is required once worldwide taxable supplies pass **$30,000** in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)); the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule doesn't apply.
- **Digital subscriptions:** once registered, subscriptions sold to Canadian readers are generally charged at the rate for the reader's province (13% HST in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia since April 1, 2025, 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I., 5% GST elsewhere plus QST in Quebec). See the CRA's [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html), [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and [digital economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) pages.
- **USD income:** convert platform payouts and USD sponsorships to Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada rate.
- **Deductions:** platform fees, Stripe fees, software, design tools and a share of home office and internet.

## Common newsletter mistakes

- **Importing contacts who didn't opt in.** That's a CASL problem and hurts deliverability.
- **Launching paid on an empty archive.** Readers need proof first.
- **Irregular sending.** Consistency builds open rates.
- **Too broad a topic.** "Money tips" competes with everyone; "money for Alberta trades apprentices" doesn't.
- **Ignoring replies.** Reader questions are your best content source.
- **Hiding sponsors.** Label paid placements clearly.
