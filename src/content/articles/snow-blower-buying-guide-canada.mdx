<QuickAnswer>

- **Short answer:** for paid driveways in most of Canada, a two-stage machine is the usual workhorse; single-stage units suit short, paved driveways with light snow, and three-stage units suit heavy, wet snow and windrows.
- **Key specs:** stages, clearing width, intake height, drive (self-propelled or not), electric start and power source.
- **Safety:** CCOHS says never put your hands down the chute; use a clearing tool with the machine off.
- **Prices:** see current price; this is a what-to-look-for guide, not a product review.

</QuickAnswer>

## What should you look for in a snow blower?

Match the machine to the snow you get and the driveways you will clear, especially the windrow the city plough leaves at the end.

| Spec | What it means | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stages | Single (auger throws snow), two (auger + impeller), three (adds an accelerator) | Two-stage for most paid work; three-stage for heavy, wet snow |
| Clearing width | Width of one pass | Wider is faster but harder to store and transport |
| Intake height | Deepest snow it takes in one pass | Matters for big storms and plough windrows |
| Drive | Self-propelled, with speed settings | Essential on two- and three-stage units |
| Start | Recoil, electric push-button, or battery | Electric start helps on cold mornings |
| Power | Gas or battery | Battery: quieter, less maintenance; check runtime against your route |
| Paved vs gravel | Skid shoes and scraper bar | Adjustable skid shoes keep gravel out of the auger |

## What should Canadian buyers know before buying?

- **Safety.** [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/landscaping/snow_throwers.html) says never put your hands or feet down the chute, turn the machine off and let parts stop before clearing a clog, and refuel outdoors with the engine cool.
- **Cold work.** Plan breaks and layers for long routes; see CCOHS on [working in the cold](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/cold/cold_working.html).
- **Used machines.** Check the model on [Recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en).
- **Taxes.** A snow blower used for business is generally claimed through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html).
