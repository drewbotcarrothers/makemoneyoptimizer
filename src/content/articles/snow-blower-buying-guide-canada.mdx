## What specs matter when you buy a snow blower for paid routes in Canada?

Stage count, clearing width, intake height and power source decide whether a machine survives a Canadian route. Single-stage machines throw snow with one auger that touches the pavement; two-stage machines use an auger plus an impeller, handle deeper and wetter snow, and can work on gravel.

| Spec | What it means | What to look for on a paid route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stages | Single-stage: auger throws snow. Two-stage: auger feeds an impeller | Two-stage for windrows, gravel and 20+ cm storms |
| Clearing width | Path per pass | 20–22 inches for walks; 24–28 inches for double driveways |
| Intake height | Deepest snow it takes in one pass | Two-stage units list 20 inches or more; single-stage battery units list 10–13 inches of clearing depth |
| Power | Corded, battery, or gas | Corded is cheapest but tethered; battery is quiet and starts in the cold; gas runs all night on a can |
| Drive | Push, auger-propelled, or self-propelled wheels | Self-propelled matters on long or sloped driveways |
| Chute | Rotation range and deflector control | 180° rotation lets you place snow where the client wants it |
| Extras | Electric start, headlights, heated grips | Headlights and electric start matter on 5 a.m. routes |

### How much battery do you need?

Battery runtime falls in the cold and with heavy snow, so match battery capacity to your longest stretch between charges. The kit listings make this easy to compare: the Greenworks PRO 80V kit comes with 2.0 Ah batteries, the EGO SNT2416 with two 10.0 Ah batteries. For a route of several driveways, a second battery or a vehicle charger plan matters more than a wider machine. Tool-only listings (EGO SNT2110, SNT2410, SNT2800) are cheaper only if you already own that brand's batteries.

## How fast does a snow blower pay for itself?

Divide the kit cost by what each paid visit leaves after salt and fuel. With the inputs below, the battery walks kit is covered by about 29 visits and the two-stage kits by 80 to 89 visits, which is roughly 2.4 versus 7 clients across a 12-visit winter.

**Illustrative example.** The $33 visit (a double driveway with walk and windrow at an assumed $40 an hour) and the $3 of salt are the labelled teaching example in [snow removal pricing](/side-hustles/snow-removal-pricing-canada/), as are the 12 visits in a "typical" winter; none are market averages or snowfall forecasts. The $2 of gas per visit for the gas machine is an assumption, and charging cost is treated as zero.

## What should Canadian buyers know before buying a snow blower?

Cold, storage, shipping time and safety rules shape the buy more than the spec sheet.

- **Buy before the first storm.** Two-stage machines arrive in large freight boxes and need assembly, oil and a test run. Order in October or November so the machine is ready before your first contracted visit.
- **Battery care.** Store batteries indoors at room temperature and charge them warm. Cold batteries deliver less runtime.
- **Gas care.** [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/landscaping/snow_throwers.html) says to start gas engines outside, refuel only when the engine is cool, use an approved container, and keep an extinguisher nearby. Let the unit adjust to outdoor temperature before clearing, and run it a few minutes after to prevent freeze-up.
- **Never clear a clog by hand.** CCOHS notes fingers have been amputated when the blade rotated unexpectedly. Shut down, remove the key, wait for parts to stop and use a tool.
- **Electric cords.** Use an outdoor extension cord rated for the machine, connected to GFCI protection ([CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/landscaping/snow_throwers.html)).
- **Cold work.** Plan breaks and layers per [CCOHS cold-work guidance](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/cold/cold_working.html).
- **Recalls.** Search the model on [Recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en), especially used machines from online classifieds.

## How do taxes treat a snow blower bought for a side hustle?

A snow blower used to earn business income is claimed through capital cost allowance on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). CRA's [classes of depreciable property](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html) page puts tools under $500 in Class 12 (100%) and tools of $500 or more in Class 8 (20%), so a cordless shovel is usually Class 12 while every blower above $500 is Class 8. Once your taxable sales pass $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters you must register for GST/HST ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)), and registrants can generally claim back the HST on gear as input tax credits. More in [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/). This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
