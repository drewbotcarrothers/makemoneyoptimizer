## How do you make a strong Rover profile in Canada?

### Step 1: Use a bright, friendly face photo

Rover's [photos help page](https://support-ca.rover.com/hc/en-ca/articles/360037388072-How-do-I-add-and-edit-photos-on-my-profile) says your profile photo appears next to your name in search and in messages. It recommends a clear, well-lit photo of your face at about 400x400 pixels. Rover's [profile tips](https://www.rover.com/blog/sitter-resources/how-to-create-the-best-headline-for-your-profile/) add: show a real smile, include an animal if you can, and avoid grainy or blurry shots.

### Step 2: Build a gallery that tells a story

Rover recommends at least four gallery photos of you walking dogs or caring for pets. The image in the first position displays largest, so lead with your best one. Add short, friendly captions. Keep photos evergreen: holiday decorations in a summer search make a profile look stale. Photos you send clients during stays can show on your profile automatically, and you can hide them.

### Step 3: Write a specific headline and experience section

"Dog lover" says nothing. Name what you can handle: senior dogs, puppies, large breeds, medication, cats. Rover's tips say to fill out your pet care experience completely. Describe a typical walk or visit, how you send updates, and any training or first aid you've done.

### Step 4: Be clear about preferences

Say which sizes, ages and needs you accept, and which you don't. Clear limits mean fewer mismatched requests and fewer declines.

### Step 5: Finish setup and the background check

Rover's [sign-up steps](https://support-ca.rover.com/hc/en-ca/articles/360037072752-How-do-I-become-a-pet-sitter-or-dog-walker-on-Rover) cover service setup (rates, availability, pet preferences), your profile, and a background check with identity verification for people in North America. After you submit, Rover says to expect a reply within 10 to 20 business days. Your services become visible once the profile is approved and the background check is passed.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch, not a real profile. Suppose you're setting up drop-in visits and walks in Halifax. Your profile photo would be you outdoors with a dog, smiling and in focus. Your gallery would open with a clear walking photo, followed by three more with captions. Your headline would read like "Calm walks for senior and large dogs; comfortable giving pills." Your preferences would say no puppies under six months, and you'd refresh one photo each season.

## How should Canadian Rover sitters set rates and get reviews?

Rover's [service fees page](https://support-ca.rover.com/hc/en-ca/articles/360037446911-What-are-the-service-fees) explains what Rover keeps from each booking. The [Rover review](/side-hustles/rover-review-canada/) summarizes the standard fee and the tiered pilot in some Canadian cities. Price after the fee, using [dog walking pricing](/side-hustles/dog-walking-pricing-canada/) for your floor. Ask happy clients for a review after each early booking, and keep your calendar current so you aren't asked for dates you can't do.

## What Rover profile mistakes do Canadian sitters make?

- **Blurry or dark photos.** Rover recommends a clear, well-lit face photo.
- **A thin gallery.** Rover suggests at least four images.
- **Generic headlines.** Specific skills earn the right requests.
- **Out-of-date availability.** It leads to declines that cost you.
