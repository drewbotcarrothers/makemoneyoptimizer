## How much does Marketplace flipping cost to start in Canada?

Marketplace flipping in Canada costs $0 when you sell your own items, $56.44 for testing and cleaning gear, $147.69 adding straps and moving blankets, or $262.83 adding a hand truck, gloves and furniture sliders. With an assumed float of $200 or $500 for the items themselves, the standard and pro starts are about $347.69 and $762.83. Gear includes 13% Ontario HST. Use your province's rate.

| Kit | What's in it | Gear with HST | With assumed float |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0 start | Your own unused items, your phone, a free account | $0 | $0 |
| Lean | KAIWEETS digital multimeter with case ($20.99) + JOREST 25-in-1 magnetic screwdriver ($15.99) + Mr. Clean Magic Eraser Extra Durable ($12.97) | $56.44 | $56.44 (sell your own items first) |
| Standard | Lean + Jack Boss ratchet straps, 1" × 15' ($26.99) + Amazon Basics moving blankets, 6-pack ($53.76) | $147.69 | about $347.69 with a $200 float |
| Pro | Standard + JUXANOS 500 lb folding hand truck ($59.99) + COOLJOB work gloves, 3 pairs ($18.99) + X-PROTECTOR felt furniture sliders, 16 pieces ($22.91) | $262.83 | about $762.83 with a $500 float |

The arithmetic: lean is ($20.99 + $15.99 + $12.97) = $49.95, × 1.13 = $56.44. Standard adds $26.99 + $53.76, so $130.70 × 1.13 = $147.69. Pro adds $59.99 + $18.99 + $22.91, so $232.59 × 1.13 = $262.83. The floats are assumptions, not recommendations, and a private seller who isn't registered for GST/HST doesn't charge it.

The float is different from the gear. Gear is spent. The float comes back when items sell, if you bought well. Set the float cap before you buy, not after.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for Marketplace flipping in Canada?

**Insurance.** No insurance is legally required to sell used items. Two questions are worth asking your own insurer: whether your home or tenant policy covers inventory you hold for resale, and whether your auto policy has anything to say about regular business trips. This guide does not state a premium. This is not insurance advice.

**Licence.** Some municipalities licence second-hand dealers or pawnbrokers. Search your city's bylaws if you plan to buy and sell at volume or from a fixed location. This guide does not state a licence fee.

**Safety rules.** Meta's [commerce policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce) prohibit recalled products and stolen goods. Search [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before reselling anything for children.

### A first-month sketch

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.**

Suppose you sell five of your own unused items for an assumed $150 in total, buying nothing. Cash in = $150. Suppose you then buy the lean kit for $56.44 and set a $100 float. You buy two small items at an assumed $35 each, use $3 of supplies on each, and drive an assumed $6 of fuel for each pair of trips. Each sells for an assumed $75.

Each flip nets $75 − $35 − $3 − $6 = $31. Two flips net $62, which covers the $56.44 lean kit with $5.56 left. The $70 spent on items came back in the sale prices, so the float is whole again. If one item needs a $25 part, that flip nets $6, the two flips net $37, and the kit isn't covered until the next flip. Do not copy $75.

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Fuel and time** for every pickup and every meetup. A no-show buyer is a trip with no sale.
- **Parts and supplies** on each item.
- **Price cuts** on items that sit. Items unsold after two or three weeks tie up the float. Lower prices before buying more.
- **Storage,** only if you choose to rent it.
- **Scams.** Facebook's [scams page](https://www.facebook.com/help/721562085854101/) says Purchase Protection covers checkout purchases only. Confirm money has arrived before an item leaves.

## Where can Marketplace flipping startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Sell your own items first.** It costs nothing and teaches prices.
- **Borrow tools** before you buy them.
- **Choose small items** that fit in any car, so straps and blankets can wait.
- **Batch pickups** on one route. Fuel is the cost most often forgotten.
- **Skip courses and bulk lots** of untested items until your log shows which flips work.

## Where should Marketplace flipping spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't cut testing tools, safe meetups, or the recall check. A $20.99 multimeter is cheaper than one dead battery. Meet in a public place, and bring someone for large items.

Don't cut the purchase log. It's the record for taxes and the only way to know which categories actually make money.

## Which tax rules apply to a Marketplace flipping budget in Canada?

Marketplace flipping income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase records, sale records, supply receipts, and a mileage log. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Flipping items on Marketplace is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and small tools used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

Selling your own used belongings now and then is a different question from buying items to resell. If you are unsure which one you are doing, ask a tax adviser.
