<QuickAnswer>

- **The rate:** self-employed Canadians pay both halves of CPP, 11.9% of net self-employment earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026, plus 8% CPP2 on earnings from $74,600 to $85,000.
- **The maximum:** $8,460.90 of regular CPP plus $832 of CPP2 for a self-employed person in 2026.
- **If you have a job too:** your T4 earnings use up the $3,500 exemption and count toward the ceilings first, so side income above $85,000 of combined earnings adds no CPP.
- **Rule of thumb:** below the ceiling, each extra $1,000 of net side profit costs $119 in CPP before income tax.
- **Partly given back:** half of base CPP is a non-refundable credit (line 31000); the rest is deducted from income (line 22200).

</QuickAnswer>

CPP on side hustle income in Canada is the Canada Pension Plan contribution you owe on net self-employment profit when you file your return, and for 2026 it is 11.9% of net earnings between the $3,500 basic exemption and $74,600, because you pay both the employee and employer shares ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/canada-pension-plan-cpp-employment-insurance-ei-rulings/cpp-ei-explained/employees-self-employed-workers-responsibilities-benefits-entitlements.html)). It surprises many first-year side hustlers because no platform or client withholds it. This guide covers every Canadian outside Quebec; Quebec residents pay QPP instead (see below). Figures are from CRA, ESDC and Retraite Québec pages checked November 3, 2026.

## Do you pay CPP on side hustle income in Canada?

Yes, if you are 18 or older, live outside Quebec, and your net self-employment earnings (plus any employment earnings) are above the $3,500 basic exemption. You calculate it on Schedule 8 when you file, and it is added to your balance owing ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/canada-pension-plan-cpp-employment-insurance-ei-rulings/cpp-ei-explained/employees-self-employed-workers-responsibilities-benefits-entitlements.html)).

"Net" matters. CPP is charged on the profit from Form [T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), after business expenses, not on gross sales or app payouts. Every legitimate deduction you claim (see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/)) lowers both your income tax and your CPP.

Self-employed people do not pay EI premiums unless they opt in to EI special benefits ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/canada-pension-plan-cpp-employment-insurance-ei-rulings/cpp-ei-explained/employees-self-employed-workers-responsibilities-benefits-entitlements.html)).

## How much CPP do you pay on side hustle income in 2026?

If side hustle profit is your only earned income, it is 11.9% of net profit above $3,500, up to $74,600, then 8% CPP2 on the slice up to $85,000. The formula:

```text
CPP (self-employed only, 2026) =
  11.9% × (net profit − $3,500), for net profit up to $74,600
+ 8% × (net profit − $74,600), for the part between $74,600 and $85,000
```

| Net self-employment profit (2026, no job) | CPP (11.9%) | CPP2 (8%) | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $3,000 | $0.00 | $0.00 | $0.00 |
| $5,000 | $178.50 | $0.00 | $178.50 |
| $10,000 | $773.50 | $0.00 | $773.50 |
| $20,000 | $1,963.50 | $0.00 | $1,963.50 |
| $30,000 | $3,153.50 | $0.00 | $3,153.50 |
| $50,000 | $5,533.50 | $0.00 | $5,533.50 |
| $74,600 | $8,460.90 | $0.00 | $8,460.90 |
| $85,000 or more | $8,460.90 | $832.00 | $9,292.90 |

Every row is the formula above applied to the CRA's 2026 figures.

## How does a T4 job change CPP on your side hustle?

Your employment earnings come first. They use up the $3,500 basic exemption and count toward the $74,600 and $85,000 ceilings, and your employer has already paid its half on them. Schedule 8 then charges the self-employed rate on the side income that falls into whatever room is left ([Schedule 8](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/5000-s8/5000-s8-lp-25e.pdf)). Your T4 boxes 16, 16A, 17 and 17A show what you already contributed ([CRA line 22200](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/line-22200-deduction-cpp-qpp-contributions-on-self-employment-other-earnings.html)).

### Illustrative example: four people, same $10,000 side profit

Assumed salaries; the side profit is $10,000 net in each case. Simplified to the rates above (Schedule 8 is the final word).

| Situation (2026) | How the $10,000 falls | CPP on the side profit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No job | $3,500 exempt, $6,500 at 11.9% | $773.50 |
| $40,000 salary | All $10,000 below the YMPE, exemption already used, 11.9% | $1,190.00 |
| $70,000 salary | $4,600 at 11.9% (to $74,600) + $5,400 at 8% (to $80,000) | $979.40 |
| $80,000 salary | All $5,000 between $80,000 and $85,000 at 8%; the rest is above the ceiling | $400.00 |
| $90,000 salary | Already above $85,000 | $0.00 |

Two things stand out. Someone with a mid-range salary pays **more** CPP on the same side profit than someone with no job, because their exemption is already used. And once salary plus side profit passes $85,000, extra side profit carries no CPP at all.

## How much of each side hustle dollar do you keep after CPP?

Below the $74,600 ceiling, each extra $1,000 of net profit costs $119 in CPP before any income tax. Between $74,600 and $85,000 it costs $80. Above $85,000 of combined earnings, $0.

**Illustrative example: a $12,000 side hustle with no other job.** Assume $15,000 of gross sales and $3,000 of expenses.

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Gross sales (assumed) | $15,000.00 |
| Business expenses (assumed) | −$3,000.00 |
| Net profit (T2125) | $12,000.00 |
| CPP: 11.9% × ($12,000 − $3,500) | −$1,011.50 |
| Income tax set-aside: an assumed 15% of net | −$1,800.00 |
| **Kept** | **$9,188.50** |

The income-tax line depends on your province and other income; [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) shows how to size it. CPP is the part people forget, and at 11.9% it is often as large as the federal income tax on a small side hustle.

## Do you get any of your CPP contributions back?

Partly, as tax relief, and fully as future pension. On Schedule 8, half of your **base** contribution (9.9% of the 11.9%) becomes a non-refundable credit on line 31000, and the other half of base, plus all of the **first additional** (2%) and **CPP2** (8%) contributions, is deducted from income on line 22200 ([Schedule 8](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/5000-s8/5000-s8-lp-25e.pdf)).

**Worked example ($10,000 net profit, no job):**

```text
Base CPP:            9.9% × $6,500 = $643.50
First additional:    2.0% × $6,500 = $130.00
Total CPP payable:                    $773.50

Line 31000 credit base:  50% × $643.50          = $321.75
Line 22200 deduction:    $321.75 + $130.00      = $451.75
```

The deduction is worth your marginal tax rate times $451.75, and the credit is worth the lowest federal rate (plus the provincial equivalent) times $321.75. So the real after-tax cost of CPP is lower than the 11.9% headline, but not by much on a small side hustle.

The contributions also count toward your CPP retirement pension, the way contributions through a job do. ESDC publishes the year's pensionable earnings figures and maximum benefits ([ESDC](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/pensions/pension/statistics/2026-quarterly-october-december.html)).

## How does QPP work on side hustle income in Quebec?

Quebec residents pay QPP instead of CPP. For 2026, a self-employed worker pays 12.6% (10.6% base plus 2% additional) on earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, then 8% on earnings from $74,600 to $85,000 ([Retraite Québec](https://www.retraitequebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/programs/quebec-pension-plan/work-contributions); [Revenu Québec](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/citizens/income-tax-return/paying-a-balance-due-or-receiving-a-refund/paying-contributions-and-premiums/contributions-and-premiums-payable-by-a-self-employed-person-or-a-member-of-a-partnership/qpp-contribution-payable-by-a-self-employed-person-or-a-member-of-a-partnership/)). Retraite Québec's table lists $819 on $10,000 and $3,339 on $30,000 of self-employment income. Quebec self-employed workers also pay QPIP premiums, calculated on the Quebec return.

## How do you plan cash for CPP on a side hustle?

Treat CPP as a second set-aside, separate from income tax, and move it out of your spending account every time you are paid.

### Step 1: Estimate this year's net profit

Use last year's T2125 or your year-to-date bookkeeping. A [simple record-keeping system](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) makes this a five-minute job.

### Step 2: Check your job's T4 earnings

If your salary is above $85,000, your side CPP is $0. Between $74,600 and $85,000, use 8%. Below $74,600 (and above $3,500), use 11.9%.

### Step 3: Set your CPP percentage

```text
My CPP set-aside rule (2026)
- Salary this year: $______
- If salary ≥ $85,000 → CPP set-aside: 0%
- If $74,600 ≤ salary < $85,000 → 8% of each side payment
- If salary < $74,600 → 11.9% of each side payment
- No job? 11.9% of profit above $3,500
```

### Step 4: Move it with every deposit

### Step 5: Check whether you need instalments

CPP owing counts toward the net tax owing that can trigger CRA quarterly instalment reminders. Read [CRA tax instalments for side hustlers](/side-hustles/cra-tax-instalments-side-hustle-canada/).

## What mistakes do side hustlers make with CPP in Canada?

- **Forgetting it exists.** No platform withholds CPP; it all lands on your return.
- **Using gross income.** CPP is charged on net profit after expenses.
- **Assuming a job covers it.** It only does if your salary is above the $85,000 ceiling.
- **Mixing it with the tax set-aside.** Two percentages, two lines on the return.
- **Missing the deduction.** Good tax software fills Schedule 8 and line 22200 for you; compare [Wealthsimple Tax, TurboTax and H&R Block](/side-hustles/wealthsimple-tax-vs-turbotax-vs-hr-block-canada/).

## What should you keep in mind about these CPP numbers?

All rates and ceilings are the CRA's 2026 figures, checked November 3, 2026; they change every January. The salary examples simplify Schedule 8, which also handles part-year situations (turning 18 or 70, CPP disability, death) and people aged 60 to 70 who are receiving CPP and may elect to stop contributing. Income-tax set-asides are placeholders. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
