Instacart and Uber Eats both pay Canadian grocery shoppers per order or batch, plus 100% of tips, but they suit different people. Instacart is grocery-first, asks for a reliable car and the ability to lift 18 kg, mails you a payment card, and builds batch pay from the expected effort, including heavy items. Uber Eats mixes grocery orders with restaurant deliveries in one app, allows bike delivery in some cities, and has you pay with a pre-authorized Plus Card or your own card and get reimbursed. Pick Instacart if you have a car and like shopping full lists; pick Uber Eats if you want grocery orders as one option among restaurant runs, or you deliver by bike.

This is a comparison. ## How is grocery shopper pay calculated on Instacart and Uber Eats in Canada?

Instacart publishes more about how a grocery batch is priced than Uber does, but neither publishes an hourly wage for Canadian shoppers outside the Ontario and B.C. floors. Judge each offer on the store, the item count, and the drive.

### Instacart batch pay

Instacart's [shopper earnings page](https://company.instacart.ca/shoppers/shopper-earnings) says batch pay reflects the expected effort: travel to the store and customer, the number and weight of items, and the expected shopping time. Batch pay can rise if the customer adds items before checkout. Heavy pay is at least $2 and applies when items weighing 8 lb or more add up to 50 lb or more. Boosts are added when there are not enough shoppers nearby. The page also describes a peak earning times chart that shows which hours pay more per batch, up to a week ahead.

If a customer zeroes out a tip after delivery without reporting an issue, Instacart says it covers the removed amount up to $10. Weekly payments arrive between Wednesday and Friday for the previous Monday to Sunday, and instant cash-out costs $0.50.

### Uber Eats grocery offers

Uber's [Toronto grocery page](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/e/deliver/grocery/toronto-on-ca/) says you choose which orders to fulfil, keep 100% of tips, and can cash out up to five times a day with Instant Pay. Uber's [main delivery page](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/deliver/) says you see what an order pays before you accept it. Uber does not publish a grocery-specific formula on those pages, so the offer screen is the number to judge.

### Ontario and B.C. floors

Both apps operate under Ontario's [digital platform workers rules](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers), which require at least the general minimum wage, determined for each work assignment performed or on a pay period basis, and B.C.'s [platform worker minimum](https://gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) of $21.89 per hour of engaged time as of June 1, 2026, plus at least $0.35 per km for delivery. Tips do not count, and time between orders is not engaged time.

## How should you compare grocery apps with your own numbers?

Compare Instacart and Uber Eats in Canada by logging the same weekend window on each: gross pay, tips, minutes inside the store, hours online, and kilometres. Grocery batches can look large and take a long time.

### Illustrative example

Suppose a shopper logs a Saturday morning on each app. On app A: two batches, $34 in batch pay, $22 in tips, 2.5 hours, 28 km. On app B: one grocery order and two restaurant runs, $38 in offers, $15 in tips, 2.5 hours, 35 km. Suppose the shopper's own vehicle cost is $0.30 per km.

- App A: $34 + $22 = $56. 28 × $0.30 = $8.40. $56 − $8.40 = $47.60, or $19.04 per hour.
- App B: $38 + $15 = $53. 35 × $0.30 = $10.50. $53 − $10.50 = $42.50, or $17 per hour.

These are assumed numbers for teaching, not a quote, an average, or a typical result. A different week or city could reverse the result. That is why the log beats any article.

## What mistakes do grocery shoppers make when choosing an app in Canada?

Grocery shoppers in Canada most often underestimate time in the store, accept heavy batches without the right vehicle, or front grocery money they cannot wait to get back.

- **Ignoring store time.** A 40-item batch in a busy store can take longer than the drive.
- **Taking water and pet food on a small car or bike.** Instacart flags heavy pay; Uber shows the order before you accept. Decline what you cannot carry safely.
- **Paying with your own card without tracking it.** Keep the receipt and confirm the reimbursement.
- **Skipping the insurance call.** Delivering is business use of the car.

## Which tax rules apply to grocery shoppers in Canada?

Grocery shopping income from Instacart or Uber Eats is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). For GST/HST, grocery delivery follows the CRA's [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the passenger-ride rule.

The groceries you buy for a customer are not your income or your expense; your pay and tips are income. If you use both apps, add them together.
