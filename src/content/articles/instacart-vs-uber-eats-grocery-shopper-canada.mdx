## How is grocery shopper pay calculated on Instacart and Uber Eats in Canada?

Instacart publishes more about how a grocery batch is priced than Uber does, but neither publishes an hourly wage for Canadian shoppers. Outside the Ontario and B.C. floors, the batch or offer screen is the only number you get.

### Instacart batch pay

Instacart's [shopper earnings page](https://company.instacart.ca/shoppers/shopper-earnings) says batch pay reflects the total expected effort: travel to the store and the customer, the number and weight of items, and the expected shopping time. It can rise if the customer adds items before checkout. Two add-ons sit inside batch pay:

- **Heavy pay:** at least $2, when items weighing 8 lb or more add up to 50 lb or more in the batch. A "heavy pay" tag shows on the batch screen before you accept.
- **Boosts:** extra pay when there are not enough shoppers nearby, which can mean the batch is farther away.

Promotions are added on top when customer demand is high, sometimes for an individual shopper. Before you accept, the batch card shows the store, delivery distance, number of items and units, and the batch earnings with the tip. Instacart says you are never penalized for declining a batch.

Instacart's peak earning times chart, which shows which hours pay more per batch up to a week ahead, is **not available in Ontario or B.C.**, according to the same page. Older guides that tell Ontario shoppers to plan around it are out of date.

### Uber Eats grocery offers

Uber's Toronto grocery page says you choose which orders to fulfil and keep 100% of tips. The national page says you see what an order pays before you accept it, and Uber's fare guide lists estimated time, distance, waiting at the merchant, multiple stops and demand as inputs. Uber does not publish a grocery-specific formula, so judge the offer on the store, the item count and the drive.

### Ontario and B.C. floors

Both apps operate under the same provincial floors.

- **Ontario.** The [Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers) requires at least the general minimum wage, [$17.95 an hour from October 1, 2026](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/minimum-wage), for each work assignment or on a pay period basis. Tips do not count.
- **British Columbia.** The province's [minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) sets $21.89 per hour of engaged time from June 1, 2026, for workers who pick up online orders and deliver them. Engaged time runs from accepting an order to completing or cancelling it, so shopping time inside the store counts. Delivery workers also get at least $0.35 per km of engaged travel by any means other than walking. Tips do not count, and waiting between orders is not engaged time.

## Which costs less to cash out, Instacart or Uber Eats?

Instacart's same-day cash-out costs less: $0.50 against Uber's $1.49 for Instant Pay. Both pay weekly for free, and Uber also offers Flex Pay once a day for $0.85.

**Illustrative. Cashing out after every shift, five shifts a week, is an assumption.**

| Habit | Instacart | Uber Eats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 cash-outs a week | 5 × $0.50 = $2.50 | 5 × $1.49 = $7.45 (Instant Pay) or 5 × $0.85 = $4.25 (Flex Pay) |
| Over 52 weeks | $130.00 | $387.40, or $221.00 on Flex Pay |
| Weekly payout only | $0 | $0 |

Instacart releases batch earnings for cash-out within minutes of delivery and the full amount, including the tip, 2 hours after delivery. Use the free weekly payout whenever you can wait.

## How does insurance differ between Instacart and Uber Eats in Canada?

Uber publishes commercial auto coverage for delivery by province. Instacart's Canadian shopper pages read for this comparison describe none. Either way, your own auto policy must stay valid, and delivering is business use of the car, so tell your insurer before your first batch.

| Province | Instacart | Uber Eats (car) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario | No auto coverage described on Instacart's Canadian pages; confirm with your insurer | Economical commercial policy: $1,000,000 third-party liability while waiting for a request, $2,000,000 from accepting to drop-off, and contingent collision and comprehensive with a $2,500 deductible if your own policy carries them ([Uber insurance](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/deliver/insurance/)) |
| Alberta | Same | Your personal policy applies first while waiting, with $1,000,000 back-up; $2,000,000 liability plus collision and comprehensive ($2,500 deductible) once a request is accepted |
| British Columbia | Same; WorkSafeBC covers app-based delivery workers for injuries, per the province's [overview](https://engage.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/121/2026/07/Overview-of-Minimum-Standards-and-Protections-for-App-based-Workers.pdf) | Uber says most basic ICBC policies allow up to 6 days a month of commercial use; beyond that you need business coverage |
| Quebec and Atlantic Canada | Same | Commercial coverage of $1,000,000 civil liability in Quebec and $2,000,000 in the Atlantic provinces once a request is accepted |

This is the clearest practical difference for a car shopper. An Uber Eats grocery order in Ontario has Uber's commercial policy behind it from the moment you go online. An Instacart batch relies on whatever your own insurer agrees to. Get the answer in writing. This is not insurance advice. ## How much could you keep on Instacart versus Uber Eats?

Compare the two apps with the same weekend window on each, after kilometres. Grocery batches pay more per order but take longer, and the kilometre cost often decides the winner.

For the per-km cost, use your own fuel, insurance, maintenance and depreciation if you track them. If not, the CRA's [2026 reasonable allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/benefits-allowances/automobile/automobile-motor-vehicle-allowances.html) of $0.73 per km for the first 5,000 business km is a full-cost benchmark. It is the rate an employer can pay tax-free for an employee's own car, not a deduction you claim. A fuel-type cost is lower, so the example shows both.

### Illustrative example: one Saturday morning on each app

**Assumed numbers for teaching, not a quote, an average or a typical result.**

On Instacart: two batches, $34 in batch pay, $22 in tips, 2.5 hours, 28 km. On Uber Eats: one grocery order and two restaurant runs, $38 in offers, $15 in tips, 2.5 hours, 35 km.

| | Instacart | Uber Eats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pay + tips | $56.00 | $53.00 |
| Kilometres | 28 | 35 |
| Net at an assumed $0.35/km | $46.20 ($18.48/hr) | $40.75 ($16.30/hr) |
| Net at the CRA's $0.73/km | $35.56 ($14.22/hr) | $27.45 ($10.98/hr) |

The arithmetic: Instacart $56 − (28 × $0.35 = $9.80) = $46.20, and $46.20 ÷ 2.5 = $18.48. Uber Eats $53 − (35 × $0.73 = $25.55) = $27.45, and $27.45 ÷ 2.5 = $10.98. The 7 extra kilometres on the restaurant runs cost more than the $3 difference in pay. A different week, store or city could reverse the result.

### Illustrative example: the B.C. and Ontario floors on one batch

**Same caveat: the batch pay, time and distance are assumptions.**

A shopper accepts an Instacart full-service batch, shops and delivers it in 1 hour 15 minutes of engaged time, drives 14 km, and the batch pay is $24 plus a $10 tip.

- **B.C.:** 1.25 × $21.89 = $27.36, plus 14 × $0.35 = $4.90, for a floor of $32.26. The $24 batch pay is $8.26 short, so the platform owes a top-up of $8.26. The $10 tip does not count toward the floor.
- **Ontario:** 1.25 × $17.95 = $22.44. The $24 batch pay is above it, so no top-up.

The same method applies to an Uber Eats grocery order: engaged time and engaged kilometres from accepting to drop-off. Long store times are where the B.C. floor matters most, because the in-store minutes count and the per-order pay may not reflect them.

## What mistakes do grocery shoppers make when choosing an app in Canada?

Grocery shoppers in Canada most often underestimate time in the store, accept heavy batches without the right vehicle, or front grocery money they cannot wait to get back.

- **Ignoring store time.** A 40-item batch in a busy store can take longer than the drive. Log minutes inside the store separately.
- **Taking water and pet food on a small car or bike.** Instacart flags heavy pay; Uber shows the order before you accept. Decline what you cannot carry safely.
- **Paying with your own card without tracking it.** Keep the receipt and confirm the reimbursement on Uber Eats.
- **Paying for daily cash-outs by habit.** Five Instant Pay transfers a week is $387.40 a year.
- **Skipping the insurance call.** Delivering is business use of the car on either app.

## Which tax rules apply to grocery shoppers in Canada?

Grocery shopping income from Instacart or Uber Eats is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). For GST/HST, grocery delivery follows the CRA's [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the passenger-ride rule.

The groceries you buy for a customer are not your income or your expense; your batch pay, offers and tips are income. If you use both apps, add them together for the $30,000 test. Cash-out fees and the business share of vehicle costs are expenses, which is why a kilometre log matters. Platform reporting and slips are covered in [tax on reselling and gig apps](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/), and the log in [vehicle expenses for delivery and rideshare](/side-hustles/vehicle-expenses-delivery-rideshare-canada/).
