A first affiliate sale in Canada usually comes from one genuinely useful page, video, or email that helps a reader choose between products they already intend to buy, with links from a programme whose terms you have read, and a disclosure the reader cannot miss. Commissions follow purchases, so the work is helping someone decide, not pushing links.

Canada's Competition Bureau treats commissions as material connections, and Google's spam policies cover thin affiliate pages. ## What should you promote for a first affiliate sale in Canada?

Promote products you understand, in a niche you already know, from retailers that ship to Canada, for a first affiliate sale. A Canadian reader who clicks through to a store that does not ship to them will not buy, and the commission never happens.

Read each programme's terms before you link: which countries count, how long the tracking window lasts, what is excluded, and whether you may use links in email or on social media. Some programmes forbid links in emails. Some forbid paid ads on their brand name.

## Where do Canadians find a first affiliate buyer?

Canadians find a first affiliate buyer through a search-friendly comparison page, a video that shows the product in use, or a newsletter whose readers asked for recommendations. Posting bare links in groups or comment sections is usually against the group's rules and rarely converts.

Google's [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) say appearing in results costs nothing and meeting requirements does not guarantee indexing. If you email links, the message is commercial: ISED's [anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) and its [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page require consent, identification, and an unsubscribe mechanism. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first affiliate page say?

A first affiliate page in Canada should answer the reader's question in the first paragraph, explain how you compared options, show Canadian prices with dates, and disclose the affiliate relationship before the first link.

**Example disclosure and opening.** This is a generic example. It is not a page that earned a commission.

> This page contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, this site may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. For a small Toronto balcony, a collapsible two-burner propane stove is usually the most practical choice; below are four options compared by weight, burner output, and price on Canadian retailer pages checked on [date].

## How do you build affiliate trust without fake reviews?

You build affiliate trust in Canada by saying exactly how you compared products, citing the pages you used, admitting downsides, and never claiming hands-on use you did not have. A recommendation that names the product's weak point is more believable than one that does not.

The Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says reviews must be based on actual experience and that a tag, discount code, or affiliate link alone is unlikely to be adequate disclosure. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe thin affiliate pages that copy merchant descriptions without adding value.

## How should you think about first affiliate income in Canada?

Think about first affiliate income in Canada as a commission rate times purchases that actually qualify, minus returns, paid on the programme's schedule. This guide does not state typical commission rates or conversion rates; read them in each programme's terms.

### Illustrative example

Suppose a comparison page sends 40 clicks in a month, three readers buy a $90 product, and the programme pays an assumed 5% commission. That is 3 × $90 × 5% = $13.50. If one buyer returns the item and the programme reverses that commission, it becomes 2 × $90 × 5% = $9.00. If the page's share of hosting is an assumed $5 a month, what is left is $4.00 before tax. These figures are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first affiliate sale look like?

A 14-day plan for a first affiliate sale in Canada should produce one approved programme, one useful comparison with a clear disclosure, and a way for readers to find it. A first commission inside 14 days is not guaranteed.

### Days 1 and 2: Read the rules

Read the Competition Bureau's influencer page and Google's spam policies. Write your disclosure sentence.

### Days 3 to 5: Pick and apply

Choose one niche and one or two programmes that ship to Canada. Read their terms and apply.

### Days 6 to 9: Build the comparison

Compare three to five products by the specifications a buyer cares about. Date every price. Name the downsides.

### Days 10 to 14: Publish and share

Publish with the disclosure near the top. Share it where you already participate and where sharing is allowed, or send it to subscribers who opted in.

## What mistakes block a first affiliate sale in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first affiliate sale in Canada are promoting products that do not ship here, burying the disclosure, and publishing thin pages copied from merchants. Each one costs the sale, the reader's trust, or search visibility.

Breaking a programme's rules on email or paid ads can close the account and forfeit commissions. Claiming you used a product you did not is a false review. Spamming groups with links gets you removed.

## Which tax rules apply to a first affiliate sale in Canada?

Affiliate income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep programme statements, payout records, and hosting or tool receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Earning affiliate commissions is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
