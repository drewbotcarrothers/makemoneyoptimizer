Charging for no-code web design in Canada usually means a fixed project price based on pages and features, a deposit before work starts, hourly billing for changes outside scope, and an optional monthly care plan, with the website builder subscription and domain paid by the client. Job Bank lists employed web designers at $20.00 (low), $33.65 (median), and $57.69 (high) an hour nationally. A freelancer prices above that employee wage because software, unpaid sales calls, revisions, and both halves of CPP come out of the same rate. ## Which pricing models work for no-code web design in Canada?

No-code web design in Canada is usually priced as a fixed project by pages and features, hourly for changes, a monthly care plan, or a value-based price for sites tied to revenue.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed project | Price for a defined set of pages, features, and revision rounds | Most small-business sites | Vague content responsibilities |
| Hourly | Rate × hours | Changes outside scope, small fixes | Clients who expect fixed totals |
| Care plan | Monthly fee for set hours of updates, checks, and backups | Clients who will not touch the site | Unlimited "small changes" |
| Value-based | Price tied to what the site is worth to the client | Booking or sales sites | Needs a strong case for value |
| Template customisation | Lower fixed price on a template with limited changes | Tight budgets | Custom requests at template prices |

Write who supplies copy and photos, the revision rounds, the deposit, what happens if content is late, and that the client owns the builder account and domain.

## How do you calculate a no-code web design rate in Canada?

Calculate a no-code web design rate in Canada from a target hourly pay, your billable share, software costs, and the hours a project takes (discovery, design, building, content entry, revisions, launch). Then turn hours into a fixed price.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

The Job Bank table is your floor check.

### Step 2: Estimate billable share

Sales calls, proposals, and learning builder updates are unpaid.

### Step 3: Add software

Your own builder plan for demos, fonts, stock photos, and design tools, divided across projects.

### Step 4: Estimate project hours

Break the site into pages and features, and add time for revisions and launch.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $45 an hour, between Job Bank's national median of $33.65 and high of $57.69. Suppose 70% of your working time is billable. The billable rate is $45 ÷ 0.70 = $64.29, which you might round to $64 an hour.

Suppose a five-page site takes 3 hours of discovery, 14 hours of design and building, 3 hours of revisions, and 2 hours of launch and training: 22 hours. The fixed price is 22 × $64 = $1,408, which you might quote as $1,400. A 50% deposit is $700 before work starts.

A care plan with 2 hours of updates and checks a month is 2 × $64 = $128, quoted as $130 a month. The client pays the website builder plan and domain directly, so those are not in your price.

## When should you raise no-code web design prices in Canada?

Raise no-code web design prices in Canada when your calendar books out, when your portfolio covers the client's industry, or when projects keep running over their hours. Change new quotes first; keep signed projects at their agreed price.

Compare quoted hours with tracked hours after every project.

## What no-code web design pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common no-code web design pricing mistakes in Canada are unlimited revisions, starting without a deposit, and doing the client's copywriting for free. Each pushes a fixed price below your rate.

Cap revisions. Take a deposit. Price copywriting and photo sourcing separately, or require the client to supply them by a date.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect no-code web design prices in Canada?

No-code web design income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

When registered, the rate depends on the place of supply, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers registration.
