## Who is grocery shopping a good fit for in Canada?

Grocery shopping in Canada fits organized people who can lift and carry, who have a car with a clean trunk, and who can work weekday evenings, Saturday mornings, and the day before a holiday. Grocery shopping is a poor fit when heavy cases are unrealistic, when an out-of-stock item causes panic, or when the car is too small for a family shop. Grocery shopping on an independent app is also a poor fit when the income has to be the same every week.

Some grocery services hire employees with scheduled shifts. Grocery shopping on independent shopper apps does not promise hours. Instacart is the clearest national example. Other apps come and go by city. If the shopper app shows no stores near you, grocery shopping is not available there, no matter what a headline says.

## How should grocery shopping pay be judged in Canada?

Grocery shopping pay in Canada is the batch amount on the screen before you accept, plus any tip that is still there after delivery, minus fuel, parking, and time. Some customers tip in the app, some add cash, and some tip nothing on a heavy order with stairs. The guide does not state an hourly wage for grocery shopping in Canada.

Price the round trip before you accept a batch because the tip preview looks large and the address is across the city. After a week, divide what you kept, after fuel and a car allowance, by hours from the moment you headed to the store until you parked at home. That includes the batch you waited on and did not get.

What moves the net is item count, how many items are out of stock, how well you know the store, heavy items such as water and pet food, distance and parking at both ends, apartment codes, weather, and how many other shoppers are online in that store.

## How do you start grocery shopping in Canada?

Starting grocery shopping in Canada means confirming stores on the shopper app, telling the insurer about paid grocery delivery in your province, and taking a small batch from a store you have walked. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: See whether stores near you are actually on the app

Open the shopper signup, not the customer app. A long customer delivery zone does not mean they are hiring shoppers. If Instacart has no stores you can reach, check once for a local alternative, then pick a different hustle. Do not drive an hour to a store just to try.

### Step 2: Tell your insurer you will deliver paid grocery orders

Personal policies often exclude carrying goods for hire. Ask about waiting in the lot, the drive, and the walk to the door. [Uber delivery driver insurance in Canada](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/deliver/insurance/) has described many basic ICBC policies in British Columbia as allowing up to six days a month of commercial use. That line is about Uber delivery, and it is Uber’s description, not a promise that a grocery app covers the car. For Instacart, assume your own policy has to allow grocery shopping until the app shows you a certificate that says otherwise. Read that certificate if it exists. The phone-call detail lives in the [food delivery guide](/side-hustles/food-delivery-side-hustle-canada/).

### Step 3: Apply with the details the signup shows

Use the identity and payout details the Shopper signup shows in the app. Confirm any background-check step the app shows. The name on the payout details should match the name on the identity details. Checks are not instant. Shop your own kitchen while you wait so the first customer list is not the first time you have compared brands.

### Step 4: Walk one store as practice

Before you are live, walk the store you will use most. Find produce, dairy, frozen, pharmacy, and the customer-service desk. Notice which aisles have no phone signal. That walk is free training.

### Step 5: Pack the car for food safety

Insulated bag upright, totes folded, a cart if you bought one, paper towels, and a way to keep raw meat away from produce. In winter, a hot car trunk is not a fridge. In summer, a closed trunk is not a freezer. Move frozen food last in the store and first into the insulated bag.

### Step 6: Take small batches first

Your first orders should be short lists from the store you walked. Learn the substitution chat. Send a clear question when the exact item is gone, and give the customer a minute to answer before you replace a specialty product with whatever is at eye level. Refund when they asked you to refund.

### Step 7: Deliver like a careful neighbour

Follow the drop-off note. Do not block a driveway. Do not leave milk in the sun to take the photo. If the order includes alcohol, follow the app’s age-check steps. Provincial rules for age-restricted items are not the same everywhere in Canada. When you are unsure, refund the item.

### Step 8: Track the batches that actually paid

Write down item count, store, distance, stairs, gross pay, tip, and minutes. After five batches you will see which store and which list length fit you. Drop the pattern that looks fine on the offer screen and falls apart in the parking garage.

### Step 9: Add a physical limit

Set a maximum you will carry up stairs alone. Water cases and dog food are how people tweak a knee and lose a month of grocery shopping. Decline the batch. The app will offer it to someone else. Instacart’s Canadian FAQ states a lift of up to 40 pounds (18 kg). Your back can have a lower number. Use yours.

## What is easy to miss on a grocery shopping batch in Canada?

Grocery shopping in Canada is won or lost on produce, on keeping raw meat away from berries, and on parking at the door the customer named. Pick produce you would take home. A bruised avocado is not close enough because the list said avocado.

Customers forgive a missing cracker more readily than chicken juice on raspberries. Keep cleaners, raw chicken, and berries in different bags.

Holiday eves and the afternoon before a snowstorm are busy, and stores are picked over. Substitutions take longer. Leave a buffer so you are not rushing a store that has no bread.

Many Canadian chains have a house label the customer already chose on purpose. Swapping it for a national brand can be the wrong call. Read the note.

Parking at the customer’s building is part of the grocery shopping batch you accepted. If the only legal stop is a short loading zone, use it and leave. Circling is unpaid.

## What mistakes do new grocery shoppers make in Canada?

New grocery shoppers in Canada get into trouble by accepting a huge batch at an unfamiliar store, by leaving frozen food in the trunk, and by texting from a personal phone. Frozen food is the last thing in the cart, not a bag that sits in the car while you finish the aisles.

Stay in the app so the platform has the record and your private number stays private. Ignoring the lift on Instacart’s Canadian FAQ, up to 40 pounds (18 kg), is how a back ends the week. Your own limit can be lower.

Grocery shopping is still vehicle work. The small-supplier GST/HST test can apply long before the week feels like a business, and the car still needs an insurer who knows.

## Which tax and insurance rules apply to grocery shopping in Canada?

Grocery shopping income in Canada is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole Canada Pension Plan contribution on the net, as described under [Contributions to the Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). GST/HST for grocery shopping follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table. In British Columbia, [Uber delivery driver insurance in Canada](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/deliver/insurance/) describes many basic ICBC policies as allowing up to six days a month of commercial use, and that line is about Uber delivery.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in one calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and your other hustles. The timing rules are on the CRA’s when-to-register page. If you also carry passengers on a rideshare app, those fares follow the stricter rule. Read [rideshare driving](/side-hustles/rideshare-driving-side-hustle-canada/), the CRA ride-sharing page, and [GST/HST and commercial ride-sharing services (GI-196)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-196/gst-hst-commercial-ride-sharing-services.html). Confirm the rate for the province where the delivery happens, including Quebec, on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Liquor, tobacco, and other age-restricted items follow provincial law. If the app offers those batches, the app’s ID steps are the minimum. When in doubt, refund the item. Municipal parking rules will cost you more than provincial tax in week one. Read the signs.
