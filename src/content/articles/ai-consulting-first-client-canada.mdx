A first AI consulting client in Canada usually comes from a small business with one repetitive writing or admin task, a fixed-fee offer to map that task and set up one AI-assisted workflow, and a written rule about what customer data never goes into the tool. Owners buy a saved afternoon they can picture, not a transformation.

Canadian privacy law applies when a business handles personal information in commercial activity, and the privacy commissioner has published principles for generative AI. ## What offer gets a first AI consulting client in Canada?

The offer most likely to get a first AI consulting client in Canada is a fixed-fee workflow audit of one task, such as drafting quote emails or summarising supplier invoices, plus one written prompt or template set the staff can reuse, and a one-page data rule. It has an end date and a deliverable the owner can open.

Avoid vague promises like "AI strategy" or "automate your business." A first client wants to know which task, which tool, how long, and what changes on Monday morning.

## Where do Canadians find a first AI consulting client?

Canadians find a first AI consulting client among businesses they already know: a past employer, a family member's clinic, a trade contractor who quotes by email, or a local business association that runs member workshops. A free 30-minute talk at an association is a better first step than cold outreach to strangers.

If you email businesses, ISED's [anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies to commercial messages. The [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page covers express and implied consent, the identification you must include, and the unsubscribe mechanism. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first AI consulting note say?

A first AI consulting note in Canada should name one task, offer a fixed audit, state what you will not do with customer data, and avoid any promise of a percentage saved.

**Example note.** This is a generic example. It is not a note that produced a client.

> Hello. Many small contractors spend evenings writing quote follow-ups. I offer a fixed-fee session that maps how your quotes are written today and sets up a reusable AI-assisted template for the follow-up email, using only the job details you choose to enter. Customer names, addresses, and phone numbers stay out of the tool unless you have a basis to use them there. You get a written one-page process at the end. Would a 20-minute call to look at one recent quote help?

## How do you prove AI consulting skill without hype?

You prove AI consulting skill in Canada with a worked sample on made-up data: a before-and-after of a task, the prompt you used, the output, and the edits a human still made. Showing the edits is more convincing than claiming the tool is flawless.

Do not claim time savings or accuracy rates you have not measured for that business. The Competition Bureau's [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) page covers claims made to promote a business interest.

## How do you handle client data in a first AI project in Canada?

Handle client data in a first AI project in Canada by keeping personal information out of AI tools unless the business has a legal basis and the tool's terms fit, and by writing that rule down before you start. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner's [generative AI principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/) include legal authority and consent, and limiting collection, use, and disclosure.

[PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) applies to private-sector organisations handling personal information in commercial activity, and some provinces have their own laws. Use the client's own accounts where possible, rather than pasting their data into yours. This is not legal advice.

## How should you price the first AI consulting project in Canada?

Price the first AI consulting project in Canada as a fixed fee for one task and a written deliverable, with any monthly support quoted separately later. This guide does not state a market rate for AI consulting.

| Path | What the client buys | What you watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-task audit | A mapped process and a reusable template | Scope creeping to "all our emails" |
| Staff workshop | A session and handout | Questions about data that need a written answer |
| Monthly support | Updates as tools change | Billing for hours you cannot define |

### Illustrative example

Suppose a contractor pays an assumed $400 for a one-task audit. Suppose discovery, building the template, testing it on three sample quotes, and the handover call take eight hours. The sketch is $400 ÷ 8 = $50 an hour before tax set-aside. If testing runs two hours over, it becomes $400 ÷ 10 = $40 an hour, which is why the scope should name one task. These figures are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first AI consulting client look like?

A 14-day plan for a first AI consulting client in Canada should produce one worked sample, a one-page offer with a data rule, and a short list of businesses you can reach through people you know. A signed project inside 14 days is not guaranteed.

### Days 1 and 2: Read the privacy pages

Read PIPEDA in brief and the OPC generative AI principles. Write your one-page data rule.

### Days 3 to 5: Build a worked sample

Pick a common task, use made-up data, and document the prompt, output, and human edits.

### Days 6 to 9: Write the offer

Write the one-task audit: scope, deliverable, fee, and what is out of scope.

### Days 10 to 14: Talk to five businesses

Contact five owners you can reach through your network. Offer the 20-minute look at one task.

## What mistakes block a first AI consulting client in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first AI consulting client in Canada are vague offers, promised savings you cannot measure, and careless handling of customer data. Each one loses the owner's trust.

Building on your personal tool accounts leaves the client stuck when you leave. Skipping the human review step lets errors reach customers. Emailing scraped lists skips consent.

## Which tax rules apply to a first AI consulting client in Canada?

AI consulting income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the scope, invoices, payments, and tool subscription receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. AI consulting is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
