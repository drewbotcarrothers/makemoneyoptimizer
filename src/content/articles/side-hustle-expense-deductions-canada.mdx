A side hustle in Canada can deduct expenses you incur to earn business income, to the extent they are reasonable and they are the business portion, on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Personal living costs, a salary you pay yourself, and most fines are not deductible. A laptop or a mower that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html), not the full price in the year you bought it.

This is general information, not tax advice. Talk to an accountant before you claim a large or unusual amount. The CRA's [business expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses.html) page and [Guide T4002](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002.html) are the lists to read for the year you file. Class rates and vehicle ceilings change. Confirm them for that year instead of copying an old blog.

Where the income goes on the return is [how to report side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/). A workspace at home has extra limits in [home office expenses](/side-hustles/home-office-expenses-side-hustle-canada/). A car has its own log in [vehicle expenses](/side-hustles/vehicle-expenses-delivery-rideshare-canada/). For side hustles that drive to clients rather than for apps, [tracking mileage for the CRA](/side-hustles/mileage-log-cra-side-hustle-canada/) shows the log layout, and [free and cheap bookkeeping tools](/side-hustles/bookkeeping-tools-side-hustle-canada/) helps keep every category tidy.

## Which side hustle expenses can you deduct in Canada?

You can deduct the business part of current expenses you incur to earn side hustle income in Canada, including the lines the CRA lists on Form T2125, when the amount is reasonable. The [expenses section](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125/expenses-section-form-t2125.html) names advertising, meals and entertainment (the allowable part), insurance, interest and bank charges, licences and memberships, office expenses, supplies, professional fees, rent, repairs, salaries you pay to employees, travel, utilities, motor vehicle expenses, capital cost allowance, and other expenses you specify.

"Enter only the business part" on that page means you leave out salary or drawings you pay yourself, goods your family consumed, charitable donations and political contributions (those have their own credit rules, not this list), interest and penalties on your income tax, most life insurance premiums, the non-business use of property, and most fines and penalties imposed after March 22, 2004.

| Cost | Usual treatment on a side hustle | What to leave out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ads, domain, marketplace fees | Current expense if you incurred them to earn income | A personal subscription you did not use for the hustle |
| Supplies you use up | Current expense | The household half of a shared purchase |
| Software for the work | Current expense | A family streaming plan |
| Accountant or bookkeeper | Professional fees | Your own time, which is not a salary to yourself |
| Phone and internet | Business percentage | The personal percentage |
| Tools and machines that last years | Capital cost allowance on the business portion | The full receipt in week one, in most cases |
| Meals with a client | Often 50% of a reasonable amount | A solo lunch that is personal |

The expense still has to earn income. A course you might take someday, ordinary clothes, or the commute to a regular job are the costs people try to force onto a T2125. If you cannot explain the link in a sentence, leave it off or ask.

## What does reasonable mean, and what is the personal-use portion?

A deductible side hustle expense in Canada has to be reasonable in the circumstances, and only the part that earns income is deductible. The meals page states the pattern clearly for food: the maximum is 50% of the lesser of what you incurred and what is reasonable. The same idea of reasonableness sits over the rest of the return. A $900 dinner to discuss a $150 invoice is a conversation you may lose.

Personal use is a fraction, not a mood. If a phone is on a plan for the household and you can show that part of the bill is the hustle, claim that part. If you cannot show it, do not claim the plan. The CRA can ask for the record. [Record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) is how you keep the fraction honest.

### Step 1: Separate the receipt into business and personal

Write the split on the receipt or in the log while you remember it. "About half" reconstructed in April is weaker than a note made the same week.

### Step 2: Keep the business part on the matching line

Office supplies do not go on motor vehicle. Home utilities do not go on ordinary utilities if they belong in the business-use-of-home calculation. The home-office page says the amount on line 9945 cannot have been claimed elsewhere on the T2125. Double-claiming the same hydro bill is a mistake, not a strategy.

### Step 3: Stop at reasonable

If a cheaper option would have done the same work, the extra may not be reasonable. The CRA does not publish a price list for "reasonable laptop." You still have to be able to explain the cost.

GST/HST you paid is part of the expense if you are not a registrant and you could not claim an input tax credit. If you are a registrant and you claimed the credit, Form T2125 tells you not to include that GST/HST in the expense again. The [registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) is when the credit exists.

## What is the difference between a current expense and capital cost allowance?

A current expense in Canada is a cost you incur and consume in the business, and you generally deduct the business part in the year. Capital cost allowance is how you deduct the cost of property that lasts, a portion each year, in the class the CRA assigns. You do not have to claim the maximum CCA in a year. Claiming less leaves a higher undepreciated balance for later.

The CCA page and Guide T4002, chapter 4, hold the classes, the rates, and the first-year limits. Those charts change, including rules that speed up or limit the first year. This guide does not quote a class rate or a passenger-vehicle ceiling, because the ceiling is published for the year and a stale number files a wrong return. Look up the year you file.

A drill, a pressure washer, a laptop, and a car are the usual capital items. Salt, fuel, paper, and a month of software are the usual current items. Classes differ. [Lawn care startup costs](/side-hustles/lawn-care-startup-costs-canada/) points a mower at CCA. [Handyman startup costs](/side-hustles/handyman-startup-costs-canada/) does the same for a drill.

### What CCA is not

CCA is not a second full deduction of a receipt you also expensed. It is not available on the personal portion. It is not automatic on a home you live in. The home-office guide explains why people often skip CCA on the house itself. Passenger vehicles have a maximum capital cost. If your car cost more than that year's ceiling, the extra is not in the CCA base. Confirm the ceiling in T4002 for the year you bought it.

If you are a partner and you use your own vehicle, the T2125 instructions point that claim to a different line than a sole proprietor's Chart A. Read the form if you are in a partnership. Most side hustles on this site are sole proprietorships, which is the simple case, not a corporation.

## How do meals and entertainment limits work for a side hustle in Canada?

The maximum you can claim for food, beverages, and entertainment in a Canadian side hustle is 50% of the lesser of the amount you incurred and an amount that is reasonable. That is the rule on [line 8523](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125/line-8523-meals-entertainment-allowable-part-only.html). It also applies to meals while you travel or attend a convention. Entertainment includes tickets, gratuities, cover charges, and room rentals for hospitality.

The 50% limit does not apply in the cases the page lists. Those include a business that regularly provides food to customers for compensation, such as a restaurant, and a meal you bill to the client and show on the bill. There is a separate exception for certain remote work sites and for up to six office parties a year if you invite all employees from a location. A solo side hustle with no employees does not have a staff party to put on that line. Read the exceptions before you use one. They are narrow.

A coffee you buy because you were out delivering is usually personal. A meal you buy for a client, that you can explain, is the kind of cost the 50% line is about. Write the name and the purpose on the receipt.

## What does an illustrative side hustle deduction look like in Canada?

An illustrative deduction is assumed arithmetic. It is not an average, a CRA example, or permission to claim these amounts.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose you detail cars on weekends in Canada and you are not a GST/HST registrant. Suppose in the year you buy soap and towels you use up, and the receipts total $300. Suppose a used extractor costs $800 and will last several years, so you do not deduct $800 as a current expense. Suppose Guide T4002 puts that machine in a class whose rate you confirm, and suppose you are allowed to claim $120 of capital cost allowance that year after the first-year rule and the business portion. The $120 is an assumption about the chart, not a rate you should copy. Suppose the phone bill is $720 and your log supports one quarter for the hustle, so $180. Suppose you buy a client a $60 meal and the 50% limit applies, so $30 is the allowable part.

Current expenses in the sketch are $300 + $180 + $30 = $510. CCA in the sketch is $120. The deduction sketch is $630, not $300 + $800 + $720 + $60. The extractor's remaining cost stays in the class for later years. The personal phone stays out.

| Item | Receipt | Amount in the sketch | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soap and towels | $300 | $300 | Used up, business |
| Extractor | $800 | $120 CCA | Assumption, not the full price |
| Phone | $720 | $180 | One quarter, assumed log |
| Client meal | $60 | $30 | 50% if the limit applies |
| Sketch total |  | $630 | Before any other limit |

Replace the class math with the year's CCA chart. If you claimed an input tax credit, remove the GST/HST from the expense. [Car detailing](/side-hustles/car-detailing-side-hustle-canada/) is the work. This table is only the shape of the claim.

A paper trail still needs a place to live. A <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="receipt file organizer" search="expanding file folder receipts" /> is a ordinary folder, not a tax product. Scan what you will not keep on paper. The folder does not make a personal cost deductible.

## Which side hustle costs are easy to claim by mistake in Canada?

People deduct the full household phone, the full car, ordinary clothes, and the principal on a mortgage. Mortgage interest can be part of a business-use-of-home claim if you meet those conditions. The principal cannot. Interest and penalties on your income tax bill are on the CRA's do-not-deduct list.

Drawings are not a wage. Moving money from the hustle account to your personal account does not create an expense. A loss is not something you manufacture with personal costs so that a day job's tax falls. The home-office rules also stop you from using workspace costs to create or increase a business loss. That limit is specific. It is on the home-office page, and it does not mean every other expense is unlimited.

Fines for parking that the law imposes, and penalties, are usually out. A parking fee at a client site can be different from a ticket. The vehicle guide separates business parking, which can be fully deductible, from the rest of the car costs, which take the kilometre percentage.

## What records support a side hustle deduction in Canada?

A deduction in Canada needs a record that shows the amount, the date, and why it earned income. The CRA's individual records page says to keep documents for at least six years, including proof beyond the official receipt. The six-year rule is explained in [record keeping for side hustles](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).

Keep the invoice, the e-transfer, and the note that says "client meal, name, purpose" or "phone, 25% business, method." For CCA, keep the purchase record until six years after the last year the property can affect a return. Information Circular IC78-10 says records for capital property are kept until six years after the last year they can enter a calculation, which is longer than six years from the purchase if you still own the asset.
