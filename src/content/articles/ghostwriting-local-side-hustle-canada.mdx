## Who does local speech and resume writing in Canada suit?

Local speech and resume writing in Canada suits careful writers, teachers, and administrators who can ask what the reader needs to know and then stop. A two-page resume and an eight-minute speech are different jobs. Say which one you are selling.

It is a poor fit if the client wants you to pretend you are a licensed career counsellor, a lawyer, or an immigration adviser. This guide does not find a national licence that you must hold to write a resume or a speech. It also does not give you someone else's professional title. If the letter is legal, medical, or an immigration form, tell the client to use the licensed person, and offer editing of their own words only if you are comfortable with that smaller scope.

French and English both matter in a lot of Canadian cities. If you write in one language only, say so in the ad.

## How do you start local speech and resume writing in Canada?

Starting local speech and resume writing in Canada means publishing a narrow offer, collecting a deposit, and delivering a draft the client can say out loud or send with their name.

### Step 1: Pick two products

Examples: a one-page resume, a cover letter, a five-minute speech, a one-page letter. Write the length and the number of revision rounds. "Any writing you need" attracts jobs you cannot finish on a weeknight.

### Step 2: Make one sample you are allowed to show

Rewrite your own resume, or a speech you have permission to share, with names removed. Do not post a client's file as a sample unless the agreement says you may.

### Step 3: Post where local people already look

A classified ad, a community board, a note at a library workshop if the library allows it. The Kijiji habit is in [getting clients on Kijiji](/side-hustles/get-clients-on-kijiji-canada/). Say the city, the product, the deposit, and that you will ask them for the facts. If you follow up by email or text, read [getting consent to send email](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email). A reply to someone who wrote to you about a quote is a different fact from a blast. The full version is [lead generation under CASL](/side-hustles/cold-email-lead-generation-casl-canada/).

### Step 4: Interview before you draft

Ask for the job posting, the audience, the stories they actually lived, and the words they do not want. A resume that lists a duty they cannot explain in an interview will hurt them. Refuse to add a credential they do not hold.

### Step 5: Take the deposit, then write

A practical term is about half before you start and the rest on delivery. Put the percentages in the note you both keep. [How to invoice clients](/side-hustles/how-to-invoice-clients-canada/) shows a simple layout. If they will not pay the deposit, you do not have a client yet.

### Step 6: If you use an AI draft, proofread it against the interview

A model can rearrange bullets. It also invents employers, dates, and smooth sentences the client never said. Read every line against your notes. Fix the voice so it sounds like them, not like a template. Tell the client, in the agreement, that you may use software and that you check the facts. You remain responsible for what you deliver.

### Step 7: Deliver in an editable file and stop at the agreed round

One revision round in the price, a date for comments, and a fee for a new round. A speech should be readable aloud in the time they asked for. A resume should match the posting they gave you.

### Step 8: Ask for one referral, in person or by a message they send

You can ask the client to pass your name along. You should not take their contact list. Ownership of the writing belongs in the note. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office's [copyright pages](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright) are the overview. A short contract habit is [a simple freelance contract](/side-hustles/freelance-contract-canada/).

## What mistakes are common in local writing in Canada?

Common mistakes in local writing in Canada are starting without a deposit, polishing a draft that contains a job the client did not do, and treating an AI paragraph as finished.

- **No deposit.** The draft is the work. Half, or another share you wrote down, arrives before that work.
- **Invented metrics.** "Increased sales by 40%" is a fact or it is a lie. If they do not have the number, do not type it.
- **A speech in your voice.** Read it aloud in theirs. Cut the lines they would not say.
- **Unlimited revisions.** Two new openings are a new invoice.
- **Emailing a neighbourhood list.** CASL still applies. Post the ad. Answer people who reply.
- **Holding the only copy.** Send the file they paid for. Do not ransom it.

## Which tax rules apply to local writing in Canada?

Fees for speeches, resumes, and letters in Canada are self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). A deposit is still recorded. How you recognize it depends on the agreement and your method. Keep the invoice either way. Self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across your side hustles. Writing is not ride-sharing.
