Affiliate marketing commission rates in Canada are set by each merchant program, not by you. Amazon.ca's standard schedule pays 0% to 10% of the sale price depending on category, and 2% for anything not listed. Sport Chek advertises up to 6%, Shopify pays up to US$150 per qualified store referral, and Hostinger starts at 40% of eligible sales. Your earnings depend on three things you can work on: which products you write about, how many readers click, and how many of those clicks buy. This guide covers the published rates, how to estimate earnings per click, and the rules that cancel commissions.

## Which Amazon.ca rule changes in 2026 affect earnings?

Amazon.ca's [operating agreement update](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/operating/compare), effective April 14, 2026, changed what counts as a qualifying purchase:

- **180-day limit:** products must be shipped, streamed or downloaded, and paid for within 180 days to qualify.
- **Paid ads:** purchases from customers referred through any paid or boosted ad linking to Amazon are disqualified, regardless of keywords, with limited exceptions.
- **Onsite commission scope:** onsite commission now applies only to direct purchases of the same product variant as the linked page.

The update also added a definition of original content that requires commentary, analysis or transformation. A page that only lists links is at risk under that definition.

## How do you estimate affiliate earnings?

```text
Orders            = clicks × conversion rate
Commission        = orders × average order value × commission rate
Earnings per click (EPC) = conversion rate × average order × rate
Page earnings per 1,000 views = EPC × clicks per 1,000 views
Bounty pages      = sign-ups × bounty
USD programs      = USD earnings × Bank of Canada rate
Profit            = commissions − hosting, domain, tools
```

**Look up the category rate first;** it changes the result more than effort does. **Use real product prices** for the average order, not the most expensive item. **Replace guesses with report data** as soon as you have a month of clicks and orders. Set aside tax and [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on the net.

## What do worked affiliate examples look like?

**Illustrative.** Clicks, conversion rates, order values and sign-up counts are my assumptions; the rates are from the program pages above.

### Example 1: Amazon.ca kitchen-tools page

- 400 clicks × 5% = 20 orders × $60 average = $1,200 of sales.
- At the 4% Kitchen & Dining rate: $1,200 × 0.04 = **$48 a month**, or $0.12 per click.
- The same sales in a 6% Home category: $72. In 1% Computers: $12. Category, not effort, made the sixfold difference.

### Example 2: Sport Chek running-shoe guide

- 300 clicks × 3% = 9 orders × $120 average = $1,080.
- If your dashboard rate is the full 6%: $1,080 × 0.06 = **$64.80 a month**, $0.22 per click. A lower dashboard rate scales the result down in proportion.

### Example 3: Shopify referrals from a "start an online store" tutorial

- 2 referrals a month that upgrade to a paid plan. If each earns the full US$150: US$300.
- At the Bank of Canada's [rate of 1.4240 on October 8, 2026](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/): US$300 × 1.4240 = **$427.20 a month**.
- Shopify says amounts vary by referral location, so check the payout table for Canadian referrals before you count on $150.

### Example 4: Hostinger link on a "how to start a blog" post

- 5 eligible sales a month × an assumed $100 first purchase × 40% = **$200 a month**.
- Hosting pays on the first purchase only, so the page has to keep ranking to keep earning.

## How do you raise affiliate earnings?

You can't raise a program's rate yourself, but you can change what each click earns.

- **Move effort to higher-rate categories.** On Amazon.ca, Home and Outdoor pay 6% and Computers 1%.
- **Add a direct program** where one exists. A retailer program at up to 6% on the same products beats Amazon's 4% Sports & Fitness rate if your readers buy there.
- **Ask for a higher rate** once you have sales history. Programs on networks such as impact.com have affiliate managers; a month of orders is your case.
- **Update pages that rank.** Prices and stock change; dead links earn nothing.
- **Add sponsorships or ads** on informational pages; see [SEO blogging pricing](/side-hustles/seo-blogging-pricing-canada/).

## What affiliate marketing earnings mistakes are common?

Common mistakes are choosing a low-rate category without noticing, sending paid-ad traffic to Amazon links, counting clicks as income before orders ship, and hiding disclosures.

The Competition Bureau's [influencer guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says commissions are a material connection and that linking to an affiliate webpage is unlikely to be enough disclosure; disclosures should be prominent and visible on all devices. It also says reviews should be based on actual experience.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect affiliate earnings?

Affiliate commissions are business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) once you run the site as a business, and you pay both halves of CPP on net earnings. GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Whether commissions from a non-resident program count as taxable supplies depends on the facts, so get advice before you near the threshold.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Program rates are what each page listed on October 9, 2026, and programs change them. Sport Chek's and Shopify's figures are "up to" ceilings, and Shopify varies by referral location. Hostinger's page doesn't state a currency or what counts as an eligible sale beyond its terms. The worked examples use assumed clicks, conversion rates and order values; your own reports are the only reliable inputs.
