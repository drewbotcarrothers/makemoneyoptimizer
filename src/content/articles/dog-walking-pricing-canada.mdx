Charging for dog walking in Canada works best per walk, priced from a target hourly pay that sits above the employee benchmark: Job Bank lists dog walker wages of $15.00 (low), $18.00 (median), and $25.50 (high) an hour nationally. A self-employed walker also carries travel between homes, waste bags, insurance, and both halves of CPP, so the per-walk price has to cover more than the walk itself. This guide suits someone who will time a real route before posting a rate.

## What do dog walkers earn per hour in Canada?

Employed dog walkers in Canada earn a median of $18.00 an hour, according to [Job Bank's dog walker wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/296217/ca) (NOC 65220, reference period 2023–2024, updated November 19, 2025). Those are employee wages. A self-employed walker sets a price per walk, and that price should work out above the employee median once unpaid travel is counted.

| Area (Job Bank, dog walker) | Low | Median | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada | $15.00 | $18.00 | $25.50 |
| Ontario | $17.95 | $19.00 | $26.00 |
| British Columbia | $18.25 | $19.50 | $25.00 |
| Alberta | $15.00 | $17.00 | $32.50 |
| Quebec | $16.60 | $18.57 | $25.00 |

This guide does not publish an "average" price per walk in Canada because no public survey we could verify collects one. Apps and local listings show what neighbours charge; check three in your area for the same walk length before you post.

## Which pricing models work for dog walking in Canada?

Dog walking in Canada is usually priced per walk by length (30 or 60 minutes), with a lower per-dog price for group walks and a weekly package for regular clients. Drop-in visits, extra dogs from the same home, and holiday days are add-ons.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per solo walk | One dog or one household, 30 or 60 minutes | Reactive dogs, puppies, seniors | Running late because the route has gaps |
| Group walk, per dog | Several compatible dogs, each pays a share | Dense blocks with friendly dogs | City rules on how many dogs one walker may handle |
| Weekly package | 3 or 5 walks a week, billed weekly or monthly | Owners who commute | Cancellations: write a notice period |
| Drop-in visit | 15–30 minutes for potty break, water, feed | Cats and short absences | Visits that quietly become walks |
| Add-ons | Second dog, holiday, early or late slot, key pickup | Covering real extra effort | Doing add-ons for free |

Group size can be a municipal rule. Toronto's [commercial dog walker permit page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/commercial-dog-walker-permit/) says permits are available for individuals to walk four to six dogs on a commercial basis, requires proof of $2,000,000 in commercial general liability insurance, and lists a 2026 fee of $305.99 for one year (tax not included). That fee and the insurance are costs your group-walk price has to carry. Other cities use different rules, so confirm locally before pricing a group walk.

## How do you calculate a dog walking rate in Canada?

Calculate a dog walking rate in Canada from your target hourly pay, the share of time lost to travel between homes, the walk length, and small per-walk costs. Then compare with local listings for the same length.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

Use the Job Bank table as a floor check. If your effective rate lands under your province's employee low, the price is too thin.

### Step 2: Measure travel between dogs

Drive or walk your intended route. Ten minutes between homes on a 30-minute walk means a quarter of your time is unpaid.

### Step 3: Add per-walk costs

Waste bags, treats, fuel or transit, and wear on gear. Insurance is an annual cost; divide it over the walks you expect in a year.

### Step 4: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both the employee and employer share of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). The [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) shows how to keep that money separate.

### Step 5: Round and write it down

Round to a clean number, publish it with what is included, and keep it for at least a season so clients can plan.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $22 an hour, between Job Bank's national median of $18.00 and high of $25.50. Suppose 30% of your working time is travel and messages, so 70% is billable. The billable rate is $22 ÷ 0.70 = $31.43 an hour.

A 30-minute solo walk is 0.5 × $31.43 = $15.71. Add an assumed $1.00 for bags, treats, and gear wear: $16.71, which rounds to $17. A 60-minute solo walk is $31.43 + $1.00 = $32.43, or about $32.

For a group walk, suppose three compatible dogs walk together for 60 minutes and each owner pays $20. That is 3 × $20 = $60 for the hour before costs. Group walks pay more per hour, which is why the permit, the insurance, and your control of the dogs matter more than the price. In Toronto, the sketch's three dogs sit below the four-dog permit level; a fourth dog would add the permit fee to your costs.

## When should you raise dog walking prices in Canada?

Raise dog walking prices in Canada when your walk slots are full, when travel or insurance costs climb, or when a dog needs more handling than the package assumed. Give regular clients written notice, usually a month, and raise new-client prices first.

A waiting list is the clearest signal. So is a route where one far-away client costs 20 minutes of travel each day. Price that client's walk higher, or replace it with a closer dog.

## What dog walking pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common dog walking pricing mistakes in Canada are ignoring travel time, pricing a reactive dog like a calm one, and offering unlimited cancellations. Each turns a full day into an underpaid one.

For the insurance, permit, and client-agreement mistakes walkers make most, and how to fix them, see the [dog walking mistakes guide](/side-hustles/dog-walking-mistakes-canada/).

Do a meet-and-greet before quoting, so a puller or a dog that cannot join a group is priced as solo. Charge for second dogs. Hold a cancellation window in writing. Do not copy the lowest app price; the platform's fee comes out of that number, and you are not paying a platform for a direct client.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect dog walking prices in Canada?

Dog walking income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Add GST/HST only once registered; registration becomes required after you exceed the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or across four consecutive quarters, counting all your taxable side hustles together.

When registered, use the rate for the province where you supply the service from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) explains voluntary registration. Weekly packages paid in advance are still income in the period you earn them; keep records of each payment.
