## How should you choose house cleaning tools in Canada?

Choose house cleaning tools in Canada for portability, surface safety, and label clarity: everything fits in a caddy you can carry on transit or from a car, nothing damages a client's stone or wood, and every bottle is in its original labelled container. These are selection criteria, not test results.

| Criterion | What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portability | Fits one caddy and one bag | You may be climbing stairs or riding transit between homes |
| Surface labels | The product names the surfaces it is for | Natural stone, hardwood, and stainless each have their own warnings |
| Fragrance-free options | At least one unscented cleaner | Some clients ask for it |
| Colour coding | Separate cloths for bathroom and kitchen | Cross-use is the complaint you can prevent cheaply |
| Original containers | Keep products labelled; dilute per the label | [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/chemicals/cleaning-products-working-safely.html) says to follow the manufacturer's instructions when diluting |
| Refill cost | What a refill costs, not only the first bottle | Consumables are the cost that returns every month |

## Which house cleaning chemicals should you never mix in Canada?

Never mix different cleaning products. [CCOHS](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/chemicals/cleaning-products-working-safely.html) says products containing bleach and ammonia must never be mixed because hazardous gases may be released, and notes that ammonia is common in window, bathroom, and glass cleaners and furniture polish. It also says not to mix vinegar, bleach, or ammonia when making homemade cleaners.

Transport Canada's [CANUTEC](https://tc.canada.ca/en/dangerous-goods/canutec/articles/improper-mixing-common-household-cleaning-products) says mixing bleach with an acid produces chlorine gas, and that bleach with ammonia-based cleaners creates chloramine vapours. It advises using products as directed and checking the safety data sheet. A small kit of three labelled products, used one at a time with ventilation, is safer than a shelf of options you might combine.

When diluting a concentrate, CCOHS says to add the concentrate to the water and follow the manufacturer's instructions, wearing safety glasses or goggles and gloves as needed.

## Which tax rules apply to house cleaning tools in Canada?

House cleaning income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), with the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution paid by the self-employed person on net income. Cleaners, cloths, and gloves are ordinary supplies. A vacuum that lasts several years can be depreciable property; the CRA says you [cannot deduct the full cost](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) of depreciable property in the year you buy it.

GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies, including your associates', pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
