They suit people who can show up with the right tools and who will decline electrical, gas, plumbing, and structural work.

## What does the first week on odd-job apps in Canada involve?

The first week on odd-job apps in Canada involves confirming the city is live, registering in the Tasker app, and waiting on identity verification before you accept anything. [How do I become a Tasker?](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260467885979-How-Do-I-Become-a-Tasker) says you download the Tasker app, which is not the app clients use, create an account, set skills and location, add at least one availability slot, and draw a work-area map. Taskrabbit then verifies the profile. The page says it should take a maximum of two working days before the email titled “Action required: Taskrabbit identity verification.” You open that link, photograph your ID and take a selfie. On iOS, Taskrabbit says to open the link in Safari. Registration cannot be finished on the website.

If the city is empty, Kijiji or a community board is the older version of the same hustle. It is slower, and it does not charge the $25. Do not invent tasks in a city the app does not serve.

## What does Taskrabbit require before you can accept a task in Canada?

Taskrabbit’s [requirements article](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260520394651-What-s-Required-to-Become-a-Tasker) says you must be at least 19, able to work in an active Canadian city, able to provide personal information including a social insurance number, and holding a Canadian bank account at a financial institution. Savings accounts, prepaid debit cards, and reloadable cards will not work, even if they accept direct deposit. You also need a valid credit or debit card. If registration is successful, Taskrabbit charges a one-time, non-refundable $25. The [registration-fee article](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260480151579-What-s-the-Registration-Fee) repeats that the fee does not guarantee you will become a Tasker, and that further fees are charged to clients, not Taskers. Read the current client fee in your account anyway. Percentages change, and this guide does not state one.

The phone must be iOS 9.0 or later, or Android 4.3 or later. There is no Windows app, on that requirements page.

[Identity verification](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260392641179-Identity-Verification-Process) says Taskrabbit uses services such as Persona and Checkr in Canada. If Checkr cannot verify you electronically, you may have to go to a Canada Post office with a form from Sterling. Register with accurate information the first time. The requirements page links a city list. Open it. A [March 24, 2026 Taskrabbit release](https://www.taskrabbit.ca/press/release/taskrabbit-launches-vehicle-integrated-moving-category-across-90-cities-in) says truck-assisted moving is live across active Canadian markets, including Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary, described as 90+ cities. That release is about moving, not about furniture assembly in every town.

## What should the first task on an odd-job app in Canada be?

The first task on an odd-job app in Canada should be one job you have already done at home, with the instructions and the bits in the bag, inside a work area you can reach without a long unpaid bus ride. Furniture assembly is the usual start. TV mounting only if you have practised finding studs. Yard cleanup only if you are not spraying. In Ontario, read the province’s page on [pesticides and home lawns and gardens](https://www.ontario.ca/page/pesticides-home-lawns-and-gardens) before you offer any pesticide. That page is an Ontario example. Other provinces have their own rules.

Electrical, gas, plumbing, and structural work are often reserved for licensed trades. The rule is provincial. In Ontario, [Skilled Trades Ontario](https://www.skilledtradesontario.ca/) is the public starting point. This guide does not list every compulsory trade. A task booked on an app does not replace a certificate. Decline the job, or stop, when the wall is not what the client described.

## How should you price the first odd-job task in Canada?

Price the first odd-job task in Canada from the time on site, the travel, the tools, and the platform fee shown in your account, and write the scope before you tap accept. Looking at other Taskers in your city is context. A rate copied from another province ignores the bus ride in front of you.

Factors: how long the piece actually takes, stairs, missing hardware, whether you are solo, and the minutes getting there. A capped visit beats an open “while you’re here.” If you cannot see the problem, charge a short diagnostic you write down, then stop and re-quote.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose you assume $40 an hour and a bookshelf the instructions say you can finish in 90 minutes, plus 20 minutes of travel each way that you decide to include. Labour in the sketch is 1.5 times $40, which is $60. Travel is 40/60 times $40, which is about $26.67. The sketch total is about $86.67 before any platform fee the account shows. Subtract that fee before you call the number profit. Do not copy $40 or $86.67. These are assumptions, not a survey and not a quote.

| A rate with no travel | A cheap hour that starts at the bus stop | The map eats the fee |

## What does a 14-day plan for odd-job apps in Canada look like?

A 14-day plan for odd-job apps in Canada should produce a finished registration in one category, or a clear note that the city is not live. A paid task is a bonus. This plan does not promise bookings.

### Days 1 and 2: Confirm the city and the one skill

Search your city on the app. Read the requirements article. Pick furniture assembly or one other job you have finished before. Write a no-list: electrical, gas, plumbing, structure, and spraying.

### Days 3 to 5: Register in the app

Draw a work area you can reach. Add one availability slot you will actually keep. Have the credit card ready for the $25, and remember the fee page says payment is not approval. Use your real name and SIN so the identity check does not bounce.

### Days 6 to 9: Finish identity, do not widen the map

Open the verification email in the browser Taskrabbit names. If you are sent to Canada Post, that errand is the work of these days. Do not add moving, mounting, and yard work because the profile looks empty.

### Days 10 to 14: One task, or a better offer

If a task matches the skill and the map, accept one and write the scope in the chat. If nothing arrives, edit the photos and the first sentence of the profile. Do not buy a truck. The moving release is not a promise of furniture jobs on your street.

## What mistakes show up in the first week on odd-job apps in Canada?

Common first-week mistakes on odd-job apps in Canada are paying the $25 and treating it as income already earned, ticking every category, and taking cash off-app for a task the platform booked. You can lose the account. If you want direct clients, build them with the example note, not by diverting an app booking.

Underpricing to win, then resenting the bus, is the next mistake. So is mounting a television you have never hung, and spraying a lawn because the category sounded like “yard.” Photos of the finished piece, and a sentence about what was not included, matter more than a clever headline. The first reviews shape the next invitations. You do not invent reviews.

A work area drawn across the whole city looks busy and bills you in transit. Shrink the map to streets you can reach with the tools on your back or in a car you already insure for the trip. Tell the auto insurer if you will haul equipment for pay. This is not insurance advice. Search the city for a business licence before the flyer goes up. This guide does not state the fee.

## Which tax and trade rules apply to the first odd-job payout in Canada?

Income from odd-job apps in Canada is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), even if a T4A never arrives, and you budget for both shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), which is separate from [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html). Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm other rates with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Trade rules are provincial. A platform profile does not replace a certificate or a municipal business licence. Search your city for the licence. This guide does not state a fee. Workers’ compensation is another provincial question if the task looks like construction rather than a bookcase. Check that province’s board before you price help from a friend.
