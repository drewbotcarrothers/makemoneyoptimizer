## How should you choose YouTube gear in Canada?

Choose YouTube gear in Canada by your format (talking head, screen recording, outdoor, Shorts), where you film, and what is visibly or audibly wrong in your last video. These are selection criteria, not test results.

| Criterion | What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format | Talking head, tutorials, vlogs, or Shorts | A desk mic suits tutorials; a clip-on mic suits walking vlogs |
| Connection | Phone or computer connector, or wireless | A mic that does not plug into your phone is a return |
| Room | Echo, traffic, furnace noise | Soft furnishings can help more than a pricier mic |
| Light | Window direction, time of day you film | Facing a window is free light |
| Software | One editor you will keep opening | [DaVinci Resolve](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve) says its free version works with virtually all 8-bit formats at up to 60 fps in Ultra HD 3840 x 2160 |
| Music rights | Licence for every track | YouTube's [Audio Library](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882) offers royalty-free music and sound effects it calls copyright-safe |

## Does YouTube gear affect monetization in Canada?

YouTube gear does not unlock monetization; eligibility thresholds do. YouTube's [Partner Program page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851) lists two routes: 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers with 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. It also says meeting a threshold does not mean automatic entry, because every channel goes through a review.

If a brand sends you free gear in exchange for coverage, the Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says to disclose material connections, including free products, clearly and prominently.

## What YouTube gear mistakes are common in Canada?

Common YouTube gear mistakes in Canada are buying a camera before a mic, filming with your back to a window, paying for two editing subscriptions, using music you do not have rights to, and upgrading gear instead of publishing. Gear does not count toward the Partner Program thresholds; published videos and watch time do.

## Which tax rules apply to YouTube gear in Canada?

Channel income from ads, sponsors, or memberships is generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Gear used to earn that income can be a business expense, but a camera or computer that lasts years is depreciable property; the CRA says you [cannot deduct the full cost](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) in the year of purchase. Claim only the business-use portion of equipment you also use personally.

GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. How platform payouts count can depend on who pays you and where they are; confirm with the CRA or a tax professional. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
