<QuickAnswer>
Online tutoring in Canada starts with one subject, one type of learner, a quiet room, a headset and a free video tool. Job Bank lists a national median of **$20.00 an hour** for employed tutors (NOC 43109). That is a wage benchmark for employees, not a cap on what a self-employed tutor charges. Platforms take very different cuts. Preply keeps 100% of every trial lesson and then 33% down to 18% of later lessons. Wyzant keeps 25%. Superprof charges nothing when you collect payment yourself. Income is reported on Form T2125, CPP applies to your net, and GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once you pass $30,000 in taxable supplies.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does online tutoring in Canada suit?

Online tutoring suits people who can explain one subject clearly, keep a calendar and show up on time. You don't need a teaching certificate to tutor privately. Parents and adult learners will, however, judge you on how clearly you explain, how organised you are and how quickly you reply.

It suits:

- **University and college students** who just finished the course a high-schooler is struggling with.
- **Teachers and retired teachers** who want evening or summer income (see [side hustles for teachers](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-teachers-canada/)).
- **Bilingual Canadians**, because French immersion and core French create steady demand (see [French tutoring](/side-hustles/french-tutoring-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Professionals** in accounting, coding, nursing or engineering who can tutor exam-specific content to adults.
- **Native English speakers** who prefer adult conversation lessons (see [teach English online](/side-hustles/teach-english-online-side-hustle-canada/)).

It doesn't suit people who need guaranteed hours. September to June is the busy season, exam weeks spike, and summer goes quiet unless you sell catch-up or prep packages (see [exam season and winter tutoring](/side-hustles/exam-season-winter-tutoring-canada/)).

## How much does online tutoring pay in Canada?

The cleanest public benchmark is Job Bank's wage data for tutors under NOC 43109 ("Other instructors"), updated November 19, 2025, using 2023–2024 Labour Force Survey data. These figures are wages paid to employed tutors (for example, at tutoring centres). They are not rates charged by self-employed tutors.

*Source: Job Bank, "Tutor – elementary school subjects in Canada," wages page, checked October 9, 2026.*

Three ways to read the table:

1. **A centre's wage is your floor, not your price.** A centre pays wages and covers the marketing, booking and payment collection. When you run your own bookings, you do all of that yourself, so your rate needs to cover the unpaid time.
2. **The "high" column shows the ceiling for employees.** Specialised subjects, exam prep and adult professional content are where self-employed tutors usually price above it, but this guide doesn't publish a typical private rate. Check three local tutors who post prices for your subject and grade.
3. **The platform takes its cut before you see the money.** The next section shows how much.

## Real Canadian online tutoring examples

These are publicly documented cases. Their results are not typical or promised.

**AMC Academy (Saanich, B.C.).** CHEK News reported on January 14, 2025 that three Grade 11 students at St. Michaels University School run an online business pairing competitive-math coaches with students around the world. Founder Ethan Curtis said, "Since June we've made more than $20,000 Canadian." The Lake Cowichan Gazette (January 18, 2025) reported that Curtis, 16, started by tutoring math contests for extra money, then brought in classmate Dominic Ely, 17, to handle marketing. In a few months the project grew from one student to more than 30, with $20,000 in revenue, six coaches and 43 volunteer staff. **Lesson:** a narrow niche (contest math) found students worldwide, and the founders added help only when demand outgrew one tutor.

**Winny French (Toronto).** In an Authority Magazine interview on Medium, Winston Burron, a French specialist student at the University of Toronto, described starting online French tutoring because he couldn't find a job flexible enough to work around school. He said he had helped more than 100 students and hired fellow university students. He also said that within six months he could comfortably pay his own tuition. **Lesson:** a skill you already have, like fluent French, can become a tutoring business before you have any formal teaching credentials.

## How do you start online tutoring in Canada? A 30-day plan

**Week 1: Pick the offer and set up**
- Day 1: Choose one subject and one learner type from the list above. Write it as one sentence.
- Day 2: List what you won't teach (other grades, other provinces' curricula, essay writing for marks).
- Day 3: Set up the room: headset, light in front, plain background. Record a one-minute test call and watch it back.
- Day 4: Create a free Calendly or Square Appointments page with 60-minute slots and a 24-hour cancellation rule.
- Day 5: Set up Wave (or a spreadsheet) for invoices, plus a separate bank account for tutoring money.
- Days 6–7: Write a one-page "how lessons work" document covering length, price, cancellations, payment and what parents get after each session.

**Week 2: Get visible**
- Create one platform profile (Preply for languages and adults, Superprof for Canadian families).
- Write a profile that names the grade, curriculum and outcome, with a short intro video if the platform supports it.
- Tell 20 people you know by text or email: parents, former teachers, neighbours. Commercial emails to people who aren't already contacts generally need express or implied consent under CASL ([consent rules](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)). See [how to land your first tutoring client](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-first-client-canada/).
- Offer two short, time-capped trial sessions on your own booking page.

**Week 3: Teach and learn**
- Run your first sessions. Keep a session log: date, topic, what worked, homework set.
- Send a two-line progress note to the parent after every session.
- Ask each family when they'd like to rebook before you end the call.

**Week 4: Review and fix**
- After five paid hours, check your real hourly pay: income minus platform fees, divided by teaching plus prep plus admin hours.
- Raise your price for new students if your calendar is filling up.
- Offer a 4- or 8-session package to families who've booked twice.

### Days 31–90

- **Weeks 5–6:** Ask your best families for a review or referral. Add a second slot block (weekends or early evenings).
- **Weeks 7–8:** Build reusable materials: practice sets, a diagnostic quiz, summary sheets. These cut your prep time.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Decide whether to stay on a platform, move repeat clients to direct booking (where the terms allow), or do both.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Plan the exam-season rush and summer packages. Consider a small group session for two or three students on the same topic.

## Workflows, templates and checklists

**Before each session**
- [ ] Review last session's log and homework
- [ ] Open the whiteboard and practice problems
- [ ] Test the headset and camera
- [ ] Phone on silent, door closed

**After each session**
- [ ] Update the session log
- [ ] Send the parent or student a progress note
- [ ] Confirm the next booking
- [ ] Log the payment as received or outstanding

**Parent progress note template**

> Hi [name], today [student] and I worked on [topic]. [He/She/They] did well with [strength] and we'll keep practising [gap]. Homework: [task]. Next session: [day/time]. Thanks!

**Cancellation policy template**

> Lessons cancelled with at least 24 hours' notice can be rescheduled at no charge. Lessons cancelled with less notice, or missed, are charged in full. Packages are valid for [X] weeks from the first lesson.

**Trial session structure (30 minutes)**
1. Five minutes: goals, current mark, upcoming tests.
2. Fifteen minutes: work through one real problem from the student's homework.
3. Five minutes: show the plan for the next four sessions.
4. Five minutes: price, booking link and the cancellation rule.

**Weekly admin checklist**
- [ ] Invoices sent for the week, payments reconciled
- [ ] Platform payouts matched to bookings
- [ ] Receipts saved (headset, software, internet share)
- [ ] Calendar opened for the next two weeks

## Worked example: a 10-hour tutoring week

**Illustrative.** The rates below are assumptions for the arithmetic, not typical earnings. Platform percentages come from the help pages cited above.

Assume six direct-booking hours at $40 and four Preply hours at $40 for a tutor still in the 33% tier, with one of those four hours a trial lesson.

- Direct: 6 × $40 = $240, with no platform fee.
- Preply regular lessons: 3 × $40 × (1 − 0.33) = $80.40.
- Preply trial: $0 (100% commission).
- Gross received: $320.40.

Now count all your time. Suppose prep and admin add 30 minutes for every hour taught, so 15 hours in total. That's $320.40 ÷ 15 ≈ **$21.36 an hour** before processor fees, CPP and income tax, close to Job Bank's $20.00 national employee median. Move two Preply hours to direct booking, or cut prep by reusing materials, and the number improves. That's the main lever in this business.

## Which tax and screening rules apply to online tutoring in Canada?

Online tutoring income in Canada is self-employment income, including prepaid packs. It's reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on their net income (QPP in Quebec). GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the first-dollar [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

- **Small-supplier test:** worldwide taxable supplies, including your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add tutoring, writing, [virtual assistant](/side-hustles/virtual-assistant-side-hustle-canada/) and any other side-hustle revenue to the same total.
- **Tutoring and GST/HST:** the CRA's [Tutoring and equivalent services](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-6/tutoring-equivalent-services.html) guidance says that tutoring an individual in an elementary or secondary school course that follows a curriculum designated by a school authority is generally exempt. Tutoring without that direct curriculum link, such as general study skills, enrichment or admissions-test prep, can be taxable. Adult language lessons fall under separate rules, so check before you charge or skip tax.
- **Rates:** once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Ontario is 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% HST (since April 1, 2025, per the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table) and Alberta 5% GST. Quebec has GST plus QST. Confirm on the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Deductions:** a share of internet, the headset, software subscriptions and platform fees can be business expenses. Keep receipts.
- **Records:** keep booking messages, e-transfers, platform payout reports and your session log. A platform's payout report doesn't replace your own records.

**Screening is provincial.** In Ontario, people in a position of trust or authority over children or vulnerable persons may be asked for a vulnerable sector check, depending on the work, as described on the province's [police record checks](https://www.ontario.ca/page/police-record-checks) page. In British Columbia, the Criminal Records Review Act requires certain organizations to make sure people who work with children under 19 complete a check through the Criminal Records Review Program ([law and policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/crime-prevention/criminal-record-check/law-policy)). The [applicant process](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/crime-prevention/criminal-record-check/applicants/process) page lists a $28 fee for a new check and $0 for a volunteer check; confirm the live fee. A parent hiring you directly may still ask for a police information check. In other provinces, ask the police service that covers your address.

## Common online tutoring mistakes

- **Teaching every subject.** Generalists compete on price; specialists get referrals.
- **No cancellation rule.** Write it before the first booking, not after the first no-show.
- **Ignoring prep time.** Count it, or your real hourly pay will be far lower than your listed rate.
- **Underpricing forever.** Raise rates for new students once your calendar is mostly full.
- **Forgetting the trial math on Preply.** Trials pay nothing, so many trials and few conversions means you're working for free.
- **Mixing money.** Use a separate account so T2125 time isn't a scavenger hunt.

## What are the limits of online tutoring as a side hustle?

Tutoring income is seasonal and depends on your hours: when you stop teaching, the money stops. Platform fees can take a large share early on, especially on Preply, where every trial is unpaid. Some families stop booking when marks improve, so you'll need a steady trickle of new students. Job Bank's wages describe employed tutors, and the operator examples above are documented cases, not typical outcomes. Some tutoring is GST/HST-exempt and some isn't, and screening rules differ by province, so confirm both for your situation before you scale.
