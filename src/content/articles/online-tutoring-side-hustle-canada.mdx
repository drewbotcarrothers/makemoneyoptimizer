## Who does online tutoring in Canada suit?

Online tutoring in Canada suits people who can teach one subject they have already used, in a room where a child or an adult learner can hear every word. A headset, a calendar you will actually honour, and a willingness to say what you will not teach matter more than a long list of subjects. Online tutoring is a poor fit if you need income this week, if your evening job cannot move, or if you dislike repeating the same explanation until it lands.

School-age tutoring is a conversation with a parent as well as a student. Adult tutoring is a conversation with the learner. Pick one at the start. Name the topic, such as linear equations, and name the student's province when you know it, because course labels differ.

## How much can online tutoring earn in Canada?

Online tutoring earnings in Canada cannot be promised as an hourly rate, because a weekday adult lesson and a Sunday rush of high-school math are different products. What you keep depends on the list below, and this guide does not publish a national tutoring rate.

- How many hours you can teach without rushing the day job
- Whether the student is a direct booking or a platform that publishes its own fee

## How do you start online tutoring in Canada?

Starting online tutoring in Canada means naming one subject, writing a cancellation rule, and teaching a short trial before you buy more gear. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Name one subject and one learner

Write a single sentence: the topic, the level, and who is in the room. "High-school linear equations for a student in Ontario" is a sentence. "All subjects, kindergarten through adult" is not. If you teach in French, say so. Bilingual demand is real in many cities, and it is a different offer from English-only tutoring.

### Step 2: Write the no-list

Say what you will not do: write the assignment, guarantee a grade, provide therapy, or supervise a child as if you were a child-care program. Licensed child care is a different legal category from a private lesson. Stay on the lesson side of that line unless you have actually looked up that province's child-care rules.

### Step 3: Set the room and record one minute

Close the door. Put the light in front of your face. Record one minute and watch it. If the sound is a hallway, buy the headset before the first paid hour. Share a sample page, not your whole desktop, the first time you use screen share.

### Step 4: Write the cancellation rule before anyone asks

Pick a window, for example 24 hours, and a consequence you will actually enforce: the hour is forfeited, moved once, or charged at a smaller amount. Put it in the first message. Inventing the rule after a no-show makes the parent the editor of your business.

### Step 5: Decide direct bookings or a platform

Direct bookings from people who already know you keep the relationship and the admin. A tutoring platform trades a fee, which you must read on that platform's own page, for introductions. Use one path for the first month. Two inboxes is how a student gets forgotten.

### Step 6: Run two short trials with a time cap

Ask two people who can tell you the truth. Cap each trial at 30 or 45 minutes. Ask what was confusing. Fix the explanation, not your self-esteem. A trial is practice. It is not a promise of a full term.

### Step 7: Price three sample hours and say the number

Price a single session, a four-pack, and a no-show under your written rule. Use the illustrative assumption only as a worksheet, then replace it with a number you can defend locally. Prepaid packs need a written expiry so a family does not claim hours two years later.

### Step 8: Keep a session log and send the note

Date, topic, what the student could do at the end, and what to try before next time. Five lines is enough. The log is also your income record when nobody issues a T4A.

### Step 9: Review after five paid hours

Count hours, cancellations, and whether the subject sentence is still true. Drop a student whose needs are outside the no-list, and say why in one calm sentence. Add a second subject only after the first one has a repeat booking.

## What should the first week of online tutoring in Canada include?

The first week of online tutoring in Canada should produce a subject sentence, a cancellation rule, a one-minute recording, and two trial conversations. Paid hours can wait until the sound and the rule exist.

- **Day 1:** Write the subject sentence and the no-list. Read your province's police-check page if the learners are children.
- **Day 2:** Record one minute. Order a headset if the recording fails. Test the hotspot.
- **Day 3:** Write the cancellation rule and a four-line session note template.
- **Day 4:** Message five people who already know you can teach. Offer two trials with a time cap.
- **Day 5:** Teach one trial. Send the note the same day.
- **Day 6:** Teach the second trial, or follow up once. Price the three sample hours on paper.
- **Day 7:** Open a separate account for any deposit. Put the next two open slots on a calendar the student can see.

## What online tutoring mistakes are common in Canada?

Common online tutoring mistakes in Canada are promising a grade, teaching every subject to fill the calendar, and treating a minor's lesson as if no adult is involved. A parent or guardian should know the time, the price, and how to reach you. A private lesson is still a position of trust when the student is a child.

Doing the student's assignment for them trains the wrong person and can breach a school's rules. Teach the method. Leave the submitted work in the student's hands.

Skipping the cancellation rule fills the week with empty chairs. A platform profile that copies another tutor's credentials is a misrepresentation. List the education and experience you actually have.

Buying a semester of software before the first paid hour spends the money the headset needed. The call tool you already have is enough for week one. Change tools when a real session shows a real gap.

## Which tax and screening rules apply to online tutoring in Canada?

Online tutoring income in Canada is self-employment income, including prepaid packs, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for online tutoring follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the booking messages, the e-transfers, and the session log. Report tutoring on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing, virtual-assistant, and any other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Online tutoring is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside. A platform's payout report does not replace that record.

Screening is provincial, and a private lesson is not automatically a licensed child-care job. In Ontario, people who work or volunteer in a position of trust or authority over children or other vulnerable persons can be asked for a vulnerable sector check, based on the type of work, as described on the province's [police record checks](https://www.ontario.ca/page/police-record-checks) page. In British Columbia, the Criminal Records Review Act applies to certain organizations, which must ensure that people who work with children under 19 complete a check through the Criminal Records Review Program. The province's [law and policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/crime-prevention/criminal-record-check/law-policy) page describes that duty, and the [applicant process](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/crime-prevention/criminal-record-check/applicants/process) page lists a $28 fee for a new check and $0 for a volunteer check. Confirm the live fee. A parent hiring you directly may still ask for a police information check even when that Act does not name you. In other provinces, ask the police service that covers your address what a vulnerable-sector check requires. This guide does not state a fee for those provinces.
