After this guide you can tell whether an odd-job app exists where you live, which tasks you should accept, and how to finish a first week of small jobs without wandering into licensed trades or unpaid driving.

Odd-job apps sell your time for furniture assembly, mounting, moving help, yard work, and errands. The app finds the customer. You still bring the skill, the tools, the punctuality, and the judgment to say no. If you already know which trade you want to be known for, you may do better with direct clients: see [handyman work](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada), [house cleaning](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-side-hustle-canada), or [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada). The app is a way to meet the first customers, not a personality.

## Who it fits

**This can fit if** you are handy in a specific, honest way — IKEA assembly, TV mounting you have practiced, basic yard cleanup, or careful moving help — and you live in a city where the app has customers. It fits people who can show up on time, photograph their work, and write a clear note about what the price includes.

**This is a poor fit if** you are outside the service area, you have no tools and no plan to buy only what the first category needs, or you say yes to electrical, gas, or plumbing you are not allowed to do. It is a poor fit if you cannot lift, if you have no way to get to a suburb, or if you need income this Friday and the background check takes longer than that.

Taskrabbit’s own newsroom is the availability check worth trusting. A March 2026 release said the platform’s truck-assisted moving category was live across its active Canadian markets, including Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary, and described coverage on the order of 90-plus Canadian cities. That is not “every town.” Open taskrabbit.ca, enter your city, and see whether Taskers and categories exist. If the city is empty, Kijiji, Facebook Marketplace, and community boards are the older version of the same hustle. They are slower. They also do not take a platform fee.

## What the work really is

You are not an employee with benefits. You set or accept a rate, you travel to a stranger’s home, and you do a defined task. The platform takes a fee. The percentage changes, so read the current Tasker terms instead of memorizing a number from a review site. Customers compare you on photos, reviews, and response time. Your first five reviews matter more than a clever headline.

Categories that suit a careful beginner:

- Furniture assembly with the instructions and the right bits
- Heavy lifting alongside another person, not heroic solo piano moves
- Yard cleanup that does not require a pesticide licence
- Mounting only when you know how to find studs and what is inside the wall
- Waiting or errands, if the hourly rate still works after transit

Categories to leave alone until you are qualified: electrical panel work, gas appliances, refrigerant, structural changes, and anything a province reserves for a licensed trade. “The customer said it was fine” is not a licence. The [handyman guide](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada) goes further on that line.

## What you might earn

Rates on these apps are visible: you see an hourly or a task price before you commit. What you keep is that price, minus the platform fee, minus travel, minus the screw you stripped and had to replace. A two-hour minimum that includes a 40-minute transit ride is a different hourly rate from a job down the street.

Do not copy a rate from another city. Toronto assembly and a smaller city’s assembly are different markets. Look at what Taskers with a few reviews actually charge in your category, then sit slightly under them until you have photos of your own work. Raise the rate when your calendar is full, not when you feel brave.

There is no guaranteed number of tasks. New profiles are quiet. Your first week might be one job. Plan for that.

## Step-by-step

### 1. Confirm the city and pick one category

Search your city on the app. If it is live, choose the category you could do tonight without a YouTube panic. Assembly is the usual honest start. Moving help is honest if you are strong and you will not move a fridge alone down icy steps.

### 2. Read the fee, the cancellation rule, and the insurance offer

Write down the platform fee, who pays it, and what happens if the customer cancels at the door. Some platforms offer a liability policy for tasks booked in the app and exclude tasks you take off-platform. Read the exclusions: vehicles, certain trades, and “I also repaired the outlet” are classic gaps. If the policy does not cover the work you will do, get your own quote before you accept it.

### 3. Build a tiny proof gallery

Assemble one piece of furniture in your own home, or help a friend, and take bright photos: before, tools, finished, and a close-up of a square corner. You do not need a lifestyle brand. You need evidence you finish the job.

### 4. Set a rate that includes travel

Decide your minimum task size and the neighbourhoods you will serve. Publish a rate you can say out loud. If the app lets you add an explanation, say what you bring (tools, shoe covers) and what you do not do (electrical, gas, plumbing).

### 5. Buy only the tools that category needs

Drill, bits including a Robertson — Canadian furniture and electrical cover plates still love a square drive — level, tape, stud finder, pencil, and a small hardware tin. Robertson bits are the non-obvious purchase. A kit imported with only Phillips bits will stall on a Canadian closet.

### 6. Accept a small first task

Say yes to a simple assembly or a short list, at a time you will not rush. Arrive ten minutes early, confirm the scope at the door, and do not expand into “while you’re here, can you look at the breaker.” That sentence is how unlicensed work starts.

### 7. Close the job in writing

In the app, note what you completed. If something could not be finished because parts were missing, say so before you leave, and record it. Future-you wants that when a review says you “didn’t finish.”

### 8. Ask for the review, then wait

One calm sentence is enough. Do not offer a discount for stars. Do not argue with the first lukewarm review in public. Fix the process.

### 9. Graduate repeat customers carefully

If someone wants you back off-app, decide on purpose. You may keep more of the fee, and you may lose the platform’s insurance and the paper trail. Invoice properly, and keep the same scope limits. The [handyman guide](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada) is the next step when those requests become the business.

## Your first week

- **Day 1:** City check. Pick one category. Read fees and insurance exclusions.
- **Day 2:** Photos of one practice assembly. Write a 60-word profile that names what you will not do.
- **Day 3:** Tools you actually lack, and only those. Shoe covers. Separate bank account.
- **Day 4:** Profile live. Set hours you can truly make, including transit.
- **Day 5:** Apply to tasks or wait for invitations. Reply quickly and specifically. “I can do this Thursday at 6, and the price includes assembly of one dresser, not mounting” is a good reply.
- **Day 6:** Do the job. Leave the space cleaner than you found it.
- **Day 7:** Log hours, travel, fee, and net. Decide whether a second category is earned. Usually it is not, not yet.

## Tips that are easy to miss

Canadian hardware is mixed. IKEA uses its own fasteners. Older homes use Robertson screws. Drywall anchors that came with a TV mount are often the wrong ones for plaster or for a tiled wall. Look at the wall before you drill, not after.

Parking in the core can cost more than the bit set. Ask about parking in the chat. A $15 garage is part of the quote if you cannot avoid it.

“Quick mount” and “the bracket is somewhere” are different jobs. If parts are missing, the clock and the scope should pause while the customer decides. Do not donate an hour to a scavenger hunt unless the rate includes it.

Shoe covers, a cloth, and a magnet for dropped screws sound fussy. They are how you get the second booking from a careful client.

Winter changes moving help. Icy stairs, salt in the hallway, and a sofa that will not fit the turn are normal. Walk the path before you lift.

## Common mistakes

Selecting every category so you look busy. You will be invited to jobs you cannot do, and the first review will say so.

Underpricing to win, then resenting the bus ride. Price the trip or shrink the map.

Doing electrical “just this once.” Provincial rules exist for a reason, and your platform insurance may exclude it anyway.

Taking cash off-app at the door for a task the platform booked, against that platform’s rules. You can lose the account. If you want direct clients, build them honestly later.

No photos and no scope note. Memory is a bad invoice.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Odd-job income is generally self-employment income, whether the app paid you or a client e-transferred you. Keep records. A T4A may or may not arrive. Report the income either way. File business details on the T2125 with your return, and budget for both shares of CPP on the net.

The GST/HST small-supplier test applies. You generally do not have to register while worldwide taxable supplies — yours and your associates’ — stay at $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Cross it in one quarter and you start charging on the supply that put you over. Cross it over several quarters and the CRA’s timing runs to the end of the following month. Read the [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page. This is not the ride-sharing “first dollar” rule. That rule is for passenger apps, explained in the [rideshare guide](/side-hustles/rideshare-driving-side-hustle-canada).

Once you are registered, the rate follows the province: HST in some, GST plus PST in others, GST plus QST in Quebec. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025. Check the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Put the tax you collect aside. It is not wages.

Trades rules are provincial. Ontario’s Electrical Safety Authority, provincial colleges of trades or their successors, and gas and plumbing regulators do not yield because a task was booked on an app. Quebec’s rules on construction and renovation are their own system. If a job needs a permit or a ticket, decline it or partner with someone who has the ticket, in the way the law allows. Municipal business licences are a separate check. Some cities want one before you advertise home services. Look up your municipality.

Workers’ compensation is another provincial trap, especially if the work looks like construction. In Ontario, WSIB has rules that can apply to construction even for sole proprietors. If you are building or renovating rather than assembling a bookcase, check the board in your province before you price the job.
