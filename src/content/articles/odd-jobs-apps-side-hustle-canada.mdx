Odd-job apps like Taskrabbit let you sell your time for furniture assembly, TV mounting, moving help, yard work and errands to people near you. In Canada, Taskrabbit charges Taskers a one-time $25 registration fee, puts its service fee on the client's bill, and lets you keep 100% of the hourly rate you set plus tips. Your real earnings depend on three things the app doesn't control: whether your city has demand, how fast and tidy you work, and how much of each job disappears into travel.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Tax:** app income is self-employment income on T2125, with both CPP shares on net profit and GST/HST registration once taxable supplies pass $30,000.

## Who are odd-job apps a good fit for?

Odd-job apps suit people who are handy in a specific, honest way, live where the app has customers, and can show up on time with the right tools. You don't need a trade ticket for assembly, light moving or yard cleanup, but you do need to be careful in strangers' homes.

Good fits:

- **People who already assemble furniture for themselves and friends.** Assembly is the most common entry category.
- **Students and shift workers** who want short jobs on their own schedule. Taskrabbit lets you set your availability and opt out of categories.
- **Car owners**, because a vehicle widens your map and opens moving and hauling tasks.
- **Retirees and tradespeople** with tools and judgment who want occasional work.
- **Newcomers** building Canadian references, as long as the app is open in their city and their work permit allows self-employment.

It's a poor fit if you live outside a served area, don't own a single tool, can't lift safely, or need guaranteed hours. A quiet first week is normal.

## Where does Taskrabbit operate in Canada?

Taskrabbit's March 24, 2026 press release says its truck-assisted moving category is live in all active Canadian markets, more than 90 cities, including Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal and Calgary. ## How does Taskrabbit pay Taskers?

Taskrabbit uses three earning structures, according to its September 15, 2025 Tasker blog post:

| Structure | How it pays | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| Self-Set Hourly | You set an hourly rate per category and keep 100% of it plus 100% of tips | Most categories, including cleaning, moving and furniture assembly |
| Task-Based | A fixed payment per task, shown up front | IKEA assembly and partner tasks |
| Pre-Set Hourly | A pre-set hourly rate | Direct-hire jobs where a client picks you |

A few rules matter for your math:

- **Fees sit on the client's invoice.** Taskrabbit's support page says the client sees an hourly rate that includes your rate plus its percentage service fee, and a separate Trust & Support fee is added. So the rate a client sees on your profile is higher than what you set.
- **No add-on charges for travel.** The February 2026 Tasker update introduced a Pricing Transparency Policy: your rate should account for gas, tolls, parking, stairs and vehicle costs, and charging those separately isn't allowed. Reimbursed expenses, such as hardware you buy at the client's request, go 100% to you.
- **Payouts** usually arrive in 1 to 3 business days via Stripe.
- **Opt out** of categories with a pricing structure you don't want under Skills & Rates in the Tasker app.
- **Referrals:** the 2026 update offered a $25 payout when someone you refer invoices their first task.

## What does the math look like?

These examples are **Illustrative**. The hourly rates, job lengths, tips and travel are assumptions; fuel uses an assumed $1.70 a litre and 9 L/100 km. Taskrabbit's service and Trust & Support fees are paid by the client, so they don't reduce these numbers.

**Illustrative: a Saturday dresser and bed frame assembly in Toronto by transit.**

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Self-set rate (assumed) | $40.00/hour |
| Time on site | 2.5 hours |
| Task pay (2.5 × $40) | $100.00 |
| Tip (assumed) | $10.00 |
| Transit, round trip (assumed) | $7.00 |
| **Profit before tax** | **$103.00** |
| Total time including 1 hour of travel | 3.5 hours |
| **Profit per hour** | **$29.43** |

**Illustrative: the same job by car, 30 km round trip.** Fuel is 30 × 0.09 L × $1.70 = $4.59, plus $6 of parking (assumed). Profit is $110.00 − $10.59 = $99.41 over 3.25 hours, about $30.59 an hour, before wear on the car.

**Illustrative: a fixed-price assembly that runs long.** A task-based job pays a set amount however long it takes. If one pays $60 (assumed) and you finish in 1.5 hours, that's $40 an hour; if a missing part stretches it to 3 hours with travel, it's $20 an hour. Track your times on fixed-price items so you know which ones to accept.

**Illustrative: a monthly side income.** Six tasks a month at the first example's $103 profit each is $618 for about 21 hours, about $29.43 an hour. Subtract the one-time $25 registration fee and tools from the first month.

|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 3 | $70 | $210 |
| Middle | 6 | $103 | $618 |
| High | 12 | $120 | $1,440 |

## What tasks can you offer? 20 ideas

1. **Flat-pack furniture assembly:** the most common entry category, with steady demand from IKEA, Wayfair and Structube buyers.
2. **Wardrobe and closet systems:** big, slow jobs that suit people who are patient and precise.
3. **Bed frames and bunk beds:** frequent move-in requests.
4. **Office chairs and desks:** quick wins for home-office setups.
5. **Patio furniture and barbecues:** seasonal spring demand.
6. **TV mounting on drywall:** only if you know how to find studs and choose anchors.
7. **Shelves, mirrors and art hanging:** small, repeatable, good for reviews.
8. **Curtain rods and blinds:** common for new renters.
9. **Moving help (labour only):** loading and unloading a client's rental truck.
10. **Heavy lifting:** moving a couch upstairs or a treadmill to the basement.
11. **Packing and unpacking:** no lifting limit, good for careful organisers.
12. **Furniture disassembly before a move:** pairs with moving help.
13. **Yard cleanup and leaf raking:** no spraying; see the pesticide note below.
14. **Snow shovelling:** winter demand in most Canadian cities.
15. **Garage and basement decluttering:** often leads to a hauling job.
16. **Errands and pickups:** Marketplace purchases, returns, store pickups.
17. **Waiting in line:** passports, product drops, event tickets.
18. **Smart-home device setup:** video doorbells and Wi-Fi cameras that don't need hard wiring.
19. **Childproofing:** cabinet latches and furniture anchors.
20. **Event setup and takedown:** chairs, tables and decor for parties.

Leave electrical panel work, hard-wired fixtures, gas appliances, refrigerant, structural changes and anything your province reserves for a licensed trade. "The customer said it was fine" isn't a licence. In Ontario, read the province's rules on [pesticides and home lawns and gardens](https://www.ontario.ca/page/pesticides-home-lawns-and-gardens) before offering any spraying.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

**Week 1: check demand and sign up.**
- Enter your postal code on taskrabbit.ca and note which categories show local Taskers and what they charge.
- Read the registration fee page, the [Fees, Payments, and Cancellation Supplemental Terms](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260514951579-Fees-Payments-and-Cancellation-Supplemental-Terms) and the Taskprotect terms.
- Apply and pay the $25 fee. Paying doesn't guarantee approval.
- Open a separate bank account for payouts. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian option.

**Week 2: build proof and set rates.**
- Assemble one piece of furniture at home or for a friend. Photograph the tools, the finished piece and a square corner.
- Write a 60-word bio naming what you do and what you don't (electrical, gas, plumbing).
- Set your rate at or slightly below Taskers with a few reviews in your category. Remember the client sees your rate plus Taskrabbit's fees.
- Pick neighbourhoods you can reach in under 30 minutes.

**Week 3: take the first three tasks.**
- Accept small jobs at times you won't rush: one bookshelf, one bed frame, one shelf hang.
- Arrive five to ten minutes early, confirm scope at the door, wear shoe covers, and take away packaging if agreed.
- Log hours, travel and payout for every task.

**Week 4: review and adjust.**
- Calculate profit per hour, including travel, and compare it with Job Bank's $25.00 handyperson median.
- Raise your rate if you're booked solid; shrink your map if travel eats the margin.
- Decide whether to add a second category, such as mounting or moving help.

The day-by-day version is in [your first week on odd-job apps](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-first-week-canada/), and per-category rates are in [how much to charge on odd-job apps in Canada](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-pricing-canada/).

## Templates and checklists

**Profile bio:**

> I assemble flat-pack furniture (IKEA, Wayfair, Structube), hang shelves and art, and help with small moves in [neighbourhoods]. I bring a drill, square and Phillips bits, a level, a stud finder and shoe covers, and I take away packaging on request. I don't do electrical, gas or plumbing work.

**Reply to a task invite:**

> Hi [name], thanks for the invite. I can do this [day] at [time]. To confirm: [items] assembled, packaging removed, nothing wired or plumbed. Is there parking or an elevator I should know about? If any parts are missing I'll stop the clock and let you decide.

**Arrival checklist:**

- [ ] Shoe covers on at the door
- [ ] Confirm the item list and the room
- [ ] Check boxes for missing or damaged parts before starting
- [ ] Ask what's behind the wall before drilling (pipes, wires, tile)
- [ ] Lay a drop cloth or flattened box under the work
- [ ] Photograph the finished job

**Wrap-up message:**

> All done: [items] assembled and checked, packaging [removed/left by the door]. [Note any missing part or issue]. Thanks for booking. If you're happy with the work, a review helps a lot.

## What mistakes do new Taskers make?

- **Selecting every category.** You'll be invited to jobs you can't do, and the first review will say so.
- **Pricing travel at zero.** Under Taskrabbit's Pricing Transparency Policy you can't add travel charges later, so build it into your rate or shrink your map.
- **Taking cash off-app for a task the platform booked.** It's against the rules and can cost you the account.
- **Doing electrical or plumbing work once.** Provincial trade rules still apply, and Taskprotect may not help.
- **No photos and no scope note.** Memory is a bad invoice.
- **Ignoring old-building hardware.** Plaster, tile and concrete need different anchors from drywall; older homes also use Robertson screws.

## What about damage, insurance and safety?

Taskrabbit's support pages say it doesn't insure users. Under Taskprotect, Taskrabbit may, at its discretion, offer up to $1,000 for damage or injury arising from a task. Clients must claim against their own insurance first, and claims must be made within 30 days ([Taskprotect terms](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260436180379-Taskprotect-Terms-Conditions)). A dropped 75-inch TV can exceed that, and off-app jobs aren't covered at all. Get a quote for commercial general liability insurance before you mount expensive screens or work off-platform.

Lift heavy items with a partner and a dolly; the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety's [lifting guidance](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/lifting-general.html) recommends planning the lift, keeping the load close, avoiding twisting and using carts.

## How do taxes and trade rules apply?

Income from odd-job apps is generally self-employment income, whether the app paid you or a client e-transferred you. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) even if no slip arrives, and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Deductible costs can include the registration fee, consumables, the business share of phone and vehicle costs, and parking; tools usually go through capital cost allowance. See [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/) and [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count all side-hustle revenue. Odd jobs aren't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply. Once registered, use the rate for the province where you supply the service; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) pages. For direct clients, <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> or <AffiliateLink program="wave" label="Wave" /> can produce invoices that show tax once you're registered.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** 13% HST once registered. Pesticide rules apply to yard work; check [Skilled Trades Ontario](https://www.skilledtradesontario.ca/) before any work that looks like a trade.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered. Construction work in Quebec is regulated by the Commission de la construction du Québec; keep to assembly, moving help and errands unless you hold the right licence.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** GST plus a separate PST; check whether PST applies to your services.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered.
- **Atlantic provinces:** HST; Nova Scotia's rate is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Workers' compensation** is provincial. Renovation-type work can bring you into your province's board even as a sole proprietor, so check before you take it on.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Taskrabbit's city averages are client-facing estimates from its cost guides (updated October 1, 2026) and include fees you don't receive; your own payout depends on the rate you set and how many invites you win. Job Bank wages describe employees in 2023 to 2024, not app workers. Public profile rates are self-published and change. Fees, policies and Taskprotect limits are as of October 9, 2026, and Taskrabbit says it can change its fees at any time. Amazon.ca prices were read on October 9, 2026. Hourly rates, tips, travel and task counts in the worked examples are assumptions.
