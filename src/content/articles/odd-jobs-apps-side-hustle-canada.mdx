## Who are odd-job apps a fit for in Canada?

Odd-job apps are a poor fit outside the service area, without tools for one category, without a way to lift or reach a suburb, when the task is electrical, gas, or plumbing reserved for a licensed trade, or when a background check will outlast the week the money is needed.

## What work do odd-job apps actually assign in Canada?

Odd-job apps in Canada are generally set up as self-employment: you set or accept a rate, you travel to a stranger’s home, and you do a defined task. The platform takes a fee, and the percentage changes, so read the current Tasker terms instead of a number from a review site.

Customers compare you on photos, reviews, and response time. Employment status can still depend on the platform’s terms and on provincial tests, so treat “self-employed” as the usual setup and confirm your own situation if a form or a contract says otherwise.

Categories that suit a careful beginner:

- Furniture assembly with the instructions and the right bits
- Heavy lifting alongside another person, not a solo piano move

Leave electrical panel work, gas appliances, refrigerant, structural changes, and anything a province reserves for a licensed trade. “The customer said it was fine” is not a licence. ## How do you start odd-job apps in Canada?

Start odd-job apps in Canada by confirming the city, reading the fee and the insurance exclusions, and accepting one small task inside a category you could do tonight. Assembly is the usual honest start. Moving help is honest if you are strong and you will not move a fridge alone down icy steps.

### 1. Confirm the city and pick one category

Search your city on the app. If the app is live, choose the category you could do tonight without a panic search.

### 2. Read the fee, the cancellation rule, and the insurance offer

Write down the platform fee, who pays it, and what happens if the customer cancels at the door. The percentage changes, so use the terms in your account. Some platforms offer a liability policy for tasks booked in the app and exclude tasks you take off-platform. Read the exclusions. Vehicles, certain trades, and an extra repair the customer added at the door are classic gaps. If the policy does not cover the work you will do, get your own quote before you accept the task.

### 3. Build a tiny proof gallery

Assemble one piece of furniture in your own home, or help a friend, and take bright photos: before, tools, finished, and a close-up of a square corner. You need evidence you finish the job.

### 4. Set a rate that includes travel

Decide your minimum task size and the neighbourhoods you will serve. Publish a rate you can say out loud. If the app lets you add an explanation, say what you bring (tools, shoe covers) and what you do not do (electrical, gas, plumbing).

### 5. Buy only the tools that category needs

Drill, bits including a Robertson — Canadian furniture and electrical cover plates still use a square drive — level, tape, stud finder, pencil, and a small hardware tin. A kit with only Phillips bits will stall on a Canadian closet.

### 6. Accept a small first task

Say yes to a simple assembly or a short list, at a time you will not rush. Arrive ten minutes early, confirm the scope at the door, and do not expand into the breaker panel. That sentence is how unlicensed work starts.

### 7. Close the job in writing

In the app, note what you completed. If something could not be finished because parts were missing, say so before you leave, and record it. You will want that note when a review says the job was unfinished.

### 8. Ask for the review, then wait

One calm sentence is enough. Do not offer a discount for stars. Do not argue with the first lukewarm review in public. Fix the process.

### 9. Move repeat customers off the app only on purpose

If someone wants you back off-app, decide on purpose. You may keep more of the fee, and you may lose the platform’s insurance and the paper trail. Invoice properly, and keep the same scope limits. ## What is easy to miss on odd-job apps in Canada?

Hardware, parking, missing parts, and winter stairs change the real hourly result of odd-job apps in Canada. Shoe covers, a cloth, and a magnet for dropped screws are how a careful client books you again.

Canadian hardware is mixed. IKEA uses its own fasteners. Older homes use Robertson screws. Drywall anchors that came with a TV mount are often the wrong ones for plaster or for a tiled wall. Look at the wall before you drill.

Parking in the core can cost more than the bit set. Ask about parking in the chat. A garage fee you cannot avoid belongs in the quote.

“Quick mount” and “the bracket is somewhere” are different jobs. If parts are missing, the clock and the scope should pause while the customer decides. Do not donate an hour to a scavenger hunt unless the rate includes it.

Winter changes moving help. Icy stairs, salt in the hallway, and a sofa that will not fit the turn are normal. Walk the path before you lift.

## How do taxes and trade rules apply to odd-job apps in Canada?

Income from odd-job apps in Canada is generally self-employment income, whether the app paid you or a client e-transferred you: keep records, report the income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) even if a T4A never arrives, and budget for both shares of [Canada Pension Plan contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on the net. GST/HST follows the small-supplier test of $30,000 or less of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates, which is separate from [GST/HST for taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html). After registration, charge Nova Scotia’s 14% HST ([charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)), add Quebec’s QST on top of GST, confirm other rates on the CRA [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and check the provincial trade authority before electrical, gas, plumbing, or structural work.

This guide is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Read the CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page for the timing when you cross the threshold in one quarter versus over four quarters. The ride-sharing first-dollar rule is for passenger apps, explained in the [rideshare guide](/side-hustles/rideshare-driving-side-hustle-canada/). Put the tax you collect aside. Confirm the current QST rate before you add QST to a Quebec invoice.

Trade rules are provincial. A task booked on an app does not replace a licence. Electrical, gas, plumbing, and structural work are often reserved for licensed trades. Check the provincial trade authority. If a job needs a permit or a ticket, decline it or partner with someone who holds the ticket, in the way the law allows. Some cities want a business licence before you advertise home services. Look up your municipality.

Workers’ compensation is another provincial question, especially if the task looks like construction or renovation rather than assembling a bookcase. Check that province’s board before you price the job.

In Ontario, pesticide application on a yard task is covered by the province’s rules for [pesticides and home lawns and gardens](https://www.ontario.ca/page/pesticides-home-lawns-and-gardens). Yard cleanup that stays off spraying avoids that question.
