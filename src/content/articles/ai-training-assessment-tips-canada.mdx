AI training assessments are the unpaid or lightly described gate in front of project work: you write, rank, or code under a time box, and the platform decides whether you see tasks. In Canada they suit someone who can block a quiet hour, write precise English, and tolerate a rejection with no second starter attempt. They do not suit someone who wants to click through on a phone between meetings. The companies' own FAQs are the rules. Community pass-rate claims are not.

Platform context is the [DataAnnotation review](/side-hustles/dataannotation-review-canada/), the [Outlier review](/side-hustles/outlier-ai-review-canada/), and [how to start AI training](/side-hustles/ai-data-annotation-side-hustle-canada/). If you are still choosing the kind of work, read [AI training vs paid studies vs freelancing](/side-hustles/ai-training-vs-studies-vs-freelancing-canada/).

## What do the assessment pages actually require in Canada?

DataAnnotation's [FAQ](https://www.dataannotation.tech/faqs), opened October 6, 2026, says:

- You can take the starter assessment only once. There are no retakes.
- Most starter assessments take about an hour. Specialist assessments (coding, math, chemistry, biology, physics, finance, law, medicine, languages) may take one to two hours.
- Workers typically hear within a few days. The company says it reviews for thoroughness and accuracy rather than speed, and that taking time to craft answers improves your chances.
- You pick a track at signup. General work asks for strong writing and critical thinking. The FAQ's baseline is a bachelor's degree or equivalent experience. Coding asks for programming experience and, in the skills answer, the ability to solve LeetCode-style problems. STEM asks for a master's or PhD, or a bachelor's plus 10 or more years. Professional tracks ask for law, finance, or medicine credentials.
- The FAQ does not say the assessment is paid. It says PayPal deposits start after you request them for work, within a few days.
- The FAQ does not name Canada. A testimonial on the marketing site is not an eligibility rule. If the form rejects a Canadian address, stop.

Outlier's [FAQ](https://outlier.ai/faq) says:

- You need at least an associate degree. Some projects need a bachelor's, master's, or PhD.
- Onboarding typically takes 30 to 90 minutes: account, expertise, skill screenings, and identity verification. Project-specific onboarding comes after that.
- The selection process is selective. The company says it looks for deep knowledge and significant experience.
- You need a valid ID and a mobile phone from the country where you live, a current resume, and a LinkedIn profile.
- You work as an independent contractor. Outlier does not sponsor visas. If you are on a visa, the FAQ says the visa must allow the work.
- You see the tasking rate before you start. Rates vary by expertise, project, and location. The FAQ refuses a single number.
- The [full-stack coding role](https://outlier.ai/coding/fshc) lists Canada among countries it is accepting. That is a role page, not a promise that every Outlier project is open. Read the role you are submitting.

Neither FAQ publishes a pass rate, a score cutoff, or a sample of a winning answer. This guide will not invent them.

## How do you prepare for an AI training assessment in Canada?

### Step 1: Pick the track you can prove

DataAnnotation says you choose the starter assessment, and you only get one. A coding assessment that expects LeetCode-style problems is a poor place to "see what happens" if you do not write code. Outlier says it reviews the resume against the domain. A LinkedIn page that does not match the degree you claim is a weak application, because both documents are in the required set.

### Step 2: Block the hour the FAQ describes

DataAnnotation says most starter assessments take about an hour and specialist ones one to two hours, and that rushing hurts. Put the phone in another room. One sitting beats three interrupted ones, because you cannot restart the starter assessment.

### Step 3: Answer the question that is on the screen

The FAQ says reviewers look for thoroughness and accuracy, and that general work is writing, reasoning, and fact-checking. Explain the judgment the prompt asks for. A short reply that could fit any prompt is the failure mode the "take your time" sentence is warning you about.

If the assessment screen forbids outside tools, follow that screen. This guide will not claim a blanket ban the FAQ does not print, and it will not suggest a workaround. Using a tool the instructions forbid is a reason to reject the attempt you cannot retake.

### Step 4: Use your own network for the identity check

DataAnnotation uses Persona. The FAQ says to turn VPNs off, including a campus or office VPN you did not install, to use a physical unexpired government photo ID, and to take the selfie without glasses, with your face fully turned when asked. A failed photo can be retried. A second person on your account is not a retry. Outlier asks for ID and a phone from the country where you live. Do not VPN into a US address to match a job post that does not list Canada.

### Step 5: Set PayPal up before you are accepted

DataAnnotation pays PayPal to the email on the account. Outlier pays weekly on Tuesdays by PayPal, Airtm, or ACH. ACH is a US bank rail. Canadians should expect PayPal or Airtm unless the dashboard offers a Canadian deposit. Enable USD. The bookkeeping is in [earning in USD from US platforms](/side-hustles/usd-platform-income-tax-canada/). A simple ledger in <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> or a spreadsheet is enough once money actually arrives. You do not need accounting software to take an unpaid assessment.

### Step 6: Keep other work while you wait

DataAnnotation says a silence can mean the application is still in review or was not accepted, and that it cannot reply to every applicant. Outlier says projects may not match your field and that project length depends on the customer. A passed assessment with an empty dashboard is still a possible outcome. Keep [Prolific](/side-hustles/prolific-review-canada/) or a freelance pitch moving. Do not quit a job because an hour-long form felt good.

## What do the advertised rates mean before you have passed?

They are the company's "starting at" or "up to" lines, and the DataAnnotation FAQ does not even agree with itself. General projects are described as starting at $25–$50+ per hour in one answer and $25–$30+ in another. Coding is $40–$150+ in one answer and $50 to $100 in another. The dollar sign is not labelled CAD or USD on that FAQ. Outlier's coding page says up to $50 USD per hour and warns that onboarding can pay less. You see the rate on the task after you are in. None of those sentences is a wage for the assessment hour.

### Illustrative example

Assumed outcome, for the arithmetic only. Suppose the DataAnnotation starter assessment takes the FAQ's "about an hour" and is unpaid, which is the assumption this guide uses because the FAQ never calls it paid. Suppose you then do 3 accepted hours in a later week at an assumed US$20 an hour. Gross is US$60. At the [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) rate of 1.4254 CAD per US dollar on October 5, 2026, US$60 is about $85.52 CAD, before PayPal conversion. The week of the assessment itself was $0. If you are not accepted, every hour stays $0. The US$20 is not a published typical rate. It is a placeholder so the unpaid gate is visible.

## What mistakes do Canadian applicants make?

- **Spending the only starter attempt tired.** DataAnnotation says there is no second one.
- **Choosing a specialist track to chase the top of the range.** The FAQ ties those tracks to credentials. A failed specialist hour is still gone.
- **Quoting "up to $50" as your rate.** Onboarding and ordinary tasks can pay less. Read the number on the task.
- **A VPN during the ID check.** DataAnnotation's FAQ tells you to turn it off, including a network you did not configure.
- **One platform.** Outlier says available projects may not match your field. DataAnnotation says access depends on demand and past quality.
- **Treating a homepage payout total as your income.** The FAQ says the company has paid over $20 million to contractors since 2020. That is a marketing total. It is not a wage.

## Which tax rules apply if the assessment turns into pay?

The assessment hour, if unpaid, is not income. Pay that follows is generally business income in Canadian dollars on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). A US platform may ask for Form W-8BEN because you are not a US person. That form goes to the payer. It does not file your T1. The GST/HST small-supplier threshold is $30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, and it is not an income-tax exemption. The short version is [the $30,000 GST/HST myth](/side-hustles/gst-hst-30000-threshold-myth-canada/).
