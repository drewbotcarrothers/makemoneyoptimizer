Instacart’s Canadian shopper pages are the requirements this guide will state. Other apps come and go by city. If no store appears near you, the week is a wait, not a secret shift. ## What does the first week of grocery shopping in Canada involve?

The first week of grocery shopping in Canada involves an account, approval, and only then a batch you choose because you know the store. Instacart’s Calgary South page says you create an account, photograph your licence and enter bank details in the app, and start seeing orders once the account is approved. The same page also says you can start shopping in as soon as 1 hour, and that the payment card usually takes 5 to 7 business days. Hold both sentences. You cannot honestly check out without the card. A marketing hour is not a promise that the card is in the mailbox.

Shop one small batch at a store you already walk. A 40-item order at a warehouse you have never seen is a bad first data point.

Instacart’s Canadian requirements, on the [Calgary South shopper page](https://shoppers.instacart.ca/instacart-jobs-in-calgary-south-ab), are 18 or older, legal authorization to work in Canada, the ability to lift up to 40 lb (18 kg) without accommodation, regular access to a reliable car, and a smartphone that can run the Shopper app. The same FAQ says the documents are your driver’s licence, social insurance number, and bank account information. Treat “1099” on that marketing page as a label to ignore, and follow the CRA pages below.

The page says full-service shoppers are paid per batch, that the estimate depends on order size, driving distance, and effort, and that shoppers keep 100 percent of tips. It also says total earnings depend on the individual. This guide does not turn that sentence into a wage. Zones and stores change. The store list in the app is the availability check. The Calgary page’s store names are an example of one zone, not a national roster.

## What should you track on the first grocery batches in Canada?

On the first grocery batches in Canada, track the estimate you accepted, the clock from store arrival to the door, the kilometres, parking you paid, and any tip that was still there after delivery. A preview tip is not wages until the order is done and the amount remains.

### Step 1: Time the shop and the drive separately

Aisle time and door-to-door time tell you different things. A long shop in a store you know is a layout problem. A long drive is a distance problem.

### Step 2: Write substitutions and refunds

Note what the customer asked and what you did. That note is how you see whether the batch was careful or rushed.

### Step 3: Write meters, ice, and spills

A downtown meter and a leaked jug belong in the same log as the payout. The app total will not include them.

**Example batch log.** This blank is not a real shopper’s record.

> Date and store. Batch estimate: $__ . Start and finish: __ . Kilometres: __ . Parking: $__ . Tip still showing after delivery: $__ . Items refunded: __ . Notes: stairs, a 40 lb case, a substitution.

## How do you judge whether a first grocery batch was worth it in Canada?

You judge a first grocery batch in Canada by the estimate you accepted minus parking and a fuel figure you chose, divided by the minutes the batch actually took. You do not set the customer’s price. You accept or decline the batch. Heavy items, an unknown store, and unpaid circling for parking are reasons to decline even when the estimate is higher.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose a batch estimate shows $18, the shop and delivery take 75 minutes, and you drive 6 kilometres. Suppose you assume fuel at $0.18 a kilometre, which is an assumption, not a national rate. Fuel is $1.08. Suppose the meter was $3. The remainder is $13.92 before wear, a phone, and a tip that might change. Divided by 1.25 hours, the sketch is about $11.14 an hour. A second batch that adds another hour of waiting in the lot makes the hour thinner. Do not copy $18 or $0.18. This is not a typical result and not a quote.

## What does a 14-day plan for grocery shopping in Canada look like?

A 14-day plan for grocery shopping in Canada should produce an approved account and, if the card has arrived, one small logged batch at a familiar store. Silence from the mailbox is a result. This plan does not promise batches, tips, or a cash-out.

### Days 1 and 2: Confirm the zone and call the insurer

Create the account only if the app shows stores you can reach. Read the requirements on Instacart’s Canadian shopper page. Tell your insurer about paid delivery before you photograph the licence.

### Days 3 to 5: Finish signup and wait for the card

Enter the bank details the app asks for. Do not shop with your own debit card because the Instacart card has not arrived. Instacart says you do not pay for the groceries yourself.

### Days 6 to 9: One small batch, or still waiting

If you are approved and the card is in hand, accept one modest order at a store you know. Frozen food goes in the insulated bag last. If you are still waiting, do not buy a wagon “to look ready.”

### Days 10 to 14: Read the log

Subtract parking and your fuel assumption from the estimates you accepted. Divide by hours. If you never got a card, the decision is whether to keep waiting on the same account. Do not open a second grocery app to escape a five-day mail window.

## Which tax rules apply to the first grocery batches in Canada?

Grocery shopping income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed shoppers pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, including associates and your other hustles, of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over four consecutive quarters. Under it, registration is usually optional. If you also carry passengers, those fares follow the stricter rule in [GI-196](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-196/gst-hst-commercial-ride-sharing-services.html). Keep batch summaries. An app may send a T4A. Confirm any rate you must charge with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Liquor or other age-restricted items follow provincial law and the app’s ID steps. When in doubt, refund the item.
