After this budget you can start one odd-job category without building a tool wall, see how a platform fee and a cross-city trip change a two-hour task, and decide what has to wait until a neighbour pays you directly.

Which cities, which tasks to refuse, and the first week are in the [odd-job apps guide](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-side-hustle-canada). This page is the money. A “full toolkit” is how people spend the first month before they have a first month.

## The quick answer

Ranges are typical Canadian retail for a single beginner category such as furniture assembly. They are not a quote. They assume you can travel by transit or a car you already own. They do not include a van.

| How you start | One-time cash, about | What that is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $80–$180 | A compact drill-driver, a bit set that includes Robertson (square) bits, a level, and shoe covers. You borrow a stud finder or buy a cheap one only if you mount things. |
| Typical | $200–$450 | A better driver, a stud finder, a small hardware assortment, work gloves, and a furniture dolly only if you accepted moving help. |
| Doing it properly | $400–$900, plus a liability quote | The typical kit for one category, a few replacement bits, and commercial liability insurance if the platform policy does not cover the work you will actually do. Read the exclusions before you treat the app’s policy as yours. |

Taskrabbit’s own newsroom, in a March 2026 release, described active Canadian markets including Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary, and coverage on the order of 90-plus Canadian cities. That is not every town. Open the app and search your city. If the city is empty, the budget below still applies to the same jobs booked on Kijiji or a community board, minus the platform fee and minus any insurance the app would have provided.

The platform fee changes. Read the current Tasker terms. Do not memorize a percentage from a review site.

## Who this budget fits

**It fits** if you can do one honest skill tonight — IKEA assembly, careful TV mounting you have practiced, basic yard cleanup, or lifting beside another person — and you live where customers exist. Photos of finished work matter more than a second drill.

**It is a poor fit** if you are outside the service area, if you will say yes to electrical, gas, or plumbing you are not allowed to do, or if you need income this Friday and the background check takes longer than that. Buying tools for a licensed trade you cannot legally perform is not a startup cost. It is a refusal.

Direct clients in the same skills are priced in [handyman startup costs](/side-hustles/handyman-startup-costs-canada). Recurring indoor work is [house cleaning startup costs](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-startup-costs-canada). Outdoor seasons are [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-startup-costs-canada) and [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-startup-costs-canada).

## Ongoing costs, and a simple break-even

**Platform fee.** Subtract whatever the current terms take before you call a rate “yours.” If the customer pays $40 an hour and the app keeps a slice, your contribution starts from what lands in the payout, not from the sticker rate.

**Travel.** A $70 assembly across the city can net poorly. Use transit fare or your real fuel and a wear set-aside. Include the trip both ways. A two-hour minimum that hides 40 minutes of transit is a different hourly rate from a job down the street.

**Consumables.** Anchors, driver bits, shoe covers, and the screw you stripped. Often $5–$25 on a task that needs hardware, and $0 on a pure assembly where the customer bought the furniture and the hardware is in the box. Say in the price who supplies parts.

**Insurance.** A monthly or annual premium if you buy your own. If you rely on the platform, re-read the exclusions when you add a category. “I also changed the outlet” is a classic gap.

**Phone.** The plan you have, unless maps push you over the cap.

There is no guaranteed number of tasks. A new profile can be quiet. Your first week might be one job. Plan the budget for that, not for a full calendar.

### How to calculate break-even

1. One-time tool cost for the one category.
2. Monthly fixed: insurance, phone add-on, invoicing software if you subscribed.
3. Variable cost of one task: platform fee already removed from the payout, plus travel, plus hardware you were not reimbursed for.

Contribution = payout received minus travel and unreimbursed parts.

Tasks to earn back the drill = tool cost ÷ contribution per task.

If you price hourly, convert travel into the rate before you compare yourself with another Tasker. Do not copy a Toronto rate onto a smaller city.

### Illustrative example

**Pretend task, for the arithmetic. Not a going rate.**

You spent $160 on a driver, bits, a level, and shoe covers. You have no insurance premium in this sketch because you read the in-app policy, the task is covered, and you are not working off-app. One assembly pays you $110 after the platform fee. You took transit both ways for $7. You used $4 of anchors the customer did not have. Time on site was 2 hours. Door to door was 3 hours.

Contribution = $110 − $7 − $4 = $99. Per hour door to door, this sketch is $33. Tool payback = $160 ÷ $99, or about two tasks like it.

Change the transit to a 40 km round trip. At 8 L/100 km and $1.60/L, fuel is 40 × 0.08 × 1.60 = $5.12, plus a wear set-aside of $0.15/km, which is $6. The contribution falls by a few dollars and the hour gets worse because the drive took time. A task that pays $110 before a long drive and a platform fee you forgot to subtract is how the drill stays unpaid. Log payouts, not profile rates.

If you add a $300-a-year liability policy, that is $25 a month to cover before tools pay back. Still cheaper than a broken television.

## Where you can spend less

One category. Assembly does not need a ladder, a saw, and a pressure washer.

Buy a used driver if it has a battery that holds a charge. Test it in the parking lot. A dead used tool is not a bargain.

Use the customer’s furniture hardware first. Your assortment is for the missing anchor, not for rebuilding their kitchen.

Stay inside a transit or short-drive radius until reviews exist. The map is a cost control.

Skip invoicing software until someone pays you outside the app. Skip a website. The profile is the storefront.

## Where not to cut corners

Liability. If the in-app policy excludes the task, get a quote or decline. A scuffed hardwood floor costs more than a year of a basic policy. This is not insurance advice.

The stud finder and the right anchor, if you mount anything. The cheap failure is a television and a child in the room.

The line between handy and licensed. Do not buy electrical or gas tools so you can “just help.” Provinces reserve that work. The [handyman guide](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada) is the longer version of the same limit. Quebec’s construction rules are their own system. Do not import another province’s forum post.

Shoe covers and a drop cloth. The mess is part of what they hired you not to leave behind.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Odd-job income is self-employment income whether the app paid you or a neighbour e-transferred you. A platform may issue a T4A. Your own record still matters. Report it on form T2125 with your T1. Both shares of CPP generally apply to the net.

**Expenses and CCA.** Bits, anchors, transit, and shoe covers are current expenses. Keep receipts. A drill that will last for years may be capital rather than a full deduction in the week you bought it. Capital cost allowance (CCA) is how the CRA lets you deduct that kind of asset over time, on the business portion, in the class for the year. Small tools and larger machines are not always treated alike. Look up the year’s rule or ask an accountant before you expense a workshop. If you drive, the log is date, destination, kilometres, and purpose. Business kilometres divided by total kilometres is the percentage. Do not claim the family car in full.

**GST/HST.** This is not ride-sharing. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and any lawn, cleaning, or repair income. Under that, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the CRA’s timetable, explained on the [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page.

**Input tax credits.** If you are registered, GST/HST on business purchases can often be claimed back as an input tax credit to the extent of commercial use. Receipts should show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Provincial rates differ. Quebec charges GST and QST. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025. Other provinces use HST or GST plus PST. Confirm on the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and do not spend the tax.

Search your city for a business licence. Fees are municipal. Use the figure on the city’s page.
