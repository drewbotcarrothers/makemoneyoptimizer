## How do ongoing costs change odd-job app break-even in Canada?

Ongoing costs for odd-job apps in Canada start from the payout after the platform fee, then travel, unreimbursed parts, insurance, and the phone plan. Odd-job apps in Canada do not guarantee a full calendar, so plan the first week as possibly one task.

**Platform fee.** Subtract whatever the current terms take before you call a rate yours. The percentage changes. Read the current Tasker terms. If an assumed sticker rate is $40 an hour and the app keeps a slice, contribution starts from what lands in the payout, not from the sticker rate. The $40 figure is an assumption, not a typical rate.

**Travel.** A $70 assembly across the city can net poorly. Use transit fare or your real fuel and a wear set-aside. Include the trip both ways. A two-hour minimum that hides 40 minutes of transit is a different hourly result from a job down the street.

**Consumables.** Anchors, driver bits, shoe covers, and the screw you stripped. Often $5–$25 on a task that needs hardware, and $0 on a pure assembly where the customer bought the furniture and the hardware is in the box. The $5–$25 band is a typical range to verify locally. Say in the price who supplies parts.

**Insurance.** A monthly or annual premium if you buy your own. If you rely on the platform, re-read the exclusions when you add a category. An added outlet repair is a classic gap.

**Phone.** The plan you have, unless maps push you over the cap.

You spent $160 on a driver, bits, a level, and shoe covers. You have no insurance premium in this sketch because you read the in-app policy, the task is covered, and you are not working off-app. One assembly pays you $110 after the platform fee. You took transit both ways for $7. You used $4 of anchors the customer did not have. Time on site was 2 hours. Door to door was 3 hours.

Contribution = $110 − $7 − $4 = $99. Per hour door to door, this sketch is $33. Tool payback = $160 ÷ $99, or about two tasks like it.

Change the transit to a 40 km round trip. At 8 L/100 km and $1.60/L, fuel is 40 × 0.08 × 1.60 = $5.12, plus a wear set-aside of $0.15/km, which is $6. The contribution falls by a few dollars and the hour gets worse because the drive took time. A task that pays $110 before a long drive and a platform fee you forgot to subtract is how the drill stays unpaid. Log payouts, not profile rates.

## How do taxes apply to odd-job app costs in Canada?

Income from odd-job apps in Canada is self-employment income whether the app paid you or a neighbour e-transferred you: a platform may issue a T4A, your own record still matters, and the income still goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with the T1, and both shares of [Canada Pension Plan contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) generally apply to the net. GST/HST is separate from the ride-sharing first-dollar rule ([taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html)); the small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and any lawn, cleaning, or repair income ([when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). After registration, charge Nova Scotia’s 14% HST ([charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)), add Quebec’s QST on top of GST, confirm other HST or GST-plus-PST rates on the CRA [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and check the provincial trade authority before electrical, gas, plumbing, or structural work.

This guide is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Bits, anchors, transit, and shoe covers are current expenses. Keep receipts. A drill that will last for years may be capital rather than a full deduction in the week you bought it. [Capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) is how the CRA lets you deduct that kind of asset over time, on the business portion, in the class for the year. Small tools and larger machines are not always treated alike. Look up the year’s rule or ask an accountant before you expense a workshop. If you drive, the log is date, destination, kilometres, and purpose. Business kilometres divided by total kilometres is the percentage. Do not claim the family car in full.

Under the small-supplier threshold, registration is usually optional. Over that threshold, registration follows the CRA’s timetable. If you are registered, GST/HST on business purchases can often be claimed back as an input tax credit to the extent of commercial use. Confirm the current input-tax-credit rules with the CRA. Receipts should show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit. Do not spend the tax you collect. Confirm the current QST rate before you add QST to a Quebec invoice.

Search your city for a business licence. Fees are municipal. Use the figure on the city’s page.
