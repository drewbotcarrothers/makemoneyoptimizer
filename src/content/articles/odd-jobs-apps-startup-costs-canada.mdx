Starting on odd-job apps in Canada, meaning furniture assembly, mounting, moving help, yard work and errands booked through an app like Taskrabbit, costs a registration fee and the tools for one category. What you're budgeting for is a drill and Robertson bits for assembly, shoe covers for strangers' floors, a stud finder and level before you mount anything, knee pads and gloves for long jobs on the floor, and liability insurance because the app's damage program isn't insurance. Amazon.ca prices below were checked on October 9, 2026. Taskrabbit's fees are from its Canadian support pages the same day.

Which cities, which tasks to refuse, and the first week are in the [odd-job apps guide](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-side-hustle-canada/). This page is the cash. A van isn't in any kit.

## How much does it cost to start odd-job apps in Canada?

Odd-job apps in Canada cost $139.07 for Taskrabbit's $25 registration and an assembly kit, $239.16 adding a stud finder, level, knee pads and gloves, $689.16 with a starting liability policy, or $787.35 with a dolly and lifting straps for moving tasks. Goods include 13% Ontario HST. The registration fee and insurance are added as listed. Use your province's rate.

| Kit | What's in it | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean assembly | Taskrabbit registration ($25) + BLACK+DECKER 20V MAX LD120VA drill with 30 accessories ($69.98) + DEWALT FlexTorq Square/Robertson bits, 10-pack ($9.98) + Hitituto non-slip shoe covers, 100 ($20.99) | $139.07 |
| Standard | Lean + CRAFTSMAN CMHT77633 stud finder ($17.59) + CRAFTSMAN 24 in. box beam level ($20.00) + COOLJOB work knee pads ($29.99) + WZQH leather work gloves ($20.99) | $239.16 |

| Pro | Standard with insurance + VEVOR furniture dolly, 2-pack ($55.90) + two-person moving and lifting straps ($30.99) | $787.35 |

The arithmetic: lean goods are ($69.98 + $9.98 + $20.99) = $100.95, × 1.13 = $114.07, plus $25 = $139.07. Standard adds $17.59 + $20.00 + $29.99 + $20.99, so $189.52 × 1.13 = $214.16, plus $25 = $239.16. With insurance, $239.16 + $450 = $689.16. The pro additions are $55.90 + $30.99 = $86.89, × 1.13 = $98.19, for $787.35.

The pillar guide prices a cheaper DEKOPRO 20V drill kit at $49.99 and a DEWALT 12V MAX kit at $169.00 the same day. The LD120VA is the middle option, with a bit set in the box.

## Who should use an odd-job app startup budget in Canada?

An odd-job app budget fits people who can do one skill well tonight, in a city where the app has clients, and who can travel by transit or a car they already own. The lean kit fits furniture assembly. The standard kit fits assembly plus shelves, curtain rods and TV mounting. The pro kit fits people who also accept moving tasks.

It's a poor fit if the plan is electrical or plumbing work, where provincial trade rules apply, or a full toolkit before the first review.

Repairs for your own clients are [handyman startup costs](/side-hustles/handyman-startup-costs-canada/). Labour-only moves are [moving help startup costs](/side-hustles/moving-help-startup-costs-canada/).

## What does Taskrabbit take from each task?

Nothing from your rate, per its Canadian support pages on October 9, 2026. Taskrabbit's [service fee page](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260411872155-What-s-the-Taskrabbit-Service-Fee) describes the service fee as charged to clients, and the pillar guide's reading of the support pages is that you keep 100% of your self-set hourly rate and tips in most categories. IKEA and partner tasks pay a fixed amount per task instead. Payouts usually arrive in 1 to 3 business days through Stripe.

An earlier version of this page told you to subtract the app's percentage from your rate. On Taskrabbit in Canada, the client pays the fee, so it doesn't come out of your rate, but it does raise what the client sees. Other apps set their own terms; read them before you set a price.

The cost that does come out of each task is **travel**. A $70 assembly across the city by car can net poorly once fuel, parking and the drive both ways are counted.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for odd-job apps in Canada?

**Taskprotect isn't insurance.** Taskrabbit's [Taskprotect terms](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260436180379-Taskprotect-Terms-Conditions) describe a program under which Taskrabbit may offer up to $1,000 at its discretion. It doesn't replace your own liability coverage.

**Liability insurance.** Zensurance's [handyman insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/contractor-insurance/handyman) says a small handyman business on residential projects typically starts at $450 a year for $2 million in commercial general liability. That's an estimate, not a quote. Ask about the categories you'll actually accept, including mounting and moving. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance advice.

**Trades and licences.** Electrical, gas and most plumbing work fall under provincial trade rules, whatever the app lets you list. Some cities licence home-based or contracting businesses. Search your city's bylaws.

### A first-month sketch

**A teaching sketch, not a forecast or a typical result.**

Suppose you pay the $25 registration, buy the lean tools for $114.07, and carry insurance at $450 a year, or $37.50 a month. You complete the pillar guide's six assembly tasks in the month at $103 profit each, $618 for about 21 hours.

$618 − $25 − $114.07 − $37.50 = $441.43 before a tax set-aside, or about $21.02 an hour in month one. Month two doesn't carry the registration or the drill. Do not copy $40 an hour.

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Travel** to every task: transit fares, or fuel, parking and wear on a car.
- **Consumables:** anchors, bits and shoe covers. Refill locally.
- **Insurance,** if you carry it, renewed yearly.
- **Fixed-price tasks that run long.** A set amount for a job with a missing part is a lower hourly rate.
- **Unpaid scope.** "Since you're here" mounting with no stud finder is how a cheap task becomes a claim.

## Where can odd-job app startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Pick one category.** Assembly needs a drill and bits, not a toolbox.
- **Borrow a stud finder and level** until mounting is regular.
- **Take transit** to compact neighbourhoods instead of driving across the city.
- **Skip moving gear** unless you accept moving tasks.
- **Buy consumables locally** as you need them.

## Where should odd-job app spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't skip the stud finder before you mount anything. Don't skip shoe covers in a client's home. Don't rely on Taskprotect as insurance. Don't accept trade work you aren't licensed for.

Keep task payouts apart from household money. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account that can hold payouts until the tax set-aside is moved.

## Which tax rules apply to an odd-job app budget in Canada?

Income from odd-job apps in Canada is self-employment income whether the app paid you or a neighbour e-transferred you, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. The registration fee, bits and shoe covers are generally current expenses. A drill may be a current expense or [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) depending on its cost and life. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html). If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST. Once registered, use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
