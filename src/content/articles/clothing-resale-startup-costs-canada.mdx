## How much does clothing resale cost to start in Canada?

Clothing resale in Canada starts at about $15–$50 when the stock is your own closet, about $50–$150 with a proper shipping kit, and about $250–$700 once you buy inventory. The fees in the table above are charged when an item sells, not before.

| How you start | First-month cash, about | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closet clear-out | $15–$50 | A few mailers, tape, and a soft measuring tape |

| With inventory | $250–$700 | The kit, $150–$500 of thrifted stock in one style, and a plain backdrop or rail for photos |

Per-sale clothing resale costs in Canada are the platform fee, the mailer, and on Depop, the label you buy. There is no monthly subscription in the paths this guide covers.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for clothing resale in Canada?

Break-even for clothing resale in Canada is the kit cost divided by what each sale nets after the fee, the mailer, and what you paid for the item. Five steps get you there.

### Step 1: Start with your own closet

Items you already own cost nothing in cash, so the first sales pay back the kit.

### Step 2: Write the fee before you list

Price each item from the fee backward. Under C$20, Poshmark's flat C$3.95 is a large share.

### Step 3: Add the mailer and, on Depop, the label

Weigh the packed parcel before you set the shipping price.

### Step 4: Subtract the stock cost

For thrifted items, subtract what you paid. Write it on the tag or in a sheet.

### Step 5: Stop buying when the bin is full of unsold stock

Unsold stock is cash sitting in a bin. Drop prices or donate before you buy more.

### Illustrative example

Suppose the kit costs $60 and you sell four thrifted items at C$30 each on Poshmark, each bought for an assumed $8. Poshmark's fee is 20% × $30 = $6 per item, and a mailer is $1. Each sale nets $30 − $6 − $8 − $1 = $15. Four sales net $60, which pays back the kit. If one item sells at C$18 instead, Poshmark's flat C$3.95 applies, and that sale nets $18 − $3.95 − $8 − $1 = $5.05. These figures are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## Where can clothing resale startup spending be lower in Canada?

Clothing resale startup spending in Canada can be lower by selling your own clothes first, using a wall and natural light instead of a backdrop, and reusing clean mailers. A scale is the one tool worth buying early if you ship by weight.

Skip courses, bulk mailer cases, and bought "mystery bundles" until your own sales show what moves.

## Where should clothing resale spending not be cut in Canada?

Clothing resale spending in Canada should not be cut on honest condition checks, a measuring tape, and safety rules for children's items. Removing a drawstring costs nothing. Listing cotton children's sleepwear you cannot verify can cost the sale and the buyer's trust.

## Which tax rules apply to a clothing resale budget in Canada?

Clothing resale income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase notes, payout reports, PayPal statements, and supply receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Reselling clothes is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
