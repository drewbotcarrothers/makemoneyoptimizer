Starting clothing resale in Canada, meaning listing used clothes, shoes and accessories on apps like Poshmark and Depop and shipping each sale, costs very little before the first sale. What you're budgeting for is mailers and a tape measure for the first listings, a scale once you ship by weight, a steamer and lint roller because creased, fuzzy clothes photograph badly, a bin so stock stays findable, and later a rack and a tripod for faster photos. ## How much does clothing resale cost to start in Canada?

Clothing resale in Canada costs $25.70 for mailers and a measuring tape when you sell from your own closet, $170.73 adding a shipping scale, a steamer, a storage bin and lint rollers, or $254.50 with a rolling rack, a phone tripod and a tape gun. Goods include 13% Ontario HST. Use your province's rate. None of these include thrifted stock or the phone you already own.

| Standard | Lean + Fuzion 86 lb shipping scale ($29.99) + BEAUTURAL handheld steamer ($41.97) + clear 62 L latch storage bin ($30.40) + PetLovers lint roller set ($25.99) | $170.73 with HST |

The arithmetic: lean is ($16.75 + $5.99) = $22.74, × 1.13 = $25.70. Standard adds $29.99 + $41.97 + $30.40 + $25.99, so $151.09 × 1.13 = $170.73. Pro adds $29.99 + $25.99 + $18.15, so $225.22 × 1.13 = $254.50. With insurance, $254.50 + $450 = $704.50.

Thrifted stock is working capital, not a kit line. If you set aside an assumed $200 for one style of stock after your closet sells, that money comes back as items sell, or sits in the bin if they don't.

## Who should use a clothing resale startup budget in Canada?

A clothing resale startup budget in Canada fits people with clothes worth more than C$20 each, a phone that takes clear photos, and the patience to measure every item. The lean kit fits a one-time closet clear-out. The standard kit fits someone listing every week and shipping by weight. The pro kit fits someone sourcing thrifted stock in one style and photographing in batches.

It's a poor fit if the plan is buying mystery bundles before you know what sells, or if most of your clothes would list under C$20, where Poshmark's flat C$3.95 is a large share.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for clothing resale in Canada?

**Insurance.** Not required by the platforms to list. A home-based liability policy starts at about $450 a year for a $2 million limit, per Zensurance's estimate, which is more than the whole pro kit, so most closet sellers wait until they hold stock or sell at markets. Your home insurance may not cover business stock. Ask your insurer.

**Children's items.** Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says all drawstrings should be removed from children's clothing and that children's sleepwear has flammability requirements. Removing a drawstring costs nothing. Listing cotton children's sleepwear you can't verify can cost the sale and the buyer's trust.

**Business licence.** Some cities licence home-based businesses. Search your city's bylaws before you scale up.

**Platform reporting.** Since 2024, platforms report seller information to the CRA under the [digital platform reporting rules](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/reporting-rules-digital-platforms.html), with an exclusion for goods sellers with fewer than 30 sales totalling no more than $2,800 in the period. That exclusion isn't a tax exemption.

### A first-month sketch

**A teaching sketch, not a forecast or a typical result.**

Suppose you buy the lean kit for $25.70 and sell six items from your own closet on Poshmark: four at an assumed $30 and two at $18. The $30 sales pay 20%, or $6, and keep $24 each. The $18 sales pay the flat C$3.95 and keep $14.05 each. Mailers are about $0.17 each.

Kept = (4 × $24) + (2 × $14.05) = $96 + $28.10 = $124.10. After six mailers ($1.02) and the lean kit ($25.70): $124.10 − $1.02 − $25.70 = $97.38. Items you bought for yourself and sold for less than you paid generally aren't income; see the tax section. Bundling the two $18 items into one $36 listing would change the fee from $7.90 to $7.20.

## Where can clothing resale startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Sell your own closet first.** Zero stock cost.
- **Reuse clean mailers and boxes** that arrive at your door.
- **Use a wall and window light** instead of a backdrop or light box.
- **Skip the scale** if every sale is a light top on Poshmark under 2.5 kg.
- **Skip courses, bulk mailer cases and mystery bundles** until your own sales show what moves.

## Where should clothing resale spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't cut honest condition checks, measurements or the children's-item safety rules above. A return or a bad review costs more than a tape measure.

## Which tax rules apply to a clothing resale budget in Canada?

Selling clothes you bought for yourself is generally personal-use property: under the CRA's Schedule 3 rules, if both the cost and the sale price are $1,000 or less, there's no gain or loss to report. Buying clothes to resell is a business, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. Mailers, fees and lint rollers are generally current expenses. A rack or scale that lasts for years may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) depending on its cost. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase notes, payout reports, PayPal statements and supply receipts. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit. Depop's tax help page says it collects GST/HST from Canadian buyers on sales within Canada; check how your platform handles tax before you register.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless the platform collects. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). More detail is in [tax on reselling and platform income in Canada](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).
