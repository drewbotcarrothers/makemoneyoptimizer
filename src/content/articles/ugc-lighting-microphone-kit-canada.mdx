## What should you buy first for UGC?

Buy a microphone first, then a light, then a better stand. Phone cameras are good enough for most UGC briefs, but phone microphones pick up room echo, and an underlit kitchen at 5 p.m. in a Canadian winter makes footage grainy.

| Priority | Why | Budget pick | Upgrade pick |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Microphone | Viewers forgive soft video before muffled audio | BOYA BY-M1, $21.67 | DJI Mic Mini 2-mic kit, $109.99 |
| 2. Soft light | Even skin tones and accurate product colour | EMART softbox, $59.99 | Elgato Key Light Neo, $99.99 |
| 3. Stand | Steady frame and repeatable angles | UBeesize tripod, $25.99 | Amazon Basics 60-inch, $42.55 |
| 4. Overhead or motion | Unboxing, cooking and walking shots | NEEWER DS026 overhead, $49.99 | DJI Osmo Mobile 7P gimbal, $145.97 |

## Which microphone should a UGC creator buy?

Pick a wired lavalier if you film seated and on a tight budget, a wireless clip-on system if you move or film two people, and a premium wireless kit only if you also record on a camera and want onboard backup recording.

## What should Canadian UGC creators check before buying?

- **Your phone's port.** A USB-C receiver won't fit a Lightning iPhone and a 3.5 mm lav needs an adapter on most current phones. Match the receiver to your phone before you order.
- **Winter light.** Short days mean window light runs out early in November to February. A key light with adjustable colour temperature lets you keep shooting after dark and still match daytime clips.
- **HST and receipts.** Prices on Amazon.ca are before tax; Ontario adds 13% and other provinces charge different rates ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)). Keep the invoices for your T2125.
- **Third-party sellers.** Some Amazon.ca listings are sold by third parties, which can change the return window and warranty handling. Check the seller line on the product page.

## How do taxes treat UGC gear?

Small accessories and supplies used for the business are generally deductible ([CRA business expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses.html)). Lasting equipment is claimed through capital cost allowance. CRA's [classes of depreciable property](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html) puts tools costing less than $500 in Class 12 (100%), but Class 12 excludes electronic communication and data processing equipment, so check the class for each item; a light stand and a wireless microphone may land in different classes. If you also use the gear personally, claim only the business-use share. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Prices on Amazon.ca change often, and some listings above are third-party offers; each price is what Amazon.ca showed on October 9, 2026. Specs such as range, battery life and CRI are the sellers' claims from the listings, not independent measurements, and the products were not tested by this site. The payback table rests on an assumed rate and equipment share; your own rates, revision load and how often you reuse the gear will change it.
