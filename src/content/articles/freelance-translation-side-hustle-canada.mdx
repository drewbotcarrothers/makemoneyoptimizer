Freelance translation in Canada is paid work rendering documents, websites and marketing copy from one language into another, most often English to French or French to English, for businesses, nonprofits, agencies and individuals. Canadian providers publish rates from $0.13 to $0.30 a word, the Literary Translators' Association of Canada recommends $0.25 a word for most literary work, and Job Bank puts the median translator wage at $33.95 an hour. You can start with free tools and a strong command of your target language; machine translation is changing the market, so specialization matters more than ever.

## Who does freelance translation suit?

Freelance translation suits people who are truly fluent in two languages, write naturally in the target language, and enjoy checking terminology until it is right. It is a poor fit if you are "conversational" rather than fluent, dislike close editing, or would be tempted to paste text into a machine translator and send it unchecked.

Bilingual is not the same as translator. Translation means producing text a native reader would accept as written in their language, with the right tone and terminology. Most professional translators work into the language they write best, and clients and revisers notice the difference.

## Do you need to be certified to translate in Canada?

You don't need certification to offer ordinary translation services, but you can't use a protected "certified" title unless a provincial body has granted it. Some documents, such as those submitted to certain government offices, require a certified translation, so check the receiving office's requirements before accepting that kind of job.

According to [CTTIC](https://www.cttic.org/certification/), titles such as "Certified Translator" are granted by provincial regulatory bodies, CTTIC administers the national exams outside Quebec, and the titles are recognized by legislation in New Brunswick, Ontario and British Columbia. [ATIO](https://atio.on.ca/membership/certification/) says "Certified" is a reserved title in Ontario, available only to members who have passed the national certification exam or the on-dossier process, and lists the certification exam at $725.00 plus tax per language combination. In Quebec, [OTTIAQ](https://ottiaq.org/en/future-members) says its members have the exclusive right by law to use certified translator (C. Tr.) under Quebec's Professional Code.

Certification pays for itself if you want official-document work: WordFrog, for example, advertises ATIO-certified French-to-English translation of official documents from $75. For a beginner, the practical rule is simple: describe yourself as a translator, list your language pair, and take non-certified work until you decide whether certification is worth pursuing.

## What does the math look like?

**Job fee = source words × rate per word**, and **effective hourly = fee ÷ (translating hours + revision hours)**. Your throughput, how many words an hour you translate and revise well, is the number that decides whether a rate works.

**Illustrative.** The per-word rates are the published figures above; the words-per-hour throughput is an assumption that varies widely by subject and experience.

| Scenario | Words | Rate | Fee | Hours (assumed) | Effective hourly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Web page at WordFrog's starting rate | 2,000 | $0.13 | $260 | 5 | $52.00 |
| Web page at the low end of Traduction AC's range | 2,000 | $0.18 | $360 | 5 | $72.00 |
| Same page, unfamiliar subject | 2,000 | $0.18 | $360 | 10 | $36.00 |
| Literary prose at the LTAC rate | 60,000 | $0.25 | $15,000 | 300 | $50.00 |

The third row is the reason to specialize: research time can halve your hourly rate on the same fee. A side hustler who translates 2,000 words a week at $0.18 earns $360 a week, or about $1,440 over a four-week month.

## What can you translate? 18 niches for Canadians

Pick two or three subjects you already understand. These niches suit Canadian translators, each with a reason.

1. **Websites for businesses entering Quebec.** Companies selling in Quebec need French versions of their sites.
2. **Retail and e-commerce product descriptions.** High volume and repeatable; how Lise Alain's agency started.
3. **Nonprofit and charity communications.** National organizations publish in both official languages.
4. **Municipal and school board notices.** Bilingual communities in New Brunswick, Ontario and Manitoba need them.
5. **Marketing and social media localization.** Adapting tone and idiom, not just words.
6. **HR policies and employee handbooks.** Bilingual workplaces need consistent policy documents.
7. **Tourism and hospitality.** Menus, brochures and tour scripts for visitors.
8. **Technical manuals.** Engineering or IT background is an advantage, and rates follow.
9. **Financial reports and investor communications.** Accuracy-critical; pays more.
10. **Health and wellness content.** Clinics and nonprofits; avoid clinical documents unless trained.
11. **Legal and official documents.** Requires certification for most official uses; pursue it through ATIO or OTTIAQ.
12. **Immigration documents in other language pairs.** Newcomers need certified translations of records; see [side hustles for newcomers](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-newcomers-canada/).
13. **Indigenous-language projects.** Huiping Zhang's company built a specialty here; fluent speakers are scarce.
14. **Literary translation.** LTAC's recommended rates apply; publishers and grants fund it.
15. **Subtitles and video scripts.** Courses, YouTube channels and corporate videos.
16. **App and software strings (localization).** Short strings in a CAT tool; steady agency work.
17. **Machine translation post-editing (MTPE).** Agencies offer it at lower rates; know your minimum.
18. **Bilingual proofreading and revision.** Checking another translator's work, billed hourly.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

### Week 1: Direction, subjects and tools

- Choose your direction (into your strongest language) and two or three subjects you know.
- Install OmegaT and build a glossary for each subject from TERMIUM Plus.
- Start the Antidote and DeepL free trials only if you will use them this month.

### Week 2: Build three samples

- Translate public texts you're allowed to use (a government page, a public-domain article, a mock product page) in each subject.
- Keep source and target side by side and ask a fluent reader to check them.
- Time yourself so you know your words per hour with revision.

### Week 3: Offer and outreach

- Write a rate card (template below) and a one-page profile with your language pair, subjects and samples.
- Apply to three translation agencies; most send a test. Read your provincial association's pages and decide whether certification is a goal.
- Pitch ten direct clients: local businesses with English-only sites, nonprofits, municipalities.

### Week 4: Deliver and build repeat work

- Use the job brief below for every project, deliver on time, and keep the translation memory.
- Ask satisfied clients for a testimonial and whether other teams need translation.
- Review your throughput and adjust your minimum fee.

## What mistakes do new translators make?

- **Using a "certified" title you don't hold.** It's a protected title in Ontario and Quebec.
- **Translating out of your strongest language.** Work into the language you write best.
- **Sending unrevised machine output.** Clients and agencies can tell; ask before using machine translation at all.
- **No minimum fee.** A 50-word job still takes setup time.
- **Ignoring Canadian conventions.** Canadian French differs from European French in vocabulary, number formats and spelling of some terms.
- **Taking legal or medical work too early.** Errors there carry real consequences; build up to it.

## Which tax rules apply to freelance translation in Canada?

Freelance translation income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Software subscriptions, dictionaries, association fees, certification exam fees and a home-office share can be deductible; [how to report side hustle income to the CRA](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/) walks through the forms.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Once registered, use the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table for Canadian clients. For foreign clients, the CRA's page on [GST/HST on imports and exports](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-imports-exports.html) says certain services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated, and you must keep evidence of the customer's non-residency.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Quebec:** Job Bank's translator median is highest here at $36.26. QPP replaces CPP, QST applies alongside GST once registered, and OTTIAQ controls the C. Tr. title.
- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered; ATIO controls the "certified" title and lists the national exam at $725.00 plus tax per combination.
- **New Brunswick:** Canada's only officially bilingual province, and one of the three provinces where CTTIC says certification titles are recognized by legislation; HST is 15%.
- **British Columbia:** certification titles are recognized by legislation, per CTTIC; PST is separate from GST.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Machine translation is reshaping this market. CBC [reported on March 20, 2025](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/ottawa/translation-bureau-to-cut-a-quarter-of-its-workforce-over-next-5-years-1.7488601) that the federal Translation Bureau planned to cut 339 positions, about a quarter of its workforce, through attrition between 2025 and 2030 as it leaned on AI. In an August 21, 2025 collection of translators' accounts published by [Blood in the Machine](https://www.bloodinthemachine.com/p/ai-killed-my-job-translators), an anonymous Quebec French-to-English freelancer said their income fell 60% in 2024 and was tracking 80% below 2020–2023 levels in 2025. Specialized, certified and creative work is less exposed than generic text. The per-word rates above are self-published by individual firms or recommended by an association, not survey averages; Job Bank wages describe employees and use 2023–2024 data; Upwork's ranges are U.S.-dollar profile rates before fees; the throughput in the math is an assumption. The tax section is general information, not advice for your situation.
