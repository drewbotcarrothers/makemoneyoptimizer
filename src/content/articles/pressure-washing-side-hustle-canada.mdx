## Who does pressure washing in Canada suit?

Pressure washing in Canada suits people who can carry a machine to a driveway, wear boots that grip wet concrete, and say no to a roof. A garage or a shed where the pump will not freeze, and a season long enough to use the machine, matter more than a wrapped trailer. Pressure washing is a poor fit if your city gives you no legal place for the wastewater, if you will work alone on a ladder, or if the only demand is two thaws a year and you financed a commercial unit.

Frozen weather is not a detail. A pump left full of water in a Canadian cold snap can crack. Store it the way the manual describes, and do not book a route in a week the hose will freeze solid.

## Where can pressure-washing water go in Canada?

Pressure-washing water in Canada is a municipal sewer question, and plain water is not automatically allowed in a storm drain. Toronto's page on [washing cars and outdoor surfaces](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/water-environment/water-sewer-related-permits-and-bylaws/sewers-by-law/washing-cars-outdoors-surfaces/) says wastewater from outdoor washing must not reach catch basins, including sidewalks, alleys, and building washes, and that power washing with water alone can still dislodge contaminants. The page also says the person hiring and the mobile wash business are both responsible.

Read your own city's sewer or environmental page before you quote. If the water from the driveway runs to a street grate, the job needs a different method or a different city rule, not a shrug. Roofs, painted siding, and wood decks are easier to damage than concrete. Leave them off the first menu.

## How do you start pressure washing in Canada?

Starting pressure washing in Canada means reading the sewer page, practising on concrete you own or have permission to mark, and selling driveways before you sell decks. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Name the surface

Concrete driveways and walks. Write the no-list beside it: roofs, ladders, painted siding you have not tested, and wood until you have a reason and a practiced hand. The no-list is the safety plan.

### Step 2: Read where the water may go

Use your city's page. Toronto readers can start with [washing cars and outdoor surfaces](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/water-environment/water-sewer-related-permits-and-bylaws/sewers-by-law/washing-cars-outdoors-surfaces/). If the water's path is a catch basin, do not take the job until you have a method the page allows.

### Step 3: Read the machine manual on a dry day

Note the tip that came in the box, the power rating, and the storage warning about freezing. Point the wand down and away from people, glass, and yourself. A narrow tip on close range marks concrete. Find that out on your own pad.

### Step 4: Price three real driveways

A single-car pad, a double, and a corner lot with a long walk. Add the cleaner and the time to coil the hose. If you are far cheaper than a local operator, you missed setup or travel.

### Step 5: Get an insurance quote that names the surfaces

Say residential concrete, whether you use a cleaner, and that you do not wash roofs. Ask whether a home policy covers the business. This is not insurance advice.

### Step 6: Wash one pad you are allowed to learn on

Time it. Photograph the before and after in daylight. Note where the water actually travelled. If it entered a grate, the method is not ready for clients.

### Step 7: Offer the service to five households

People who already know you, or houses on a street you can reach without a long drive. Leave a one-page note with the surface, the price, and the water limit.

### Step 8: Do the first paid driveway as a drill

Confirm the tap, parking, and pets. Keep the wand moving. Stop if the surface is not the concrete you priced. Invoice the same day.

### Step 9: After three jobs, decide on attachments

A surface cleaner can speed a large pad. Buy it when the hours say so, not because a video used one. Winterize the pump before the first hard freeze, the way the manual says.

## What is easy to miss with a pressure washer in Canada?

Pressure washing in Canada goes wrong when the wand pauses on one spot, when cleaner sits on plants, and when the machine is stored wet in an unheated shed. Keep the tip moving, rinse plants if the label says to, and follow the manual's freeze warning before a cold night.

Pre-treat oil spots only with a product meant for them, and still expect a shadow. Tell the client a shadow may remain. Glass and outdoor outlets are not targets. Cover or avoid them, and do not run the cord through standing water.

A neighbour's car downwind will catch the mist. Park and aim with that in mind, or ask them to move the car. The apology after the fact does not un-spot the paint.

## What pressure washing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common pressure washing mistakes in Canada are buying a commercial washer before any client, sending dirty water to a catch basin, and taking a roof because the client asked. Storage and a loan do not care that the route is still a theory.

Toronto's outdoor-washing page treats power-washing wastewater as something that must stay out of catch basins. Other cities write their own rule. Guessing is how a bylaw complaint starts.

Wood, mortar, and old paint come off faster than people expect. The first paid surfaces stay concrete. A second person on the job, if you hire one, is a workers' compensation question in that province. Check the board before you call the person a favour.

## Which tax rules apply to pressure washing in Canada?

Pressure washing income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for pressure washing follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreements, the e-transfers, and the receipts for the machine, cleaner, and fuel or electricity you can support. Report pressure washing on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add lawn, snow, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Pressure washing is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work happens. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.
