Pressure washing in Canada is a seasonal exterior-cleaning service, mostly driveways, walkways, patios, decks and house siding, sold by the job from about April to October. Canadian companies publish prices from $125 for a Calgary driveway to $445–$1,395 for a Windsor soft house wash, and a beginner can start with an electric washer and a surface cleaner for under $400 on Amazon.ca. The rule that trips people up is runoff: in Toronto, nothing but rain and melted snow may flow into a street catch basin.

## Who does pressure washing suit?

Pressure washing suits people who like physical, visible work with a satisfying before-and-after, and who can move 25–50 kg of equipment. It's a natural add-on for anyone already doing [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada/), [window cleaning](/side-hustles/window-cleaning-side-hustle-canada/) or [car detailing](/side-hustles/car-detailing-side-hustle-canada/), because the clients are the same homeowners.

It suits:

- **Students** with summers free, especially in Ontario where Summer Company can fund equipment.
- **Weekend workers** who can stack three or four jobs on a Saturday.
- **People with a truck, SUV or trailer**, though one Guelph teen profiled below tows his machine behind a bicycle.
- **Handy homeowners** who already own a washer and want to test demand.

It doesn't suit people who can't work on ladders or in heat, who want winter income without a second service (see [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/)), or who don't want to deal with chemicals and water-runoff rules.

## Worked math: a Saturday of driveways

**Illustrative.** Job prices, time per job and costs are assumptions. Prices sit at the low end of DT Cleaning's published $150–$250 driveway range, as a newer operator might charge.

- 4 driveways × $150 = **$600** for the day.
- Each takes about 1.5 hours including setup, pre-treat, surface cleaning, rinse and pack-up, plus 30 minutes of travel: 4 × 2 hours = 8 hours.
- $600 ÷ 8 hours = **$75 an hour** gross.
- Assume $40 for fuel, cleaning solution and wear: $560 ÷ 8 = **$70 an hour** before tax, CPP and equipment depreciation.

| Scenario | Jobs per Saturday | Avg price | Saturdays (Apr–Oct) | Season gross |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 2 | $125 | 20 | $5,000 |
| Middle | 3 | $175 | 24 | $12,600 |
| High | 4 | $225 | 26 | $23,400 |

Adding a $200–$300 house wash to the same visit as a driveway roughly doubles the ticket without a second trip. Bundles are where the hourly rate climbs.

## How do you quote a pressure washing job?

Quote on site where you can, or from photos and a satellite view, then confirm before you start. Measure the area (length × width) and adjust for these factors:

| Factor | What raises the price |
|---|---|
| Surface | Wood and siding need low pressure and more care; concrete is fastest per square foot |
| Size | Bigger areas take longer; use square footage bands for driveways |
| Condition | Years of moss, algae or oil need pre-treatment and dwell time |
| Height | Second-storey siding needs extension wands or ladders and more setup |
| Access | No outdoor tap, long hose runs, tight side yards or stairs |
| Water control | Jobs near catch basins need drain covers or water recovery |
| Add-ons | Steps, walkways, bins, interlock re-sanding, deck sealing |

Set a minimum charge so small jobs still cover travel and setup; Cleaning In Action's published minimum is $75. Write what's included and what isn't (for example, "oil stains may lighten but not disappear"), and give a price for the job rather than an hourly rate, as all three companies above do.

**A season calendar**

| Period | Main work | Sell now |
|---|---|---|
| April–May | Driveways, walkways and decks after winter salt and sand | Spring bundles, deck staining referrals |
| June–August | Siding soft washes, patios, fences, bins | Storefront and condo contracts |
| September–October | Fall deck and patio cleanups | Next spring's early-booking list, snow removal |
| November–March | Equipment maintenance; store the pump with antifreeze protection | Pre-book spring with past clients |

## Where can pressure-washing water go?

Treat wash water as something you must control, not something you can let run down the driveway into the street. The City of Toronto's sewers bylaw page says no substances other than rain and melted snow may flow into an outdoor catch basin, and lists "any water used for cleaning" and "soaps and other cleaning products" among the things that count as a spill. It suggests letting wash water soak into a permeable surface such as gravel, or sending it to the sanitary sewer.

In practice:

- **Plan the runoff before you start.** Direct it onto lawn or gravel where possible.
- **Block or cover catch basins** near the work area with a drain cover or berm.
- **Use the least chemical possible** and choose biodegradable cleaners; never rinse degreaser or oil-stain remover into the street.
- **For commercial lots, garages and restaurant patios**, ask the city about recovery or vacuum equipment before quoting; those jobs carry oil and grease.

Other cities have similar sewer-use bylaws. Search your city's name plus "sewer use bylaw" before your first job.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

### Week 1: Equipment and rules

- **Day 1:** Read your city's sewer-use or storm-drain bylaw and business-licence rules.
- **Day 2:** Choose electric (decks, patios, small driveways) or gas (driveways and houses). Buy a surface cleaner with it.
- **Day 3:** Practise on your own driveway and a friend's patio; time each job.
- **Day 4:** Get a liability insurance quote. A cracked window, etched concrete or stripped deck stain are the claims to ask about.
- **Day 5:** Set prices by surface and size, using the published lists above.
- **Days 6–7:** Take before-and-after photos for your flyer and social posts.

### Week 2: First clients

- Drop 150–200 flyers on streets with older driveways and decks.
- Post before-and-after photos in local community groups, following each group's rules.
- Offer neighbours a "street day" discount when three houses on the same street book.
- See [your first pressure washing client](/side-hustles/pressure-washing-first-client-canada/) for scripts.

### Week 3: Do the work well

- Walk every job with the client first; note cracks, loose mortar and old stains that won't come out.
- Photograph before and after every job.
- Ask for a review the same day.

### Week 4: Systemise

- Set up quotes and invoices in Jobber or Wave.
- Track time per job and adjust prices.
- Offer a spring and fall maintenance plan to every past client.

### Days 31–90

- **Weeks 5–6:** Add soft washing for siding once you're comfortable with cleaners and dilution.
- **Weeks 7–8:** Pitch storefronts and small commercial lots on a recurring contract.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Bundle with window cleaning or bin cleaning for bigger tickets.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Book fall deck and patio cleanups, and line up [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/) for winter.

## Which tax rules apply to pressure washing?

Pressure washing income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on your net profit (QPP in Quebec).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

- **GST/HST:** registration is required once worldwide taxable supplies pass **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). The first-dollar [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule doesn't apply. The "High" scenario above passes $23,000 in one season, so a second service can push you over.
- **Rates once registered:** 13% HST in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia (since April 1, 2025), 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I., 5% GST in Alberta and the territories, GST plus PST in B.C., Saskatchewan and Manitoba where PST applies, and GST plus QST in Quebec. Confirm with the [CRA rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Equipment:** washers and trailers are capital property, claimed over time through capital cost allowance rather than in full in year one.
- **Supplies:** cleaning solutions, nozzles, hoses, fuel and insurance are deductible expenses.
- **Grants:** Summer Company funding can have tax consequences; keep the paperwork.

## Common pressure washing mistakes

- **Too much pressure on wood and siding.** Furring a deck or forcing water behind vinyl creates a repair bill. Use low pressure and wider tips.
- **Wand-only driveways.** Without a surface cleaner, stripes show and jobs take longer.
- **Letting wash water reach the street.** It's a bylaw issue in Toronto and many other cities.
- **Promising stain removal.** Oil, rust and old tire marks may lighten but not vanish; say so in the quote.
- **No before photos.** Pre-existing cracks get blamed on you.
- **Ignoring add-ons.** Steps, walkways and bins on the same visit are easy upsells.
