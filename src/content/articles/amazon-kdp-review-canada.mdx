Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) lets Canadians self-publish ebooks and paperbacks for free and sell them on Amazon.ca, Amazon.com, and other Amazon stores. As of October 2026, KDP offers a 35% or 70% ebook royalty, and the 70% band on Amazon.ca runs from C$2.99 to C$12.99 ([KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560)). Canadian authors receive royalties by direct deposit in Canadian dollars. Amazon does not publish typical author earnings, and this review does not estimate them; most of the outcome depends on the book, the category, and your marketing.

Royalty and payment rules come from KDP's help pages, fetched October 4, 2026. USD amounts are converted at the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4246 CAD per USD for October 2, 2026 ([Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/)). The beginner path is [ebook publishing](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-side-hustle-canada/), with [ebook pricing](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-pricing-canada/), [startup costs](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-startup-costs-canada/), and [your first sale](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-first-sale-canada/).

## Is Amazon KDP available to Canadian authors?

Yes. Canadians can publish through KDP, and KDP's [payment options page](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GJD7DFFX6X88AK46) lists direct deposit in CAD for a Canadian bank account for every marketplace, from Amazon.ca and Amazon.com to the European, Japanese, Indian, Mexican and Australian stores. Amazon handles the currency conversion. Publishing is free; your costs are things like editing, cover design, and any proof copies.

## What royalties does KDP pay Canadian authors?

It depends on format, price, and marketplace. As listed in October 2026:

| Format | Royalty | Conditions | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ebook, 35% option | 35% of list price (excluding VAT) | Any eligible price | [Ebook royalties](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200644210) |
| Ebook, 70% option | 70% of list price (excluding VAT), less delivery costs | Price within the 70% band (Amazon.ca: C$2.99 to C$12.99); 70% territories only; in-copyright works only | Ebook royalties; [price requirements](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560) |
| Delivery cost (70% option) | Average 0.06 USD per unit (about $0.09 CAD), varies by file size | Deducted from 70% royalties | Ebook royalties |
| Paperback on Amazon.ca | 50% at C$13.98 or less; 60% at C$13.99 or more | Minus printing costs | [Paperback royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330) |

KDP says works in the public domain, or consisting mainly of public domain content, can only use the 35% option. The 70% band widened on July 7, 2026, from $2.99–$9.99 to $2.99–$12.99 on Amazon.com, with equivalent changes in other stores.

### Illustrative example

Assumed numbers, not KDP figures: an ebook listed at C$4.99 on Amazon.ca (treated here as the price before any applicable tax) on the 70% option, and a paperback listed at C$19.99 with an assumed C$5.00 printing cost.

- Ebook: 70% × $4.99 = $3.49, less an assumed $0.09 delivery cost = about $3.40 per sale
- Paperback: 60% × $19.99 = $11.99, less $5.00 printing = $6.99 per sale
- The same ebook at C$13.99 would fall outside the 70% band and earn 35%: 35% × $13.99 = $4.90

## How does KDP pay Canadians in Canadian dollars?

By electronic funds transfer to a Canadian bank account, in CAD, with no minimum threshold for EFT ([KDP thresholds](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201207800)). KDP pays monthly, about 60 days after the end of the month in which sales were reported (90 days for Expanded Distribution), and direct deposits can take one to five business days to appear ([KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GK2MKZUL6U3SFBPZ)). Royalties accrue separately for each marketplace.

## How does US tax withholding affect Canadian KDP authors?

KDP says royalties for ebook sales on Amazon.com, and for print sales on Amazon.com, Amazon.co.jp, Amazon.com.au, and Amazon.ca, are subject to 30% US tax withholding unless you qualify for a reduced treaty rate ([KDP tax withholding](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201274690)). Canada has an income tax treaty with the United States, so complete KDP's tax interview (which generates a Form W-8) and claim treaty benefits if they apply to you. KDP notes the W-8 expires at the end of the third calendar year after signing, and withholding reverts to 30% if it lapses. If US tax is withheld, a foreign tax credit on [Form T2209](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2209.html) may reduce Canadian tax on the same income.

## What are the pros and cons of KDP for Canadians?

**Pros**
- Free to publish; no inventory with print on demand.
- CAD direct deposit from every marketplace, with no EFT threshold.
- 70% ebook royalty in a wider price band since July 2026.
- Access to Amazon's buyers in many countries.

**Cons**
- About 60 days' wait for each month's royalties.
- Paperback royalties shrink by printing costs, especially on low list prices.
- US withholding paperwork, and 30% if the tax interview is skipped or lapses.
- Discoverability is hard; Amazon controls ranking and pricing rules.

## Who is Amazon KDP best for in Canada?

KDP suits Canadian writers who can finish and market a book, especially in non-fiction niches with clear search demand or genre fiction series. It suits people expecting quick income less well, given the 60-day payment lag and the work of building readership. ## What mistakes do Canadians make on KDP?

- **Pricing outside the 70% band.** On Amazon.ca, above C$12.99 the ebook drops to 35%.
- **Skipping the tax interview.** Withholding defaults to 30%.
- **Ignoring printing costs.** Check the paperback calculator before setting a price.
- **Publishing public domain content expecting 70%.** It is limited to 35%.
- **Not tracking per-marketplace royalties.** Each marketplace accrues separately; keep the monthly reports.

## What pitfalls do new KDP publishers commonly hit in Canada?

A common pitfall is a stack of low-content books, such as notebooks and planners, published as if the pile itself were a business. KDP defines that category, limits how many new titles you can create each week, and requires a disclosure when text, images, or translations are AI-generated. Amazon does not publish typical earnings for these books, and this review does not estimate them.

- KDP's [low-content books](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GGE5T76TWKA85DJM) page says a low-content book has minimal or no content on the interior pages and is generally repetitive, meant to be filled in. Examples include notebooks, planners, journals, and log books. You must check the Low-content box, or the book will be rejected. These books are not eligible for a free KDP ISBN, and Expanded Distribution is not supported.
- KDP's [create a book](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G202172740) page limits new titles to 2 per book format each week for creation, and 2 new titles per book format each week for submission. The limits reset every Sunday at 12:00 a.m. UTC. Updates to titles you have already submitted do not count against the submission limit.
- KDP's [content guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390) require you to inform KDP of AI-generated text, images, or translations when you publish a new book or republish an edited one. Cover and interior artwork count. If an AI tool created the actual content, KDP still calls it AI-generated after you edit it. If you created the content and used AI only to edit, refine, or brainstorm, KDP calls that AI-assisted and does not require the disclosure. You still have to confirm the content meets the guidelines, including intellectual property rights.

## How is KDP income taxed in Canada?

KDP royalties from a book you wrote and sell commercially are generally business or professional income reported in Canadian dollars on Form T2125, with editing, cover design and advertising as expenses. Keep KDP's monthly royalty reports and your CAD deposit records, and claim a foreign tax credit if US tax was withheld.
