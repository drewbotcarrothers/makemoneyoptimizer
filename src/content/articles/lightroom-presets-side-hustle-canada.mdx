## Who does selling Lightroom presets in Canada suit?

Selling presets in Canada suits photographers and video editors who already have a consistent style, a portfolio that shows it, and an audience (social followers, clients, or a niche community) who want that look. It's a poor fit if your edits change a lot between shoots, if you have no way to reach buyers, or if you need steady income soon. Generic presets compete with thousands of free ones.

## How do you build a preset or LUT pack people will buy?

A sellable preset pack does one job well for a specific kind of photo, and shows real results on several images. Niche examples include real estate interiors, food photos in restaurant light, Canadian winter landscapes, or skin-tone-safe portrait looks.

- **Test on varied images.** Different cameras, skin tones, and lighting. A preset that only works on your camera won't satisfy buyers.
- **Include instructions.** How to install, which settings to tweak (exposure and white balance usually), and which file types are included.
- **Offer formats buyers need.** Lightroom presets for desktop and mobile; LUTs in common formats for video editors.

## What rules apply to marketing presets in Canada?

Marketing presets in Canada has to stay honest. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) says the Competition Act prohibits promoting a product through claims that are false or misleading in any material respect. For presets, that means before-and-after images should use the preset as sold, without extra hidden retouching that the preset doesn't do.

Copyright protects your work and limits what you can use. CIPO's [guide to copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/guide-copyright) says a work is protected the moment it is created and fixed in a material form, and registration is voluntary. Only use sample photos you took or have permission to use, and get consent from recognisable people in your previews.

## How do you start selling Lightroom presets in Canada?

Starting a preset business in Canada means choosing a niche, building and testing a small pack, and listing it where your audience already shops.

### Step 1: Pick one niche

Choose a style and use case you shoot often, such as real estate, food, or snowy outdoor portraits.

### Step 2: Build a small pack

Five to ten presets that work together beat fifty random ones.

### Step 3: Test on outside images

Ask a few photographer friends to try the pack on their own files and report what needed adjusting.

### Step 4: Make honest previews

Before-and-after images using only the preset, plus a short note on which settings buyers will usually tweak.

### Step 5: Choose a storefront

Etsy brings marketplace traffic; Gumroad or your own store keeps more per sale when you bring buyers yourself. Compare in [Teachable vs Gumroad vs Payhip](/side-hustles/teachable-vs-gumroad-vs-payhip-canada/).

### Step 6: Promote where photographers gather

Short before-and-after reels, Pinterest pins, and niche forums. [Pinterest affiliate content](/side-hustles/pinterest-affiliate-side-hustle-canada/) and [faceless Instagram pages](/side-hustles/faceless-instagram-side-hustle-canada/) cover those channels.

## What mistakes are common when selling presets in Canada?

Common preset-selling mistakes in Canada are making generic packs, over-editing previews, and skipping instructions.

- **Generic packs.** "Moody" or "bright and airy" alone is a crowded search.
- **Previews that oversell.** Extra retouching makes the preset look better than it is and invites refunds.
- **No install guide.** Buyers on mobile often need step-by-step help.
- **Using others' photos.** Get permission for every sample image.
- **Ignoring fees.** Price after platform fees, not before.

## Which tax rules apply to selling presets in Canada?

Preset sales income in Canada is generally self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with expenses such as software subscriptions and platform fees. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable sales pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). Gumroad says it acts as merchant of record and collects sales tax where it has obligations, and Etsy has its own rules; see [Etsy shop taxes](/side-hustles/etsy-shop-side-hustle-canada/) and [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).
