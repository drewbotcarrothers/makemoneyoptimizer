Black Friday and Boxing Day reselling in Canada means buying discounted goods during the big sales (Black Friday is Friday, November 27, 2026, and Boxing Day is Saturday, December 26, 2026) and reselling them for more, either during the gift rush before Christmas or after the holidays. It works only when the resale price, after platform fees and shipping, beats what you paid by enough to cover your time. Use Canadian fee rules: Poshmark Canada takes C$3.95 on sales under C$20 and 20% at C$20 or more, Depop charges sellers outside the U.K., U.S., and Australia 10% plus processing, and Vinted is no longer available in Canada. Plan shipping around Canada Post's closures, which in 2026 include Christmas Day on December 25 and the Boxing Day holiday observed on Monday, December 28.

## How does holiday sale reselling work in Canada?

Holiday sale reselling has two windows. Before Christmas, you buy Black Friday and Cyber Monday (November 30, 2026) deals and resell to gift shoppers who missed them. After Christmas, you buy Boxing Day clearance and resell when stock elsewhere runs low, or hold seasonal items until next year. The table is a planning aid, not a profit forecast.

| Window | What you buy | When you sell | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Black Friday to Cyber Monday | Discounted toys, electronics accessories, brand-name clothing, games | Late November to mid-December | Shipping delays before Christmas |
| Boxing Day | Clearance clothing, footwear, home goods, seasonal decor | January to spring, or next fall for decor | Holding stock for months |
| Year-round thrift and garage sales | Used items in good condition | Any time, more demand in the gift season | Safety and recalls on used goods |

## When should you ship holiday resales in Canada?

Ship early and check delivery standards, because Canada Post does not deliver on holidays. Canada Post's [holiday schedule](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/kb/other-products-services/post-office/find-out-operating-hours-on-holidays.page) says it is closed on national, provincial, and territorial holidays with no collection or delivery, and lists Remembrance Day on November 11, Christmas Day on December 25, and Boxing Day observed on December 28 for 2026. Use the [delivery standards tool](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/tools/delivery-standards.page) to check how long a parcel takes between your postal code and the buyer's, and watch for the holiday sending guidelines Canada Post posts for December. Tell buyers in your listing when you will ship, and stop promising Christmas delivery once the timing no longer works.

## What can't you resell in Canada?

You cannot resell products that are banned, recalled, or do not meet Canadian safety rules. Health Canada's [guide to second-hand products](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says it is illegal to sell certain products such as baby walkers, that cribs not meeting current requirements cannot be sold or given away (traditional drop-side cribs have been prohibited since December 29, 2016), and that sellers should check whether a product has been recalled before selling it. It also advises checking that all parts are present and working, that instructions and warning labels are included, and that items are sanitary; if unsure, do not sell it. Check items against the [recalls and safety alerts database](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en). Retailers also set their own purchase limits and return policies, so read them before buying in quantity.

## How much can you make reselling a Boxing Day deal?

Profit is the resale price minus what you paid, the platform fee, and any shipping you cover. The labelled example shows the arithmetic.

### Illustrative example

The prices below are assumptions to show the method, not a forecast for any product.

- **Buy:** a jacket on Boxing Day clearance for an assumed $45, including tax.
- **Sell on Poshmark Canada in January:** at an assumed $80. Fee 20% × $80 = $16, so you receive $64. The prepaid label is included.
- **Profit:** $64 − $45 = $19, before your time.
- **Same jacket at an assumed $55 resale:** fee $11, you receive $44, so you lose $1.
- **Break-even resale price on Poshmark** (20% tier): $45 ÷ 0.8 = $56.25.

Work out the break-even before you buy, not after.

## What mistakes do people make reselling holiday deals in Canada?

The common mistakes are buying without checking resale prices, shipping too late, and ignoring safety rules.

- **Buying because it is cheap**, not because it sells. Check sold listings before you buy.
- **Using U.S. fee tables** or planning to list on Vinted, which is closed in Canada.
- **Promising Christmas delivery** without checking Canada Post's delivery standards and holiday closures.
- **Reselling recalled or banned children's products.**
- **Tying up cash in seasonal stock** you will have to store until next year.
- **Not tracking cost of goods**, which you need to work out profit and tax.

## Which tax rules apply to holiday reselling in Canada?

Buying items to resell for profit is generally business income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with your cost of goods sold as an expense. The CRA's [reporting rules for digital platforms](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/reporting-rules-digital-platforms.html) require certain platform operators to collect and report information on sellers of goods to the CRA each year. Add sales from every platform for the GST/HST [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). More detail is in [tax on reselling and gig apps](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/) and [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).
