## Which admin jobs does a Canadian side hustle need tools for?

Go through the table once and tick each job off. The details that matter:

- **Money in and tax money aside.** A dedicated account for business payments and a second one for tax does more for your records than any app. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> and online banks have low-fee options.
- **Receipts.** The CRA says receipts should show the date, the seller and a description of what you bought ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html)). A photo, saved in a folder by month, covers it.
- **Invoices.** Number them, date them, and include what you did and the amount. Once registered for GST/HST, add your registration number and the tax.
- **Vehicle log.** If you'll claim vehicle expenses, the CRA expects a logbook with the date, destination, purpose and kilometres of each business trip ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/motor-vehicle-records.html)).
- **Contracts, calendar and clients.** Save each signed quote with the client's name and date; keep one calendar and a simple client list.
- **Tracking and filing.** One row per transaction, with a category that matches the T2125 expense lines; accounting software such as <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can do this from bank feeds when the spreadsheet gets slow. File with certified tax software that supports Form T2125, or a tax professional.

A new pet sitter sets up in one evening: a second chequing account for client e-Transfers, a savings account with an automatic weekly transfer for tax, a "2026 receipts" folder on their phone, a quote and invoice template, a calendar for bookings, and a spreadsheet with tabs for income, expenses and kilometres. ## How do you choose between free and paid admin tools?

Start free and upgrade one job at a time, when it takes more than a few minutes a week or causes mistakes. Paid software doesn't change what the CRA expects; it only changes how long it takes you. Paid tools used for the business are generally deductible business expenses; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).

## How do you track which side hustles actually pay?

To track which side hustles actually pay in Canada, record income, expenses and hours for each hustle separately, then compare profit per hour once a month. A single spreadsheet with one row per transaction and a "hustle" column is enough. The CRA asks for a separate Form T2125 for each business or professional activity if you have two or more ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125.html)), so splitting your numbers by hustle now saves sorting them at tax time.

Combined totals hide the weak hustle: a busy delivery week and a slow Etsy month can average out to "fine" while one of them loses money per hour. Shared costs, such as a phone used for both, are split on a reasonable basis.

### Step 1: Create one sheet with a hustle column

Use the columns in the table above. Free spreadsheet tools are enough; you can move to accounting software later.

### Step 2: Run business money through one account

A separate account means your bank statement becomes most of your income and expense list. See [separating business and personal money](/side-hustles/business-bank-account-side-hustle-canada/#how-do-you-keep-business-and-personal-money-separate-after-the-account-is-open).

### Step 3: Log hours as you go

A notes app or calendar entry per session is enough. Include travel, quoting and messaging time.

### Step 4: Keep vehicle use per hustle

The CRA asks for a logbook of business kilometres to support vehicle expenses ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/motor-vehicle-records.html)). Note which hustle each trip was for. See [the CRA mileage log guide](/side-hustles/mileage-log-cra-side-hustle-canada/).

### Step 5: Review monthly

Total each hustle's income, expenses and hours, and calculate profit per hour.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers. Someone runs three hustles in November:

| Hustle | Income | Expenses | Hours | Profit per hour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Food delivery | $600 | $180 (fuel, phone) | 35 | $12.00 |
| Dog walking | $480 | $20 (bags, treats) | 20 | $23.00 |
| Etsy printables | $90 | $25 (fees) | 6 | $10.83 |

Delivery brings in the most money, but dog walking pays the most per hour. Printables keep selling without new hours, so they are judged over a longer period. The obvious move is to shift a few delivery hours to dog walking if there's demand.

### What should you do with the numbers each month?

- **Cut or reduce the lowest earner per hour** if it stays lowest for several months. [When to quit a gig app](/side-hustles/when-to-quit-a-gig-app-canada/) walks through this for app work.
- **Raise prices on the hustle with a waitlist.** See [building a waitlist](#how-do-you-run-a-side-hustle-waitlist-under-casl).
- **Watch the combined GST/HST total.** The $30,000 small supplier test adds up all your businesses; see [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).

## What does a quarterly side hustle money check-in cover?

A quarterly side hustle money check-in is a short routine you do at the end of March, June, September and December: match your records to your bank account, add up taxable sales for the GST/HST small supplier test, top up your tax savings, check whether instalments apply, and see which work is paying. Quarters are the natural unit in Canada because the CRA measures the $30,000 GST/HST threshold over calendar quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)), and instalment due dates fall in each quarter ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/payments/payments-cra/individual-payments/income-tax-instalments/due-dates.html)).

Block 30 minutes in the first week after each quarter ends and run the same five checks.

### Step 1: Reconcile records

Match each business account transaction to an invoice or receipt. The CRA expects records that support your income and expenses ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html)). Fix gaps now while you still remember them.

### Step 2: Run the GST/HST small supplier test

Total this quarter's taxable sales from all your businesses, then add the previous three quarters. The CRA's rules have two triggers ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)):

- **Over $30,000 in a single calendar quarter:** you stop being a small supplier on the sale that takes you over, and must charge GST/HST on that sale.
- **Over $30,000 over four consecutive quarters, but not in one:** you stop being a small supplier at the end of the month after the quarter in which you went over.

If you're getting close, read [GST/HST registration for side hustles](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) before next quarter. Ride-hail drivers register from the first fare regardless.

### Step 3: Top up tax savings

Multiply this quarter's profit by your set-aside rate and compare it with what's in the tax account. Remember self-employed CPP: both portions, 5.95% each in 2026 ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/payroll-deductions-contributions/canada-pension-plan-cpp/cpp-contribution-rates-maximums-exemptions.html)).

### Step 4: Check instalments

You may have to pay instalments if your net tax owing is more than $3,000 ($1,800 in Quebec) this year and in either of the two previous years ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/payments/payments-cra/individual-payments/income-tax-instalments/who-pays-instalments.html)). The CRA sends instalment reminders to people likely to have to pay. Due dates are March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15. See [CRA tax instalments for side hustles](/side-hustles/cra-tax-instalments-side-hustle-canada/).

### Step 5: Review profit per hour

For each hustle, income minus costs divided by hours. Drop or reprice what isn't working; see [the pricing worksheet](/side-hustles/side-hustle-pricing-worksheet-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers. A freelance writer does a check-in on July 3. Records match, apart from one missing software receipt, which they download. Taxable sales were $6,500 this quarter and $21,000 over the last four, so they're not near $30,000 yet but will watch next quarter. Their tax account holds $3,100; profit this quarter was $5,900 and their set-aside rate is 25%, so they move $1,475 for this quarter. No instalment reminder arrived. Profit per hour on blog posts beats newsletter editing, so they decide to quote newsletters higher.

### How does the December check-in differ?

It adds year-end work: a full year of receipts, your vehicle's odometer reading, platform slips in the new year, and planning the return. See [side hustle tax prep before January](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-prep-before-january-canada/).

## How do you build a simple client acquisition system?

A client acquisition system for a Canadian side hustle is a small routine you repeat every week: show up in two channels where your customers already look, record every enquiry in one tracker, reply and follow up on a fixed schedule, and ask happy clients for a referral or review. It doesn't need software, and the legal guardrails are below.

### Step 1: Pick two channels

Choose where your customers already look. Local services can use a [Google Business Profile](/side-hustles/google-business-profile-side-hustle-canada/), community groups and flyers; online freelancers can use a marketplace profile and direct outreach. Two channels done consistently are easier to judge than six done occasionally.

### Step 2: Make one clear offer

Every channel should say the same thing: what you do, where, for whom, and how to book. [The one-page business plan](/side-hustles/side-hustle-business-plan-canada/) is where you write it.

### Step 3: Track every lead

A spreadsheet with date, channel, name, request, quote, follow-up date and outcome. After a month, you can see which channel actually produces paying clients.

### Step 4: Reply fast, quote clearly

A quick, specific reply with a price or a clear next step. Use [the pricing worksheet](/side-hustles/side-hustle-pricing-worksheet-canada/) so your quotes are consistent.

### Step 5: Follow up once

If someone goes quiet after a quote, one polite follow-up a few days later is reasonable. CASL says implied consent from an inquiry lasts six months ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)); for ongoing promotional emails, ask for express consent and include an unsubscribe.

### Step 6: Ask for referrals and reviews

After a job goes well, ask: "If you know anyone who needs this, I'd be grateful for a referral," and send your review link. Never write reviews yourself or ask friends to review work they didn't receive.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers. A new house cleaner picks two channels, a Google Business Profile and a neighbourhood Facebook group, and logs 18 enquiries over six weeks: 11 from the group (5 book) and 7 from the profile (4 book). One follow-up on every unanswered quote wins two more. The profile converts better per enquiry, so they spend more time on photos and reviews there.

## How do you run a side hustle waitlist under CASL?

A side hustle waitlist in Canada is a simple list of people who want your service when a spot opens. Build one when you're consistently full: collect a name, contact details, the service they want and their timing, ask for clear permission to contact them, and fill openings in a fair, stated order. If you'll send them emails or texts that promote your business, Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) applies. ISED says express consent is someone agreeing, verbally or in writing, to receive messages from you ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)).

### When should you start a side hustle waitlist in Canada?

Start one the second or third time in a month that you turn down work you'd have liked: the first snowfall for [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/), spring for [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada/), or the weeks before holidays for [pet sitting](/side-hustles/winter-pet-sitting-house-sitting-canada/).

### Step 1: Pick one place to collect names

A free form tool, a spreadsheet, or the signup form in an email service. One list beats scattered texts and DMs.

### Step 2: Ask for consent clearly

Use an unticked checkbox with plain words, such as: "Yes, email me when a spot opens and send occasional updates from Northside Dog Walking. You can unsubscribe any time." ISED's guidance says express consent means the person said yes to being contacted, and that you should clearly identify yourself and your business ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)).

### Step 3: Say how the list works

State the order (first come, first served, or by route), roughly how often spots open if you know, and that joining doesn't guarantee service.

### Step 4: Contact people in order and give a deadline

"A Tuesday morning spot opened. Reply by Friday if you'd like it." If they pass, move to the next name.

### Step 5: Clean the list

Remove people who found someone else, who asked to be removed, or whose consent has run out.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers. A house cleaner with eight regular clients replies to new enquiries with a waitlist form (name, email, neighbourhood, preferred day, unticked consent box). When a regular client moves away, they email the first person on the list in that neighbourhood, give three days to accept, and fill the spot without advertising. The growing list feeds their next [price review](/side-hustles/first-year-side-hustle-price-review-canada/).

### What CASL, Competition Act and privacy rules apply to clients and waitlists?

- **CASL** covers commercial electronic messages, such as emails and texts that promote your services ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en)). Express consent has no time limit unless withdrawn; implied consent from an existing business relationship can last up to two years, but only six months after an inquiry or application ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)). Identify yourself in every message, include an easy unsubscribe, act on it within 10 business days, and note when and how each person consented. See [email marketing for side hustles](/side-hustles/email-marketing-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Competition Act:** base reviews and testimonials on actual experience ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act)), and don't use fake urgency or scarcity, such as countdowns that reset or untrue claims about limited spots ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations)).
- **Privacy:** PIPEDA, or a substantially similar provincial law in Alberta, B.C. and Quebec, applies to personal information you collect in commercial activity ([Office of the Privacy Commissioner](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)). Collect only what you need, say what you'll use it for, keep the tracker and list secure, and don't share or sell them.

## What admin mistakes do side hustlers make in Canada?

- **Buying software before having a process,** or receipts scattered across email, a wallet and a camera roll, with no backup.
- **Using a personal account for business payments,** or topping up tax savings from personal money in April instead of from the business account each quarter.
- **Tracking income but not hours or expenses.** Profit per hour needs all three, and expenses change the ranking. Judge long-tail products, like printables, over more than one month.
- **Checking once a year,** when the GST/HST test and instalments are quarterly, or counting one hustle for GST/HST instead of all your businesses. Do not ignore an instalment reminder.
- **Trying every channel at once, with no lead tracker and slow replies,** so you can't tell what works.
- **Following up forever, a pre-ticked consent box, or adding enquiry emails to a newsletter** without consent.
- **Fake reviews or fake scarcity.**
- **A waitlist with no order or deadline, or one that never leads to a price review.** A long, growing list is a pricing signal.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
