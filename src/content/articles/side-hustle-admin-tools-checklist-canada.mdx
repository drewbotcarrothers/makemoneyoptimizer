A side hustle admin setup in Canada needs to cover nine jobs: taking payments, holding tax money, capturing receipts, sending invoices, logging vehicle use, storing contracts, booking time, tracking income and costs, and filing the return. You don't need an app for each. The CRA lets you keep records on paper, electronically or both ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records.html)), and generally expects them kept six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records/where-keep-your-records-long-request-permission-destroy-them-early.html)). This checklist tells you what each job needs and links to the deeper guide.

## Which admin jobs does a Canadian side hustle need tools for?

Go through the list once and tick each job off.

### Step 1: Money in and tax money aside

A dedicated account for business payments and a second one for tax. That pair does more for your records than any app. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> and online banks have low-fee options.

### Step 2: Receipts

The CRA says receipts should show the date, the seller and a description of what you bought ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html)). A photo, saved in a folder by month, covers it.

### Step 3: Invoices

Number them, date them, and include what you did and the amount. Once registered for GST/HST, add your registration number and the tax.

### Step 4: Vehicle log

If you'll claim vehicle expenses, the CRA expects a logbook with the date, destination, purpose and kilometres of each business trip ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/motor-vehicle-records.html)).

### Step 5: Contracts and quotes

Save each signed quote or contract with the client's name and date.

### Step 6: Calendar and client list

One calendar for all bookings, and a simple client list. If you'll email clients offers, ISED's CASL guidance on consent and unsubscribe applies ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)).

### Step 7: Income and expense tracking

One row per transaction, with a category that matches the T2125 expense lines. Accounting software such as <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can do this from bank feeds when the spreadsheet gets slow.

### Step 8: Tax filing

Use certified tax software that supports Form T2125, or a tax professional. See [reporting side hustle income to the CRA](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch. A new pet sitter sets up in one evening: a second chequing account for client e-Transfers, a savings account with an automatic weekly transfer for tax, a "2026 receipts" folder on their phone, a quote and invoice template, a calendar for bookings, and a spreadsheet with tabs for income, expenses and kilometres. Total software cost: nothing. Six months later, invoicing 15 clients by hand takes too long, so they move invoicing to an accounting app and keep everything else as it was.

## How do you choose between free and paid admin tools?

Start free and upgrade one job at a time, when it takes more than a few minutes a week or causes mistakes. Paid software doesn't change what the CRA expects; it only changes how long it takes you. For bookkeeping apps specifically, see [free and cheap bookkeeping tools](/side-hustles/bookkeeping-tools-side-hustle-canada/).

## What admin setup mistakes do side hustlers make in Canada?

- **Buying software before having a process.**
- **Receipts in three places:** email, a wallet and a camera roll.
- **No backup** of electronic records.
- **Using a personal account for business payments.**
- **Waiting until April** to build the spreadsheet.

## How does an admin setup help at tax time in Canada?

Each job feeds the return: deposits give gross income, receipts and the mileage log support expenses, and the tax account pays the bill. Paid tools used for the business are generally deductible business expenses; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).
