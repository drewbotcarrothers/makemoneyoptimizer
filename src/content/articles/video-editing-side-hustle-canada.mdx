## Who does video editing in Canada suit?

Freelance video editing in Canada suits people who are patient with software, notice when a cut lands half a beat late, and can turn around revisions on time. It is a poor fit if your computer struggles to play back footage smoothly, if you dislike criticism of your work, or if you need paid hours immediately; building a portfolio takes unpaid practice first.

The work is a mix of craft and service. Creators rebook the editor who understands their style, keeps a consistent look, and delivers on the day the upload is scheduled. Reliability tends to matter as much as flashy transitions.

## How much can video editing earn in Canada?

Video editing earnings in Canada depend on the client, the length and complexity of the edit, and how efficient you become, so this guide does not publish a national rate. What you keep depends on:

- Per-video, per-minute, or hourly pricing, and how many revision rounds are included
- Raw footage length versus finished length
- Captions, motion graphics, colour correction, and thumbnails, if you offer them
- Whether clients are in Canada or abroad (tax and payment fees differ)
- Platform fees if you find clients through a freelance marketplace

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote or a market rate. Suppose a creator pays an assumed $40 for each short-form clip and orders 8 clips a month: 8 × $40 = $320. Suppose each clip takes 1.5 hours including revisions: 8 × 1.5 = 12 hours, and $320 ÷ 12 ≈ $26.67 an hour before software, storage, and a tax set-aside. If you get faster and each clip takes 1 hour, the same $320 over 8 hours is $40 an hour. Speed comes from practice and templates, not from cutting corners on the brief.

## How do you start freelance video editing in Canada?

Starting freelance video editing in Canada means learning one editor well, building a small portfolio, and pitching creators whose content you already understand. The steps below follow that order.

### Step 1: Pick one editor and learn it properly

The free version of [DaVinci Resolve](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve) is one option; whatever you choose, learn the keyboard shortcuts, export settings, and caption tools. Switching tools every week slows everything down.

### Step 2: Make three sample edits

Use your own footage or royalty-free footage you're allowed to use. Make one short-form clip with captions, one longer edit with music and pacing, and one before-and-after that shows what you changed. Keep each under a minute to watch.

### Step 3: Choose a niche you understand

Gaming, fitness, cooking, education, or local business content each have their own pacing and style. Editing in a genre you watch makes your first pitches more convincing.

### Step 4: Write a simple offer

Example: "Five Shorts from one long video, with captions, delivered in five days, two revision rounds." Name the deliverable, the turnaround, the number of revisions, and the file format.

### Step 5: Pitch ten creators

Look for creators with steady uploads but few short-form clips, or inconsistent editing. Send a short message with one sample made from their public content (as a demo, not posted) and your offer. Freelance marketplaces are another route; compare their fees in [Fiverr vs Upwork in Canada](/side-hustles/fiverr-vs-upwork-canada/).

### Step 6: Set up a clean handoff

Agree how footage arrives (shared drive or transfer link), how you'll deliver, and how feedback comes back. Keep client footage private and delete it when the project ends if that's what you agreed.

### Step 7: Deliver, invoice, and ask for a testimonial

Deliver on time, invoice the same day, and ask whether the creator would like a regular monthly batch. A written agreement on scope and revisions prevents most disputes.

## What mistakes are common in freelance video editing in Canada?

Common freelance video editing mistakes in Canada are unlimited revisions, unlicensed music, and buying gear before any client. Each one costs time or money that a short written brief would have saved.

- **Unlimited revisions.** Put the number of rounds in the offer, and price extra rounds.
- **Using music or footage you don't have rights to.** Use the creator's licensed library or properly licensed tracks; a copyright claim on a client's channel is a fast way to lose the client.
- **Overspending on gear.** A free editor and the computer you own can carry the first clients.
- **Vague deadlines.** Agree the delivery date and the time zone if the client is outside Canada.
- **No backups.** Keep project files on a second drive until the client confirms the final.

## Which tax rules apply to video editing in Canada?

Freelance video editing income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all your side-hustle revenue.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Once registered, charge the rate that applies to the client's province on the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) page. Many editors work for creators outside Canada. The CRA's page on [GST/HST on imports and exports](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-imports-exports.html) says certain services supplied to a non-resident, including some services made in Canada and provided by electronic means, may be zero-rated, and that suppliers must keep evidence of the customer's residency. Confirm how your specific service is treated before you leave tax off an invoice. Software, storage, and a share of your computer can be business expenses if you keep receipts; see [side hustle expense deductions in Canada](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).
