Freelance video editing as a side hustle means turning other people's raw footage into finished YouTube videos, Shorts, Reels, TikToks, podcasts and ads, then delivering them online. You don't need a film degree or expensive software: DaVinci Resolve is free, and the clients who hire part-time editors (YouTubers, coaches, podcasters, small brands) care more about retention and turnaround than credentials. Job Bank puts the median wage for video editors in Canada at $34.62 an hour, and the best-documented Canadian example, Toronto's Tuan Le, started out charging $20 for 20-minute videos.

## Who does freelance video editing suit?

Editing suits people who can sit with footage for hours, notice when a cut feels slow, and enjoy fixing small things nobody will consciously see. The work is done at a desk on your schedule, which makes it one of the more flexible side hustles for people with a day job.

Good fits:

- **Gamers and hobby YouTubers** who already cut their own montages. Tuan Le got into editing by cutting gameplay montages as a teenager.
- **Students in film, media or communications programs**, who already have software skills and need a portfolio.
- **Podcasters and social media managers** who want to add a higher-value service.
- **Parents and shift workers** who can edit at night; most clients only care about the delivery deadline.
- **Bilingual editors** in Quebec, New Brunswick and Ontario, who can caption and edit French content that many editors can't.

It's a weaker fit if you need same-week income (portfolios and trust take time), can't work on a computer for long stretches, or dislike revisions. Expect clients to ask for changes on nearly every first draft.

## What do video editors actually sell?

Most part-time editors pick one or two of these and get good at them before adding more:

1. **Long-form YouTube edits:** 8- to 20-minute videos with cuts, b-roll, music, sound effects, zooms and captions.
2. **Short-form clips:** 15- to 60-second vertical videos for TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts, often cut from a client's long videos or podcasts.
3. **Podcast video:** multicam edits of video podcasts plus a set of clips. See the [podcast editing side hustle](/side-hustles/podcast-editing-side-hustle-canada/) for the audio side.
4. **Talking-head course and coaching videos:** clean cuts, slides and captions for coaches and course creators.
5. **Local business promos:** restaurant, real estate, gym and trades videos for social media.
6. **Ad creative:** multiple hooks and versions of the same ad for brands testing on Meta and TikTok. This pairs with [UGC creators](/side-hustles/ugc-side-hustle-canada/) who film but don't edit.
7. **Event highlight reels:** weddings, conferences and sports, usually with a shooter.
8. **Add-ons:** captions, colour correction, audio clean-up, thumbnails (see [graphic design](/side-hustles/graphic-design-side-hustle-canada/)), and channel management.

## How much can freelance video editors earn in Canada?

There's no official freelance rate survey for video editing, so the most reliable Canadian benchmark is Job Bank's wage data for employed editors. It's a useful floor for thinking about what your hour is worth, not a promise of freelance rates.

| Job Bank title (NOC grouping) | Low | Median | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Video editor (NOC 51110, editors)](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/297656/ca) | $20.00/hr | $34.62/hr | $50.00/hr |
| [Film editor (NOC 51120, producers, directors and related)](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/5379/ca) | $22.00/hr | $41.03/hr | $70.00/hr |

Both pages were updated November 19, 2025, using a 2023 to 2024 reference period. Job Bank places "video editor" in the broader editors group, which also includes print and web editors, so treat the figure as indicative.

Freelance rates vary much more than wages. What public operator stories show:

- **Starting out:** Tuan Le told CNBC he charged $20 CAD for roughly 20-minute videos when he started editing for YouTubers. Rachel Kisela told Creator Spotlight she started with $20 jobs on Upwork during COVID.
- **Retainers:** once Le was producing short-form for businesses, his company charged $2,000 a month per client at first, and later $10,000 to $16,000 a month (CNBC, January 16, 2026). That is agency pricing with a team, not a solo side hustle.
- **Platform cuts:** whatever you charge on Fiverr, you keep 80%. On Upwork the service fee ranges from 0% to 15% depending on the contract, and you see it before you propose.

## How do you start? A 30-day launch plan

**Week 1: pick a lane and set up.**
- Choose one service (for example, YouTube long-form for finance channels, or Shorts for podcasts).
- Install DaVinci Resolve and learn the edit page basics on a sample project.
- Set up folders: Client / Project / Footage, Audio, Graphics, Exports.
- Open a separate bank account for business income.

**Week 2: build three portfolio pieces.**
- Download permissively licensed footage, or re-edit your own videos, or offer two free edits to creators you like in exchange for permission to show the work.
- Make one long-form sample (5 to 10 minutes), one vertical short, and one before-and-after.
- Put them on a simple portfolio page or an unlisted YouTube playlist.

**Week 3: open your channels.**
- Create a Fiverr gig with three packages, or an Upwork profile with your playlist.
- Build a list of 50 direct prospects (YouTubers, podcasters, local businesses) in a spreadsheet.
- Send 10 personalised pitches a day with a free sample edit of one of their clips where possible.

**Week 4: deliver and systematise.**
- Deliver the first job early; ask for a review or testimonial.
- Write your revision policy (two rounds included) and file-handover checklist.
- Set up invoicing (Wave or FreshBooks) and a tax set-aside account.
- Offer a monthly retainer to anyone who orders twice.

**Months 2 and 3:** keep pitching at least 30 prospects a week until you have two retainer clients, raise prices for new clients after every three five-star jobs, and add one upsell (thumbnails or captions).

## Real editors on the record

**Tuan Le (Toronto, Canada).** Le immigrated to Canada from Vietnam as a teenager, taught himself editing by cutting gameplay montages, and began editing for League of Legends, finance and dropshipping YouTubers at $20 CAD for roughly 20-minute videos. After four months at Toronto Film School he left, worked unpaid for three months at a Toronto content company to learn the business side, and shot restaurant videos in exchange for meals. He founded ShortsCut, a short-form production company, in January 2023, made his first hire in February 2023 and had 15 staff by the end of 2025. CNBC reported that ShortsCut brought in $1.08 million in 2025 with a net profit of just over $488,000, based on documents it reviewed, and that it charges $10,000 to $16,000 a month per client ([CNBC Make It, January 16, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/16/video-production-company-brought-in-over-a-million-dollars-in-2025.html)). NDTV's account of the same story says Le earned $8,500 in his first year ([NDTV, January 10, 2026](https://www.ndtv.com/offbeat/canadian-entrepreneur-builds-1-4-million-business-after-learning-skills-on-youtube-10597071)). The lesson for a side hustle: his early income was small, and the jump came from cold outreach and a repeatable short-form offer.

**Rachel Kisela (not Canadian).** Kisela told Creator Spotlight she started with $20 Upwork jobs during COVID, later led an editing team for MrBeast, and built a six-figure freelance business in which editing provides most of her income, alongside Etsy products and speaking. She also founded EditHers, a community for women editors ([Creator Spotlight, September 30, 2025](https://www.creatorspotlight.com/p/rachel-kisela)). Her path shows how platform gigs can lead to bigger clients once you have a portfolio.

Neither story is typical. Le now runs an agency with a team, and Kisela edits full time.

## What mistakes do new editors make?

- **Unlimited revisions.** State how many rounds are included and what an extra round costs.
- **Unlicensed music.** Using a popular song can get a client's video muted or claimed. Confirm who holds the music licence.
- **No backups.** Keep client footage on at least two drives until the project is paid and archived.
- **Pricing per hour on platforms.** Fixed packages reward getting faster; hourly rates punish it.
- **Too many niches.** A portfolio of finance YouTube edits wins finance clients faster than a mix of everything.
- **Missing deadlines.** Reliability is the main reason clients keep editors. Pad your delivery dates.
- **Ignoring the hook.** Short-form clients care about the first three seconds; open with the strongest line.

## Which tax rules apply to video editing in Canada?

Freelance editing income is self-employment income. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Deductible costs can include software subscriptions, platform fees, music and stock licences, cloud storage, a share of your internet, and a home office if you qualify; computers and drives usually go through capital cost allowance. See [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/). USD payouts from Fiverr and Upwork are converted to CAD.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count other side-hustle revenue too. Video editing isn't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Services to clients outside Canada may be zero-rated exports; see the CRA's [GST/HST on imports and exports](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-imports-exports.html). For invoicing and bookkeeping, compare [FreshBooks vs Wave vs QuickBooks](/side-hustles/freshbooks-vs-wave-vs-quickbooks-canada/); <AffiliateLink program="wave" label="Wave" /> and <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> both send invoices in CAD.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered. Toronto has the densest market of agencies and creators.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered; check Revenu Québec. French and bilingual editing and captioning are in demand.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** provincial sales tax rules are separate from GST; check whether your services are taxable provincially.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Job Bank wages describe employees in broad occupational groups (the "video editor" title sits in NOC 51110 alongside other editors), using a 2023 to 2024 reference period; freelance rates can be higher or lower. Software and platform fees were read on October 9, 2026, and Amazon.ca prices that day change often. The CNBC and NDTV figures describe one company that grew into an agency with staff, and Kisela's figures are self-reported to Creator Spotlight; neither is typical. All rates, hours and video counts in the worked examples are assumptions. The USD conversion uses the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4240 on October 8, 2026.
