## What title and IP rules apply to developers' side work in Canada?

Three things to check:

- **The "engineer" title.** [Engineers Canada](https://engineerscanada.ca/become-an-engineer/use-of-professional-title-and-designations) says that to practise engineering and use the title "engineer," you must be licensed by the regulator in the province or territory where you use it. It lists "software or data engineer" as an area of concern: in most provinces, you can't use the title without a licence, even if your employer assigned it. In 2023, Alberta changed its rules to let tech workers use "software engineer" without an APEGA licence. On your freelance site, titles like "software developer" avoid the issue.
- **Code ownership.** Section 13(3) of the [Copyright Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-13.html) makes the employer the first owner of copyright in work made in the course of employment, unless there's an agreement to the contrary. Employment contracts can add IP-assignment clauses. Build side projects on your own time and hardware, and don't reuse employer code.
- **Conflict of interest.** Employers can restrict outside work. The federal [conflict-of-interest FAQ](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/values-ethics/conflict-interest-post-employment/frequently-asked-questions.html) for public servants says outside activities should be on your own time, not use employer property, and not use non-public information from your job. Private-sector policies vary, so read yours.

## How should developers price side work?

Price small sites per project and maintenance per month, and agree on scope in writing. [Website maintenance pricing](/side-hustles/website-maintenance-pricing-canada/) and [no-code web design pricing](/side-hustles/no-code-web-design-pricing-canada/) explain the models.

### Illustrative example

Assumed numbers, not market rates: a developer has 5 maintenance clients at an assumed $100 a month each and spends an assumed 1.5 hours per client per month.

- Monthly revenue: 5 × $100 = $500
- Monthly hours: 5 × 1.5 = 7.5
- Effective hourly rate: $500 ÷ 7.5 ≈ $66.67 before hosting and tax

## What mistakes do developers make with side hustles?

- **Calling yourself a software engineer without a licence.** Outside Alberta, that's a risk.
- **Reusing employer code.** It may not be yours.
- **Undefined scope.** "Small changes" grow; write them down.
- **Building custom when no-code would do.** Clients pay for results.
- **Holding client credentials loosely.** Use a password manager and the client's own accounts.

## How is a developer side hustle taxed in Canada?

Report freelance income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Software, hosting you don't pass on, and a share of equipment can be deductible; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/). Register for GST/HST once you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html); [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) explains the steps. Read [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
