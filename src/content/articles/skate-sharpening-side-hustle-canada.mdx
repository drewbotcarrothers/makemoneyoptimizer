A skate sharpening side hustle in Canada sharpens hockey, figure and recreational skates for neighbours, teams and arena regulars, usually from a garage bench or a table near a rink. Canadian shops and small operators that publish prices charge $8 to $12 a pair for a standard sharpen, and one GTA mobile service adds a $3.99 delivery fee for pickup and drop-off. A portable Sparx Sharpener 3 lists at $1,149.99 in Canada, so the machine alone takes roughly 115 pairs at $10 to pay back. Prices were read on each company's page on October 19, 2026.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Standard sharpen, per pair:** $8 (RADIUS), $9.95 (Leaside Hockey Shop, Toronto), $10 (Hockey Lion, Circuit Cycle & Sports, Woldy in Ile des Chenes, MB, ProSkate ROH), $12 (ProSkate flat-bottom V).
- **Mobile pickup and drop-off:** Pro Edge (GTA) lists $9.99 a pair plus tax and a $3.99 delivery fee, and says a pair takes 10 to 15 minutes.
- **Machine:** Sparx Sharpener 3, $1,149.99 on Sparx Hockey's Canadian store; grinding rings $94.99 each; a starter pack $264.99.
- **Upsells shops publish:** profiling $30 (Leaside) to $50 including a sharpen (ProSkate), heat molding $20 to $30, rivets $2 to $4.
- **Break-even on the machine alone:** $1,149.99 ÷ $10 = about 115 pairs, before rings and tax.

Two things stand out. First, prices cluster tightly around $10, so competing on price leaves little room; convenience (same-day, pickup, arena-side) is what the smaller operators sell. Second, the shops make extra money on profiling, molding and blade replacement, which need more skill and equipment than a standard sharpen.

## What equipment does a skate sharpening service need?

The core purchase is a sharpening machine; the rest is consumables and small hand tools. Prices are from Sparx Hockey's Canadian store, read October 19, 2026.

| [Sparx Sharpener 3](https://ca.sparxhockey.com/products/sparx-sharpener-3) | $1,149.99 | Portable; hockey and goalie skates; figure skates need an adapter sold separately; on-board dust filter; includes honing stone, leather strop, travel guard |
| [Grinding ring](https://ca.sparxhockey.com/collections/grinding-rings) (Radius or FIRE profiles) | $94.99 each | One ring per hollow or profile you offer |
| Starter pack | $264.99 | Listed in the same grinding-ring collection |

Sparx markets the Sharpener 3 to "players of all levels" and it is portable, which suits a garage bench or a rink-side table. Traditional shop sharpeners exist too, but this guide did not find a Canadian page with a published price for one, so it does not list them. If you plan to offer figure skating sharpening, note that figure blades have toe picks and a different rocker; Woldy's page stresses that figure skates are sharpened "completely differently" from hockey skates.

**Which hollows to stock.** Woldy lists 1/2", 9/16" and 5/8" as common choices and RADIUS defaults to 1/2". Starting with one or two rings that cover your local customers' most common hollows keeps ring spending down.

## Illustrative example: how many pairs pay back the machine?

This example uses sourced prices (Sparx Sharpener 3 $1,149.99, one extra grinding ring $94.99, a $10 sharpen, the most common price above) and assumed weekly volumes. It ignores ring wear, electricity, tax and your time, so treat it as a floor for break-even, not a profit forecast.

Formula: pairs to break even = (machine + extra ring) ÷ price per pair = ($1,149.99 + $94.99) ÷ $10 = about 125 pairs.

| Assumed pairs per week | Weeks to 125 pairs | Gross in a 20-week season (assumed length) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 25 | $1,000 |
| 15 | about 9 | $3,000 |
| 30 | about 5 | $6,000 |

At 5 pairs a week, the machine does not pay back in one 20-week season. At 15 or more, it does within the first half. A team contract (sharpening a whole roster before each weekend) is the quickest way to the higher rows.

## What does a first season look like?

| Weeks | Focus | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 | Set up and practise | Buy the machine and one or two rings; sharpen your own and family skates; learn to check edges are level |
| 3 to 4 | First customers | Offer neighbours and teammates a $10 sharpen; post a simple price list and drop-off hours |
| 5 to 8 | Teams and rinks | Contact minor hockey and ringette team managers about roster sharpening before games; ask arena staff whether a notice board is allowed |
| 9 to 20 | Routine | Fixed drop-off times, same-day return, a pickup option if you can drive; track pairs per week to see whether a second ring or profiling is worth adding |

**Copyable price list:**

```text
Skate sharpening - $[10] per pair (hockey, figure, goalie)
Hollow: tell us yours (1/2", 9/16", 5/8") or we match your current edge
Drop-off: [days/times] - ready same day by [time]
Pickup and drop-off: +$[__] within [area]
Team rate: [__] pairs or more, $[__] per pair
Payment: e-transfer, cash or card at pickup
We don't do repairs or blade replacement - ask and we'll refer you
```

**Workflow per pair:** label the skates with the customer's name and hollow, inspect blades for chips or loose rivets, sharpen, check both edges are level, hone off burrs, wipe the blades, put on guards if supplied, and record the pair in your log.

## What mistakes do new skate sharpeners make?

- **Pricing above local shops.** A standard sharpen is $8 to $12 a pair at the shops this guide lists.
- **Buying the machine before you have demand.** The Sparx Sharpener 3 is $1,149.99, plus $94.99 for each grinding ring.
- **Leaving out delivery.** Pro Edge charges $9.99 plus a $3.99 delivery fee for pickup and drop-off.
- **Not selling upsells.** Shops list profiling at $30 to $50, heat molding at $20 to $30 and rivets at $2 to $4.

## Which tax rules apply to a skate sharpening side hustle in Canada?

Income from sharpening is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). The machine is a capital purchase and rings are supplies; how to claim them is a question for a tax professional. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once your taxable sales pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters, under the CRA's [small supplier rules](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
