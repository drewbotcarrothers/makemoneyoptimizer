## What rules apply to writers' side hustles in Canada?

Two rules come up most:

- **Anti-spam.** [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) focuses on commercial electronic messages, which ISED describes as messages that encourage participation in a commercial activity, whether or not there's an expectation of profit. That covers cold pitches by email and newsletter marketing, so follow the consent and identification rules on ISED's site.
- **Copyright.** Section 13(3) of the [Copyright Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-13.html) makes an employer the first owner of copyright in work an employee makes in the course of employment, unless an agreement says otherwise. Freelancers aren't employees, so who owns the work is usually set by the contract. Read clauses that assign all rights before signing.

If you write for your employer by day, check your contract for moonlighting or IP clauses before freelancing in the same field.

## How should writers price freelance work?

Price per project or per piece once you know how long a piece takes you; per-word pricing is common but easy to underprice. The [writing rates guide](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-pricing-canada/) explains the options.

### Illustrative example

Assumed numbers, not market rates: a writer charges an assumed $250 for a 1,200-word blog post and spends an assumed 4 hours on research, drafting, and one revision.

- Effective hourly rate: $250 ÷ 4 = $62.50
- Per word: $250 ÷ 1,200 ≈ $0.21

## What mistakes do writers make with side hustles?

- **No sample in the niche.** One relevant sample beats ten unrelated ones.
- **Cold emailing without checking CASL.** Learn the rules before you pitch.
- **Signing away all rights by default.** Read the IP clause.
- **Unlimited revisions.** Set a number.
- **Waiting for a blog to pay quickly.** Your own publishing builds slowly.

## How is a writing side hustle taxed in Canada?

Report freelance income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Writing services follow the $30,000 GST/HST small-supplier test ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). Book royalties have their own rules, covered in [ebook publishing](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-side-hustle-canada/) and [ebook pricing](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-pricing-canada/). Read [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) and [home office expenses](/side-hustles/home-office-expenses-side-hustle-canada/).
