Moving help in Canada is usually priced per helper per hour with a minimum booking, plus add-ons for stairs, long carries and disassembly, while the client rents and drives the truck. This guide covers what helpers and moving firms charge, a rate formula, four worked quotes, templates and the rules that apply when you write an estimate. It suits labour-only helpers, not licensed moving companies.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Market rates:** Taskrabbit's cost guide (updated October 1, 2026) lists average moving-help rates from $24 an hour in Halifax to $35 in Montreal, with average invoices of $52 to $118.
- **Employee benchmark:** Job Bank's national median for furniture movers and mover helpers is $22.00 an hour.
- **Full crews with a truck:** Cactus Moving in Toronto lists 2 movers plus a 16-foot truck at $119 to $179 an hour with a 3-hour minimum.
- **Formula:** rate per helper = (target hourly pay + gear wear per hour) ÷ billable share of your time.
- **Illustrative quote:** 2 helpers × 4 hours × $35 = $280, or $316.40 with 13% HST if you're registered in Ontario.
- **Estimates:** in Ontario, consumer moving contracts over $50 must be in writing, and the final bill can't exceed the estimate by more than 10% without the client's agreement.

## Which pricing models work for moving help in Canada?

Moving help in Canada is usually priced hourly per helper with a minimum, as a flat price for a single item, or as a fixed price for a small, well-described move. Stairs, long carries and disassembly are add-ons.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hourly per helper, with minimum | Rate × helpers × hours, minimum 2–3 hours | Most apartment moves | Clients who are not packed |
| Single-item flat price | One price for a couch, an appliance, or a dresser | Marketplace pickups | Stairs and tight turns |
| Fixed price for a small move | One number for a described load | Studios and dorms | Scope creep: "just a few more boxes" |
| Add-ons | Stairs per flight, long carry, assembly | Covering real extra effort | Doing them for free |

Add-ons are for clients you book directly. On Taskrabbit, the Pricing Transparency Policy in its [February 2026 Tasker update](https://www.taskrabbit.ca/blog/taskrabbit-2026-tasker-update-built-from-your-feedback/) says gas, parking, stairs and vehicle costs must be built into your hourly rate, not charged separately. The [odd-job app rates guide](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-pricing-canada/) covers app pricing.

Labour-only means the client rents and drives the truck. If you carry goods in your own vehicle, you need different insurance, and in Ontario the [consumer rules for movers](https://www.ontario.ca/page/hiring-mover) apply to your estimates and contracts.

## How do you calculate a moving help rate in Canada?

To set a moving help rate, start with the hourly pay you want. Add the cost of gear wear per hour, then divide by the share of your working time that clients pay for.

**Rate per helper = (target hourly pay + gear wear per hour) ÷ billable share**

1. **Target hourly pay.** Start from your province's Job Bank row and Taskrabbit's rate for your city. A target near the employee median is a floor, not a goal.
2. **Gear wear.** Add up blankets, straps, gloves, wrap and dollies, then divide by the hours you expect to work before replacing them. A starter kit priced on Amazon.ca in the [moving help guide](/side-hustles/moving-help-side-hustle-canada/) came to $168.40.
3. **Billable share.** Travel, quotes and messages are unpaid. If you're paid for 3 of every 4 hours you work, your billable share is 0.75.
4. **Minimum booking.** Set a 2- or 3-hour minimum so a short job still pays for the trip.
5. **Tax and CPP.** Set money aside for income tax and both CPP shares. See the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

**Illustrative:** you want $25 an hour, gear wear is about $1 an hour, and 75% of your time is billable. ($25 + $1) ÷ 0.75 = $34.67, which rounds to $35 per helper. That sits right at Taskrabbit's Toronto and Montreal averages and below Hustle Moving Co.'s $45 team-leader pay.

**Quote 1: labour-only one-bedroom, Toronto, two helpers.**

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| 2 helpers × 4 hours × $35 | $280.00 |
| Minimum booking | 3 hours (exceeded) |
| HST 13% (only if registered) | $36.40 |
| **Total if registered** | **$316.40** |
| **Total if not registered** | **$280.00** |

Four hours matches Hustle Moving Co.'s reported average move. Taskrabbit puts a full-service local one-bedroom at $400 to $900, so $280 for labour only is a reasonable price for a client who rents the truck.

**Quote 2: a Marketplace couch pickup.** Two helpers, about one hour of work, 2-hour minimum: 2 × 2 × $35 = $140.00. If you're registered in Ontario, add 13% HST for $158.20. Tell the client before the day that the minimum applies, because a $140 bill for an hour of work surprises people.

**Quote 3: a third-floor walk-up, Calgary.** Two helpers for 3 hours (2 × 3 × $35 = $210), plus a $25-per-flight stairs add-on for three flights ($75), for $285.00. Check the add-on against the time it actually takes. If the stairs add 45 minutes for both helpers, that's 2 × 0.75 × $35 = $52.50 of time, so $75 covers it with some margin. If you're registered, add 5% GST for $299.25.

**Quote 4: a flat-rate studio move with your own van.** You charge $300 flat for two people over five hours including travel. Fuel for 60 km at an assumed 12 L/100 km and $1.70 a litre is 60 × 0.12 × $1.70 = $12.24. That leaves $287.76, or $143.88 per person, about $28.78 an hour each. This is before vehicle wear and the commercial insurance you need when you carry customers' goods. A flat price with your own van pays less per hour than labour-only at $35, so price van jobs higher or skip them.

These add-ons are for direct clients. Say that the client supplies the truck and fuel, books the elevator, and pays any condo move fee. ## What must a moving estimate include in Ontario and Quebec?

In Ontario, a consumer moving contract over $50 must be in writing. It must itemize prices and show the total, the dates and the payment terms. The province suggests also naming the mover's insurer and policy number and stating the crew size and estimated hours. If the contract includes an estimate, the final price can't be more than 10% above it unless the client agrees to extra supplies or services ([Government of Ontario, Hiring a mover](https://www.ontario.ca/page/hiring-mover)). Contracts made in the client's home come with a 10-day cooling-off period, and a mover can't hold belongings to force a renegotiation.

In Quebec, the [Office de la protection du consommateur](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/consumer/good-service/housing-renovation/moving/tip-sheet/) says a contract made by phone or online must be confirmed in writing within 15 days. It advises consumers to pay little or no deposit before the move and to look for movers with at least $1 million in third-party liability insurance. Other provinces have their own consumer rules, so check yours.

Taskrabbit's March 2026 release cites a figure that nearly 27% of Canadians who moved in the past year reported unexpected fees. Clear, written pricing is one way to stand out from movers who add surprise charges.

## How do you raise moving help prices in Canada?

Raise moving help prices when month-end weekends are booked out, when your moves keep running longer than quoted, or when your city's Taskrabbit average rises above your rate.

- **Peak pricing.** Post a higher rate for the last and first days of each month. Taskrabbit's guide notes month-end demand is intense in Toronto and Vancouver.
- **Off-peak discount.** Keep your standard rate and offer weekday or mid-month savings, which fills quiet days without cutting your base rate.
- **New clients first.** Quote the new rate to new clients. Give repeat clients and referrers 30 days' notice.
- **Review the add-ons.** If stairs or long carries keep costing more time than you charge, raise those before the hourly rate.

## What templates help with moving help pricing?

**Quote reply (labour only):**

> Thanks for reaching out. For a one-bedroom with you renting the truck, we'd send 2 helpers at $[35] per helper per hour, with a [3]-hour minimum. Moves like this usually take about [4] hours, so plan for about $[280]. Stairs are $[25] per flight if there's no elevator. Could you confirm the floor at both ends, whether the elevator is booked, how far the truck can park, and a list of large items?

**Estimate lines (adapt for your province):**

> Crew: [2] helpers. Estimated hours: [4]. Rate: $[35] per helper per hour, [3]-hour minimum.
> Add-ons: stairs $[25] per flight × [ ]; long carry $[20].
> Estimate: $[280]. The final price won't exceed the estimate by more than 10% without your OK.
> Insurer: [name], policy [number].
> Client supplies the truck, fuel, elevator booking and any building move fee.

**Rate increase notice:**

> Hi [name], from [date], my moving help rate goes to $[38] per helper per hour, with month-end weekends at $[42]. Bookings made before [date] keep the current rate. Thanks for the repeat work and referrals.

## What moving help pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common moving help pricing mistakes in Canada are skipping a minimum, quoting without asking about stairs and elevators, and starting before the client is packed. Each one can turn a three-hour job into five hours at the same price.

Ask about floors, elevator booking, parking distance and large items before you quote. Put in writing that waiting time for an unpacked client is billable. Agree on payment terms in writing before the day. If you take a deposit, keep it small: in Quebec, the OPC advises consumers to pay little or no deposit before a move.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect moving help prices in Canada?

Moving help income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). You must register for GST/HST once your taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), either in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. That total counts all your taxable side hustles together.

Once you're registered, charge the rate for the province where the service is supplied, from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html), such as 13% HST in Ontario or 5% GST in Alberta. Self-employed people pay both shares of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net income. The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) explains voluntary registration.
