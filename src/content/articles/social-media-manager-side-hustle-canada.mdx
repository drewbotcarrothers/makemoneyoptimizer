<QuickAnswer>
Social media management in Canada means planning, creating, scheduling and reporting on posts for businesses that don't have time to do it themselves, usually on a monthly retainer. Job Bank's closest benchmark, NOC 11202 (advertising, marketing and public relations professionals), shows a **$35.58 national median hourly wage** for employees, with provincial medians from $28.85 in Manitoba to $37.02 in Ontario. Free tools cover the basics: Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram, and Buffer's free plan for up to three channels. Start with one platform and one type of local business. Get written approval rules and access through business tools, not passwords. Follow CASL when you pitch by email, and report the income on T2125.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does social media management in Canada suit?

Social media management suits organised people who can write short copy, make simple graphics or videos, and stay on a schedule. You don't need a marketing degree. You need samples, reliability and a clear package.

It suits:

- **Writers and designers** who already create content (see [freelance writing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada/) and [graphic design](/side-hustles/graphic-design-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Virtual assistants** adding a higher-value service (see [virtual assistant](/side-hustles/virtual-assistant-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **People who've grown their own accounts**, for example on TikTok, Reels or Instagram (see [TikTok and Reels](/side-hustles/tiktok-reels-side-hustle-canada/) and [faceless Instagram](/side-hustles/faceless-instagram-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Former retail, hospitality or real estate staff** who understand how local businesses sell.

It doesn't suit people who dislike deadlines, client feedback or being "on" during evenings and weekends, when many local businesses want posts and replies.

## Real Canadian social media businesses

These are publicly documented examples. Their results are not typical.

**Monarch Social Media (Toronto).** In a March 10, 2025 Toronto Guardian profile, founder Holly Medwid said she started the agency during the pandemic lockdowns, when business owners kept asking her how to use social media to stay in touch with customers while their storefronts were closed. Nearly four years in, the agency had grown to a team of six serving medium-sized businesses across North America. She described a retainer model: every client starts with a six-month retainer, billed at the start of each month and due within 15 days. **Lesson:** retainers with a minimum term make income predictable, and billing in advance avoids chasing payments.

**Mad Social Agency (Winnipeg).** A September 8, 2025 Winnipeg Free Press "Made in Manitoba" story was headlined "400+ brands in 5+ years: Winnipeg-based digital marketing firm Mad Social Agency continues to evolve." **Lesson:** a social-first agency can be built from a Prairie city, serving brands well beyond it.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

**Week 1: Choose and prepare**
- Day 1: Pick one platform and one business type, for example Instagram for independent cafés in your city.
- Day 2: Study 10 local accounts in that niche. Note what the best ones post and how often.
- Day 3: Create three sample posts using public facts about a real or fictional business, clearly labelled as samples.
- Day 4: Define your starter package in numbers: posts per month, platforms, revisions, reporting and response time.
- Day 5: Write your approval and access rules (template below).
- Days 6–7: Set up Calendly, Wave and a one-page service sheet.

**Week 2: Find leads**
- List 30 local businesses with a visible gap: no posts in months, blurry photos or unanswered comments.
- Visit in person, call, or send a short message. If you send commercial emails, follow [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) (CASL): identify yourself, include contact details and an unsubscribe option, and only email addresses that are conspicuously published for business enquiries with no "no unsolicited messages" note.
- Tell your network what you're offering. See [landing your first social media client](/side-hustles/social-media-manager-first-client-canada/).

**Week 3: Run a paid test**
- Offer a two-week paid test at a clear price, with a defined number of posts.
- Get access through Meta Business Suite partner access or the client adding you as a team member, never by sharing their password. Turn on [multi-factor authentication](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts/multi-factor-authentication) on your own accounts, which Get Cyber Safe says to enable whenever it is offered.
- Publish only approved posts and keep a log.

**Week 4: Report and convert**
- Send a one-page report: what was posted, reach, engagement, follower change and what you'd do next month.
- Offer a monthly retainer with a minimum term and payment in advance.

### Days 31–90
- **Weeks 5–6:** Build reusable templates in Canva for each client: brand colours, fonts and post types.
- **Weeks 7–8:** Batch content: shoot on-site once a month and schedule two to four weeks ahead.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Ask your first client for a testimonial and a referral.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Add a second client in the same niche, so you can reuse what you've learned, or add a second platform for your first client.

## How to structure your packages

Clients compare packages, not hourly rates. Build three tiers so the middle one looks like the obvious choice:

| Tier | What's included | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | One platform, a set number of posts a month, one revision round, a monthly report | Small local businesses testing outsourcing |
| Growth | Two platforms, more posts including short videos, comment replies during set hours, a monthly report and call | Busy owners who want to stop posting themselves |
| Content day add-on | One on-site visit for photos and clips, edited into the month's posts | Restaurants, gyms, salons and trades with a visual product |

Put numbers on every line (posts, platforms, revisions and response hours), and price each tier so your effective hourly rate stays where you want it after the time estimate in the worked example. Charge setup separately for the first month's audit, brand templates and access work.

## Content pillars for local businesses

Most local accounts run out of ideas, not time. Give each client four or five recurring content pillars:

1. **The product or service:** what it is, how it's made, what it costs.
2. **The people:** staff introductions and the owner's story.
3. **Proof:** customer reviews (with permission), before-and-afters, finished jobs.
4. **Education:** tips, FAQs and "how to choose" posts.
5. **Community:** local events, partner businesses and seasonal moments.

Rotate the pillars week by week. A content day can capture enough material for several weeks of posts across all five.

## Handling negative comments and reviews

Set the rules before anything goes wrong:

- **Respond, don't argue.** A short, polite public reply plus an offer to continue privately.
- **Escalate fast.** Refund requests, safety complaints, legal threats and media enquiries go to the owner the same day.
- **Never delete legitimate criticism** unless it breaks the platform's rules (hate, spam, personal information).
- **Keep a log** of issues and how they were resolved. It protects you and the client.
- **Pause scheduled posts** during a crisis or a sensitive news event, so an upbeat promo doesn't go out at the wrong moment.

**Client onboarding checklist**
- [ ] Signed agreement: scope, price, term, payment terms and cancellation
- [ ] Access granted through business tools (Meta Business Suite partner access, a TikTok Business Centre invite, LinkedIn page admin)
- [ ] Brand assets: logo, colours, fonts and photo folder
- [ ] Voice notes: words to use, words to avoid, emoji yes or no

Posts without a response by the deadline will [be held / go out as drafted]. Replies to comments and DMs happen during [hours]. Complaints, refunds and anything legal go straight to you.

## Worked example: two retainer clients

**Illustrative.** The package price and hours are assumptions for the arithmetic, not market rates. The benchmark comes from Job Bank, and the tool prices from the vendor pages above.

Assume two café clients, each on a package of 12 posts a month on Instagram and Facebook, at an assumed $600 a month each. You use Meta Business Suite (free) and Canva's free plan.

- Revenue: 2 × $600 = $1,200 a month.
- Time: suppose 3 hours planning and shooting, 6 hours creating, 2 hours scheduling and replying, and 1 hour reporting per client. That's 12 hours per client, 24 hours in total.
- Effective rate: $1,200 ÷ 24 = **$50 an hour** before tax and CPP.

Compare that with Job Bank's $35.58 national employee median. The gap pays for your tools, both halves of CPP, unpaid sales time and months without a client. If a client wants daily posting or round-the-clock replies, the same price drops your effective rate fast. That's why the package needs written numbers.

## Rules that apply to client posts

- **Advertising claims:** posts must not be false or misleading. The Competition Bureau's guidance on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) applies to your clients' advertising, including posts you write. Don't invent reviews, results or "limited time" pressure.
- **Influencer and paid posts:** if a client pays or gifts creators, the material connection has to be disclosed clearly.
- **Commercial emails and DMs:** CASL covers commercial electronic messages, including some messages sent through social platforms. Get consent and identify the sender.
- **Photos of people:** get permission before posting customers or staff, especially children.
- **Platform rules:** contests and giveaways must follow each platform's promotion rules. Contest laws vary, so check the rules for your client's province before running one.

## Which tax rules apply to social media management in Canada?

Social media management income is self-employment income. It's reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on their net income (QPP in Quebec). GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the first-dollar [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

- **Small-supplier test:** worldwide taxable supplies, including your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive quarters. Two retainers at $600 a month total $14,400 a year, but add any other side-hustle income too.
- **Rates:** once registered, charge the rate for the place of supply. Ontario is 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% HST (since April 1, 2025, per the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table) and Alberta 5% GST. Quebec adds QST. Confirm with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Clients outside Canada:** services to non-residents may qualify as zero-rated exports. Check the CRA's rules before you leave tax off an invoice.
- **Deductions:** software subscriptions, a share of your phone and internet, equipment and travel to client shoots can be business expenses.
- **Records:** keep contracts, invoices and payment records, plus a mileage log for on-site shoots.

## Common social media management mistakes

- **Taking client passwords.** Use business-manager access, so you can be removed cleanly and the client keeps ownership.
- **Unlimited revisions.** Write one round into the agreement.
- **Selling followers.** Promising follower counts invites shortcuts that can break platform rules and misleading-advertising rules.
- **Too many platforms too soon.** One platform done well beats four done badly.
- **No minimum term.** Results take time, and month-to-month clients churn early.
- **Answering complaints yourself.** Route refunds, legal issues and angry customers to the owner.
