A 30-day freelance writing launch plan for Canada uses week 1 to pick a niche and write samples, week 2 to set rates and build a portfolio and prospect list, week 3 to pitch every weekday, and week 4 to deliver, invoice, and refine. It suits clear writers who can meet deadlines, take edits without fuss, and keep pitching through silence.

## What happens in week 1 of a freelance writing launch plan in Canada?

Week 1 is positioning and proof: choose what you write about, produce samples, and secure the accounts you will work from.

### Days 1 and 2: Choose a niche

List subjects you know from work, study, or long interest, and the formats clients buy (blog posts, newsletters, product descriptions, case studies). Pick one niche and one or two formats to start.

### Days 3 to 5: Write samples

Write two or three original samples of 600 to 900 words in your niche. Do not invent statistics or quotes; cite real sources the way a client would expect.

### Days 6 and 7: Set up and secure your tools

A free word processor and a spreadsheet are enough to start. Turn on multi-factor authentication on email and cloud accounts; Get Cyber Safe's [MFA page](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts/multi-factor-authentication) says to enable it whenever a site, app, or device allows it.

## What happens in week 2 of a freelance writing launch plan in Canada?

Week 2 is the offer: set rates and terms, publish your samples somewhere linkable, and build a prospect list.

### Days 8 and 9: Set rates and terms

Use the [pricing guide](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-pricing-canada/) to choose per-word, per-piece, or hourly pricing. Write a one-page agreement covering scope, revision rounds, deadlines, payment, and who owns the work. This is not legal advice.

### Days 10 and 11: Build a portfolio page

Put samples on a free portfolio page or a shared folder, with a short bio and contact details. A paid site can wait until a client asks for a URL.

### Days 12 to 14: Build a prospect list

List 30 to 50 businesses or publications in your niche that publish the format you write. Note who to contact and whether they publish contact details for pitches or say not to contact them.

## What happens in week 3 of a freelance writing launch plan in Canada?

Week 3 is pitching: send a few tailored pitches every weekday, follow up once, and optionally test a marketplace.

### Days 15 to 17: Pitch within CASL

Canada's anti-spam law requires consent before sending commercial electronic messages. ISED's [consent page](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) lists questions to ask first, including whether the person published their contact information online or gave you a business card, and says to look closely for any "don't contact" instructions. Keep pitches specific, identify yourself, and stop when asked. This is not legal advice.

### Days 18 and 19: Test one marketplace, if it suits you

Some writers find first clients on marketplaces. The [Fiverr vs Upwork comparison](/side-hustles/fiverr-vs-upwork-canada/) and [Upwork vs LinkedIn](/side-hustles/upwork-vs-linkedin-finding-clients-canada/) cover fees and fit. Pick one, not three.

### Days 20 and 21: Follow up

Send one polite follow-up to pitches with no reply after about a week. Track every pitch, reply, and outcome in a spreadsheet.

## What happens in week 4 of a freelance writing launch plan in Canada?

Week 4 is delivery and review: do excellent work for whoever says yes, invoice cleanly, ask for a testimonial, and decide what to change.

### Days 22 to 25: Deliver the first piece

Confirm the brief in writing, deliver on time, and handle edits within the agreed rounds.

### Days 26 and 27: Invoice and get paid

Send an invoice with your name, the client, the work, the amount, and payment terms. A spreadsheet works at first; <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> is one option once invoices pile up.

### Days 28 to 30: Ask for a testimonial and review the month

Ask a satisfied client for a short testimonial you can quote with permission. Review your pitch log: which niches and messages got replies? Adjust for month two.

### Illustrative example

A teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast: suppose you send 3 pitches each weekday in week 3 (15 pitches) and 2 a day in week 4 (10), for 25 pitches. If 1 in 10 became a paid piece at a price P, that would be 2 or 3 pieces. If P were $150, that is $300–$450 before tax. Your reply rate and price will differ, and many writers take longer.

## What freelance writing launch mistakes should you avoid in Canada?

Common launch mistakes are pitching everyone with the same template, ignoring consent rules, writing samples on topics you do not want to be hired for, working without a written agreement, and paying for tools before a client exists. More are in [freelance writing mistakes](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-mistakes-canada/).

## Which tax rules apply when launching freelance writing in Canada?

Freelance writing income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and the self-employed pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
