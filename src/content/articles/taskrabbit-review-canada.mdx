TaskRabbit is a legitimate way for Canadians in its service cities to get paid for furniture assembly, mounting, moving help, cleaning, and other local tasks. As of October 2026, Canadian Taskers pay a one-time $25 registration fee and, according to TaskRabbit, "all fees are charged to Clients, not Taskers" ([TaskRabbit](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260480151579-What-s-the-Registration-Fee)). On self-set hourly tasks you keep 100% of your hourly rate plus tips. TaskRabbit does not publish typical Tasker earnings, and this review does not estimate them; income depends on your city, categories, rate, and how many invitations you accept.

## Is TaskRabbit available in Canada?

Yes, in specific cities. TaskRabbit's [locations page](https://www.taskrabbit.ca/locations) lists Canadian cities including Toronto and much of the GTA, Ottawa, Hamilton, London, Kingston, Windsor, Montreal, Quebec City, Gatineau, Vancouver, Victoria, Burnaby, Surrey, Nanaimo, Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg, and Halifax. City guides cover local demand in [Toronto](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-toronto-canada/), [Vancouver](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-vancouver-canada/), [Calgary](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-calgary-canada/), [Ottawa](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-ottawa-canada/), [Halifax](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-halifax-canada/) and [Victoria](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-victoria-canada/). To become a Tasker in Canada, TaskRabbit's [requirements page](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260520394651-What-s-Required-to-Become-a-Tasker) says you need to:

- be at least 19 years old
- be able to work in an active Canadian city
- provide personal information for ID verification, including a SIN
- have a Canadian bank account (savings accounts, prepaid debit cards and reloadable cards won't work)
- have a valid credit or debit card for the $25 registration fee
- have a smartphone for the Tasker app

Registration happens only in the Tasker app. TaskRabbit says profile verification takes up to two working days, followed by an ID and selfie check ([TaskRabbit](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260467885979-How-Do-I-Become-a-Tasker)). Paying the fee does not guarantee approval.

## What fees does TaskRabbit charge Canadian Taskers?

Only the $25 registration fee, according to TaskRabbit. The client-side [service fee](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260411872155-What-s-the-Taskrabbit-Service-Fee) is a percentage of the client's total, excluding reimbursements, and the rate a client sees includes both your rate and that fee. TaskRabbit says Taskers receive 100% of any reimbursements.

| Item | Who pays | Amount (as of October 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration fee | Tasker | $25, one time, non-refundable |
| Service fee | Client | A percentage of the task total, shown to the client |
| Trust and support fee | Client | Shown on the client invoice |
| Reimbursements (supplies you buy for the task) | Client | Paid 100% to the Tasker |
| Tips | Client | Paid 100% to the Tasker on self-set hourly tasks |

## How does pay work on TaskRabbit in Canada?

It depends on the earning structure shown on each invitation. TaskRabbit's [Canadian blog](https://www.taskrabbit.ca/blog/how-pricing-works-on-taskrabbit-and-what-it-means-for-your-earnings/) describes three:

- **Self-set hourly:** you set your own rate per category and get 100% of it plus 100% of tips. Used in most categories.
- **Pre-set hourly:** a set hourly rate for some direct-hire jobs.
- **Task-based:** a fixed payment per task, such as IKEA assembly and partner tasks.

The [rates policy](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260502667035-Tasker-Rates-Minimum-Hours-Policy) says hourly tasks have a one-hour minimum, longer tasks are invoiced in 15-minute increments, and Taskers can opt into a two-hour minimum if they disclose it. You can opt out of categories whose earning structure you don't want.

### Illustrative example

Assumed numbers: a Tasker sets an assumed $45 hourly rate for furniture assembly. A job takes 2 hours 30 minutes, and the Tasker buys $20 of brackets the client agreed to reimburse.

- Time: 2.5 × $45 = $112.50
- Reimbursement: $20, paid in full
- Total to the Tasker before any tip: $112.50 + $20 = $132.50

## How do TaskRabbit payouts work in Canada?

By direct deposit in Canadian dollars to an active chequing account. TaskRabbit's [direct deposit page](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-ca/articles/46260413164315-Setting-Up-Your-Direct-Deposit) says savings accounts, prepaid debit cards and reloadable cards are not valid. Its Canadian blog says payments are processed through Stripe and most arrive within 1 to 3 business days after task completion, with tips paid alongside.

## What are the pros and cons of TaskRabbit for Canadians?

**Pros**
- No commission on your self-set hourly rate; client pays the service fee.
- Reimbursements and tips paid in full.
- Clients arrive through the app, so you spend less time marketing.
- Fast direct deposit payouts in CAD.

**Cons**
- Only available in listed cities.
- $25 non-refundable fee, with no guarantee of approval.
- Fixed-price and pre-set hourly tasks limit your pricing control in those categories.
- Client-facing price includes the service fee, so you compete on a higher sticker price than off-app work.
- You supply your own tools, transport, and insurance decisions.

## What mistakes do Canadians make on TaskRabbit?

- **Setting rates too low to win the first jobs.** Factor in travel time, supplies, and taxes.
- **Not logging reimbursable supplies.** Add them to the invoice; you get 100% back.
- **Accepting categories with an earning structure you dislike.** You can opt out.
- **Using a savings account for payouts.** Payouts fail without a chequing account.
- **Skipping the business-use question with your insurer.** Ask what applies when you work in clients' homes.

## How is TaskRabbit income taxed in Canada?

Taskers are independent contractors, so TaskRabbit income is self-employment income reported on Form T2125, and the $25 registration fee, tools, and supplies are potential business expenses. Platforms like TaskRabbit fall under the CRA's [reporting rules for digital platform operators](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/reporting-rules-digital-platforms.html), which require platforms to report seller information and income to the CRA.
