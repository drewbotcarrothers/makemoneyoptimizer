## Which pricing models work for TikTok and Reels clients in Canada?

Short-form video for clients in Canada is usually sold per video, as a monthly package, or as a filming-day rate, with usage terms on top. Monthly packages suit businesses that need steady posting; per-video pricing suits tests.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per video | One price per finished clip, set number of revisions | First jobs, one-off promotions | Unlimited revisions |
| Monthly package | Set number of videos per month, filmed in one or two sessions | Local businesses that post weekly | Scope creep into posting and replies |
| Filming day + editing | Day or half-day on site, edit priced per clip | Restaurants, events, shops | Travel time and parking |
| Raw footage only | Unedited clips for the client's team | Businesses with an editor | Usage terms still apply |
| Usage add-on | Extra fee when clips run as paid ads or beyond a set period | Clips used in ads | Not writing the period down |

## How do you calculate a TikTok and Reels video price in Canada?

Calculate a short-form video price in Canada from your hourly target, the hours per clip, a share of equipment, and a usage fee for ads or long terms. Then compare with what local businesses already pay.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly rate

Start at or above the Job Bank median for your province and add for unpaid admin and pitching.

### Step 2: Time one clip from brief to delivery

Planning, shot list, filming, editing, captions, music checks, export, and one revision. Most first estimates are too short.

### Step 3: Add travel and equipment

Travel time to the client and a monthly share of phone, mic, lights, and editing software.

### Step 4: Write the usage terms

Organic posting on the client's own accounts for a set period is the base price. Paid ads, whitelisting through your account, or longer use cost extra.

### Step 5: Set aside tax and CPP, then round

Self-employed people pay both shares of CPP; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $45 an hour, between Job Bank's national median and high for video editors. Each 30-second clip takes 2 hours: 30 minutes planning, 30 minutes filming within a shared session, 45 minutes editing, and 15 minutes on one revision. Labour is 2 × $45 = $90 per clip.

A monthly package of 8 clips is 16 hours, or $720. Add one 45-minute round trip per filming session, two sessions a month: 1.5 hours × $45 = $67.50. Add an assumed $40 a month as the client's share of equipment and software. The total is $827.50, which you might round to $825 a month, or about $103 per clip.

If the client wants to run two clips as paid ads for 3 months, you might add an assumed 30% of those clips' price: 2 × $103 × 0.30 = $61.80, rounded to $60.

## When should you raise TikTok and Reels prices in Canada?

Raise short-form video prices in Canada when your calendar is full, when clips take longer than you priced, or when clients start using them in paid ads. Give monthly clients notice, often 30 days, and change new-client prices first.

A package that keeps growing in shots, locations, or revisions needs a new price, not just a new month.

## What TikTok and Reels pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common short-form video pricing mistakes in Canada are pricing only the editing time, leaving usage open-ended, and agreeing to post and reply to comments for free. Each one turns a package into a part-time job at a low rate.

If you post a paid clip on your own account, the Competition Bureau's [influencer guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says to disclose material connections such as payment or free products, clearly and in the post itself. Put revisions, usage, and what "posting" includes in writing. The [TikTok and Reels startup costs guide](/side-hustles/tiktok-reels-startup-costs-canada/) helps you count the equipment share.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect TikTok and Reels prices in Canada?

Video income from clients in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your businesses. When registered, use the rate from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html).
