Bike and e-bike delivery in Canada means carrying restaurant meals, groceries, parcels or documents by bicycle, either through an app like DoorDash or Uber Eats or for a local courier company. It's one of the few side hustles you can start without a car. Since 2024 and 2025, B.C. and Ontario have set minimum-pay rules for app-based delivery: B.C.'s floor is $21.89 an hour of engaged time plus at least $0.35 per kilometre (from June 1, 2026), and Ontario's Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act guarantees at least the general minimum wage per assignment or pay period. You need a bike, a smartphone, a good lock and, in Ontario, a helmet if you ride an e-bike.

<QuickAnswer>
- **What it pays:** Job Bank's latest medians (2023–2024 data, updated November 19, 2025) are $23.00 an hour for couriers and $20.00 for food delivery drivers in Canada. App pay is per trip, with provincial floors in B.C. and Ontario.
- **B.C. pay floor:** $21.89 per hour of engaged time (accepting an order to completing or cancelling it) plus at least $0.35 per km for delivery by bike, e-bike or vehicle, according to the B.C. government. Waiting between orders isn't covered.
- **Ontario pay floor:** since July 1, 2025, delivery platform workers get at least the general minimum wage, $17.95 from October 1, 2026, per assignment or pay period, not counting tips.
- **E-bike rules (Ontario):** max assisted speed 32 km/h, max 120 kg, motor 500 W or less, rider 16 or older, approved helmet required.
- **Startup cost:** a bike you already own plus a lock (Kryptonite Kryptolok Mini-7 was $77.73 on Amazon.ca on October 9, 2026), lights and an insulated bag.
- **Tax:** app couriers are usually self-employed; report on Form T2125, pay both CPP shares on profit, and register for GST/HST past $30,000.

## How much do bike couriers earn in Canada?

There are two kinds of bike delivery work, and they pay differently.

**Courier company jobs.** Traditional couriers carry documents, cheques and parcels for law firms, banks and offices. Job Bank's courier wages (occupation ID 2427) put the national median at [$23.00 an hour](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/2427/ca), with a low of $17.50 and a high of $35.00. Provincial medians are $23.08 in Ontario, $22.41 in Quebec, $22.00 in B.C., $25.00 in Alberta and $23.80 in Nova Scotia. These figures cover all couriers, including drivers, not just cyclists.

**App-based food delivery.** Job Bank's food delivery driver wages show a [$20.00 national median](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/21666/ca), with a low of $15.00 and a high of $29.35; the Ontario and B.C. medians are both $20.00 and Quebec's is $19.00. Delivery apps don't pay by the hour. You earn per order, plus tips and any promotions, so your effective rate depends on how many orders you take in each hour.

### The pay floors for app delivery

**British Columbia.** The province's [minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) sets a separate floor for online platform workers who deliver orders. As of June 1, 2026, it's $21.89 per hour of engaged time, up from $21.43 in 2025 and $20.88 in 2024. Engaged time "starts when they accept an order or request, and ends when they complete it or it's cancelled." Delivery workers also get a distance expense allowance of at least $0.35 per kilometre "while using a form of transport other than walking," which includes bikes. B.C.'s general minimum wage is $18.25.

**Ontario.** The [Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers) came into force July 1, 2025. It covers ride share, delivery and courier work offered through an app or website, "regardless of whether they are employees or independent contractors." Workers must be paid at least the general minimum wage under the Employment Standards Act, calculated per work assignment or per pay period, and tips don't count toward it. Ontario's general minimum wage rose to $17.95 on October 1, 2026, [CBC News reported](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/manitoba/minimum-wage-manitoba-saskatchewan-ontario-pei-nova-scotia-9.7364970). The Act also requires written pay information within 24 hours of each assignment and two weeks' written notice before a removal of 24 hours or more, with some exceptions.

In both provinces, the floor covers only engaged time, not the minutes you spend waiting for the next order. That's why time logged in and time paid can be very different.

## What are the rules for riding an e-bike for delivery?

Rules are provincial, and some cities add their own limits on trails and paths.

**Ontario.** The province's [e-bike page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/riding-e-bike) says an e-bike must have a maximum assisted speed of 32 km/h, weigh no more than 120 kg including the battery, and have a motor of 500 watts or less, with no modifications to exceed those limits. Riders must be 16 or older, wear an approved bicycle or motorcycle helmet, keep the bike in good working order and follow the same rules of the road as other cyclists. Some municipal trails and bike lanes prohibit e-bikes.

**Other provinces.** B.C., Quebec, Alberta and the rest set their own power-assisted bicycle rules. Check your provincial transportation ministry and your city's bylaws before buying an e-bike for delivery.

**Platform rules.** DoorDash's Canadian sign-up page lists the requirements as being 18 or older, using "any car, scooter, or bicycle (in select cities)," consenting to a background check and providing two pieces of government ID plus a Business Number or Social Insurance Number ([DoorDash](https://dasher.doordash.com/en-ca)). Check the current requirements in each app for your city.

## A 4-week plan to start bike delivery

### Week 1: Set up your bike and your area

- Get a tune-up or check the brakes, tires and chain yourself.
- Buy or borrow a U-lock, front and rear lights, and a helmet.
- If you're buying an e-bike, check it meets your province's rules (in Ontario: 32 km/h, 120 kg, 500 W, 16+).
- Map a delivery zone you can ride comfortably: dense, flat if possible, and close to restaurant clusters.

### Week 2: Sign up and get approved

- Apply to DoorDash and Uber Eats, and check whether bike delivery is offered in your city.
- Have two pieces of government ID and your SIN ready, and expect a background check.
- Read the pay explanation each platform must give you. In Ontario, the operator must provide pay calculation, pay period and rating information within 24 hours of giving you access.
- Order an insulated backpack if the app doesn't supply one.

### Week 3: Do short test shifts

- Ride 2 to 3 hours at the lunch rush and 2 to 3 at dinner.
- Turn down orders that are too far, too heavy or too many drinks for a bike. DoorDash told CBC that large retail orders or those with several beverages go by car.
- Log every shift: time logged in, engaged time, orders, kilometres, pay and tips.
- Charge your phone and battery pack before each shift.

### Week 4: Optimize

- Calculate your hourly earnings for time logged in and for engaged time, and compare them with your province's floor.
- Shift your hours to the busiest windows in your area.
- Try multi-apping (running two apps at once) only if each platform's terms allow it, and never accept more than you can deliver safely.
- Decide whether you'll keep riding through winter or treat this as a three-season side hustle.

## Templates and checklists

### Pre-shift checklist

- Tires pumped, brakes working, chain lubed
- Lights charged and mounted
- Phone charged, battery pack in bag, mount secure
- Lock and key (or combination) with you
- Insulated bag clean and dry
- Rain gear packed if the forecast is mixed
- Helmet on (required on e-bikes in Ontario)

### Pitch to a local business for direct deliveries

> Hi [Owner], I'm a local bike courier covering [neighbourhood]. I can deliver your orders within [3 km] for a flat $[X] per drop, invoiced weekly, with a photo at each drop-off. I'm available [days and hours]. Would you like to try it for two weeks?

## Real Canadian examples

**Mark Huggan, Phantom Couriers (Vancouver).** The Tyee profiled Huggan's company in September 2026. He has been a courier since 1995 and started out printing flyers advertising his bike courier service and sliding them under office doors. Law firms still trust bike couriers with cheques and court documents. Huggan said he once employed more than 30 bike couriers; by the time COVID reached Vancouver he had five, and now he has one staff bike courier. He told The Tyee he pays $30 an hour. The company has expanded into car and truck couriering, luggage storage and a digital mail service, and its courier service gained 250 clients this year. Vancouver had 412 licensed bike couriers in 1998, 82 by 2016 and 10 today, according to the article ([The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/09/14/Vancouver-Last-Bike-Couriers/)). Huggan's own summary: "I do think it's a dying, cool business, but I think it's necessary."

**Phantom's courier on the road.** The same article follows the company's last staff bike courier, who asked to go by a pseudonym. He made 16 stops by 10:40 a.m. on a 30- and 60-minute deadline schedule and spent about two hours that day waiting in banks. "Of all the jobs in this pay realm, it's the best I've worked," he said. The article also cites WorkBC data that couriers in B.C. average $43,990 a year, with hourly pay from minimum wage to $32.

**Alex Polt, Winnipeg.** CBC Manitoba spoke with Polt in August 2023, eight months after he arrived from Ukraine. He rode for two food delivery companies with a thermal backpack, protective glasses and a helmet. "Sometimes I get three at the same time, so you need to be really fast to deliver it and you need to be careful to not spill drinks," he said. In the same story, a labour studies professor at the University of Manitoba noted that app couriers are often independent contractors paid per delivery ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/manitoba/food-bicycle-winnipeg-couriers-safety-1.6928592)).

## Worked math: what a weekend of bike delivery earns

*Illustrative example. App pay varies by city, time and demand; these numbers apply the provincial floors to an assumed shift.*

**B.C. rider, 4-hour dinner shift:**

| Item | Assumption | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time logged in | 4 hours | n/a |
| Engaged time | 2.5 hours (assumed) | n/a |
| Minimum for engaged time | 2.5 × $21.89 | $54.73 |
| Distance allowance | 30 km engaged × $0.35 | $10.50 |
| **Guaranteed minimum** | | **$65.23** |
| Per hour logged in | $65.23 ÷ 4 | $16.31 |

If your app pay for those orders is higher than the floor, you get the higher amount. Tips come on top.

**Ontario rider, same shift:** 2.5 engaged hours × $17.95 = $44.88 minimum, or $11.22 per hour logged in, before tips. Waiting time isn't covered in either province, so cutting idle time is the biggest lever on your real hourly rate.

**Courier company job:** at the Job Bank courier median of $23.00, a 4-hour shift is $92.00 before deductions. Hourly employees are generally paid for waiting time during a shift, too.

**Costs to subtract (self-employed):** lock, lights, bag, tune-ups, tires, brake pads, phone data and, for e-bikes, charging and battery replacement. Keep receipts, because these may be deductible business expenses.

## Taxes for bike couriers

Delivery app couriers are usually treated as self-employed, which means:

- **Report income and expenses** on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Use your app's annual earnings statement and your shift log.
- **CPP:** you pay both the employee and employer shares of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net self-employment income.
- **GST/HST:** the CRA's first-dollar registration rule in its [taxi and ride-sharing guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) covers passenger transport, not food or parcel delivery. Delivery couriers follow the normal [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters.
- **Expenses:** bike repairs, parts, locks, lights, bags, the business share of your phone plan and e-bike charging may be deductible. Keep receipts and your log.

If you work for a courier company as an employee, you'll get a T4 and your employer deducts CPP, EI and income tax.

## Provincial notes

- **British Columbia:** delivery platform workers get $21.89 per engaged hour plus at least $0.35/km from June 1, 2026. Job Bank courier median $22.00; WorkBC data cited by The Tyee averages $43,990 a year for couriers.
- **Ontario:** the Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act guarantees at least the general minimum wage ($17.95 from October 1, 2026) per assignment or pay period. E-bike riders must be 16+ and wear a helmet; e-bikes are limited to 32 km/h, 120 kg and 500 W. Courier median $23.08.
- **Quebec:** courier median $22.41 and food delivery median $19.00. Self-employed income also counts for QPP, which replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered.
- **Alberta:** courier median $25.00, the highest of the provinces listed here; minimum wage is $15.00 (CBC).
- **Manitoba:** courier median $21.50; minimum wage is $16.40 from October 1, 2026 (CBC). A 2023 CBC story found bike delivery growing in Winnipeg.

## What should you keep in mind before you commit?

Bike delivery is easy to start, but the pay floors apply only to engaged time, so slow nights can earn well below minimum wage per hour logged in. Weather cuts into your season. Ice, snow and early darkness make winter riding slower and riskier, and many riders stop or switch modes. Traffic is the real hazard: a Toronto Metropolitan University occupational health researcher told CBC that risks are amplified when workers rush. Bike theft is common, so lock up every time. Traditional bike courier work is shrinking, too: Vancouver went from 412 licensed bike couriers in 1998 to 10 today. Treat bike delivery as flexible extra income, track your real hourly rate, and don't ride faster than conditions allow to chase an order.
