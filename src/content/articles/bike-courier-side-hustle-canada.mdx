## Who does bike delivery in Canada suit?

Bike and e-bike delivery in Canada suits people who are comfortable riding in city traffic, live within easy reach of busy restaurant streets, and can work the lunch and dinner peaks. It is a poor fit if you live in a spread-out suburb where orders are far apart, if you're not confident riding in traffic, or if you need steady income through a Canadian winter without a backup plan.

Distance is the key variable. A bike is efficient over short urban hops and struggles with long suburban runs. Before signing up, look at a map of where restaurants cluster near you and how far typical deliveries would be.

## What are the rules for delivering by bike or e-bike in Canada?

Delivering by bike in Canada means meeting the app's requirements and your province's cycling and e-bike rules. Each app sets its own requirements by city, and each province regulates e-bikes, so check both before you ride.

**App requirements.** DoorDash's [Canadian Dasher page](https://dasher.doordash.com/en-ca) lists that Dashers must be 18 or older, that a car, scooter, or bicycle can be used (bicycles in select cities), and that you consent to a background check and provide two pieces of government ID and a valid Business Number or Social Insurance Number. Other apps publish their own requirements in their Canadian sign-up pages.

**E-bike rules, Ontario example.** Ontario's [riding an e-bike](https://www.ontario.ca/page/riding-e-bike) page says riders must be 16 or older, wear an approved bicycle or motorcycle helmet, and follow the same rules of the road as other cyclists. The e-bike must have a maximum assisted speed of 32 km/h, a maximum weight of 120 kg including the battery, a motor no greater than 500 watts, and two independent braking systems. Removing the pedals makes it a motor vehicle that needs a licence, insurance, and registration. Municipal bylaws can ban e-bikes on some paths, lanes, and sidewalks.

Other provinces set their own e-bike rules, so check your provincial transportation ministry. Cities can also restrict where you ride and park.

## How much can bike delivery earn in Canada?

Bike delivery earnings in Canada depend on the app's pay model, tips, city density, and how many orders you can complete in an hour, so this guide does not publish a national rate. What you keep depends on:

- Order density near you, and the distance of each delivery

## How do you start bike delivery in Canada?

Starting bike delivery in Canada means checking the rules, preparing your bike, and testing a few peak shifts. The steps below follow that order.

### Step 1: Check where bike delivery is allowed

Open the Canadian sign-up page for each app you're considering and confirm bikes are accepted in your city. [DoorDash](https://dasher.doordash.com/en-ca) says bicycles are allowed in select cities.

### Step 2: Check your province's e-bike rules

If you'll ride an e-bike, read your province's page. In Ontario, that is [riding an e-bike](https://www.ontario.ca/page/riding-e-bike). Don't modify the motor or remove the pedals.

### Step 3: Tune up the bike and gear up

Brakes, tires, and chain first. Add lights, a lock, a phone mount, and an insulated bag.

### Step 4: Sign up and complete screening

Provide the ID and information the app asks for and wait for approval. Read the app's rules on deactivation, tips, and insurance.

### Step 5: Ride a test route

Before your first shift, ride the restaurant streets near you at dinner time. Note bike lanes, hills, and buildings with tricky entrances.

### Step 6: Work two peak shifts and record everything

Log start and end times, deliveries, pay, tips, and kilometres. Compare the hourly result with what you expected.

### Step 7: Plan for winter

Decide whether you'll ride in snow, switch to transit-and-foot delivery where allowed, or take winter off. Cold drains e-bike batteries and phones faster.

## What mistakes are common in bike delivery in Canada?

Common bike delivery mistakes in Canada are buying an e-bike before testing demand, riding without lights, and ignoring local e-bike rules. Each one costs money or safety.

- **Buying expensive gear first.** Test with the bike you have.
- **No lights or reflective gear.** Evening shifts and winter afternoons are dark.
- **Illegal e-bikes.** A modified or over-powered e-bike may be treated as a motor vehicle.
- **Sidewalk riding.** Many cities ban it; follow local bylaws.
- **Not tracking shifts.** Without a log you can't tell which hours are worth riding.

## Which tax rules apply to bike delivery in Canada?

Bike delivery income in Canada from delivery apps is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Food and parcel delivery follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) for GST/HST. The CRA's first-dollar registration rule on its [taxi and ride-sharing page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) applies to carrying passengers, not delivering food, unless you also drive passengers. Keep receipts for bike repairs, gear, and the business share of your phone plan. See [tax on reselling and gig app platform income](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).
