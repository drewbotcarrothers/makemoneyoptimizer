<QuickAnswer>
Printables are digital files, such as planners, checklists, worksheets, wall art and budget sheets, that buyers download and print at home. In Canada, you can sell them on Etsy (US$0.20 per listing, a 6.5% transaction fee, and 3% + C$0.25 payment processing for Canadian shops), on Gumroad (10% + $0.50 per direct sale) or on Payhip (5% per sale on the free plan). Nothing ships, so margins are high, but competition is heavy. You need original work or a licence that allows resale. Canva's licence restricts reselling Pro elements on their own. Report the income on T2125, and track GST/HST against the $30,000 threshold.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does selling printables in Canada suit?

Printables suit people who can design clean, useful pages and who'll keep adding products for months. Each sale is small, so income comes from a catalogue and from search traffic over time.

It suits:

- **Organisers and planners** who already build their own systems.
- **Teachers and tutors** who create worksheets and classroom materials.
- **Designers and illustrators** (see [graphic design](/side-hustles/graphic-design-side-hustle-canada/) and [Canva creator](/side-hustles/canva-creator-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Spreadsheet fans** who can build budget and tracker templates.
- **Etsy sellers** adding digital items with no shipping (see [Etsy shop](/side-hustles/etsy-shop-side-hustle-canada/)).

It doesn't suit people expecting passive income from a handful of files. Read [the reality of passive income](/side-hustles/passive-income-reality-canada/) first. Searches for popular printables return thousands of listings, so originality and niche choice matter.

## Worked math: one $8 printable

**Illustrative.** The $8 price is an assumption. The fee rates come from the sources above.

| Platform | Fees on one $8 sale | You keep |
|---|---|---|
| Etsy (6.5% + 3% + $0.25, plus a US$0.20 listing renewal, about C$0.28) | $0.52 + $0.24 + $0.25 + $0.28 = $1.29 | ≈ $6.71 |
| Gumroad direct (10% + $0.50) | $0.80 + $0.50 = $1.30 | ≈ $6.70 |
| Gumroad via Discover (30%) | $2.40 | ≈ $5.60 |
| Payhip free plan (5%, before processor fees) | $0.40 | ≈ $7.60 before processor fees |

Etsy's regulatory operating fee and currency conversion aren't included. Selling 100 copies a month at about $6.70 is roughly $670 a month. Reaching 100 sales of one file is the hard part, which is why sellers build catalogues and bundles.

## Real Canadian printable sellers

These are publicly documented interviews. Their results are not typical.

**Emily McDermott (Pretty Arrow).** In a Niche Pursuits interview (last updated July 7, 2024), McDermott said she lives in Canada and makes $100,000 a year from her Etsy shop selling budgeting spreadsheets. **Lesson:** a focused niche, budgeting, can support a whole catalogue of related digital products.

**Mallaury Agyei (Premium PLR for Bloggers, Toronto).** In a September 9, 2024 Niche Pursuits interview, Agyei, who lives in Toronto, said she earns $5,000 a month selling private-label-rights templates and printables on Etsy and her own website. She started with five products, worked between her children's naps, and began with food-focused printables before adding other niches. She had previously sold a blog for around $25,000. **Lesson:** start small in one niche, use search, and sell on your own site as well as a marketplace.

## Copyright, licences and Canva

A printable has to be a file you have the right to sell. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office explains [copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright). Your own writing, layouts and drawings are the safest starting material.

If you design in Canva, read the [Canva Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/) before listing. It allows many uses of designs, but it says you may not copy, download or distribute Pro content as a standalone item. A printable that's mostly a Canva element with your name on it may not be yours to sell. Selling "editable Canva templates" through Canva's template-sharing links is a separate model with its own rules.

Etsy's [Creativity Standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity) and [Seller Policy](https://www.etsy.com/legal/sellers) set what counts as "made by" you for digital items, including AI-assisted designs. Read both before you list.

| | One original PDF | A pack of Canva elements resold as templates |
|---|---|---|
| Rights | Yours, if you created them | Whatever the current Canva licence allows, which may be less than a shop listing implies |
| Risk | Low, if no one else's art or fonts are embedded | Takedown or licence breach |
| What to check | Font licences and any stock images | The Canva licence and Etsy's policies |

## File formats, sizes and delivery

Buyers judge a printable by how easily it prints. Decide these before you design:

| Choice | Options | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Paper size | Letter (common in Canada and the U.S.), A4 (common elsewhere), half-letter for planner inserts | Offer Letter and A4 together so international buyers aren't left out |
| Format | Flattened PDF, editable PDF with form fields, spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel), image files for wall art | Flattened PDFs protect your fonts; editable files sell for more but need clear instructions |
| Colour | Full colour, low-ink, and black-and-white versions | A low-ink version reduces complaints about printer ink |
| Delivery | Marketplace instant download, or a link in a PDF to a shared folder for large bundles | Test the download yourself on a phone and a computer |

For spreadsheet products, include a short "how to make a copy" page, so buyers using Google Sheets don't request edit access to your master file.

## Getting found: Etsy search, Pinterest and your own site

**Etsy search**
- Put the main phrase buyers use near the start of the title, such as "Budget Planner Printable."
- Use all available tags with specific phrases, such as "Canadian budget planner" or "biweekly paycheque budget."
- Write the first lines of the description for people, not keywords: what it is, who it's for and what's included.
- Your first photo matters most in search results. Test a clean mockup against a close-up of a page.

**Pinterest**
- Create several pins per product with different images and headlines.
- Link pins to the listing or to a blog post that features the product.
- Pin consistently rather than in bursts.

**Your own site**
- A simple blog with "how to" posts (for example, how to set up a sinking-funds budget) can link to the related printable.
- Offer a free one-page sample in exchange for an email address, then share new products with that list.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

**Week 1: Niche and first product**
- Day 1: Pick one niche from the list, and write down who buys it and why.
- Day 2: Study the top 20 listings in that niche. Note their price range, page count, formats and what reviews praise or complain about.
- Day 3: Outline your first product: page list, sizes (letter, plus A4 if you sell abroad) and format (PDF, or an editable spreadsheet).
- Days 4–6: Design it. Check every font and graphic licence.
- Day 7: Print it yourself on a home printer, then fix margins, ink-heavy backgrounds and typos.

**Week 2: Listing assets**
- Create mockup images showing the printed product in use.
- Write a title and description that state exactly what's included, the file format, the sizes and that it's a digital download.
- Add an instructions PDF: how to download, print settings and terms of use.

**Week 3: Launch**
- Open an Etsy shop, or a Payhip or Gumroad page if you have an audience.
- List the first product plus two related variations, such as colour themes or sizes.
- Pin your mockups on Pinterest, linking to the listing (see [Pinterest affiliate](/side-hustles/pinterest-affiliate-side-hustle-canada/) for how pins drive traffic).

**Week 4: Learn**
- Check views, favourites and conversion. Improve the first photo and the title.
- Add two more products in the same niche.
- Bundle the first five into a discounted set.

See [your first printables sale](/side-hustles/printables-first-sale-canada/) for launch-week tactics.

### Days 31–90
- **Weeks 5–6:** Add two products a week in the same niche.
- **Weeks 7–8:** Start an email list on your own site with a free sample printable.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Plan seasonal products (back-to-school, holidays, New Year planners) at least two months ahead.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Review which listings sell. Make more of what works and retire or improve listings with no views.

## Mockups that sell digital products

Buyers can't hold a printable, so your images do the selling:

1. **Lead image:** the product printed and in use, such as a planner on a desk or wall art in a frame.
2. **Inside pages:** show every page type, not just the cover, so buyers know exactly what they get.
3. **What's included:** one image listing the page count, sizes and formats.
4. **How it works:** a simple graphic: buy, download, print.
5. **Size comparison** for wall art, showing the print in a room setting.

Use mockup templates you're licensed to use commercially, and keep the colours true to the file, so buyers aren't disappointed when they print.

## Handling questions, refunds and file problems

Digital products bring a predictable set of questions. Prepare answers in advance:

- **"I can't find my download."** Send instructions for the platform's purchases page, and offer to email the file directly if needed.
- **"It printed the wrong size."** Point to the "fit to page" and "actual size" print settings in your instructions PDF.
- **"Can I edit it?"** Be clear in the listing whether the file is editable before purchase.
- **"Can I use it for my business or classroom?"** Answer from your written terms of use, and consider a separate commercial licence listing.
- **Refunds:** state your refund policy for digital downloads in the listing and shop policies, following the marketplace's own rules. Many sellers offer a replacement file or a fix instead of a refund for file issues.

Fast, friendly replies protect your reviews, which matter in marketplace search. Save your answers as message templates on the platform, so replying takes seconds. Then add the most common questions to the listing description, so future buyers find the answer before they ask.

## Pricing and bundles

Look at what similar listings charge, then price by value, not page count. A budget system that saves someone hours is worth more than a single checklist. Common tactics:

- **Bundles:** group related products at a discount to raise the average order.
- **Tiers:** a basic version and a premium version with more pages or editable files.
- **Seasonal sales:** use platform coupons sparingly, rather than permanent discounts.
- **Freebies:** a free sample on your own site can grow an email list.

## Which tax rules apply to selling printables in Canada?

Printable sales are business income. They're reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on their net income (QPP in Quebec). GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the first-dollar [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

- **Small-supplier test:** worldwide taxable supplies, including your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive quarters. Count all your side-hustle revenue together.
- **Rates:** once registered, charge the rate for the buyer's province. Ontario is 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% HST (since April 1, 2025, per the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table) and Alberta 5% GST. Quebec adds QST. Confirm with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Book rebate:** the CRA's [GI-065](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-065/point-sale-rebate-on-books.html) (August 2014) lists electronic and digital books, and agendas, among goods that don't qualify for the point-of-sale rebate on books.
- **Marketplaces:** check each order's tax line and the platform's tax help pages to see what was collected for you.
- **Deductions:** platform fees, design software, fonts, a printer and paper for testing, and a share of internet can be business expenses.

## Common printables mistakes

- **Copying popular designs.** Listings get removed and shops get closed.
- **Reselling Canva elements as-is.** Read the licence.
- **Skipping the test print.** Margins and dark backgrounds can ruin a home print.
- **Vague listings.** Buyers who expected a physical item leave bad reviews. Say "digital download" clearly.
- **Too many niches at once.** A focused shop builds repeat buyers.
- **Ignoring A4.** International buyers often need it.

## What are the limits of selling printables?

Printables have low prices and heavy competition, so meaningful income usually takes dozens of products and months of work. Platform fees take a noticeable share of small sales. Policy changes on Etsy or other platforms can affect visibility overnight. Copyright and licence mistakes can cost you listings or your shop. The McDermott and Agyei results come from interviews and aren't typical. Recheck each platform's live fee page before you set prices.
