A beginner can start with a camera they already own plus a $89 flash, build a portfolio at community events and price up from there.

## Who does event photography suit?

Event photography suits people who can anticipate moments, ask a group of strangers to squeeze in, and keep shooting when the light is bad. It is a poor fit if you freeze in crowds, can't commit to evenings and weekends, or dislike the hours of culling and editing after the event.

Unlike a portrait session, an event doesn't pause for you. The cake gets cut once, the diploma is handed over once, and the speech happens once. Preparation, backup gear and knowing the schedule are what make the gallery complete. The editing is real work too: Kamloops photographer Andrea Heath told iNFOnews that covering an event can mean culling and editing up to a thousand photos, and that one brand client wanted edited photos within two hours.

## Who owns event photos in Canada?

Under [section 13 of the Copyright Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-3.html), the author of a work is generally the first owner of the copyright, and the old subsection about commissioned photographs has been repealed. Work made in the course of employment is different: the employer generally owns it unless there's an agreement to the contrary.

In practice, write down what the client may do with the photos: personal use, social media, marketing or print. If a business wants event photos in paid ads, list that use and price it. If you want to use the photos in your portfolio, ask permission in the agreement too, especially when children or private gatherings are involved. This is not legal advice.

## What does the math look like?

**Event income = hours booked × hourly rate.** Your real hourly rate = event income ÷ (event hours + travel + editing hours).

**Illustrative.** The beginner rate and the editing hours are assumptions; the $250 rate is the low end of the Vancouver mid-tier range.

| Scenario | Booked hours | Rate | Event income | Editing + travel (assumed) | Real hourly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner birthday | 2 | $100 (assumed) | $200 | 3 hours | $40.00 |
| Grad party | 3 | $150 (Vancouver low end) | $450 | 4 hours | $64.29 |
| Corporate evening | 3 | $250 (mid-tier low end) | $750 | 5 hours | $93.75 |
| Full-day conference | — | $1,600 (full-day low end) | $1,600 | 8 hours on top of 8 on site | $100.00 |

A real-hourly formula check for the grad party: $450 ÷ (3 + 4) = $64.29. Two such events a month is $900 in revenue. Subtract gear amortization and a tax set-aside before treating it as take-home. The single biggest lever is editing time: a consistent preset and a strict culling routine shrink the denominator.

3. **Baby showers and gender reveals.** Daytime, indoor, small groups.
4. **Milestone birthdays and anniversaries.** Families want group portraits plus candids.
5. **Retirement and farewell parties.** Workplaces book these and pay by invoice.
6. **Corporate holiday parties.** December demand; good for repeat bookings.
7. **Conferences and AGMs.** Speakers, panels and networking shots; the full-day rates above.
8. **Networking events and chamber of commerce mixers.** Builds a local business network fast.
9. **Product launches and store openings.** Businesses want social content the same day.
10. **Community festivals and parades.** Volunteer coverage builds a portfolio, as Andrea Heath did.
11. **Charity galas and fundraisers.** Sponsors need photos for reports.
12. **Races, tournaments and sports days.** Action photography; Andrea Heath's sports-event work led to brand contracts.
13. **Dance recitals and school concerts.** Parents buy galleries; check the venue's photography rules.
14. **Cultural and religious celebrations.** Weddings aside, many communities hold large family events and want bilingual or culturally fluent photographers.
15. **Real estate open houses with people.** Agents want lively event shots for social posts.
16. **Headshot stations at events.** Book a corner of a conference and photograph attendees back to back.
17. **Restaurant and bar events.** Menu launches and themed nights need social content.
18. **Photo booth add-on.** Teisha Gentles turned a single photo booth bought as a side project into a full-time event business (below).

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

### Week 1: Gear check and practice

- Test your camera and flash in a dim room: bounce the flash off a white ceiling and behind you, and learn your autofocus mode for moving people.
- Buy spare batteries and cards. Build a Lightroom or Darktable preset you can apply to every photo.
- Write a one-page shot list template (below).

### Week 2: Shoot two free or low-cost events

- Offer to photograph a community event, a friend's party or a nonprofit fundraiser in exchange for portfolio rights. Andrea Heath built her skills by volunteering at community events.
- Deliver within 72 hours. Time yourself on culling and editing so you know your real hours per event.

### Week 3: Portfolio, prices and paperwork

- Pick 30 to 40 of your strongest images across two or three event types for a gallery site.
- Write a rate card with a two-hour minimum, a half-day and a full-day price.
- Draft your agreement: date, hours, deposit, delivery time, usage licence, portfolio permission and cancellation terms.

### Week 4: Book the first paid events

- Message ten local organizers: event venues, party planners, chambers of commerce, schools' parent councils, small businesses planning holiday parties.
- Offer a launch rate to the first three clients, take a deposit, and ask every client for a review and a referral after delivery.

## What mistakes do new event photographers make?

- **No backup.** A single card or battery failure can lose an event. Carry spares and swap cards.
- **Underpricing editing.** Quote the hours on site, but price for the editing behind them.
- **No written licence.** Copyright stays with you; spell out what the client can do with the photos.
- **Missing key moments.** Get the schedule and a point person for must-have groups.
- **Slow delivery.** Clients share photos while the event is fresh; aim for days, not weeks.
- **Photographing people who opted out.** Ask the organizer how guests can opt out, and respect it.

## Which tax rules apply to event photography in Canada?

Event photography income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Cameras, lenses and flashes are usually capital property deducted over time through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) rather than all at once; software, gallery subscriptions, insurance and the business share of vehicle costs are regular expenses. See [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/) and [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count print sales, photo booth rentals and other side-hustle revenue. Once registered, charge the rate from the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table for the province where the event takes place.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered; Job Bank's Ontario photographer median is $21.00, but the high end reaches $50.00.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered. Job Bank's Quebec median is $24.00.
- **British Columbia:** PST is separate from GST; Vancouver's published event rates are among the highest above.
- **Alberta:** GST only; Edmonton's published hourly range is $300–$500.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table; Job Bank's median is $25.12.

[Stock photography](/side-hustles/stock-photography-side-hustle-canada/) earns from the same camera between events, and [Lightroom presets](/side-hustles/lightroom-presets-side-hustle-canada/) turn your editing style into a product. Event video and highlight reels are [video editing](/side-hustles/video-editing-side-hustle-canada/). Posting event photos for businesses is [social media management](/side-hustles/social-media-manager-side-hustle-canada/), and design work for event signage is [graphic design](/side-hustles/graphic-design-side-hustle-canada/). Creator-style content for brands is [UGC](/side-hustles/ugc-side-hustle-canada/). If you'd rather work the event than photograph it, see [event staffing](/side-hustles/event-staffing-side-hustle-canada/) and [seasonal holiday jobs](/side-hustles/seasonal-holiday-jobs-canada/).
