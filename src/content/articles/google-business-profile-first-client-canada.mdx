## What offer gets a first profile management client in Canada?

The offer most likely to get a first profile management client in Canada is a flat monthly plan with a written checklist: four posts, a set number of new photos, review replies within a stated time, holiday-hours updates, and a short monthly summary. The owner can see every item on the public profile.

Make it cancellable with notice and say what is out of scope, such as ads, website changes, or social media. If the profile needs a cleanup first, quote that as a separate one-time fee.

Check eligibility before you pitch. Google's [policy overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en) says a profile requires in-person contact with customers during stated hours; online-only businesses are not eligible.

## Where do Canadians find a first profile management client?

Canadians find a first profile management client among local businesses they already use or know: a barber, a family restaurant, a trades contractor, a clinic. Search Maps in your neighbourhood and note profiles with unanswered reviews, old photos, or no posts.

Talking to an owner in person, when you are already a customer, is the simplest first contact. If you email, ISED's [anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies to commercial messages. Its [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page covers express and implied consent, the identification a message needs, and the unsubscribe mechanism. Do not use a bought list. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first profile management note say?

A first profile management note in Canada should name a visible gap, describe the monthly checklist, say the owner keeps ownership, and avoid any ranking promise.

**Example note.** This is a generic example. It is not a note that produced a client.

> Hello. Your Google listing has 14 reviews without a reply and photos from several years ago. I manage Business Profiles for local businesses each month: four posts, new photos taken on site, replies to every review within two business days, and holiday hours. You stay the owner and add me as a manager on my own Google account, which you can remove at any time. I do not buy or incentivise reviews, and I cannot promise a position on Maps. Would a short call help?

## How do you prove profile management skill without fake reviews?

You prove profile management skill in Canada with a sample monthly report, sample review replies written for public reviews you did not manage, and a profile you have managed with the owner's permission. Writing reviews for a business, or asking friends to, is a policy breach, not proof.

Google's Maps [prohibited content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) bars paid or incentivised reviews, selectively asking only happy customers, conflict-of-interest reviews, and posting from multiple accounts. Google's [review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) say to share a review link or QR code and to reply to reviews. That is the method you can offer.

## How do you set up access for a first client in Canada?

Set up access for a first client in Canada by asking the owner to invite your Google Account as a manager from the profile's people and access settings. Google's [owners and managers page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en) says managers can edit business info, post, add photos, and respond to reviews, but cannot add or remove users or remove the profile.

Do not ask for the owner's password, and do not become an owner unless the business asks in writing. Keep the business's real-world name as Google's [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) describe: no keywords, taglines, or phone numbers added to the name. Use the phone number and website already on the profile in posts, because the [posts policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en) says posts with unverified contact information may be removed.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first profile management client look like?

A 14-day plan for a first profile management client in Canada should produce a written checklist, a sample report, reply templates, and conversations with five businesses. A signed plan inside 14 days is not guaranteed.

### Days 1 and 2: Read Google's rules

Read the policy overview, representation guidelines, owners and managers page, review tips, posts policy, and Maps prohibited content policy. Write your refusal list.

### Days 3 to 5: Build the samples

Write a sample monthly report and reply templates for positive, neutral, and negative reviews.

### Days 6 to 9: Find five neglected profiles

Search Maps near you. Pick five eligible businesses with visible gaps that you can reach in person or through a published contact route.

### Days 10 to 14: Talk to owners

Visit or contact the five owners with the specific gap and the monthly offer. If one agrees, get manager access, confirm the fee in writing, and start the checklist.

## What mistakes block a first profile management client in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first profile management client in Canada are promising Maps rankings, asking for passwords, and offering to boost reviews with incentives. Each one either loses the owner's trust or breaks Google's policies.

Stuffing keywords into the business name risks suspension. Posting contact details not on the profile risks removal. Emailing a scraped list of businesses skips anti-spam consent.

## Which tax rules apply to a first profile management client in Canada?

Profile management income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the written agreement, monthly invoices, payments, and a mileage log for photo visits. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Managing Business Profiles is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
