## Who does website maintenance in Canada suit?

Website maintenance in Canada suits people who already know how a small site is edited: a page builder, WordPress, or a shop admin they have used on their own project. Evenings are enough for one client. The work is a poor fit if you panic when an update fails and you have no backup, if you want the hosting account in your name so the client cannot leave, or if you will promise a search ranking.

Google's [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) say it does not cost money to be eligible to appear in Search, and that meeting the requirements does not mean a page will be shown. Maintenance keeps the site available and updated. It does not buy a position.

## How do you start website maintenance in Canada?

Starting website maintenance in Canada means writing a care list, practising it on a site you control, and taking client access only in the client's accounts. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Write the care list

Updates for the software the site runs. A backup you can point to. A check that the home page loads and the contact path works. A note to the owner. A cap on hours. Anything else is a second line: new pages, new products, ads.

### Step 2: Practise on a site you own

Update it. Take a backup or an export. Restore nothing unless you have to, but know where the file is. If you do not have a site, maintain a test install you created, not a stranger's.

### Step 3: Read the patching guidance

The Cyber Centre's [Top 10](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/top-10-it-security-actions) puts patching operating systems and applications near the top of the list. Its [remote-work tips](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/cyber-security-tips-remote-work-itsap10116) say to run updates, use a firewall and anti-virus, and avoid sending sensitive information over public Wi-Fi. Do admin from a network you trust. You are not selling the Cyber Centre's whole program to a cafe website. You are borrowing the patching habit.

### Step 4: Put access in the client's name

Domain, hosting, and the admin user. If the domain is .ca, CIRA's [presence requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/legal-policy-and-compliance/canadian-presence-requirements/) say who may register it. The client, if they qualify, should be the registrant. Your login can be a user they can delete. Do not register their name in your personal account.

### Step 5: Limit what you look at

A contact form stores personal information. The OPC's [PIPEDA brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) explains duties for personal information in commercial activity, and notes that Alberta, British Columbia, and Quebec have their own private-sector laws for activity inside those provinces. You do not need to read the inquiry mailbox to update a plugin. This is not legal advice. If a task needs customer records, say so and stop until the owner agrees in writing.

### Step 6: Offer the list to five businesses that already have a site

A shop, a tutor, a trades page. Show the checklist. Name the hour cap and the fee. Do not offer a free month with unlimited edits.

### Step 7: Do the first month as a drill

Update. Confirm the backup. Load the site on a phone. Email what you changed and what you did not touch. Stay inside the hours.

### Step 8: Invoice the same day

Separate your fee from any hosting receipt you are passing through. If you are not registered for GST/HST, do not add a tax line.

### Step 9: After two months, keep or rewrite the list

If every month ran over because of new copy, the next note prices copy separately. Honour the cap you already sold, or bill the extra only if the note allowed it.

The first week should produce a care list, one practice run, and five offers. A client is a bonus.

## What website maintenance mistakes are common in Canada?

Common mistakes in Canada are holding the domain hostage, working from a public network, and promising that updates make the site unhackable. The Cyber Centre's remote-work note is plain about public Wi-Fi and updates. A care email that says "fully secure" is a claim you cannot keep. Say what you checked.

Another mistake is mixing a new build into a maintenance price. Building the first site is a different job. If the client has no site, use the no-code guide and hand off maintenance after the page exists.

Do not sell ranking. Search Essentials say eligibility is not a guarantee of being shown. A broken contact form is a maintenance bug. A desire to be first in the city is not.

Another quiet mistake is updating a live shop during the hour the owner takes orders. Ask when the site can be touched. If an update fails, the backup you named in the care list is the way back, which is why the first month includes finding that backup before you change anything. A note that says "updated some plugins" without naming them is not a record the owner can use next year.

## Which tax rules apply to website maintenance in Canada?

Website maintenance income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Recheck the small-supplier threshold on the CRA page when you revisit prices.

Keep the monthly notes and the e-transfers. If you pay hosting and bill it back, keep that receipt separate so the markup is visible. Report the work on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add design, writing, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Maintenance is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.
