Website maintenance is a monthly care plan for a site a client already has: updates, backups you can restore, security and uptime checks, small edits and a short report. Most small-business sites run on WordPress, and most WordPress security problems come from plugins, so a careful checklist is worth paying for. Canadian freelancers publish care plans from about $59 to $169 a month, and a dashboard tool to manage many sites costs from nothing to a few dollars per site.

## Who does website maintenance suit?

It suits people who already know how a small site is edited (WordPress, a page builder or a shop admin) and who follow checklists reliably. Evenings are enough for a handful of clients, and most work can be scheduled.

Good fits:

- **Web designers** who want recurring income from sites they've already built.
- **Developers with a day job** who can handle a broken plugin calmly. See [side hustles for software developers](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-software-developers-canada/).
- **Office and marketing staff** who've run a company WordPress site.
- **Introverts** who'd rather send a monthly report than make sales calls; the work is mostly solo. See [side hustles for introverts](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-introverts-canada/).
- **Virtual assistants** adding a technical service for existing clients.

It's a poor fit if you've never restored a backup, want the client's domain or hosting in your name, or can't respond within a day when a site goes down.

## What does a care plan include?

A care plan is a fixed list of monthly tasks with a cap on extra work. The published Canadian plans below share a common core.

| Task | Typical frequency | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Plugin, theme and core updates | Weekly or monthly | Patchstack found 91% of 2025's new WordPress vulnerabilities were in plugins |
| Backups stored off the server | Daily or weekly | A backup you can't restore is not a backup |
| Test restore | Quarterly | Proves the backup works |
| Uptime and SSL monitoring | Every 1 to 5 minutes | The client hears from you before customers notice |
| Security scan and firewall | Continuous or weekly | Patches can't arrive faster than attacks |
| Contact form and checkout test | Monthly | Silent form failures cost leads |
| Broken link and speed check | Monthly | Small fixes keep the site usable |
| Small content edits | 30 to 60 minutes a month | The most-requested extra |
| Monthly report | Monthly | Shows the client what they paid for |

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security's [Top 10 IT security actions](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/top-10-it-security-actions) include patching operating systems and applications. That's the practical core of a care plan.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

**Week 1: build your system.**
- Write the care checklist and your plan tiers (see templates below).
- Set up a free ManageWP or MainWP dashboard, an UptimeRobot account and Bitwarden.
- Connect your own site or a test site. Run updates, take a backup and restore it to a staging copy.

**Week 2: practise and price.**
- Break and fix your test site on purpose: deactivate a plugin, restore from backup, roll back an update.
- Compare published Canadian plans and set three tiers. A common pattern is updates and backups, then monitoring and security, then reports and edit time.
- Write the agreement: what's included, edit-time cap, response times, hourly rate for extras.

**Week 3: find the first clients.**
- Offer the plan to sites you already know: past design clients, your employer's partners, community groups.
- Run a free audit for five local businesses: WordPress version, outdated plugins, SSL expiry, broken forms. Send each owner a one-page summary.
- Use the pitch email below.

**Week 4: onboard and report.**
- Onboard the first client with the access checklist; the client keeps ownership of domain and hosting.
- Take a full backup before touching anything, then run the first updates on staging if available.
- Send the first monthly report and invoice the same day.

The step-by-step first-client process is in [how to get your first website maintenance client in Canada](/side-hustles/website-maintenance-first-client-canada/), and plan pricing is in [how much to charge for website maintenance in Canada](/side-hustles/website-maintenance-pricing-canada/).

## Templates and checklists

**Monthly care checklist:**

- [ ] Full backup taken and stored off the server
- [ ] Core, theme and plugin updates applied (staging first for major versions)
- [ ] Site checked after updates: home page, key pages, forms, checkout
- [ ] Security scan clean; failed logins reviewed
- [ ] Uptime and SSL expiry checked
- [ ] Unused plugins and admin accounts removed
- [ ] Test form submission received by the client
- [ ] Edit requests completed and logged against the cap
- [ ] Report sent and invoice issued

**Access and onboarding checklist:**

- [ ] Client is the registrant of the domain and the owner of the hosting account
- [ ] You have your own named admin account, not the client's login
- [ ] Credentials shared through a password manager, not email or text

- [ ] Backup destination owned by the client (their cloud storage or host)

## What mistakes do new providers make?

- **Updating without a backup.** Take one first, every time, and test restores.
- **Owning the client's domain or hosting.** It looks like help and becomes a hostage situation. The client should be the registrant; `.ca` domains also have [Canadian presence requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/legal-policy-and-compliance/canadian-presence-requirements/).
- **Unlimited edits.** Cap them in minutes and bill extras hourly.
- **Promising rankings.** Google's [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) say meeting its requirements doesn't guarantee a page will be shown. Maintenance keeps the site healthy; it doesn't buy a position.
- **Reading customer data you don't need.** Form entries and orders are personal information.
- **Admin work on public Wi-Fi.** The Cyber Centre's [remote work tips](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/cyber-security-tips-remote-work-itsap10116) recommend updated devices and secure connections.
- **Relying on the host alone.** Patchstack's 2026 report describes hosting companies as largely powerless against WordPress vulnerability attacks.

## What about privacy and accessibility?

Customer data on a business site, such as contact form entries, orders and newsletter sign-ups, is personal information collected in commercial activity. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner's [PIPEDA brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) explains the federal rules. Limit what you open, delete exports when you're done, and report suspected breaches to the client immediately.

Ontario's AODA requires businesses and non-profits with 50 or more employees in Ontario to make public websites meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA (except live captions and pre-recorded audio descriptions), as of January 1, 2021 ([Ontario: How to make websites accessible](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible)). Checking that new content keeps alt text, headings and form labels is a service you can add.

## Which tax rules apply to website maintenance?

Care-plan income is generally self-employment income. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Tool subscriptions, the business share of internet and phone, and a headset can be deductible; a computer usually goes through capital cost allowance.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Website maintenance isn't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply. Twenty clients at $149 a month is $2,980 a month, or $35,760 a year, so the threshold can arrive sooner than you expect. Once registered, the rate generally depends on where your client is located; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) pages.

Recurring invoices are easier with <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> or Wave; see [FreshBooks vs Wave vs QuickBooks](/side-hustles/freshbooks-vs-wave-vs-quickbooks-canada/). Several tools bill in US dollars, so record the CAD amount from your card statement.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** 13% HST once registered; AODA web accessibility rules apply to clients with 50 or more employees.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP and QST applies alongside GST once registered. Quebec also has its own private-sector privacy law, enforced by the Commission d'accès à l'information, which matters for clients' forms and cookies.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** GST plus a separate PST; check whether PST applies to software or services you resell, such as plugin licences.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered.
- **Atlantic provinces:** HST; Nova Scotia's rate is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Published care-plan prices are self-published by each provider and were checked on October 9, 2026; they don't show how many clients each provider has. Upwork's rates are worldwide medians in US dollars. Job Bank wages describe employees in 2023 to 2024. Tool prices are US-dollar list prices on October 9, 2026, converted at the Bank of Canada's October 8, 2026 rate, and your card issuer's rate will differ. Patchstack's figures cover vulnerabilities found in 2025. Client counts, plan prices and hours in the worked examples are assumptions.
