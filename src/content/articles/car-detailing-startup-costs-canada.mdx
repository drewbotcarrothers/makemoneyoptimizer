## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for car detailing in Canada?

Ongoing car detailing costs in Canada are soap, towels you retire, fuel if you drive, and insurance that does not shrink in a quiet January, and the break-even sketch further down keeps this guide's assumed fees. Retail ranges in the tables are typical Canadian retail prices to verify locally, not a study. Car detailing break-even in the sketch is a method, not a contract price.

**Soap and towels.** The repeating supplies. A salty week uses more product. Retire a towel that has grit in it. Replacing a cheap pack is cheaper than a paint complaint.

**Water and power.** An interior can run from a spray bottle. An exterior needs a tap the client can point at and a drain the bylaw allows. If the only path is a catch basin, the exterior is not for sale that day.

**Vehicle.** $0 extra if you already drive a car that holds the buckets. Log the kilometres if you claim them. A van payment is not a startup line.

**Insurance.** The premium does not shrink because February was too cold to wash.

**Unpaid scope.** A second vehicle "while you are here," engine-bay work, and a coating that was never on the note are how a priced interior becomes an open afternoon.

### Step 1: Add the one-time gear

One-time gear means the bucket kit, or the bucket kit plus a vacuum if you had to buy one.

### Step 2: Add the fixed costs that do not care how many cars you saw

Fixed costs mean an insurance premium you accepted, and invoicing software only after a second invoice would otherwise live in a text.

### Step 3: List the variable cost of the visits you did

Variable cost per visit means product used, towels you threw out, kilometres, and the time, including the drive.

Contribution equals fees collected minus variable costs. Gear is earned back when contribution covers the startup. If a three-row vehicle with pet hair pays the same as a small sedan, rewrite the next note. Honour the written price for the car you already booked.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a contract price and not a survey of Canadian detailing fees.**

Buckets, soap, towels, interior cleaner, glass cleaner, and a brush cost $90, inside the lean band. You already own a vacuum. Liability insurance in this sketch is $180 for the year, which is an assumption, not a quote. You complete four interior-only visits at an assumed $90 each. Fees = $360. You bought one more bottle of interior cleaner at $12. You drove, and this sketch does not add a kilometre claim or a fuel number.

Variable supplies beyond the opening basket = $12. Insurance $180. Contribution after those = $360 − $12 − $180 = $168. Startup gear $90 is covered inside those four visits in this sketch, with a remainder of $78.

Hour test: four visits at 2.5 hours each are 10 hours, and $168 ÷ 10 = $16.80 an hour in this sketch before a tax set-aside. A $400 extractor on visit one flips the sketch. Do not copy $90.

## Which tax rules apply to a car detailing budget in Canada?

Car detailing income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both the employee and employer portions, on the net. Soap and towels you use up are current expenses, while a vacuum or extractor that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion. GST/HST for car detailing uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the notes, the e-transfers, and the receipts for soap and fuel. Report detailing on Form T2125 with the T1. Capital cost allowance recovers the cost of a lasting machine over time on the business portion, in the class the CRA assigns, not necessarily in full on the receipt date. If you drive, keep a kilometre log and claim the business portion only.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add cleaning, pressure washing, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. The CRA's when-to-register page explains the timing. Car detailing is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. This guide does not explain input tax credits. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work happens. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate, including Nova Scotia and Quebec, on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set any tax you must collect aside. A deposit is not spending money until that tax is separated.

Your contract does not erase negligence. Search your city for a business licence and use that city's fee if there is one. This guide does not state another city's fee. Detailing is not a licensed trade in the way electrical work is, and a municipal licence is still a separate question from a sewer bylaw. If you pay a helper, check that province's workers' compensation rules before you call the person a favour.
