## What does Premier Service tell shoppers in Canada?

Premier Service's shopper page says you sign up with a short form, browse missions near you, do the visit, and submit a survey or photos. It says there is no fee to join, and that you see how much the mission pays before you apply. Work includes mystery shopping, merchandising, price checks, and brand-ambassador shifts. The page says shoppers who file on time and write clearly are offered the next assignments first.

On payment, the page is not one sentence. It says Brand Builders are paid every month via PayPal, and the FAQ says shoppers are paid weekly, with the mission paid two weeks after QA approval, also via PayPal. Use the timing the scheduler confirms for your mission. Do not average the two sentences into a promise.

The legitimacy answer on that page says Premier Service never sends unsolicited cheques. If you receive a cheque using the company name, do not cash it. That matches the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre's description of fake shopper cheques, including ones that borrow a real organization's name. The company lists a Montreal address and a US office. Sign up on premierservice.ca, not from a link in an unexpected email.

## What does BARE International tell Canadian evaluators?

BARE's evaluator page says iSecretShop is only available in the US, US territories, and Canada, and that you register on Apollo in a browser. The pay section is aimed at independent contractors paid through Tipalti after you have accepted a privacy policy and been assigned an opportunity. The invite link expires in 7 days, so register when it arrives.

For a Canadian evaluator, the page says an eCheck can be sent in CAD or USD. It says selecting CAD should allow a direct deposit to a Canadian bank account, and it tells you to confirm with your bank. If your address is outside the US, you are not asked for a US social security number. You sign a certificate of non-US residency. The page says local tax reporting is your responsibility. That is consistent with reporting the income yourself in Canada. It is not tax advice from BARE, and it is not advice from this guide.

Read the fee table before you pick PayPal. The page lists PayPal at $3 per transaction plus a transmission fee of 2%, with a $20 cap for a non-US recipient. International ACH is listed as free on BARE's side, with a warning that your bank may convert currency and may charge its own fee. A rejected eCheck can cost $25 if the banking details were wrong. Those are BARE's published figures. They are not a shop fee.

## How do you sign up without joining a fake company?

### Step 1: Read the scam page before the signup page

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre's [job scam page](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/scams-fraudes/job-emploi-eng.htm) describes a mystery shopper, "quality control officer," or similar notice, a cheque, instructions to deposit it and forward money or buy gift cards, and a survey that is only a prop. The cheque later fails and you owe the bank. Verify the company on its own website and phone number. Never pay a deposit to get work.

### Step 2: Open the company site yourself

Use the MSPA directory, or the two sites in the table, typed into the browser. The how-to for the visit itself is [mystery shopping](/side-hustles/mystery-shopping-side-hustle-canada/). A course that charges you to reveal "secret companies" is the product MSPA says it does not recognize.

### Step 3: Apply only where the fee is visible before you accept

Premier Service says you see the mission fee first. If a listing hides the pay until after you have spent money, skip it. Reimbursed purchases are still your cash until the company pays them back. Track the reimbursement separately from the fee.

### Step 4: Stay local

An assignment across the city can cost more in transit than it pays. The beginner guide's illustrative sketch shows how a small fee disappears once travel is counted. Combine visits only when each brief still allows the timing.

### Step 5: Write the report the same day

Premier Service says on-time, detailed surveys are how you get offered more missions. MSPA's FAQ says writing and following the directions are the job. Late or vague reports are how a real assignment becomes unpaid.

### Step 6: Keep the payment record

Log the company, the date, the fee, the reimbursement, and the method. BARE's Tipalti dashboard can export a history. Premier Service's PayPal payments should match your log. That log is what you use at tax time.

## What mistakes do Canadians make when they pick a company?

- **Cashing a cheque because the logo looks familiar.** Premier Service's own page says an unsolicited cheque is not from them. The Anti-Fraud Centre describes the same trick with other names.
- **Paying for a certification before the first shop.** The companies in the table say signup is free.
- **Choosing PayPal at BARE without reading the $3 plus 2% line.** A small shop fee can disappear into the payout fee.
- **Assuming an MSPA logo in an email is the directory.** Go to the association site and search. Spoofed pages are why MSPA tells shoppers to use the current list.
- **Treating "shops available now" as a schedule.** Both companies match work to location and to how you write. A quiet month is normal.

### Illustrative example

Assumed mission, not a Premier Service or BARE quote. Suppose a shop shows a $18 fee before you apply, reimburses a $12 purchase, and takes 90 minutes including the report and a $6 transit fare. Net cash after transit is $18 − $6 = $12, plus a reimbursed item you had to buy first. The hourly sketch is $12 ÷ 1.5 = $8 an hour, only inside these assumptions. If BARE pays that $18 by PayPal and the published PayPal fee is $3 plus 2% of the transfer, the payout fee on $18 is $3 + $0.36 = $3.36, and the net is lower. Whether that fee applies to your payment depends on the method you select in Tipalti. The $18 is an assumption. It is not a published typical shop.

## Which tax rules apply to mystery shopping companies in Canada?

Shoppers are usually independent contractors. Report fees as income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). BARE's page says a non-US resident signs a certificate that BARE will not report the income to the IRS, and that local reporting is yours. A US 1099 is not the Canadian return. Keep reimbursements out of the income line if they only repaid a purchase the brief required, and keep the receipt either way. The longer walkthrough is the tax section of [mystery shopping](/side-hustles/mystery-shopping-side-hustle-canada/).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
