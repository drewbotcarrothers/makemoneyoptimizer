AI consulting for a small business in Canada is helping that business do one named task with a tool it is allowed to use, written down so the owner can repeat it without you. It suits people who already use a tool on their own work and who will say what the tool gets wrong. It is a poor fit if the offer is "we will replace your staff," or if you need the client's customer list in a chat window on day one.

This guide does not publish a day rate. There is no official Canadian price for an afternoon of setup. A number from a conference slide is not your invoice. Privacy law still applies when the task touches personal information. The tool's monthly price is on the vendor's page. This guide does not state it.

## Who does AI consulting in Canada suit?

AI consulting in Canada suits people who can sit with an owner and finish one task: a first draft of a routine email the owner will edit, a checklist turned into a prompt the staff can reuse, a spreadsheet column explained in plain language. A computer you have is enough. The work is a poor fit if you will paste a client's customer export into a consumer chat tool, if you cannot show your own example, or if the sales line is a guarantee of time saved.

The owner stays responsible for what they send to customers. You are responsible for not overstating what you set up. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) is the advertising standard. "This will cut your payroll in half" is a claim. Do not make it.

AI consulting earnings in Canada cannot be promised as an hourly rate. What you keep depends on the list below. This guide does not publish a national consulting fee.

## How do you start AI consulting in Canada?

Starting AI consulting in Canada means picking one task, proving it on fake data, and writing what you will not touch. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Name one task a shop already does every week

Reply to a repeated question. Turn a price list into a tidy first draft. Summarize a meeting the owner already wrote down. One task. Not "digital transformation."

### Step 2: Run it on data you invented

Fake names, fake prices. Save the prompt and the output. Mark what you had to fix by hand. That fix list is the honest part of the sale.

### Step 3: Write the limits

No customer lists, no health information, no employee files, no passwords, unless a later agreement says otherwise and you understand the privacy duty. The default is no.

### Step 4: Read the privacy outline

The OPC's [PIPEDA brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) says the Act applies to personal information collected in commercial activity, and that Alberta, British Columbia, and Quebec have substantially similar provincial laws for collection inside those provinces. Cross-border handling can bring PIPEDA back into play. The [fair information principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/p_principle/) include consent, limited collection, and safeguards. The OPC's [guide for businesses](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda-compliance-help/guide_org/) is the longer version. This is not legal advice. If the task needs real personal information, the client needs a reason and a safeguard, and you may be the wrong person to design that.

### Step 5: Write a one-page offer

The task, the hours or the fixed fee, what you deliver (a procedure and a short call), what you will not do, and that the owner checks every customer-facing sentence. No ranking promise. No payroll promise.

### Step 6: Show the sample to five owners you already know

A shop, a clinic's front desk only if you stay on fake data, a trades business. Ask which weekly sentence they retype. Do not ask them to export their contacts "so you can see."

### Step 7: Do the first job inside the note

Use their wording only if they paste a single public sentence, not a database. Deliver the procedure as a document they own. Delete extras.

### Step 8: Invoice the task the same day

Name the task. If you are registered for GST/HST, show it. If you are not, do not invent a tax line.

### Step 9: After two jobs, keep the task or narrow it

If every owner asked for bookkeeping, send them to a bookkeeper. If they asked for a zap between two apps, that may be the automation guide instead. Stay with the task you can check.

## What should the first week of AI consulting in Canada include?

The first week should produce one sample on fake data, a limit list, and five conversations. A paid job is a bonus.

- **Day 1:** Name the task. Invent ten lines of sample text.
- **Day 2:** Run the tool. Write down every error you corrected.
- **Day 3:** Read the PIPEDA brief. Write the data you will refuse.
- **Day 4:** Draft the one-page offer with a fee you can explain. The fee is yours. This guide does not set it.
- **Day 5:** Talk to five owners. Listen for the repeated sentence.
- **Day 6:** If someone wants the job, confirm the limits in writing before you start.
- **Day 7:** If nobody books, improve the sample. Do not add a second tool.

## What AI consulting mistakes are common in Canada?

Common mistakes in Canada are pasting a client export into a consumer tool, promising accuracy, and selling a subscription you have not priced. Privacy principles include limiting collection to what the purpose needs. A whole inbox is rarely what a three-reply template needs.

Another mistake is calling the output professional advice. A draft reply is not legal advice, tax advice, or medical advice. Label it as a draft the owner must edit. If you are not a regulated professional, do not borrow the title.

Do not train a public demo on a client's real customers to "show the room." Use the fake sample. The impressive version that leaks data is the one you should not have built.

A quieter mistake is subscribing the client to three tools in the first week. One tool, on one task, is enough to learn whether the owner will actually edit the draft. A second subscription is a cost you should name only after the first procedure is in use. If the owner will not review the output, stop. The tool does not become careful because you were hired.

## Which tax rules apply to AI consulting in Canada?

AI consulting income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the scope notes and the e-transfers. Report the work on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Consulting is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside. A tool subscription you pay in US dollars is a receipt to convert for your records. It is not a tax ruling.
