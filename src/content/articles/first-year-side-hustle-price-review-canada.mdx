A first-year price review for a Canadian side hustle means looking back at twelve months of actual income, expenses and hours, working out what you really earned per hour after costs, CPP and tax, and setting next year's price from that rather than from a guess. The CRA's Form T2125 already sorts your year into income and expense lines ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125.html)), so the numbers you need for your return are the numbers you need here. Two outside checks keep the review honest: Job Bank's median wage for the nearest employed job, and Statistics Canada's inflation figures.

This is the numbers side. For how to tell clients about a change, see [how to raise your side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/). For setting a price from scratch, start with your hustle's pricing guide, such as [snow removal pricing](/side-hustles/snow-removal-pricing-canada/), [graphic design pricing](/side-hustles/graphic-design-pricing-canada/) or [online tutoring pricing](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-pricing-canada/).

| Review item | Source | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross income | Invoices, platform statements, bank deposits | What clients paid |
| Expenses by category | Receipts sorted by T2125 line | What the work cost |
| Hours worked | Calendar or time log | Including travel, quoting and admin |
| Net per hour | Income minus expenses, divided by hours | Your real rate before tax |
| Wage check | Job Bank median for the closest job | Whether you'd earn more as an employee |
| Cost changes | Your receipts; Statistics Canada CPI for context | Whether your old price now buys less |
| Demand signals | Waitlist, enquiries, lost quotes | Whether the market will pay more |

## What numbers do you need for a first-year review?

Gather them once and keep them for your tax return. The CRA generally requires you to keep records for six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records/where-keep-your-records-long-request-permission-destroy-them-early.html)), so nothing here is extra paperwork.

- **Total your income.** Every client payment and platform payout. [Record-keeping for side hustles](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/) explains what to keep.
- **Sort expenses by category.** Supplies, advertising, fees, phone, vehicle and insurance. [Side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/) lists the common ones.
- **Count every hour.** Billable time plus travel, quoting, messaging and bookkeeping. Unbilled hours are how a price that looked fine on paper pays less.
- **Split by service.** If you offer two services, review each one. [Tracking which side hustles pay](/side-hustles/track-which-side-hustles-pay-canada/) shows a simple per-service sheet.

## How do you work out your real hourly rate after tax and CPP?

```text
Net per hour       = (income − expenses) ÷ all hours
CPP (self-employed) = (net − $3,500) × 11.9%   (2026, up to $74,600 of earnings,
                      before any CPP already paid through a job)
Tax set-aside      = net × your chosen %
Kept per hour      = (net − CPP − tax set-aside) ÷ all hours
Raise needed       = target kept per hour ÷ current kept per hour − 1
Clients you can lose and still earn the same = 1 − old price ÷ new price
```

Self-employed people pay both halves of CPP. In 2026 that's 5.95% each on pensionable earnings between the $3,500 basic exemption and the $74,600 maximum, up to $8,460.90 for a self-employed person ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/payroll-deductions-contributions/canada-pension-plan-cpp/cpp-contribution-rates-maximums-exemptions.html)). If you also have a job, those contributions share the same exemption and maximum. Income tax depends on your total income and province; [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) explains how to choose a percentage.

## What do worked first-year reviews look like?

**Illustrative.** Income, expenses, hours, prices and the 15% tax set-aside are assumed; CPP, CPI and Job Bank figures are from the sources above. Each assumes this is the person's only work for CPP.

### Review 1: dog walker with a waitlist

- 360 walks at $25 = $9,000. Expenses $1,200 (leash set, treats, insurance, transit). 450 hours including travel and messages.
- Net $7,800 ÷ 450 = **$17.33 an hour**, under Job Bank's $18.00 dog walker median.
- CPP: ($7,800 − $3,500) × 11.9% = $511.70. Tax set-aside 15%: $1,170. Kept: $6,118.30, or **$13.60 an hour**.
- Five people on the waitlist. Raise to $28 a walk: the same 360 walks bring $10,080, and 1 − $25 ÷ $28 = 10.7% of walks could go before revenue falls below $9,000. Prices for [dog walking](/side-hustles/dog-walking-pricing-canada/) in your city are the market check.

### Review 2: house cleaner earning just under the employee median

- $18,000 income, $2,400 expenses (supplies, mileage), 800 hours. Net $15,600 ÷ 800 = **$19.50 an hour**, just under Job Bank's $19.74 house cleaner median.
- CPP: ($15,600 − $3,500) × 11.9% = $1,439.90. Tax set-aside: $2,340. Kept: $11,820.10, or **$14.78 an hour**.
- Calendar is full. Raise from $40 to $45 an hour for new clients now and existing clients after 30 days' notice. At the same hours, income becomes $18,000 × $45 ÷ $40 = $20,250, well under the GST/HST line. [House cleaning pricing](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-pricing-canada/) has local rate anchors.

### Review 3: snow removal and the gasoline line

- Fuel last season: $900. If your own fuel cost rose in line with gasoline's 22.8% CPI increase: $900 × 1.228 = $1,105.20, or $205.20 more.
- Spread over 30 driveways × 20 visits = 600 visits: $205.20 ÷ 600 = **$0.34 a visit**.
- That alone doesn't justify a 10% raise. If the review also shows routes running long, fix the route or the price; check the [snow removal price sheet](/side-hustles/snow-removal-price-sheet-driveway-size-canada/) for driveway-size pricing.

### Review 4: graphic designer approaching the GST/HST threshold

- $26,000 of taxable sales over the last four quarters; Job Bank's graphic designer median is $31.25.
- A 15% raise at the same volume: $26,000 × 1.15 = $29,900, just under $30,000. A 20% raise: $31,200, over the threshold.
- Once taxable supplies pass $30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, across all your businesses, registration is required ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). Read [GST/HST registration for side hustles](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) before you pick 15% or 20%.

## How do you decide whether to raise, hold or change the offer?

Combine the numbers with what clients did.

| What the review shows | Likely move |
| --- | --- |
| Full calendar or waitlist, kept hourly below your target | Raise |
| Full calendar, kept hourly at or above target | Hold or small raise in line with your cost increases |
| Gaps in the calendar, few price objections | Hold price, fix marketing or service area |
| Gaps and frequent price objections | Change the offer: packages, minimums, a tighter area or [a second service](/side-hustles/add-second-service-side-hustle-canada/) |
| Net hourly below the Job Bank median | Raise, cut unpaid hours, or reconsider the hustle |

A waitlist is the clearest raise signal; see [building a side hustle waitlist](/side-hustles/side-hustle-waitlist-canada/). Write one new number per service, the date it starts and who it applies to.

```text
Tax set-aside (___%) ......................... $______
Kept per hour ................................ $______
Job Bank median, closest job ................. $______
My cost change (receipts) .................... ___%
Waitlist / quotes won / quotes lost .......... __ / __ / __
Decision: raise / hold / change offer
New price ______  Starts ______  Applies to: new clients / everyone
Projected taxable sales next 4 quarters ...... $______ (under $30,000?)
```

```text
Hi [Name], thank you for a great first year. From [date, at least
30 days out], [service] will be $[new] instead of $[old]. My costs and
demand have both gone up, and this keeps your spot on my schedule.
Your bookings before [date] stay at the current price.
```

```text
Hi [Name], instead of a price change this year I'm moving to [monthly
packages / a 2-hour minimum]. For you that means [what changes], at
$[price]. If it doesn't suit, reply and we'll sort out an option.
```

## What mistakes do people make in a first-year price review?

- **Using gross income as the result.** Expenses and unbilled hours change the picture.
- **Forgetting CPP.** At 11.9% of net above $3,500, it's a real cost of self-employment.
- **Raising by headline CPI.** Your own costs may have risen more or less than 3.0%.
- **Raising when you're half-booked.** Fix the offer or the marketing first.
- **Copying a competitor's price** without knowing their costs or hours.

## How does a price review connect to your side hustle taxes?

The review uses the same totals as your T2125, so doing it in December or January gives you a head start on filing. If your net income jumped, check whether you'll owe [tax instalments](/side-hustles/cra-tax-instalments-side-hustle-canada/). ## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Job Bank wages are 2023–2024 employee pay, national medians, and don't match every side hustle exactly. The CPI figures are national 12-month changes to August 2026 and will be out of date after the next release. The CPP figures are for 2026; check the CRA table for the year you review. The worked reviews use assumed incomes, hours, prices and a flat 15% tax set-aside; your tax depends on total income and province.
