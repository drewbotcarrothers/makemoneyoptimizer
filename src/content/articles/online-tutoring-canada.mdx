Online tutoring pairs subject strength with teaching patience. For Canadians with reliable internet and a quiet space, it can be a structured side option — especially evenings and weekends. This sample guide stays practical.

## What students often need

- Curriculum support (math, sciences, languages, essay structure)
- Test prep with clear practice plans
- Adult upskilling (software basics, business English, coding fundamentals)

Specialize where you’re genuinely strong. “I can teach anything” rarely converts.

## Setup that looks professional

- Stable video, headset, and backup hotspot if possible
- Shared notes or whiteboard workflow
- Simple booking and cancellation policy
- Quiet background and punctual starts

Parents and adult learners pay for clarity and reliability as much as credentials.

## Finding learners (ethical outreach)

- Referrals from teachers, community centres, and parents you already know
- Tutoring platforms (trade fees for distribution)
- Local digital boards — be transparent about qualifications

Avoid exaggerated score-improvement promises. Help learners build habits; don’t sell miracles.

## Session design

1. Diagnose gaps with a short diagnostic.
2. Set one measurable goal for the month.
3. Mix explanation, guided practice, and independent work.
4. Send a brief recap after sessions.

## Money and boundaries

- Decide package vs. hourly pricing; packages reduce no-shows for some tutors.
- State payment timing up front.
- Protect personal contact channels if you prefer platform messaging.
- Track prep time — unpaid prep is still work.

## Canadian considerations

Income is typically self-employed. Keep invoices and platform statements. If you tutor across provinces or use international platforms, still consider Canadian tax reporting obligations. Confirm details with qualified advice.

## Two-week pilot

1. Offer 4–6 sessions in one subject.
2. Collect feedback with two questions: clarity and pace.
3. Calculate net hourly including prep.
4. Decide whether to expand, niche further, or stop.

Tutoring scales with reputation. Stay honest about what you can teach, and learners will often send the next referral.
