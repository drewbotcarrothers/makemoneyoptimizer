## Do you need a camera to start stock photography?

No. A recent phone is enough to learn what sells, and you should upload 50 to 100 phone images before spending on a camera. A dedicated camera helps once you shoot in low light, need shallow depth of field or want larger files for cropping; it doesn't fix weak subjects.

| Option | When it is enough | When to upgrade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recent phone | Bright daylight, flat lays, simple product and food shots | Indoor and evening shots look noisy; you crop a lot |
| APS-C mirrorless kit | Most stock subjects: lifestyle, food, travel, objects | You need a fast prime for low light or blurred backgrounds |
| Kit plus prime lens | Food, portraits, details, interiors | Rarely needed for stock beyond this |

## Which camera should a Canadian stock photographer buy?

Pick an APS-C mirrorless kit from a brand with affordable lenses. Consider video too: Adobe pays a higher royalty rate on video (35%) than on photos (33%).

## How many downloads does the gear take to pay back?

At Adobe's example royalty of about US$0.99 per photo licence, the Canon starter kit takes about 864 downloads to pay back. If the same camera also earns paid shoots, an illustrative $250 event covers it in 5 jobs.

**Illustrative example.** The US$0.99 per download is Adobe's own example for a US$29.99, 10-licence plan ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/payments-earnings/royalties-pricing/royalty-rates-assets.html)); other plans pay less or more. Converted at the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4240 on October 8, 2026: US$0.99 × 1.4240 = **$1.41 CAD**. The $250 shoot is the teaching sketch from [event photography](/side-hustles/event-photography-side-hustle-canada/), not a market rate.

Formula: **downloads to pay back = kit cost with HST ÷ royalty per download in CAD**

| Canon plus | $1,779.20 | **1,262** | **8** |
| Sony video | $1,908.99 | **1,354** | **8** |

The stock sketch in [how to sell stock photos](/side-hustles/stock-photography-side-hustle-canada/) assumes 500 photos each licensed once a year, about 500 downloads a year, so stock alone would take close to two years to cover even the starter kit. That's why many stock contributors either start with a phone or pair stock with paid local shoots.

## What should Canadian stock photographers check before buying?

- **US dollar income.** Adobe pays royalties in US dollars once you reach US$25 ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/payments-earnings/payment-taxes/payment-requirements.html)); report it in Canadian dollars. See [USD platform income tax](/side-hustles/usd-platform-income-tax-canada/).
- **Cold-weather shooting.** Winter landscapes and snow scenes are Canadian stock subjects, but lithium batteries drain faster in the cold. Carry the spare battery in an inside pocket.
- **Canada-version models.** Some listings, such as the Samsung T9 above, say "Canada Version". Check warranty terms with the seller, especially for third-party offers.
- **Accessory bundles.** Bundles with added cards, bags or tripods can cost more than buying the kit and accessories separately; the Nikon bundle above was $90 more than the plain kit.

## How do taxes treat camera gear?

A camera, lens or tripod used for stock is equipment claimed through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html). CRA's [classes of depreciable property](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html) page lists Class 8 (20%) for most equipment of $500 or more and Class 12 (100%) for tools under $500, with exclusions, so a $247 lens and a $929.99 camera may sit in different classes. Memory cards and other consumables are [business expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses.html). If you also use the camera personally, claim only the business-use share. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
