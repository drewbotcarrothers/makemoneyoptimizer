## What are the best side hustles for students in Canada?

The best student side hustles in Canada are the ones you can schedule in two- to four-hour blocks and drop during exam weeks. The picks below are grouped by what you already have: a subject you're good at, time near campus, or a laptop.

### If you're good at a subject: tutoring

[Online tutoring](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-side-hustle-canada/) and [teaching English online](/side-hustles/teach-english-online-side-hustle-canada/) use what you just learned. Evening sessions fit around classes, and demand rises before midterms and finals; see [exam-season tutoring](/side-hustles/exam-season-winter-tutoring-canada/) for that timing. Startup cost is usually a headset and a quiet room.

### If you have free time near campus: pets and kids

[Dog walking](/side-hustles/dog-walking-side-hustle-canada/) and [babysitting](/side-hustles/babysitting-side-hustle-canada/) are local, short, and paid per visit or per hour. Neighbours and faculty families near campus are a natural first client list. [House cleaning](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-side-hustle-canada/) also works on set days that match your timetable.

### If you have a closet full of things: reselling

[Clothing resale](/side-hustles/clothing-resale-side-hustle-canada/) and [general reselling](/side-hustles/reselling-side-hustle-canada/) can start with what you already own, which keeps the risk close to zero. ### If you have a laptop: small online jobs

## How many hours can international students work in Canada?

International students in Canada can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular school terms, and unlimited hours during scheduled breaks, but only if they meet every IRCC condition. IRCC says you must be a full-time student at a designated learning institution, have started your program, be in a program of at least six months that leads to a degree, diploma, or certificate, have a valid study permit with work conditions, and have a SIN ([IRCC, work off campus](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/work-off-campus.html)).

A few details from the same IRCC page matter for side hustles:

- **You can't start before your program starts.** IRCC says you can only start working when your study program begins.
- **Self-employment counts.** IRCC says self-employed students must track their hours, and hours include time spent earning wages, being paid for a service or for selling a product, or collecting a commission.
- **"Work" includes some unpaid activity.** IRCC defines work to include unpaid activity that would normally be paid or would count as valuable work experience.
- **Breaks are different.** During a break scheduled by your school, you can work unlimited off-campus hours. If your program has no scheduled breaks, the 24-hour limit applies all year.
- **Remote work for a foreign employer** doesn't count toward the 24 hours, as long as you still meet your study permit conditions.
- **Some students can't work off campus at all**, including those only enrolled in ESL/FSL, general interest courses, or prerequisite courses.

On-campus work is separate. IRCC's [on-campus page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/work-on-campus.html) says there is no limit to on-campus hours during terms or breaks for eligible students.

Going over the limit is serious. IRCC says working more than 24 hours a week during terms violates your study permit conditions and can cost you your student status and future permits. Its page on the [consequences of unauthorized work](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/admissibility-enforcement/consequences-unauthorized-work.html) lists removal from Canada and a five-year ban among the possible outcomes. When in doubt, read the conditions printed on your permit and ask your school's international student office. This is not legal or immigration advice.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not advice about your permit. Suppose an international student works 16 hours a week at an off-campus retail job and also tutors online for local clients. If tutoring adds 6 paid hours a week, the off-campus total is 16 + 6 = 22 hours, inside the 24-hour limit. Add a 4-hour dog-walking shift and the total becomes 26, which is over. A simple weekly log of every paid hour makes this easy to check.

## Do students need a SIN for a side hustle in Canada?

Yes. A SIN is needed to work in Canada and to file taxes. Service Canada's [SIN page for temporary residents](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/sin/temporary-residents.html) says temporary residents' SINs start with "9" and expire on the same date as the study or work permit, so update it when you get a new permit. IRCC adds that your study permit must carry an off-campus work condition before you can get a SIN for off-campus work.

## What mistakes do students make with side hustles in Canada?

The common student side hustle mistakes in Canada are taking on too many hours before exams, spending on gear before the first paid job, and (for international students) not counting self-employed hours.

- **Ignoring the calendar.** Block exam weeks first, then fill the remaining time.
- **Buying before earning.** Most picks above start with what you own.
- **Not tracking hours.** International students carry the burden of proof for their hours.
- **Skipping records.** Keep a simple log of income and costs from the first job; see [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Assuming student income isn't taxable.** It usually is; a low total may mean little or no tax, but it still gets reported.

## Which tax rules apply to student side hustles in Canada?

Student side hustle income in Canada is generally taxable, whether you're a domestic or international student. Self-employment income goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). If you are over 18 and your net self-employment earnings are above $3,500 a year, you generally pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) outside Quebec. GST/HST registration only becomes mandatory once your taxable sales pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), which few student side hustles reach.
