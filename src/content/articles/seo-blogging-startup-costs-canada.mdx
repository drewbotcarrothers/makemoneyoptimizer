## How much does an SEO blog cost to start in Canada?

An SEO blog in Canada starts at about $0–$30 on a free platform, about $200–$320 for prepaid hosting and a domain, and about $320–$700 with paid design and software. The biggest single line is usually prepaid hosting, because introductory prices often require a long term paid up front.

| How you start | First-year cash, about | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0–$30 | A free blogging platform subdomain, or a domain only, and a computer you own |
| Typical | $200–$320 | A prepaid shared hosting term (the Hostinger example above is CAN$201.12 for 48 months), a domain after any free first year, and free WordPress |
| Fuller | $320–$700 | The typical column plus a premium theme, a paid design tool, and bookkeeping software |

Prices from vendor pages are introductory and change. Read the renewal price, not only the monthly headline.

## Who should use an SEO blog startup budget in Canada?

An SEO blog startup budget in Canada fits people who can write useful pages on one subject for many months with no income guaranteed. The lean column fits someone testing whether they will keep writing. It is a poor fit if you need money this season, or if the plan is to copy other sites' articles or buy links.

Selling pages to clients instead is [freelance writing startup costs](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-startup-costs-canada/). A blog that recommends products with commissions is [affiliate marketing startup costs](/side-hustles/affiliate-marketing-startup-costs-canada/).

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for an SEO blog in Canada?

SEO blog break-even in Canada is the income from ads or commissions minus hosting, the domain, and any tool, usually measured over a year because pages take time. This guide does not publish a traffic or earnings figure.

### Step 1: Write ten useful pages before buying tools

If you cannot finish ten, a paid tool will not change that.

### Step 2: Read Google's spam policies

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe tactics that can lower a site or remove it from results. Scaled copied content and bought links waste the hosting you paid for.

### Step 3: Spread the prepaid term across its months

CAN$201.12 over 48 months is about CAN$4.19 a month on that plan.

### Step 4: Apply for ads only when the site qualifies

AdSense's [eligibility page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724) requires your own content that meets its policies and an age of 18 or over. Approval is not guaranteed.

### Step 5: Add the renewal before you call it profit

The renewal price is the real long-run cost.

## Where can SEO blog startup spending be lower in Canada?

SEO blog startup spending in Canada stays lower on a free platform until you have published enough to know you will continue, a free theme, and free Google tools. Skip paid link schemes completely. They cost money and are against Google's spam policies.

## Where should SEO blog spending not be cut in Canada?

SEO blog spending in Canada should not cut backups, a domain you control in your own name, or time to check sources. A site you cannot restore after an error loses every page you wrote. A domain registered in someone else's account is not yours.

## Which tax rules apply to an SEO blog budget in Canada?

SEO blog income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep hosting and domain invoices, ad and affiliate payment reports, and tool receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. An SEO blog is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
