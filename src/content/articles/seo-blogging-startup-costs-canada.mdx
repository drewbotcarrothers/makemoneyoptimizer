## How much does an SEO blog cost to start in Canada?

An SEO blog in Canada costs $0 on a free platform, $227.27 for four years of shared hosting, $511.62 with Canva Pro and three months of a keyword tool, or $1,318.50 with cloud hosting and a bigger keyword tool. Totals add 13% Ontario HST to Hostinger and Canva; Ahrefs is added in CAD without tax. Use your province's rate, and check whether tax is added at checkout.

| Kit | What's in it | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $0 start | Free platform subdomain, Google Search Console, Ahrefs Free, a computer you own | $0 |
| Lean | Hostinger Premium, 48 months (CAN$201.12) with a free first-year domain | $227.27 with HST |
| Standard | Lean + Canva Pro ($142/year) + Ahrefs Starter for 3 months (US$87, about C$123.89) | $511.62 |
| Pro | Hostinger Cloud Startup, 48 months (CAN$537.12) + Canva Pro + Ahrefs Lite for 3 months (US$387, about C$551.09) | $1,318.50 |
| Pro with insurance | Pro + writer's insurance at about $50/month (about $600/year) | about $1,918.50 |

The hosting in every paid kit covers four years, so the year-one share of the lean kit is about $56.82 with HST. The keyword tool is the line you'd cancel after a research sprint.

## Who should use an SEO blog startup budget in Canada?

An SEO blog budget fits someone who can research a page, cite a source, and keep publishing for months with little feedback. The $0 start fits anyone testing whether they'll finish ten posts. The lean kit fits someone who's committed and wants a domain they own. The pro kit fits someone running more than one site or expecting real traffic; the cloud plan is overkill for a first blog.

It's a poor fit if you need income in the next few months. The pillar guide reports the 2026 Blogging Income Survey's median of 12 months to a first dollar. ## What are the ongoing costs after year one?

- **Domain renewal from year two,** at your registrar's price. NamesPro.ca lists .ca at $15.68 a year, as a reference.
- **Hosting renewal after the term.** Hostinger Premium renews at CAN$15.39 a month (CAN$184.68 a year before tax) and Cloud Startup at CAN$36.29 a month. Put the date in your calendar.
- **Canva Pro and any keyword tool,** if you keep them.
- **Insurance,** about $50 a month if you buy the Zensurance-style policy.
- **Your time,** which is the biggest cost and isn't in any table here.

**Correcting a common shortcut.** Many budgets quote only the intro price. On Hostinger's page, Premium's CAN$4.19 a month is a 75%-off price on a 48-month term. The regular price for that term is CAN$805.92, and the renewal is CAN$15.39 a month. Budget the renewal, not the banner.

## How do ads and affiliate links pay a Canadian blog?

- **AdSense** has no traffic minimum. Google's [revenue share page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/180195) says publishers receive 80% of revenue for AdSense for content after the advertiser platform's fee. Its [eligibility page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724) requires your own content that meets AdSense policies and an age of 18 or over. Approval isn't guaranteed.
- **AdSense payment thresholds in CAD:** Google's [threshold table](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1709871?hl=en) lists C$10 to choose a payment method and C$100 to be paid. Identity verification is now required at $0, before ads show.
- **Larger ad networks** have traffic minimums. The pillar guide covers Journey by Mediavine (1,000 sessions a month) and Raptive (25,000 pageviews a month).
- **Affiliate programs** pay a share of sales. Amazon.ca Associates' fixed rates run from 0% to 10% by category; the details are in [affiliate marketing startup costs](/side-hustles/affiliate-marketing-startup-costs-canada/).

## How many pageviews does it take to break even?

**Illustrative. RPM (revenue per 1,000 pageviews) varies widely by topic, audience and ad network. The C$10 RPM is an assumption, not a typical result. The second column uses the US$33.16 average RPM reported for ad-led bloggers in the [2026 Blogging Income Survey](https://www.productiveblogging.com/how-much-do-bloggers-earn/) (129 respondents, published May 2026), converted to about C$47.22; most of those bloggers use larger ad networks, not a new AdSense account. Verify the arithmetic.**

| Kit | Cost to cover | Pageviews at an assumed C$10 RPM | Pageviews at the survey's average ad RPM (C$47.22) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0 start | $0 | 0 | 0 |
| Lean | $227.27 | 22,727 | 4,814 |
| Standard | $511.62 | 51,162 | 10,835 |

For example, $227.27 ÷ $10 × 1,000 = 22,727 pageviews. Those are cumulative pageviews over the life of the kit, not per month, and the lean kit covers four years of hosting. The survey average is a mean from bloggers who already earn; a new site shouldn't plan on it.

### A first-year sketch

**Illustrative example. A teaching sketch, not a forecast.** Suppose you buy the lean kit ($227.27) and earn $0 in months 1 to 8 while you publish, then an assumed $15 a month in months 9 to 12 from ads and affiliate links: 4 × $15 = $60. Year-one cash: $60 − $227.27 = −$167.27. Spread the hosting over its four years and the year-one share is about $56.82, so a fairer view is $60 − $56.82 = $3.18 before a domain renewal, tax and your time. These totals are assumptions. Don't copy $15.

## Do you need a licence or insurance for a blog in Canada?

Usually not a blogging-specific licence. If you publish under a business name that isn't your own legal name, most provinces require you to register it; in Ontario that's $60 online, per the province's [fee page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit). Some cities licence home-based businesses, so check your city's site. Insurance is optional; Zensurance's [writer and author insurance](https://www.zensurance.com/writer-and-author-insurance) page says $1 million of professional indemnity "costs around $50 per month," an estimate, not a quote. This is not legal or insurance advice.

## Where can SEO blog startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Publish on a free platform** until you've finished ten posts.
- **Use Ahrefs Free and Search Console** before you pay for a keyword tool. If you do pay, buy one month for a research sprint and cancel.
- **Use a free theme and Canva Free.**
- **Skip paid link schemes completely.** Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe link schemes and scaled low-value content as tactics that can lower a site or remove it from results. They cost money and risk the hosting you paid for.

## Where shouldn't you cut SEO blog spending?

Don't cut backups, a domain registered in your own name, or the time to check sources. A site you can't restore after an error loses every page you wrote. A domain in someone else's account isn't yours. Keep ad and commission payments apart from household money; <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account that can hold payouts until a tax set-aside is moved.

## What should you know before relying on these numbers?

Hostinger, Ahrefs and Canva prices are what each page said on October 9, 2026, and intro prices change often. Ahrefs bills in US dollars, so your card statement depends on the rate and your card's foreign-exchange fee. RPM and earnings figures are assumptions or survey averages, not predictions for your site. Re-check each link before you prepay.

## Which tax rules apply to an SEO blog budget in Canada?

SEO blog income in Canada is self-employment income when you're carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same total. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Hosting, domains and tool subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. A prepaid multi-year hosting term may need to be spread over the years it covers; ask a tax adviser. Equipment that lasts for years, like a computer, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. If you're registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.
