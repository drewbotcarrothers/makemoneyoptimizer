## How do Uber and Lyft calculate driver pay in Canada?

Uber and Lyft both show Canadian drivers an upfront take-home amount before they accept, and neither posts a fixed per-kilometre rate for upfront trips, so the offer screen is the number to judge against your own cost per kilometre.

**Uber.** Uber's post on [upfront offers in Canada](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/blog/more-transparency-with-upfront-offers-in-canada/) (August 27, 2024) says the offer is your expected take-home fare, net of Uber's service fee, and includes surge and per-trip components such as the Zero Emissions Vehicle Incentive and pet fee. It is based on time, distance, market conditions and demand at the destination. Uber no longer posts set time and distance rates. Tips, wait-time fees, cancellation fees and Quest promotions come on top. The service fee varies within a set range: usually slightly lower when the pickup takes longer than usual and slightly higher when it is shorter. If a trip's time or distance differs substantially from the estimate, the fare is adjusted on the receipt. Uber's [Ontario DPWRA disclosure](https://help.uber.com/driving-and-delivering/article/digital-platform-workers%E2%80%99-rights-act-dpwra-disclosure-rideshare-ontario-canada-?nodeId=776a036a-d892-4850-9b39-19673731f240) adds that the offer is also net of per-trip regulatory fees and the sales tax on Uber's fees, and that you are paid by weekly instalment, daily Flex Pay or Instant Pay.

**Lyft.** Lyft's [rate card page](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en-ca/driver/articles/115013080008) says earnings are based on Upfront Pay in most regions and on a rate card of base fare, time and distance where Upfront Pay is not available. You keep 100% of tips, and bonuses such as Ride Streaks are extra. Lyft says the fees it charges riders do not change what you earn.

### How fast each app pays you

| Payout | Uber | Lyft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Default | Weekly instalment | Weekly, starting every Tuesday for the Lyft week (Monday 5 a.m. to Monday 4:59 a.m.); most banks show it by Friday |
| Faster | Daily Flex Pay or Instant Pay | Express Pay to a debit card: $1.25 per transfer in Canada, minimum $6.75 balance, up to 5 cash-outs every 24 hours |
| Free instant | See Uber's in-app terms | Lyft Direct (Payfare business debit card): instant payout after every ride, no payout fee |

On Lyft, cashing out with Express Pay every day of a five-day week costs $6.25 a week, which is $325 over 52 weeks. Lyft Direct or the free Tuesday deposit avoids that.

## What minimum earnings rules apply to Uber and Lyft drivers in Ontario and B.C.?

Ontario and British Columbia set an earnings floor for rideshare drivers that applies to both apps, so the floors do not make one app better than the other, but they do change the math in a slow week. Tips do not count toward either floor.

- **Ontario: $17.95 an hour.** The [Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers), in force since July 1, 2025, requires at least the general minimum wage for each work assignment, or across a pay period. Ontario's general [minimum wage](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/minimum-wage) rose from $17.60 to $17.95 on October 1, 2026. A work assignment starts when you accept a request and ends when it is done; time spent online waiting is not counted. Lyft's Ontario page states the same $17.95 per booked hour, averaged over two weeks, where booked time means driving to the pickup plus time with the rider, before tips, tolls and select bonuses, with any top-up paid the following Wednesday.
- **British Columbia: $21.89 an hour plus $0.45 per km.** B.C.'s [minimum wage page](https://gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) lists $21.89 per hour of engaged time for ride-hail workers from June 1, 2026 (up from $21.43), plus at least $0.45 per km driven during engaged time. The rate is indexed to inflation.

### Worked example: what the floor guarantees

**Illustrative. The hours and kilometres below are assumptions, not typical results.**

Suppose a driver is online for 4 hours and spends 2.5 of those hours on accepted trips, including the drive to each pickup, covering 60 km while engaged.

- **Ontario:** 2.5 × $17.95 = **$44.88** minimum for the assignment time, before tips. If fares for that time came to $40, the platform owes a $4.88 top-up. The 1.5 hours spent waiting earn no floor.
- **B.C.:** 2.5 × $21.89 = $54.73, plus 60 × $0.45 = $27.00, for a floor of **$81.73** before tips.

The floor is a minimum, not a forecast. Spread across the 4 hours online, the Ontario floor works out to $11.22 an hour, and that is before gas.

## How should you compare Uber and Lyft with your own numbers?

Compare Uber and Lyft in Canada by logging the same windows on each app: fares after the platform fee, tips, hours online, and every kilometre including the drive to pickups. Then subtract a per-kilometre cost.

For the per-km cost, use your own fuel, insurance, maintenance and depreciation if you track them. If you do not yet, the CRA's [2026 reasonable allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/benefits-allowances/automobile/automobile-motor-vehicle-allowances.html) is a full-cost benchmark: **$0.73 per km** for the first 5,000 business km and $0.67 after that in the provinces ($0.77 and $0.71 in the territories), as [announced by Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/01/government-announces-the-2026-automobile-deduction-limits-and-expense-benefit-rates-for-businesses.html). It is the rate an employer can pay tax-free for an employee's own car, not a deduction you claim. A fuel-only number is lower, which is why using it makes the longer-distance app look better than it is.

A driver logs one Saturday evening on each app. App A: $118 in fares, $16 in tips, 4 hours online, 120 km. App B: $104 in fares, $22 in tips, 4 hours online, 95 km.

| | App A | App B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fares + tips | $134.00 | $126.00 |
| Cost at an assumed $0.35/km (fuel-type cost) | $42.00 | $33.25 |

At a fuel-only cost the two apps tie. At a full-cost rate, App B wins by $10.25 for the evening, because it drove 25 fewer kilometres. The fare total alone picked the wrong app. GST/HST, which rideshare drivers collect on fares, and income tax are not shown.

## What mistakes do drivers make when choosing between Uber and Lyft in Canada?

Drivers in Canada most often pay for an inspection before checking eligibility, treat fares as income, or judge an app on gross fares.

- **Booking an inspection first.** Lyft's age-25 rule and the Toronto vehicle-age cut-offs end many applications.
- **Treating the fare as income.** Rideshare fares carry GST/HST you remit.
- **Paying for speed.** $1.25 Express Pay cash-outs add up when a free option exists.
- **Counting waiting time toward the floor.** Neither province pays for time online without a trip.
- **Not calling the insurer.** Uber's GTA page says Ontario drivers keep a personal policy alongside the rideshare policy.

## Which tax rules apply to Uber and Lyft drivers in Canada?

Rideshare drivers on Uber or Lyft must register for GST/HST from their first fare, because the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing rule](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) does not use the $30,000 small-supplier threshold for passenger rides. Lyft's Ontario page asks for an HST number and its B.C. page asks for a GST number.

Driving income from both apps goes on one [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). The kilometre log you keep for the comparison is the same log you need for [vehicle expenses for delivery and rideshare](/side-hustles/vehicle-expenses-delivery-rideshare-canada/).
