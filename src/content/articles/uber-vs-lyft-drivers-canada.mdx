Uber and Lyft both pay Canadian rideshare drivers through an upfront offer you see before accepting, both pass on 100% of tips, and both put you under the CRA's first-dollar GST/HST rule for passenger rides. The practical differences are eligibility and reach: Lyft's help centre lists a minimum age of 25 and operates in fewer Canadian regions, while Uber's requirements vary by city and its Canadian pages cover more places. If you are under 25 or outside Lyft's regions, Uber is the only one of the two you can use; if you qualify for both, compare them in the same hours with your own log.

## How do Uber and Lyft calculate driver pay in Canada?

Uber and Lyft both show Canadian drivers an upfront amount before they accept, and neither publishes a fixed per-kilometre rate for upfront trips. The offer screen is the number to judge, against your own cost per kilometre.

### Uber's upfront offers

Uber's post on [upfront offers in Canada](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/blog/more-transparency-with-upfront-offers-in-canada/) says the offer shows your expected take-home fare, is net of Uber's service fee, and includes surge and some per-trip components. It is based on time and distance, market conditions, and demand at the destination, and Uber no longer posts set time and distance rates. Tips, wait-time fees, and cancellation fees are added after the trip. Uber's service fee varies from trip to trip within a set range, and a weekly summary shows what Uber collected. If the trip's time or distance is substantially different from the estimate, the fare is adjusted and the receipt explains why.

### Lyft's Upfront Pay and rate card

Lyft's [rate card page](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en-ca/driver/articles/115013080008) says earnings are based on Upfront Pay in most regions, and on a rate card of base fare, time, and distance where Upfront Pay is not available. You keep 100% of tips and may see bonuses such as Ride Streaks. Lyft says the fees it charges passengers do not affect what you earn. [Getting paid](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en-ca/all/articles/9328619602-Getting-paid) says weekly payouts start every Tuesday for the previous Lyft week, and Express Pay carries a $1.25 fee in Canada.

## What minimum earnings rules apply to Uber and Lyft drivers in Ontario and B.C.?

Ontario and British Columbia set a floor for rideshare drivers on engaged time, and those floors apply to both apps, so they do not make one app better than the other. Tips are excluded in both provinces.

- **Ontario:** the [digital platform workers page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers) says workers must get at least the general minimum wage for work assignments, checked per assignment or per pay period. Lyft's Ontario page states its own figure: a minimum of $17.95 per booked hour over two weeks, where booked time includes driving to the pickup and time with the rider.
- **British Columbia:** the province's [minimum wage page](https://gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) lists $21.89 per hour of engaged time for ride-hail workers as of June 1, 2026, plus at least $0.45 per km while operating a motor vehicle. Lyft's B.C. page repeats the $21.89 figure.

Time spent waiting for a request is not engaged time in either province.

## How should you compare Uber and Lyft with your own numbers?

Compare Uber and Lyft in Canada by logging the same windows on each app: gross fares after the service fee, tips, hours online, and every kilometre including the drive to pickups. Then subtract a per-kilometre cost from your own fuel, insurance, and maintenance bills.

### Illustrative example

Suppose a driver logs a Saturday evening on each app. On app A: $118 in fares, $16 in tips, 4 hours online, 120 km. On app B: $104 in fares, $22 in tips, 4 hours online, 95 km. Suppose the driver's own cost is $0.35 per km.

- App A: $118 + $16 = $134. 120 × $0.35 = $42. $134 − $42 = $92, or $23 per hour online.
- App B: $104 + $22 = $126. 95 × $0.35 = $33.25. $126 − $33.25 = $92.75, or about $23.19 per hour online.

These are assumed numbers for teaching, not a quote, an average, or a typical result. GST/HST, which rideshare drivers collect on fares, and income tax are not shown. The fare total alone would have picked the wrong app.

## What mistakes do drivers make when choosing between Uber and Lyft in Canada?

Drivers in Canada most often pick by sign-up bonus, skip the GST/HST registration, or assume Lyft is available because the app downloads. Check the region list and the age line before paying for an inspection.

- **Paying for an inspection before checking eligibility.** Lyft's 25 minimum and Toronto's vehicle-age rules end many applications.
- **Treating the fare as income.** Rideshare fares carry GST/HST you remit.
- **Running both apps at once on day one.** Learn the airport, venue, and pickup rules on one app first.
- **Not calling the insurer.** Uber's GTA page says drivers still need a personal policy alongside the rideshare policy in Ontario.

## Which tax rules apply to Uber and Lyft drivers in Canada?

Rideshare drivers on Uber or Lyft generally must register for GST/HST from their first fare, because the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing rule](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) does not use the $30,000 small-supplier threshold for passenger rides. Lyft's Ontario page asks for an HST account number, and its B.C. page asks for a GST number.

Driving income goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), whichever app paid it, and both apps' income goes on the same form if you drive for both. The kilometre log is also what you need for [vehicle expenses for delivery and rideshare](/side-hustles/vehicle-expenses-delivery-rideshare-canada/). The registration steps are in [GST/HST registration for side hustles](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).
