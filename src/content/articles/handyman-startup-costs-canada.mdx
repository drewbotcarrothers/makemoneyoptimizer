What to offer, what to refuse, and how to price a first week are in the [handyman side hustle guide](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada). This page is the kit. A truck full of unused specialty tools is a debt, not a brand. You do not need a van or a wrap.

## The quick answer

Ranges are typical Canadian retail for a careful beginner’s bag: assembly, shelves, caulking, a sticky door, a fence board, a surface plumbing swap you can shut off and test. They are not a quote. They do not include a vehicle.

| How you start | One-time cash, about | What you can honestly do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $120–$250 | A drill-driver, Robertson and Phillips bits, a level, a tape, a stud finder, and a small set of hand tools. You refill anchors per job instead of building a warehouse. |
| Typical | $250–$500 | A driver you trust, a hardware assortment, a work light, shoe covers, a drop cloth, and a utility knife you will keep. |
| Doing it properly | $450–$900, plus a liability quote | The typical bag, a few better hand tools, and insurance described as small residential repairs — not panels, gas, or structural work. Specialty tools wait until a paid job needs them. |

The line moves by province. Electrical beyond what a homeowner may do, gas, opening a wall to move a drain, structural changes, refrigerant, and asbestos are not starter purchases. In Ontario people mean the Electrical Safety Authority for electrical rules. Other provinces have their own regulators. Quebec’s construction rules are often stricter about who may do paid renovation. Buying a panel tool “just in case” is how unlicensed work starts. Do not put it in the budget.

## Who this budget fits

**It fits** if you already fix things in your own home, you can say what you will not do, and you can carry a small bag on transit or in a car you have. Evenings and Saturdays are when homeowners are home.

**It is a poor fit** if you need to learn on someone’s only bathroom, if you will say yes to anything with a dollar sign, or if the jobs are so far apart that the drive is the shift. A $90 shelf across the region is a bad shelf.

If the first customers should come from an app, price that path in [odd-job app startup costs](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-startup-costs-canada) and keep the same limits. Outdoor clients who will ask about a gate are in [lawn care startup costs](/side-hustles/lawn-care-startup-costs-canada) and [snow removal startup costs](/side-hustles/snow-removal-startup-costs-canada).

## Ongoing costs, and a simple break-even

**Hardware you are not reimbursed for.** The leak in handyman math. Say who supplies materials. If you buy a $12 supply line, it is either in the price or it is a receipt you show the client. Guessing “it was only a few dollars” all month is how a fair hourly rate becomes a poor one.

**Travel.** Fuel or transit, both ways, plus a wear set-aside if you drive. Include it in a fixed price. A job down the street and a job across the region are different products.

**Consumables.** Caulk tips, blades, anchors, shoe covers, tape. Often $0–$30 on a small visit and more when you are filling a list of punch-work. Track a month before you invent a percentage.

**Insurance.** The premium. It is a fixed cost even in a quiet week.

**Platform fees,** only if you also take app tasks. Read the current terms. Off-app work may be outside the app’s insurance. Price your own policy for the work you invoice yourself.

**Quotes that go nowhere.** Time is a cost. Cap diagnostic visits: a short paid look, then a price, or you stop. Unpaid estimating across the city will wipe out the drill faster than a lost bit.

### How to calculate break-even

1. One-time tool cost.
2. Monthly fixed: insurance, software, a baseline of blades and shoe covers.
3. Variable per job: travel, materials you ate, parking.

Contribution = amount collected minus variable cost. If you collected GST/HST because you are registered, set that slice aside before you call the rest contribution.

Jobs to earn back the kit = tool cost ÷ contribution per job.

Prefer a fixed price for a defined scope. Hourly rates punish you for being fast. Use hourly only when the scope is unknown, and cap it.

### Illustrative example

**Pretend job, so the arithmetic is visible. Not a rate for your city.**

Driver, bits, level, stud finder, and a drop cloth cost $220. Insurance in this sketch is $35 a month. You have not subscribed to software. One shelf and a curtain rod pay $150, materials included. You spent $18 on anchors and brackets. The round trip was 16 km. At 8 L/100 km and $1.55/L, fuel is 16 × 0.08 × 1.55 = $1.98. Wear at $0.12/km is $1.92. On site for 1.5 hours, door to door closer to 2.

Variable cost = $18 + $1.98 + $1.92 = $21.90. Contribution = $150 − $21.90 = $128.10. This sketch is about $64 per door-to-door hour before insurance.

Tool payback = $220 ÷ $128, or about two jobs like it. The insurance takes about a third of one such job a month. A $90 version of the same shelf with a 40 km drive and a second hardware-store trip for the wrong anchor can contribute less than the insurance slice. Write the assumption in the quote: drywall over wood studs, not concrete. If the assumption is wrong, stop and reprice. Do not “make the $150 work” with an extra unpaid hour and a different anchor system.

## Where you can spend less

Three skills, written down. Refer the rest. Rent a specialty tool for a one-off. Use the client’s materials when they are the right ones. Transit and a small bag are a valid start. Do not lease a van to look legitimate. Email and e-transfer are enough for the first two jobs.

## Where not to cut corners

The stud finder, the right anchor, and the sentence about what you do not touch. A falling television is the claim and the injury. Gas connectors and panels are how a side hustle becomes unlicensed contracting. This is not legal advice. It is a budget limit: do not buy the tool for the job you must refuse.

Liability insurance that matches small repairs. A leak you caused under a sink is ordinary. A home policy may exclude business. Describe the work honestly. This is not insurance advice.

Shoe covers, a drop cloth, and a look at the floor before you leave. The mess is part of the price.

Permits. A “small deck” can still need one. If nobody will pull it, shrink the job or decline it. The permit fee is not yours to ignore, and it is not a tool you can substitute with a saw.

Shutoffs. If you cannot find the water shutoff and test the repair, you are not ready to open that fitting, however small the part price was.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Handyman income is self-employment income. Invoice it, keep receipts for materials and tools, and report it even if nobody issues a T4A. The T2125 is the usual form with your T1. Both shares of CPP generally apply to the net.

**Vehicle log and CCA.** If you drive to jobs, a kilometre log supports a vehicle claim: date, destination, kilometres, purpose. Business kilometres divided by total kilometres is the percentage you apply to fuel, insurance, and maintenance. Guessing “about half my gas” is how those claims fall apart. Anchors, caulk, and blades are current expenses. A drill that lasts for years may be capital. Capital cost allowance (CCA) spreads that cost over time on the business-use portion, in the class the CRA assigns. Do not deduct a van in full in the month you bought it for three shelves. Look up the year you file, or ask an accountant when equipment is a large part of the return.

**GST/HST.** Small-supplier test: worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and your other hustles. Lawn, snow, and repairs are one pot if they are all you. The CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page explains the timing. This is not the ride-sharing rule that requires GST/HST from the first fare.

**Input tax credits.** Once registered, GST/HST you pay on tools, hardware, and other business purchases can often be recovered as an input tax credit to the extent of commercial use. Receipts should show the tax. Pass that saving into your pricing honesty: you still do not spend the tax you collected from clients. If you are not registered, you do not add GST/HST to the invoice and you do not claim the credit.

Charge the provincial rate when you are registered. Quebec adds QST on top of GST. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025. Confirm on the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Municipal permits and business licences are local. Use the fee on the city’s page. If you pay a helper, check that province’s workers’ compensation rules first.
