The line between a handyman purchase and a licensed-trade purchase moves by province. Electrical work, gas, opening a wall to move a drain, structural changes, refrigerant, and asbestos are not starter purchases. Check the provincial trade authority before you budget for those jobs. Buying a panel tool “just in case” is how unlicensed work starts. Leave that tool out of the budget.

## How do ongoing costs change handyman break-even in Canada?

Ongoing costs for handyman work in Canada are unreimbursed hardware, travel, consumables, insurance, and quotes that go nowhere. Price handyman work in Canada as a fixed amount for a defined scope, and cap an hourly diagnostic when the scope is still unknown.

Hourly rates punish you for being fast.

**Hardware you are not reimbursed for.** The leak in handyman math. Say who supplies materials. If you buy a $12 supply line, it is either in the price or it is a receipt you show the client. Guessing “it was only a few dollars” all month is how a fair hourly rate becomes a poor one.

**Travel.** Fuel or transit, both ways, plus a wear set-aside if you drive. Include travel in a fixed price. A job down the street and a job across the region are different products.

**Consumables.** Caulk tips, blades, anchors, shoe covers, tape. Often $0–$30 on a small visit and more when you are filling a list of punch-work. The $0–$30 band is a typical range to verify locally. Track a month before you invent a percentage.

**Insurance.** The premium is a fixed cost even in a quiet week.

**Platform fees,** only if you also take app tasks. Read the current terms. The percentage changes. Off-app work may be outside the app’s insurance. Price your own policy for the work you invoice yourself.

**Quotes that go nowhere.** Time is a cost. Cap diagnostic visits: a short paid look, then a price, or you stop. Unpaid estimating across the city will wipe out the drill faster than a lost bit.

### 1. Add up the one-time tool cost

That cash is the numerator when you ask how many jobs earn the kit back.

### 2. Add monthly fixed costs

Monthly fixed costs are insurance, software, and a baseline of blades and shoe covers.

### 3. Subtract variable cost from what you collected

Variable cost per job is travel, materials you ate, and parking. Contribution = amount collected minus variable cost. If you collected GST/HST because you are registered, set that slice aside before you call the rest contribution. Jobs to earn back the kit = tool cost ÷ contribution per job.

Driver, bits, level, stud finder, and a drop cloth cost $220. Insurance in this sketch is $35 a month. You have not subscribed to software. One shelf and a curtain rod pay $150, materials included. You spent $18 on anchors and brackets. The round trip was 16 km. At 8 L/100 km and $1.55/L, fuel is 16 × 0.08 × 1.55 = $1.98. Wear at $0.12/km is $1.92. On site for 1.5 hours, door to door closer to 2.

Variable cost = $18 + $1.98 + $1.92 = $21.90. Contribution = $150 − $21.90 = $128.10. This sketch is about $64 per door-to-door hour before insurance.

Tool payback = $220 ÷ $128, or about two jobs like it. The insurance takes about a third of one such job a month. A $90 version of the same shelf with a 40 km drive and a second hardware-store trip for the wrong anchor can contribute less than the insurance slice. Write the assumption in the quote: drywall over wood studs, not concrete. If the assumption is wrong, stop and reprice. Do not “make the $150 work” with an extra unpaid hour and a different anchor system.

## How do taxes and vehicle claims work for handyman work in Canada?

Handyman income in Canada is self-employment income: invoice the income, keep receipts for materials and tools, report the income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with the T1 even if nobody issues a T4A, and budget for both shares of [Canada Pension Plan contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on the net. GST/HST registration for handyman work uses the small-supplier test of $30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and other hustles, which is separate from [GST/HST for taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html). After registration, charge Nova Scotia’s 14% HST ([charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)), add Quebec’s QST on top of GST, confirm other rates on the CRA [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and check the provincial trade authority before electrical, gas, plumbing, or structural work.

This guide is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

The CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page explains the timing. Lawn, snow, and repairs are one pot if they are all you. Confirm the current QST rate before you add QST to a Quebec invoice.

If you drive to jobs, a kilometre log supports a vehicle claim: date, destination, kilometres, purpose. Business kilometres divided by total kilometres is the percentage you apply to fuel, insurance, and maintenance. Guessing a share of fuel is how those claims fall apart.

Anchors, caulk, and blades are current expenses. A drill that lasts for years may be capital. [Capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) spreads that cost over time on the business-use portion, in the class the CRA assigns. Do not deduct a van in full in the month you bought it for three shelves. Look up the year you file, or ask an accountant when equipment is a large part of the return.

Once registered, GST/HST you pay on tools, hardware, and other business purchases can often be recovered as an input tax credit to the extent of commercial use. Confirm the current input-tax-credit rules with the CRA. Receipts should show the tax. Pass that saving into your pricing honesty: you still do not spend the tax you collected from clients. If you are not registered, you do not add GST/HST to the invoice and you do not claim the credit.

Municipal permits and business licences are local. Use the fee on the city’s page. If you pay a helper, check that province’s workers’ compensation rules first.
