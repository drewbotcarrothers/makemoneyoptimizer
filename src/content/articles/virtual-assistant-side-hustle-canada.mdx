A virtual assistant (VA) in Canada is a self-employed person who handles administrative, scheduling, inbox, customer service, bookkeeping-support or social media tasks for clients remotely. You can start with the computer and phone you already have, a free calendar and document suite, and one or two clearly defined services. Employed administrative assistants in Canada earn a median of $26.44 an hour (Job Bank, November 2025), and freelance VAs need to charge more than that to cover their own CPP, tools and unpaid admin time.

<QuickAnswer>

- **Pay benchmark:** administrative assistants (NOC 13110) earn a median of $26.44 an hour in Canada, ranging from $19.23 to $36.88 (Job Bank, updated November 2025). British Columbia's median is highest at $28.50.
- **Platform cuts:** Upwork charges freelancers a 0% to 15% fee per contract. Fiverr pays sellers 80% of each order.
- **Real Canadian VAs:** Calgary's Bobbie Racette started as a part-time VA after an oil-and-gas layoff, grew to 19 clients before hiring help, and built Virtual Gurus. Vancouver-area VA Anastasia Olson found her first clients among fellow jewellery designers in 2016.
- **What clients buy:** inbox and calendar management, scheduling, customer email, data entry, invoicing support, social media scheduling and travel booking, often as a monthly hours package.
- **Tax and privacy:** report on Form T2125, pay 11.9% CPP on net earnings above $3,500, register for GST/HST after $30,000, and handle client data in line with PIPEDA.

</QuickAnswer>

## Who VA work suits

VA work rewards organized, dependable people who like keeping other people's work on track. It suits you if:

- You have office, administrative, customer service, legal, medical office or retail management experience.
- You're comfortable with Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, email, calendars and spreadsheets.
- You communicate clearly and promptly in writing.
- You want flexible remote work that fits around a job, school or family. VA work also appears on our list of [side hustles for stay-at-home parents](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-stay-at-home-parents-canada/).

It's a weaker fit if you dislike repetitive tasks, can't keep regular response times, or want to work only on your own schedule with no client deadlines.

## What VAs earn in Canada

There's no official survey of freelance VA rates in Canada. The closest benchmark is Job Bank's wage data for administrative assistants (NOC 13110), updated November 19, 2025 from the 2023–24 Labour Force Survey:

| Region | Low | Median | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canada | $19.23 | $26.44 | $36.88 |
| British Columbia | $20.00 | $28.50 | $36.54 |
| Ontario | $19.00 | $26.67 | $37.95 |
| Alberta | $19.78 | $26.43 | $37.00 |
| Quebec | $20.00 | $26.00 | $36.06 |
| Nova Scotia | $18.30 | $25.00 | $35.58 |
| Manitoba | $17.00 | $24.32 | $34.66 |

As a freelancer you pay both halves of CPP, buy your own software, and spend unpaid time on sales and admin. That's why freelance rates need to be meaningfully higher than the employee median to match the same take-home value.

### Platform fees

- **Upwork:** a Freelancer Service Fee of 0% to 15% per contract, shown in the offer and fixed once the contract starts. Upwork's example: bill $22.22 an hour at a 10% fee to net $20.
- **Fiverr:** you keep 80% of each order, including extras and tips.

Marketplaces help you find a first client, but direct clients from referrals and networking usually pay better because there's no platform cut. Compare the two big ones in [Fiverr vs Upwork](/side-hustles/fiverr-vs-upwork-canada/) and read our [Upwork review](/side-hustles/upwork-review-canada/).

## Real Canadian virtual assistants

- **Bobbie Racette, Virtual Gurus (Calgary).** Racette told Women of Influence in 2020 that she'd been laid off from an oil and gas job, her EI was running out, and she was "borderline homeless." She started charging "pretty low rates" as a VA, enough to pay her rent, and when she reached 19 clients she brought in another VA. In a Long Calgary interview she said she started part-time while working at a coffee shop, ran everything on Excel sheets, and made about $265,000 in sales in the first year by herself. Virtual Gurus later became a marketplace matching clients with remote assistants.
- **Anastasia Olson (Vancouver area, B.C.).** In an interview with The VA Handbook, Olson said she spent over 20 years as a legal administrative assistant, left that field in 2012, and set up her VA business in 2016. Her first clients were fellow jewellery designers from her own circle, and she now specializes in product-based businesses because she "speaks the same language."

Both started with what they already knew: Racette with the administrative work Alberta companies had cut, Olson with an industry she'd worked in herself.

## Choosing a niche

A niche makes your marketing easier and lets you learn one industry's tools deeply. Olson's background as a jewellery designer is what made her a natural fit for product-based businesses. Use your own history the same way.

| Niche | Typical tasks | Tools you'll likely use | Good fit if you've worked in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real estate agents | Showing schedules, listing uploads, transaction checklists, client follow-ups | CRM, e-signature, calendar | Real estate, legal or office admin |
| Coaches and consultants | Calendar, client onboarding, invoicing, newsletters | Calendly, email marketing, invoicing apps | Customer service, education |
| Online stores | Customer email, product uploads, order issues, reviews | Shopify, help desk, spreadsheets | Retail, e-commerce |
| Trades and home services | Booking jobs, quotes, invoices, review requests | Jobber, QuickBooks, Google Business Profile | Construction office, dispatch |
| Podcasters and creators | Guest booking, show notes, social scheduling | Scheduling tools, Canva, publishing platforms | Media, marketing |
| Professional practices | Inbox, scheduling, document prep | Microsoft 365, practice software | Legal, medical or dental office |

Pick one, build two sample deliverables for it (a sample SOP or a sample weekly report), and mention the niche in every outreach message.

## Setting your rate: a worked example

**Illustrative example.** Say you want the take-home value of the Job Bank median of $26.44 an hour and expect to bill 70% of your working hours, with the rest going to admin and finding clients (the 70% is an assumption).

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Target value per working hour | Job Bank median | $26.44 |
| Add the CPP gap (11.9% vs 5.95%) | $26.44 × 1.0595 | $28.01 |
| Adjust for 70% billable time | $28.01 ÷ 0.70 | $40.02 per billable hour |
| On Upwork at a 10% fee | $40.02 ÷ 0.90 | $44.47 per hour billed |
| On Fiverr (you keep 80%) | $40.02 ÷ 0.80 | $50.03 per hour of work priced in |

**Illustrative packages.** Many VAs sell monthly blocks of hours, billed in advance:

| Package | Hours/month | Price at $40/hour |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | 10 | $400 |
| Standard | 20 | $800 |
| Priority | 40 | $1,600 |

Three Standard clients would bring in $2,400 a month, about $28,800 a year, just under the $30,000 GST/HST threshold. More pricing approaches are in [how to price VA services](/side-hustles/virtual-assistant-pricing-canada/).

## Your launch plan: the first 30 days

### Week 1: choose your offer

- List your past jobs and the admin tasks you did well.
- Pick a niche (realtors, coaches, e-commerce stores, trades businesses, podcasters) and two or three services.
- Write a one-paragraph offer: who you help, what you do, and the package price.

### Week 2: set up

- Create a simple one-page site or LinkedIn profile with your offer, packages and booking link.
- Set up Calendly, an invoicing tool and a password manager.
- Write a service agreement and a privacy/confidentiality clause (checklist below).
- Open a separate account; options in [KOHO vs Wealthsimple vs EQ Bank](/side-hustles/koho-vs-wealthsimple-vs-eq-bank-side-hustle-canada/).

### Week 3: find clients

- Tell your network what you do. Racette and Olson both started with people they already knew.
- Send 5 to 10 personalized messages a day to small businesses in your niche, following CASL rules for commercial emails.
- Apply to a few relevant Upwork jobs each day if you're using marketplaces.
- Scripts are in [how to land your first VA client](/side-hustles/virtual-assistant-first-client-canada/).

### Week 4: onboard and deliver

- Run a 30-minute discovery call, then send a proposal within 24 hours.
- Onboard with the checklist below, deliver a weekly summary, and ask for a testimonial after the first month.

### Days 31 to 90

| Weeks | Focus | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | Document every client process as a checklist | Faster delivery |
| 7–8 | Convert hourly clients to monthly packages | Predictable income |
| 9–10 | Ask for referrals; add one higher-value service | Two to three steady clients |
| 11–12 | Raise rates for new clients; review time logs | Higher effective hourly rate |

## What a VA's working week looks like

Most side-hustle VAs work in short, scheduled blocks rather than being on call all day. A sample week with two clients on 20-hour packages might look like this:

| Block | Client A (realtor) | Client B (online store) |
|---|---|---|
| Daily, 30 minutes morning | Inbox triage, flag urgent messages, confirm showings | Answer customer emails, check new orders |
| Monday | Update listing spreadsheet and CRM | Upload new products and update stock counts |
| Wednesday | Schedule social posts for the week | Process returns and exchanges |
| Thursday | Prepare transaction checklist for upcoming closings | Draft the weekly newsletter |
| Friday | Send weekly summary and hours used | Send weekly summary and hours used |

Batching similar tasks keeps your time logs clean and makes it easy to show clients exactly where their hours went.

## Discovery call questions

A 20- to 30-minute discovery call tells you whether a client is a good fit before you quote. Ask:

1. What tasks take up time you'd rather spend elsewhere?
2. Which tools do you use for email, calendar, files, payments and customer management?
3. How many hours a week do those tasks take you now?
4. What does "done well" look like for each task?
5. How fast do you expect replies, and during which hours?
6. Who else will I work with, and who approves my work?
7. What information is sensitive, and how do you currently share logins?
8. What's your budget range, and do you prefer hourly billing or a monthly package?

End the call by summarizing what you heard and telling the client when to expect your proposal.

## Turn every recurring task into a checklist

Writing a short standard operating procedure (SOP) for each recurring task makes you faster, reduces mistakes and makes it possible to hand work to a subcontractor later, the way Racette did when she brought in her first VA at 19 clients.

**SOP template**

> **Task:** [Name, e.g., "Weekly newsletter send"]
> **Client:** [Name] · **Frequency:** [Weekly, Thursdays by 2 p.m.]
> **Tools:** [List with links]
> **Steps:**
> 1. [Step]
> 2. [Step]
> 3. [Step]
> **Done when:** [Clear finish line, e.g., "Sent to list; test email checked on phone; link clicks working"]
> **Watch out for:** [Common mistakes]
> **Time budget:** [e.g., 45 minutes]

Once you have SOPs, look for steps you can automate. Moving form submissions into a spreadsheet, sending appointment reminders or creating invoices from booked calls are common candidates for tools like Zapier.

## Privacy and security

Canadian VAs handle client inboxes, customer lists and sometimes financial details. PIPEDA governs how private-sector organizations collect, use and disclose personal information in commercial activity, so treat client data carefully:

- Use delegated access or a password manager instead of asking for passwords by email.
- Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere.
- Don't download client data to personal devices unless needed, and delete it when the contract ends.
- Follow CASL if you send marketing emails on a client's behalf: consent, identification and an unsubscribe option.

## Taxes for virtual assistants

- **Income tax:** VA income is self-employment income. Report revenue and expenses (software, a share of home office costs, internet, phone, headset) on Form T2125.
- **CPP:** 11.9% of net earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026 (QPP in Quebec).
- **GST/HST:** you're a small supplier until taxable supplies pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Services to clients outside Canada may qualify as zero-rated exports; check CRA's rules before invoicing foreign clients. Once registered, rates depend on where your Canadian client is, such as 13% HST in Ontario or 14% in Nova Scotia.
- **Set money aside** from every payment; [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) shows the math.

## Provincial notes

- **B.C.:** highest Job Bank median for admin assistants ($28.50), a useful anchor when pricing for local clients.
- **Ontario and Alberta:** medians of $26.67 and $26.43; Toronto and Calgary have dense small-business markets.
- **Quebec:** bilingual VAs can serve both language markets; QPP replaces CPP and QST applies once registered.
- **Atlantic Canada and Manitoba:** lower local medians, but remote clients can be anywhere in Canada or abroad.

## What to watch out for

- **Underpricing.** Racette described starting at "pretty low rates." It's common, but raise rates once you have testimonials.
- **Scope creep.** Define what's included in each package and quote extra work.
- **Unpaid hours.** Track every task, including quick messages.
- **Client concentration.** One client leaving can cut your income in half. Aim for three or more.
- **Security slips.** Mishandled passwords or data can end a client relationship and create liability.
- **Burnout from always being available.** Set working hours and response times in your agreement.
