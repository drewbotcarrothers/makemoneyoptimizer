A ski or snowboard instructor side hustle in Canada usually means a part-time weekend job at a resort snow school, after a Level 1 course from the Canadian Ski Instructors' Alliance (CSIA) or the Canadian Association of Snowboard Instructors (CASI). Level 1 courses for 2026/27 cost about $500 to $610 at the resorts and associations below, and four resorts' 2026/27 job postings offer Level 1 instructors $19.00 to $21.00 an hour to start, close to Job Bank's $19.00 national median. Fees and wages here were read on each organisation's own page on October 19, 2026.

<QuickAnswer>

- **CASI Level 1 (snowboard):** $552.98 early or $608.28 regular, plus CASI membership of $112.42 a year plus tax for Canadian residents ([CASI](https://casi-acms.com/courses/level-1-instructor/)).
- **CSIA Level 1 (ski):** $545 at Grouse Mountain, lift tickets not included, ages 14+, ability level 5 or higher; $499 early or $524 at Whitewater ([Grouse](https://www.grousemountain.com/products/csia-instructor-certification), [Whitewater](https://whitewatermountainresort.com/snow-school/courses-certifications/instructor-courses/)).
- **Pay:** Job Bank's ski instructor wages run from a $15.44 low to a $31.00 high nationally, with a $19.00 median; the B.C. median is $23.74 ([Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/21200/ca)).
- **Resort pay for 2026/27 (Level 1):** Blue Mountain, Ont. $19.00; Cypress Mountain, B.C. $19.50+; Revelstoke $19.91 to $23.29; Whistler Blackcomb from $21.00 (Level 1 and 2 posting), all read October 19, 2026.
- **Course timing:** Grouse runs Level 1 sessions from December 11 to 13, 2026 through April 2027; Whitewater's first is December 19 to 21, 2026.
- **Tax:** resort pay is usually employment income shown on a T4 slip, not self-employment income.

</QuickAnswer>

## How does teaching skiing or snowboarding pay in Canada?

Teaching skiing or snowboarding in Canada pays an hourly wage from the resort's snow school, so the side hustle is a part-time seasonal job rather than a business. Job Bank's [ski instructor wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/21200/ca), updated November 19, 2025 from Labour Force Survey data for 2023-2024, lists these hourly figures:

| Region | Low | Median | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada | $15.44 | $19.00 | $31.00 |
| British Columbia | $18.25 | $23.74 | $35.00 |
| Alberta | $15.50 | $20.00 | $35.00 |
| Ontario | $17.95 | $18.50 | $31.00 |
| Quebec | $16.60 | $18.75 | $29.00 |
| Manitoba | $16.40 | $17.50 | $24.04 |
| Nova Scotia | $17.00 | $17.00 | $24.34 |
| New Brunswick | $15.90 | $16.80 | $25.00 |

The Job Bank occupation is broader than ski schools (it sits in the recreation, sport and fitness instructor group), and the reference period is before 2026 minimum wage increases. Treat the median as a starting point for what to ask in the interview, and the high column as what certified, experienced instructors reach.

### What do snow schools require besides the course?

The same postings list what you need on the first day:

- **A Level 1 certificate:** CSIA, CASI or PSIC Level 1 (Cypress and Whistler also accept recognised foreign equivalents; Blue Mountain accepts another recognised organisation's certification).
- **A police check:** Blue Mountain asks applicants 18 or older for a Vulnerable Sector Check; Cypress asks for a Police Information Check with vulnerable sector screening dated April 2025 or later; Revelstoke asks for a criminal record check less than 3 months old.
- **Minimum age:** 16 at Cypress; Whistler's Level 1 and 2 posting accepts minor hires from 14.
- **Holiday availability:** Blue Mountain lists Christmas, Family Day weekend and March Break; Cypress says winter holiday availability is a requirement.
- **A season commitment (Cypress):** part-time instructors must be available at least 18 days and work 12, including at least 8 peak or holiday days and 10 consistent weekends, evening or night days; full-time instructors must be available 64 days and work 52.

## Illustrative example: when does a Level 1 course pay for itself?

This is an illustrative example. The course fee, membership and wage are sourced; the hours you get and the lift and gear costs are assumptions.

**Snowboard route (CASI):**

- Sourced: course $608.28 (regular) + membership $112.42 = $720.70 before tax.
- Assumed: $60 for lift access during the course and $0 in new gear because you already ride.
- Up-front cost: $720.70 + $60 = $780.70.
- Sourced wage: the national median of $19.00 an hour.
- Hours to earn it back before tax: $780.70 ÷ $19.00 = 41.1 hours.

| Weekend hours (assumed) | Weeks to recover $780.70 at $19.00 | Weeks at the B.C. median of $23.74 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 a week | 6.8 | 5.5 |
| 10 a week | 4.1 | 3.3 |
| 14 a week | 2.9 | 2.3 |

**Ski route (CSIA at Grouse):** $545 + an assumed $60 for half-price mountain admission across the course = $605, so $605 ÷ $19.00 = 31.8 hours at the national median. Add the CSIA membership when you register; check the current fee on the CSIA site.

Over an assumed 12-week season at 10 hours a week and $19.00: 12 × 10 × $19.00 = $2,280 gross, or $2,280 − $780.70 = $1,499.30 after the snowboard course costs, before tax and the drive.

This is illustrative. The hourly rates and Cypress's 12-day part-time minimum are from the postings above; the 6 hours a day and the number of extra days are assumptions.

| Season (assumed) | Blue Mountain at $19.00 | Cypress at $19.50 | Revelstoke at $19.91 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12 days × 6 hours = 72 hours | $1,368 | $1,404 | $1,433.52 |
| 20 days × 6 hours = 120 hours | $2,280 | $2,340 | $2,389.20 |
| 30 days × 6 hours = 180 hours | $3,420 | $3,510 | $3,583.80 |

Formula: days × hours a day × hourly rate. At Cypress, each requested private hour adds $15 to $20 on top, so 10 requested private hours over a season would add $150 to $200. Before tax, and before the $605 to $780.70 course costs worked out above.

## How do you get hired at a snow school?

You get hired at a snow school by applying in the fall, booking a Level 1 course early in the season (often at the same resort), and telling the school which days you can commit to. A first-season plan:

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| October | Check your resort's snow school job posting and Level 1 dates; register before early-price deadlines |
| November | Apply; confirm the course dates you can attend; sort a lift pass or course-day tickets |
| December | Take the 3-day Level 1 course (Grouse's first session is December 11 to 13, 2026) |
| January | Teach your first lessons, usually children and beginners on weekends |
| February | Holiday weeks (Family Day, March break planning) are the busiest; offer extra days |
| March to April | Ask about a Level 2 module for next season; keep your hours log |

## Which tax rules apply to ski instructor pay in Canada?

Ski instructor pay from a resort is usually employment income. The CRA's [line 10100 page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/line-10100-employment-income.html) says employment income, including wages and tips, is usually shown in box 14 of your T4 slip, and lets you claim the Canada employment amount. Tips not shown on a T4 can be reported with additional CPP contributions, as that page explains. Private lessons you sell yourself, where a resort allows them, would be self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html).

The free pass is the other tax question. The CRA's [recreational facilities page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/benefits-allowances/recreational-facilities-club-dues.html) says use of an employer's in-house recreational facility is not a taxable benefit when it is available to all employees free or for a minimal fee, and is taxable at fair market value when only a select group gets it. That page does not mention ski passes by name, and Whistler's posting lists free passes for dependents as a full-time benefit, so ask payroll how passes show up on your T4. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
