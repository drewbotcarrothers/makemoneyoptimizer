## Which insurance mistakes do dog walkers make in Canada?

The main insurance mistake dog walkers make in Canada is assuming personal insurance covers paid pet care. It usually does not.

### Mistake 1: No liability insurance

Liability insurance can cover claims if a dog in your care injures someone or damages property. Ask a broker about pet-care business coverage and whether it includes care, custody, and control of animals.

### Mistake 2: Assuming home insurance covers it

The FCAC's [home insurance page](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/insurance/home.html) says home insurance isn't business insurance, that you must notify your insurer of a home-based business, and that using your home for purposes your insurer isn't aware of may cancel your policy. This matters most if you board dogs at home.

## Which local rule mistakes do dog walkers make in Canada?

The main local rule mistake dog walkers make in Canada is not checking city bylaws on commercial walking, leashes, and parks.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring city permits

Toronto's [commercial dog walker permit page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/commercial-dog-walker-permit/) says permits are available for individuals to walk four to six dogs on a commercial basis. It requires $2,000,000 of commercial general liability insurance with the City as an additional insured. The fee is $305.99 for one year, and violations can lead to suspension. Other cities have their own rules; search your city's website.

### Mistake 4: Walking too many dogs at once

Even where no permit applies, more dogs means less control. Set a limit you can manage safely, and group dogs by size and temperament.

## Which client and money mistakes do dog walkers make in Canada?

The main client mistake dog walkers make in Canada is working without a written agreement and without records.

### Mistake 5: No written agreement

Cover walk times, keys and lockbox codes, what happens in an emergency, consent and a spending limit for vet care, cancellations, and payment dates.

### Mistake 6: Careless with client details

Addresses, alarm codes, and key locations are sensitive. [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) applies to personal information collected in commercial activity, with substantially similar laws in Alberta, British Columbia, and Quebec. Keep codes out of group chats and label keys with a code, not an address.

### Mistake 7: No income records

Log each walk and payment. The CRA's [keeping records page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records.html) explains what to keep.

## How do you set up dog walking to avoid these mistakes in Canada?

Set up dog walking in Canada to avoid these mistakes by sorting insurance, city rules, and a client agreement before your first paid walk.

### Step 1: Get insurance quotes

Ask about pet-care liability and any requirements your city sets.

### Step 2: Check your city's bylaws

Look for commercial walker permits, dog limits, and park rules.

### Step 3: Write a one-page agreement

Include emergencies, vet consent, keys, and cancellations.

### Step 4: Set up secure key and code storage

Label keys with codes, not addresses.

### Step 5: Start a walk and payment log

Record date, client, dogs, and amount.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote or average. Suppose a walker in Toronto takes five dogs at once and needs the City permit at $305.99 a year. If liability insurance costs $500 a year (assumed) and the walker earns $20 per dog per group walk (assumed), the first-year fixed costs are $305.99 + $500 = $805.99. At five dogs per walk, that is $100 a walk, so just over 8 walks ($805.99 ÷ $100 = 8.06) cover those fixed costs. Skipping the permit to save that money risks suspension.
