Upwork and LinkedIn are the two places Canadian freelancers most often look for clients, and they work in opposite ways. On Upwork, clients post jobs, you spend Connects to send proposals, and Upwork handles the contract and payment in exchange for a fee. On LinkedIn, you make yourself findable with a profile and a free Service Page, clients message you, and you handle scope, contract and payment yourself. There's no platform fee, but there's also no built-in payment protection.

Fees and plans below are from Upwork's and LinkedIn's own pages, checked October 9, 2026. Upwork prices in US dollars. Conversions use the [Bank of Canada's](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) October 8, 2026 rate of 1.4240 and are approximate.

## What do Upwork's fees actually include?

Upwork's [service fee article](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062538-Learn-about-the-Freelancer-Service-Fee) says the fee ranges from 0% to 15% per contract and pays for payment protection, fraud prevention and dispute resolution. You see the percentage when a client sends an offer or when you submit a proposal. Once the contract begins it's fixed. If a client is refunded, Upwork returns the service fee on that amount. Upwork says it doesn't offer regular discounts on the fee.

The [pricing breakdown](https://www.upwork.com/resources/is-upwork-free), updated July 23, 2026, adds:

- **Basic is free** and may include 10 Connects a month. Extra Connects cost US$0.15 each, with a minimum purchase of 10 (US$1.50, about C$2.14). The number needed per proposal varies by job.
- **Freelancer Plus is US$19.99 a month.** It includes 100 Connects, full access to Upwork's AI assistant, job alerts, more profile customization and a 0% fee on direct contracts. The 0%–15% standard service fee is the same on both plans.
- **Service fees are only charged when you're paid.** Plus fees and Connects are paid when you buy them.
- **Payout methods vary by location.** The page lists bank transfer, PayPal, Payoneer and M-Pesa.

Clients pay their own fee too: 3%–5% on Basic or 8%–10% on Business Plus, plus contract initiation fees in some cases. That doesn't come out of your earnings, but it does make the client's real cost higher than your rate.

**Don't rely on a single remembered rate.** If a guide quotes one fixed Upwork percentage, check it against the current help page, which describes a 0%–15% fee set per contract and shown before you propose.

## How does LinkedIn's Service Page work?

LinkedIn's [Service Pages FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a569534) says Service Pages are free landing pages for freelancers and small businesses, available everywhere except China. A Service Page makes your services more discoverable on LinkedIn and through search sites such as Google, and any LinkedIn member can message you for free about them. Clients can also search linkedin.com/services.

Without Premium, your profile shows a list of the services you offer. With Premium Business, Sales Navigator or Recruiter Lite, it shows a Services Showcase with a media carousel, a Request proposal button, a service description preview, your ratings and your most recent four- or five-star review. You also become eligible for additional project requests. LinkedIn's help pages don't print a Premium price; it's shown when you sign in, so this guide doesn't state one.

LinkedIn's [get started](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550345) page says you manage requests from the Service Page admin view. It shows your average response time if you reply within 24 hours, and your response rate if you answer at least 80% of inquiries.

## How much do you keep on one project?

**Illustrative. The US$1,000 project, the fee rates and the 40 Connects are assumptions within Upwork's published 0%–15% range and US$0.15 Connect price. Conversions use 1.4240. Verify the arithmetic and your actual fee before you propose.**

| Route | Arithmetic | You keep (USD) | About in CAD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upwork at 10% | 1,000 − 100 − 6 in Connects | US$894 | C$1,273.06 |
| Upwork at 15% | 1,000 − 150 − 6 in Connects | US$844 | C$1,201.86 |
| LinkedIn lead, invoiced directly | 1,000 | US$1,000 | C$1,424.00, before your processor's fee and any bank conversion |

On this project the LinkedIn route keeps C$150.94 to C$222.14 more. That's what you're paying Upwork for: posted jobs, a contract system and payment protection on funded milestones. The LinkedIn number also assumes the client pays on time. Ask for a deposit and use a short written contract.

## What should you charge on Upwork to hit a Canadian rate?

**Illustrative. The C$50 target is an assumption, not a typical rate.**

Upwork's own example: to take home US$20 an hour at a 10% fee, charge US$22.22. The same method works for a CAD target:

1. Convert your take-home target to USD: C$50 ÷ 1.4240 = US$35.11.
2. Divide by (1 − your fee): at 10%, US$35.11 ÷ 0.90 = US$39.01 an hour. At 15%, US$35.11 ÷ 0.85 = US$41.31.
3. Set the higher figure if you don't know the fee yet, then check the fee shown on the proposal screen.

Set an annual reminder to recheck, because the exchange rate moves your CAD take-home even when your USD rate doesn't change. Rate ranges by type of work are in [freelance writing rates](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-pricing-canada/).

## When does Freelancer Plus pay for itself?

**Illustrative; verify the arithmetic.** Plus costs US$19.99 a month. Its clearest saving is on direct contracts, where Basic pays 5% and Plus pays 0%. US$19.99 ÷ 0.05 = US$399.80. If you bill more than about US$399.80 a month (about C$569.32) through direct contracts, Plus costs less than the 5% fee. Plus also includes 90 more Connects than Basic; at US$0.15 each that's US$13.50 of Connects, worth something only if you'd actually use them.

If you mostly win marketplace jobs and propose a few times a month, Basic plus the occasional Connects purchase is usually cheaper. Count how many Connects you used last month before upgrading.

## Who should pick Upwork or LinkedIn in Canada?

Many freelancers use both: Upwork for inbound work while they build a portfolio, and LinkedIn for higher-value referrals once they have one.

| If you… | Lean toward | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Have no network yet | Upwork | Posted jobs and payment protection on funded milestones |
| Have years of industry experience and contacts | LinkedIn | Free Service Page and warm introductions |
| Want the platform to handle payment | Upwork | Contracts and payment run through Upwork |
| Want to keep the whole fee | LinkedIn | No platform fee |
| Sell to Canadian businesses in CAD | LinkedIn | You set the invoice currency |
| Already have clients you'd like to bill through a platform | Upwork direct contracts | 5% on Basic, 0% on Plus |

## How do you use each platform well?

### On Upwork

1. Fill in the profile and portfolio before spending Connects.
2. Propose only to jobs that match your samples. Every proposal spends Connects.
3. Check the fee shown on the proposal screen and price around it.
4. On fixed-price work, start only after the milestone shows as funded, and deliver through Submit Work for Payment. Upwork's protection page says that starts a 14-day countdown to automatic payment. It also says Upwork can't help recover payment for a milestone that wasn't funded in advance.
5. Keep messages, files and scope changes on Upwork.

A proposal structure is in the [Upwork proposal template for Canadians](/side-hustles/upwork-proposal-template-canada/).

### On LinkedIn

1. Create the free Service Page, list your services and write a plain About section.
2. Reply within 24 hours where you can, since LinkedIn displays your response time.
3. Move a serious lead to a written scope, a deposit and an invoice.
4. If you email prospects outside either platform, Canada's [anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies to commercial electronic messages.

## What mistakes do Canadian freelancers make on Upwork and LinkedIn?

- **Proposing to every job.** Connects cost money; pick jobs that match your samples.
- **Pricing in CAD on Upwork.** Upwork prices in USD; convert your target rate first.
- **Starting an unfunded milestone.** Upwork says it can't help recover that payment.
- **No contract from a LinkedIn lead.** LinkedIn doesn't protect payment; use a deposit and a written scope.
- **A blank Service Page.** Add services, an About section and samples so requests have context.
- **Cold-emailing without consent.** Check Canada's anti-spam rules before bulk outreach.

## Which tax rules apply to Upwork and LinkedIn freelancers in Canada?

Freelance income from either source is generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) in Canadian dollars. Upwork fees, Connects, Freelancer Plus and LinkedIn Premium used for the business are generally deductible expenses. Platforms can report earnings to the CRA, as explained in [tax on reselling and gig apps](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).

For direct LinkedIn clients you send your own invoices; invoicing software such as <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> or a template both work.
