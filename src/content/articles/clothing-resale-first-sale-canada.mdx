A first clothing resale in Canada usually comes from a clean item you already own, in a style and size you understand, listed with flat measurements, daylight photos of every flaw, and a price set after the app's fee. Buyers of used clothes mostly worry about fit and condition, so a listing that answers both is the one that sells.

Poshmark and Depop are the two clothing apps this guide covers for Canadian sellers, and they charge differently. ## What should you list for a first clothing resale in Canada?

List clean, undamaged pieces from your own closet in one style you know for a first clothing resale in Canada: outerwear, denim, or shoes in sizes you can describe. A recognisable brand helps search, but a plain item with exact measurements also sells.

Skip anything with stains you cannot remove, broken zips, or strong odours. Set children's sleepwear aside: Health Canada's [second-hand products guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says sleepwear up to size 14X has flammability requirements, and that all drawstrings should be removed from children's clothing.

## Where do Canadians find a first clothing buyer?

Canadians find a first clothing buyer through the app's own search and feeds, mainly on Poshmark Canada and Depop. Buyers search by brand, size, and item type, so those words belong in the title.

Share a closet or shop link with friends who asked, not in groups that ban selling. Respond to offers and questions the same day. Buyers often message several sellers at once.

## What should a first clothing listing say?

A first clothing listing in Canada should give the brand, item type, tagged size, flat measurements, material from the label, and every flaw. Facts in the first two lines answer most buyer questions.

**Example listing text.** This is a generic example. It is not a listing that produced a sale.

> Women's waterproof rain jacket, tagged size M. Pit to pit 52 cm, length 70 cm, measured flat. Shell 100% nylon per the care label. Small mark on the left cuff, shown in photo 5. Washed and stored in a smoke-free home. Ships within 2 business days.

Do not write "like new" on worn items, or "authentic" on a brand item you cannot verify. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) covers claims made to promote a product.

## How do you show condition without fake reviews?

You show clothing condition in Canada with daylight photos of the front, back, label, and each flaw, plus a measurement photo with the tape visible. A new account has no reviews, so the photos and measurements carry the trust.

Do not ask friends to buy and review items to seed your profile, and do not use stock photos of a new item for a used one. Reviews come from real buyers.

## How should you price the first clothing resale in Canada?

Price the first clothing resale in Canada by checking sold listings for the same brand and item, then running the fee sum for the app you plan to use. This guide does not state typical resale prices.

| Path | Fee basis | What you watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poshmark under C$20 | Flat C$3.95 | A large share of a low price |
| Poshmark C$20 or more | 20%, label included | Parcels over 2.5 kg |
| Depop | 10% plus PayPal; label extra unless a Depop label is used | Pricing shipping correctly |

### Illustrative example

Suppose you list a jacket from your closet at C$45 on Poshmark. The fee is 20% × $45 = $9, so you keep $36, with the label included. If you accept an offer of C$38 instead, the fee is 20% × $38 = $7.60, and you keep $30.40. On Depop at $45 with $12 shipping charged and a $12 label you buy, the selling fee is 10% × $57 = $5.70, PayPal is 2.90% × $57 + $0.30 = $1.95, and you keep $57 − $5.70 − $1.95 − $12 = $37.35. These figures are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first clothing resale look like?

A 14-day plan for a first clothing resale in Canada should produce ten measured listings from your own closet and a fee sheet. A sale inside 14 days is not guaranteed.

### Days 1 and 2: Read the fee pages

Read Poshmark's getting started guide, Depop's seller fees page, and PayPal's fee page. Put the sums in a sheet.

### Days 3 to 5: Pull, wash, and photograph

Choose ten items in one style. Wash them. Photograph in daylight, including flaws and labels.

### Days 6 to 9: Measure and list

Measure flat. Write titles with brand, item, and size. List each item on the app whose fee suits its price.

### Days 10 to 14: Answer and ship

Answer questions with measurements. Ship within two business days of a sale. Lower prices on items with no interest after a week.

## What mistakes block a first clothing resale in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first clothing resale in Canada are missing measurements, hidden flaws, and prices set without the fee. Each one leads to no sale, a return, or a payout smaller than expected.

Buying a pile of thrifted stock before the first sale ties up cash. Listing under C$20 on Poshmark without counting the C$3.95 fee shrinks the margin. Slow shipping invites cancellations.

## Which tax rules apply to a first clothing resale in Canada?

Clothing resale income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase notes, payout reports, PayPal statements, and mailer receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Reselling clothes is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
