Publishing an ebook in Canada means writing a book, formatting it as an EPUB or PDF, and selling it through Amazon KDP, Kobo Writing Life, other stores or your own checkout. Royalties on Amazon.ca and Kobo are 70% for books priced from C$2.99 (on Amazon.ca, up to C$12.99), and ISBNs are free to eligible Canadian publishers through Library and Archives Canada. Most authors earn little: the Writers' Union of Canada's 2018 survey put median net writing income under $4,000. Canadian indie authors who publish series, though, have built full-time incomes.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Royalties (checked October 9, 2026):** Amazon KDP pays 70% on Amazon.ca for ebooks priced C$2.99–C$12.99 (the top of the band rose from $9.99 on July 7, 2026), minus a delivery fee averaging $0.06 a copy. Outside that band it pays 35%. Kobo Writing Life pays 70% at C$2.99 and up and 45% below that.
- **Per-sale math:** a C$7.99 ebook earns about C$5.55 on Amazon.ca at 70% and about C$5.59 on Kobo. At C$14.99 it drops to 35%, about C$5.25.
- **ISBNs:** Library and Archives Canada issues them to eligible Canadian publishers. New accounts can take up to about two months to approve.
- **Income reality:** the Writers' Union of Canada's 2018 survey found an average net writing income of $9,380 and a median under $4,000. Ontario cozy-mystery author Reagan Davis went full-time after publishing 12 books in three years, with 60% of her income from Kindle Unlimited.
- **Tax:** report royalties on T2125, pay CPP on the profit, and register for GST/HST past $30,000. The point-of-sale rebate on printed books doesn't apply to ebooks.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does ebook publishing suit?

It suits people who can finish a manuscript, who know a subject or genre well, and who are willing to publish more than one book. It's quiet, flexible work, but the business side (covers, descriptions, ads, newsletters) matters as much as the writing.

It suits:

- **Nonfiction experts** who can turn a process into a short practical guide: a trade, a hobby, a Canadian admin process.
- **Genre fiction writers** (romance, cozy mystery, fantasy, thrillers, middle-grade) who can write a series; the Canadian authors below all point to series as the turning point.
- **Writers who already freelance** and want an asset that sells after the work is done; see [freelance writing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Course creators and bloggers** who already have an audience to sell to.

It doesn't suit people who need income in the next few months, or who plan to publish AI-generated filler. Readers' reviews punish thin books quickly. If you'd rather sell a single page, see [printables](/side-hustles/printables-side-hustle-canada/); if the reader should edit the file, see [Notion templates](/side-hustles/notion-templates-side-hustle-canada/).

**How KDP calculates royalties.** At 35%, the royalty is 35% of the list price without VAT. At 70%, it's 70% of the list price without VAT, minus delivery costs, which KDP says average **$0.06 per unit** and vary by file size ([KDP royalties](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200644210)). The 70% option applies only to in-copyright works sold in the listed territories. Since **July 7, 2026**, the 70% price band on Amazon.ca runs from **C$2.99 to C$12.99**, up from a C$9.99 ceiling ([KDP price requirements](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560)).

**Kindle Unlimited.** Books enrolled in KDP Select earn from a monthly fund split by pages read. The Canadian Press reported the fund was **US$45.2 million in November 2022**. Kobo Plus, Kobo's subscription, pays based on time spent reading.

A deeper look at Amazon's terms is in the [Amazon KDP review for Canadian authors](/side-hustles/amazon-kdp-review-canada/), and direct sales are covered in [selling on Gumroad from Canada](/side-hustles/gumroad-side-hustle-canada/).

## How much do Canadian authors earn?

Most authors earn little from writing, and a small number earn a living, usually with many books in a series. The best Canadian dataset is the Writers' Union of Canada's income survey of its members and other writers, conducted in spring 2018.

**Writers' Union of Canada income survey 2018 (2017 incomes)**

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Average net income from writing | $9,380 |
| Median net income from writing | Less than $4,000 |
| Change since 1998, after inflation | 78% lower |
| Respondents below the poverty line from writing income | More than 85% |
| Revenue from self-published titles | Fourth-largest source of writing income |

The survey covers all writers, most traditionally published, and is now several years old. It's still the clearest Canadian benchmark: a single ebook rarely replaces a job. Your own figures are in your KDP and Kobo dashboards.

## Worked math: what does each sale earn?

**Royalty formulas (sourced):**

- KDP 70%: 0.70 × (list price − applicable VAT − delivery cost)
- KDP 35%: 0.35 × (list price − applicable VAT)
- Kobo: 0.70 × list price (excluding tax) at C$2.99 or more; 0.45 × list price below

**Illustrative example: royalty per copy at different prices.** Prices are assumptions; rates are from KDP and Kobo (checked October 9, 2026). The KDP rows use the $0.06 average delivery cost and assume no tax is deducted from the list price.

| List price (CAD) | Amazon.ca royalty | Kobo royalty |
|---|---|---|
| C$0.99 | 35% × 0.99 = C$0.35 | 45% × 0.99 = C$0.45 |
| C$2.99 | 70% × (2.99 − 0.06) = C$2.05 | 70% × 2.99 = C$2.09 |
| C$4.99 | 70% × (4.99 − 0.06) = C$3.45 | 70% × 4.99 = C$3.49 |
| C$7.99 | 70% × (7.99 − 0.06) = C$5.55 | 70% × 7.99 = C$5.59 |
| C$12.99 | 70% × (12.99 − 0.06) = C$9.05 | 70% × 12.99 = C$9.09 |
| C$14.99 | 35% × 14.99 = C$5.25 | 70% × 14.99 = C$10.49 |

Pricing just above C$12.99 on Amazon.ca earns less than pricing at C$7.99, because the rate falls to 35%. Kobo has no upper limit on its 70% rate.

**Illustrative example: direct sale of a US$15 PDF guide on Gumroad.** Gumroad takes 10% + US$0.50 = US$2.00, so you keep US$13.00, about **C$18.51** at the Bank of Canada rate of C$1.4240 per US$1 (October 8, 2026). The price is an assumption.

**Illustrative example: a year with one book.** Copies per month are assumptions; the royalty uses the C$7.99 Amazon.ca row (C$5.55).

| | Low | Middle | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copies per month | 10 | 50 | 200 |
| Royalties per year | C$666 | C$3,330 | C$13,320 |
| Writing and editing hours (assumed, one-time) | 120 | 120 | 120 |
| Effective hourly rate in year one | C$5.55 | C$27.75 | C$111 |

The low column is closest to what most first books do. Every Canadian example below reached a living through multiple books, not one.

**Illustrative example: what ads cost against royalties.** If an ad click costs C$0.50 (assumed) and 1 in 10 clickers buys a C$7.99 book (assumed), each sale costs C$5.00 in ads against a C$5.55 royalty. That's a C$0.55 profit per sale on book one; the math only works when readers buy the rest of the series.

## What tools do you need?

You need a writing tool, a formatter, a cover, an editor (human or at least software), and somewhere to sell. All of these have free starting options.

**Writing and formatting (pick one or two):**

- **Reedsy Studio** (free core writing and formatting, per [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/write-a-book)): exports print-ready and EPUB files.

- **Draft2Digital's formatter** (free to use; fees apply when you distribute).
- **Vellum** (Mac) or **Atticus** (Windows, Mac, web): paid formatting apps many indie authors use; see current price on their sites.

## Do you need an ISBN and legal deposit?

You don't need an ISBN to sell on KDP (Amazon assigns its own ASIN), but Canadian publishers can get ISBNs free. If you want your book in libraries and other stores under your own imprint, get one.

- **ISBN:** ISBN Canada provides free ISBNs to eligible publishers and publications ([LAC](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/publishers/isbn.html)). Eligible Canadian publishers create an ISBN Canada account with Library and Archives Canada ([apply page](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/publishers/isbn/apply.html)). The page warns of a backlog: new accounts may take up to about two months to approve. Apply while you're writing.
- **Legal deposit:** section 10 of the [Library and Archives of Canada Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/L-7.7/section-10.html) requires publishers who make a publication available in Canada to provide copies, subject to the regulations. LAC's [legal deposit page](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/publishers/legal-deposit/about.html) explains what it collects.
- **Copyright:** your original text is protected automatically; the Canadian Intellectual Property Office explains registration on its [copyright page](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright).

This is not legal advice.

## How to start: a 12-week plan

### Weeks 1–2: Pick the book

- Choose a nonfiction topic you can write 15,000–25,000 words on, or a fiction genre you read constantly.
- Study the top 20 books in your Amazon.ca and Kobo category: prices, page counts, covers, review complaints.
- Outline: 8–12 chapters for nonfiction; for fiction, outline book one and sketch books two and three.
- Apply for an ISBN account if you want one.

### Weeks 3–8: Write the draft

- Write 1,000–1,500 words a day, five days a week.
- Don't edit while drafting; mark gaps with [TK] and keep moving.
- Start an email list with a one-page freebie or bonus chapter.

### Weeks 9–10: Edit and design

- Self-edit in three passes: structure, clarity, line edits.
- Send to 3–5 beta readers who read the genre.
- Commission or make the cover; write the description and pick keywords and categories.

### Weeks 11–12: Publish and launch

- Format and check the EPUB on a phone and an e-reader.
- Choose KDP Select (exclusive) or wide distribution.
- Publish on Amazon.ca and other stores; set a launch price.
- Email your list, post where your readers are, and ask readers for honest reviews (never pay for or fake reviews).

### Months 4–12

- Start book two immediately; series and consistent releases are what the Canadian authors below credit.
- Track sales per store monthly.
- Test small ads only once you have two or more books.

## Real Canadian authors and their numbers

These are publicly documented. They were chosen because they've spoken about their results, not because their results are typical.

**Reagan Davis (Ontario, cozy mysteries).** The Canadian Press reported in December 2022 that Davis, who writes under a pseudonym, self-published her "Knitorious Murder Mysteries" and reached **12 books in three years**. Mostly through Kindle Unlimited, the series earned enough that she set aside her knitwear-pattern business to write full-time. About **60% of her income comes from KU** and 40% from ebook and print sales. **Lesson:** a steady stream of books for a voracious genre audience built the income.

**Rae Knightly (Port Coquitlam, B.C., middle-grade science fiction).** In the same report, Knightly said she self-published "Ben Archer and the Cosmic Fall" because traditional publishing could take years, and that the turning point came **after book three**, because readers are more likely to try an author with several books out. Treating writing as a business changed things for her. **Lesson:** plan a series before you publish book one.

**Elsie Silver (British Columbia, romance).** The Globe and Mail reported in January 2026 that Silver self-published her first novel while caring for a toddler, writing from 5 to 7 a.m. By the end of her first series she was selling enough, **mostly ebooks, to replace her freelance editing income**, and she released a book about **every four months**. A book in her second series went viral, with one BookTok video seen by more than **two million people**. It hit No. 1 on Amazon and led to a traditional deal for paperbacks, while she kept her North American ebook and audio rights. **Lesson:** frequent releases and a Canadian setting built a fan base before the viral moment.

## Common ebook publishing mistakes

- **One book and done.** Every author above credits a series or a backlist.
- **Pricing above C$12.99 on Amazon.ca** and dropping to the 35% royalty.
- **A cover that doesn't match the genre.** Readers judge books by thumbnails.
- **No email list.** Store algorithms change; your list doesn't.
- **Skipping editing.** Early reviews mentioning typos stick for years.
- **Buying ads for book one** before you have read-through to later books.
- **Using images or text you don't have rights to**, including AI output you can't verify.
- **Assuming ebooks get the book rebate.** The CRA's [GI-065](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-065/point-sale-rebate-on-books.html) lists electronic and digital books among items that don't qualify.

## Which tax rules apply to ebook sales in Canada?

Royalties and direct sales are self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net profit (QPP in Quebec).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

- **GST/HST registration:** required once worldwide taxable supplies from all your businesses pass **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). The [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule doesn't apply.
- **Stores and platforms:** the CRA's [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) describes GST/HST rules since July 1, 2021 for platforms that facilitate digital sales to Canadians. Direct sales from your own Payhip store are different: once registered, you charge tax yourself.
- **No book rebate on ebooks:** GI-065, dated August 2014, lists electronic and digital books among goods that don't qualify for the point-of-sale rebate on printed books; its rate table is out of date, so use the current [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Rates once registered:** 13% HST in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia (since April 1, 2025), 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I., 5% GST elsewhere plus QST in Quebec.
- **U.S.-dollar royalties:** record them in Canadian dollars using a consistent rate such as the [Bank of Canada's](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/).
- **Deductions:** editing, cover design, formatting software, ISBN-related costs (ISBNs themselves are free), ads, a share of home office and internet.

Keep a ledger per payout, separate from personal spending; <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> or a separate bank account works, and <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can track expenses.

## Provincial notes

- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP; QST applies once registered. French-language editions open a separate market served by Quebec bookstores and libraries.
- **Ontario and Atlantic Canada:** HST of 13%–15% once registered and selling directly.
- **Arts funding:** some provinces and the Canada Council for the Arts fund writers; grants appear in the Writers' Union survey as a source of writing income. Check your provincial arts council's rules on whether self-published work is eligible.
