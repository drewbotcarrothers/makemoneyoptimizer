## What does it cost to start rideshare driving in Canada?

Rideshare driving in Canada can start from about $150–$350 when the car already passes, about $400–$900 once a dash cam and small inspection repairs are included, or about $800–$2,500 or more with winter tires, a written insurance quote for the province where the car is plated, and a fuel float. Those figures are everyday Canadian retail and typical local fees, to verify where you live, and they assume you already own a car that meets the age, door, and condition rules. Uber is the rideshare app described on the Canada pages linked here; other apps may operate in some cities, and the in-app checklist is the list that counts.

| How you start | Cash before the first fare, about | What that usually includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $150–$350 | A sturdy phone mount, a charger, rubber mats, a basic clean, and a safety inspection if your city or the app requires one and the car already passes. |
| Typical | $400–$900 | The lean list, a dash cam, a proper interior detail, and small repairs the inspection names (wipers, a bulb, a tire that was marginal). |
| Doing it properly | $800–$2,500 or more | Everything above, winter tires if you lack them, a written insurance quote for passenger work, and a fuel float. The insurance line is often the largest new cost. If the car fails the age rule, no bag of accessories fixes the budget. |

[Uber vehicle requirements in Canada](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/drive/requirements/vehicle-requirements/) describe a 4-door car, 10 years or newer for UberX, registered in your name, not salvaged or rebuilt, in good condition, and inspected by a licensed mechanic. Cities can be stricter. [Uber driver requirements by city](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/drive/requirements/regulatory/) says vehicles in Toronto, Mississauga, Oakville, and Brampton must be 7 years old or newer, and elsewhere in the GTA 10 years old or newer, plus age 21 and a full G licence or equivalent (G1 and G2 are not accepted), and a safety standards certificate. Read your city before you buy mats.

If the car is too old, the honest startup cost of rideshare driving is a different car, which is outside a side-hustle starter budget. Stop there.

## Who is this rideshare budget a fit for in Canada?

This rideshare driving budget in Canada fits drivers who already like a clean car, can work nights or weekends, and will show the decal and the insurance papers. Passengers are a different job from parcels. Skip the spend if you already dislike the work.

The same budget is a poor fit in a one-car household that needs the car every evening, on a vehicle you would not put a stranger in, or where the app will not accept your city or your car. Winter night driving raises both demand and crash risk.

Meals, grocery batches, and boxes are the cheaper vehicle tests: [food delivery startup costs](/side-hustles/food-delivery-startup-costs-canada/), [grocery shopping startup costs](/side-hustles/grocery-shopping-startup-costs-canada/), and [package courier startup costs](/side-hustles/package-courier-startup-costs-canada/). The GST/HST rule below is the part those jobs do not share.

## How do ongoing costs change rideshare break-even in Canada?

Ongoing costs for rideshare driving in Canada are fuel, wear, the insurance difference for the province where the car is plated, cleaning, tolls, phone data, and the GST/HST you must remit on commercial ride-sharing. Count hours online, including empty waiting. The marketing summary on an insurance page is not your policy.

**Fuel and wear per kilometre.** Use your consumption and the price you paid, plus a per-kilometre set-aside for oil, tires, and brakes. That set-aside is planning, not the tax deduction. Self-employed drivers usually claim actual expenses with a log.

**Insurance.** Often the largest monthly line once passenger use is declared. [Uber driver insurance in Canada](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/drive/insurance/) says commercial coverage through Economical applies while you are available for a trip, that waiting-period coverage can differ by province, and that your personal policy must still be in force. Ask your insurer what remains yours when the app is off, waiting, or en route to the first pickup. British Columbia ride-hailing is not an Ontario endorsement. [ICBC ride-hailing insurance](https://www.icbc.com/insurance/commercial/ride-hailing) describes company basic coverage and optional products, and this budget states no ICBC premium. Quebec licensing is the [SAAQ](https://saaq.gouv.qc.ca/en/) site in general, not a ride-hail fee. Saskatchewan and Manitoba use public auto insurers. Use the checklist for the province where the car is plated.

**Cleaning.** Car washes and a bottle of cleaner. A weekly habit might be $10–$30, a range to verify locally. A neglected car ends the shift early because riders cancel.

**Tolls, airport fees, and parking.** Only when a trip creates them. Airport queues can be an hour of unpaid standing. Local rules decide where app drivers may wait. The unpaid hour belongs in your hourly math.

**Phone data.** $0–$30 a month if you need an add-on, a range to verify locally. The app and navigation are part of the shift.

**GST/HST remittances.** For commercial passenger ride-sharing, registration is required below $30,000. You charge the tax and you remit it. Money in the account is not yours if it is tax. Details are in the Canada note.

### 4. Add the one-time startup cash

One-time startup is the mount, mats, clean, inspection, and any repair the inspection required.

### 5. Add the monthly fixed costs

Monthly fixed costs are extra insurance, extra data, and a wash habit you always pay.

### 6. Subtract variable cost from the fare screen

Variable cost of one shift is fuel, a wear set-aside, tolls, parking, and the GST/HST portion of fares that you will remit if it was included in what you collected. Contribution = what you keep after variable costs, not the fare screen alone. Shifts to recover gear = startup ÷ contribution per shift. Shifts to cover the month = monthly fixed ÷ contribution per shift. Count hours online, including empty waiting.

**Illustrative example.** The sketch below is the break-even arithmetic for this guide. The figures are assumed. They are not a city average and not a promise.

**Round numbers for arithmetic only. Not a city average and not a promise.**

Inspection $140. Mats and mount $70. You already had winter tires. Startup = $210. Insurance endorsement in this sketch is $120 a month. Data add-on $0.

One Saturday the screen shows $180. You drove 120 km. At 8 L/100 km and $1.60/L, fuel is 120 × 0.08 × 1.60 = $15.36. Wear at $0.12/km is $14.40. A $15 wash is in this shift. Variable costs before tax = $44.76.

This sketch uses 5% GST, as in Alberta, and treats $180 as GST-included. The GST slice is about $180 × 5/105 = $8.57, which you do not spend. Contribution = $180 − $44.76 − $8.57 = $126.67. Five hours online, including an airport wait, is about $25 an hour in this sketch, before the $120 insurance.

Insurance takes about one of these Saturdays. The $210 of gear takes about two more. A 13% or 15% HST province changes the tax slice. Replace the inputs. Do not budget rent on this paragraph.

## How do taxes and vehicle claims work for rideshare driving in Canada?

Rideshare driving income in Canada is self-employment income, GST/HST applies from the first commercial ride-sharing fare, and the vehicle claim is the business portion supported by a kilometre log. This guide is general information, not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Use the checklist for the province where the car is plated.

Keep summaries, receipts, and a kilometre log: date, where you went, kilometres, and why. Business use is business kilometres divided by total kilometres. Apply that percentage to fuel, insurance, maintenance, and to any [capital cost allowance (CCA)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the car. You generally pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. The usual form with your T1 is [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). A T4A does not replace the log. You cannot deduct a personal vehicle in full, or the whole purchase in year one. CCA spreads a lasting asset over time, on the business portion, in the class the CRA assigns. Rates change. Look them up for the year you file.

**GST/HST from the first fare.** The CRA says self-employed taxi operators and commercial ride-sharing drivers must register for GST/HST even if they are small suppliers, and must charge GST/HST on those fares. Registration is effective when you start supplying the rides. The sources are [GST/HST for taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) and [GI-196](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-196/gst-hst-commercial-ride-sharing-services.html). Commercial ride-sharing, in that sheet, is passenger transportation arranged with an electronic platform. Food and parcel delivery follow the small-supplier test on the CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page.

GI-196 also says that if you are otherwise a small supplier, the mandatory registration can apply only to the ride-sharing services. You do not automatically charge GST/HST on unrelated side work, and you do not claim input tax credits on that other work, unless you ask to register more broadly or your combined taxable supplies exceed $30,000. The CRA’s own example: $20,000 of ride-sharing plus $15,000 of other taxable supplies is $35,000, you are no longer a small supplier, and you charge tax on all of it.

**Input tax credits.** On the ride-sharing work you are registered for, GST/HST paid on expenses for that commercial activity can often be recovered as an input tax credit. Receipts need to show the tax. Leave ITCs off unrelated hustles if your registration is the ride-sharing-only kind described above.

Charge the provincial rate. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST beside GST. Quebec charges GST and QST; confirm the current QST rate with Revenu Québec. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 ([Charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)). Confirm on that table or the CRA [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and move the tax out of spending money the week you collect it.

Vehicle age, inspections, and municipal licences are local. Winter-tire dates are provincial. Uber’s GTA page is one city’s list, not a national average. Refresh the age caps, the insurance summary, and the tax rates when you next update this budget.
