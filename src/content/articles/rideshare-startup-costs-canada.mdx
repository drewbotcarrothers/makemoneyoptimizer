## Who is this rideshare budget a fit for in Canada?

This budget fits drivers who already like a clean car, can work nights or weekends, and will show the decal and the insurance papers. The lean kit fits a two-week test in a car that already passes. The standard kit fits someone driving most weekends who wants a record of every trip. The winter-ready kit fits regular shifts from November to April.

It's a poor fit in a one-car household that needs the car every evening, on a vehicle you wouldn't put a stranger in, or where the app won't accept your city or your car. Winter night driving raises both demand and crash risk.

Meals, grocery batches and boxes are the cheaper vehicle tests: [food delivery startup costs](/side-hustles/food-delivery-startup-costs-canada/), [grocery shopping startup costs](/side-hustles/grocery-shopping-startup-costs-canada/) and [package courier startup costs](/side-hustles/package-courier-startup-costs-canada/). ## What do insurance and licences cost a rideshare driver in Canada?

**Insurance.** [Uber's insurance page](https://www.uber.com/ca/en/drive/insurance/), read October 9, 2026, says it maintains a commercial auto policy issued by Economical, and that your personal policy must be in force to drive. Coverage differs by province:

| Province | What Uber's policy provides while you're on the app |
| --- | --- |
| Ontario | Waiting for a request: $1 million third-party liability, standard accident benefits, contingent collision and comprehensive ($2,500 deductible). Request accepted to drop-off: $2 million third-party liability on the same terms. |
| Alberta, Nova Scotia, Newfoundland and Labrador | Your personal policy applies first while waiting, with $1 million back-up; $2 million from acceptance to drop-off. |
| Quebec | $1 million civil liability in all on-app periods. |
| British Columbia | Your own ICBC policy applies until you accept a request; then a $2 million Uber policy with contingent physical damage ($2,500 deductible). See [ICBC ride-hailing insurance](https://www.icbc.com/insurance/commercial/ride-hailing). |
| Saskatchewan | Uber pays SGI so basic plate insurance doesn't exclude ridesharing, plus $2 million of liability above SGI's $200,000. |

Contingent collision and comprehensive only applies if you carry that coverage on your own policy. Uber also says you're responsible for telling your broker or insurer that you rideshare. This guide doesn't publish an endorsement price because insurers quote individually. This is not insurance advice.

**Licences.** In Toronto, the City doesn't issue PTC driver licences directly to individuals. The [app applies on your behalf](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/private-transportation-companies-uberfacedrive-drivers/drivers-for-private-transportation-companies/), shares your criminal reference check and driver's record with the City, and pays $18.32 per driver plus an $8.65 Accessibility Fund fee each year. Other cities set their own licences and fees; check your city's page.

**Correcting a vague line on this page.** An earlier version of this guide listed a "city fee" for registration without saying who pays it. For Uber and Lyft drivers in Toronto, the City's page says the company submits the PTC licence fee, so it isn't on your card. Taxi and limousine driver licences are different and cost the driver $233.78 in 2026, per the City's [vehicle-for-hire driver page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/limousine-service-company-owner-driver/taxicab-or-limousine-driver-vehicle-for-hire-driver/).

## How do ongoing costs change rideshare break-even in Canada?

**Insurance.** Often the largest monthly line once passenger use is declared. Ask your insurer what remains yours when the app is off, waiting, or en route to the first pickup.

**Fuel and wear.** Fuel is your measured litres per 100 km times today's price times kilometres, including empty kilometres. Wear is a set-aside per kilometre for tires, brakes and depreciation. Self-employed drivers claim actual expenses on the business-use share with a log.

**Cleaning.** Car washes and cleaner. A neglected car ends the shift early.

**Cash-out fees.** Lyft's Express Pay in Canada is $1.25 a transfer; weekly payouts are free. Uber offers weekly pay, daily Flex Pay and Instant Pay.

**Tolls, airport fees and parking.** Only when a trip creates them. Airport queues can be an hour of unpaid waiting.

**GST/HST remittances.** You charge the tax and you remit it. Money in the account isn't yours if it's tax.

### A Saturday sketch

**Round numbers for arithmetic only. Not a city average and not a promise.**

Suppose you buy the standard kit for $339.26, and the inspection costs an assumed $140 with no repairs, for a startup of $479.26. You already had winter tires. Suppose the insurance endorsement in this sketch is $120 a month, an assumption, not a quote.

One Saturday the screen shows $180. You drove 120 km. At 8 L/100 km and an assumed $1.60/L, fuel is 120 × 0.08 × 1.60 = $15.36. Wear at $0.12/km is $14.40. A $15 wash is in this shift. Variable costs before tax = $44.76.

This sketch uses 5% GST, as in Alberta, and treats $180 as GST-included. The GST slice is about $180 × 5/105 = $8.57, which you don't spend. Contribution = $180 − $44.76 − $8.57 = $126.67. Five hours online, including an airport wait, is about $25.33 an hour before the $120 insurance.

Insurance takes about one of these Saturdays a month. The $479.26 startup takes about four more ($479.26 ÷ $126.67 = 3.8). A 13% or 15% HST province changes the tax slice. Replace the inputs. Don't budget rent on this paragraph.

## Where can a rideshare budget in Canada stay smaller?

- **Pass the inspection in the car you own** before you buy accessories.
- **Start with the lean kit.** The dash cam can wait until you've driven enough to want the record.
- **Wash the car yourself** until riders mention it.
- **Take the free weekly payout** instead of paying per cash-out.
- **Claim input tax credits** on gear bought for the registered ride-sharing activity, with receipts that show the tax.

Don't skip the insurer conversation, the tires your city requires, or the mechanic's repairs. A claim denied because passenger use wasn't declared costs more than any endorsement. ## How do taxes and vehicle claims work for rideshare driving in Canada?

Rideshare driving income in Canada is self-employment income, GST/HST applies from the first commercial ride-sharing fare, and the vehicle claim is the business portion supported by a kilometre log. This guide is general information, not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Use the checklist for the province where the car is plated.

Keep summaries, receipts, and a kilometre log: date, where you went, kilometres, and why. Business use is business kilometres divided by total kilometres. Apply that percentage to fuel, insurance, maintenance, and to any [capital cost allowance (CCA)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the car. You generally pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. The usual form with your T1 is [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). A T4A does not replace the log. You cannot deduct a personal vehicle in full, or the whole purchase in year one. CCA spreads a lasting asset over time, on the business portion, in the class the CRA assigns. Rates change. Look them up for the year you file.

**GST/HST from the first fare.** The CRA says self-employed taxi operators and commercial ride-sharing drivers must register for GST/HST even if they are small suppliers, and must charge GST/HST on those fares. Registration is effective when you start supplying the rides. The sources are [GST/HST for taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) and [GI-196](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-196/gst-hst-commercial-ride-sharing-services.html). Commercial ride-sharing, in that sheet, is passenger transportation arranged with an electronic platform. Food and parcel delivery follow the small-supplier test on the CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page.

GI-196 also says that if you are otherwise a small supplier, the mandatory registration can apply only to the ride-sharing services. You do not automatically charge GST/HST on unrelated side work, and you do not claim input tax credits on that other work, unless you ask to register more broadly or your combined taxable supplies exceed $30,000. The CRA’s own example: $20,000 of ride-sharing plus $15,000 of other taxable supplies is $35,000, you are no longer a small supplier, and you charge tax on all of it.

**Input tax credits.** On the ride-sharing work you are registered for, GST/HST paid on expenses for that commercial activity can often be recovered as an input tax credit. Receipts need to show the tax. Leave ITCs off unrelated hustles if your registration is the ride-sharing-only kind described above.

Charge the provincial rate. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST beside GST. Quebec charges GST and QST; confirm the current QST rate with Revenu Québec. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 ([Charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)). Confirm on that table or the CRA [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and move the tax out of spending money the week you collect it.

Vehicle age, inspections, and municipal licences are local. Winter-tire dates are provincial. Uber’s GTA page is one city’s list, not a national average. Refresh the age caps, the insurance summary, and the tax rates when you next update this budget.
