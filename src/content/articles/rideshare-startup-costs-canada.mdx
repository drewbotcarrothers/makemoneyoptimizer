After this budget you can tell whether your car is even allowed to carry passengers, what to ask an insurer, and how many fares it takes to earn back an inspection and a set of mats — without treating a Saturday screenshot as take-home pay.

How the signup, city rules, and first week actually work is the [rideshare driving guide](/side-hustles/rideshare-driving-side-hustle-canada). This page prices the start. The two decisions that move the number are the vehicle itself and the insurance. Floor mats are not the decision.

## The quick answer

Ranges below are everyday Canadian retail and typical local fees, to verify where you live. They assume you already own a car that meets the age, door, and condition rules in your city. They are not a quote and they do not include a car loan.

| How you start | Cash before the first fare, about | What that usually includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $150–$350 | A sturdy phone mount, a charger, rubber mats, a basic clean, and a safety inspection if your city or the app requires one and the car already passes. |
| Typical | $400–$900 | The lean list, a dash cam, a proper interior detail, and small repairs the inspection names (wipers, a bulb, a tire that was marginal). |
| Doing it properly | $800–$2,500 or more | Everything above, winter tires if you lack them, a written insurance quote for passenger work, and a fuel float. The insurance line is often the largest new cost. If the car fails the age rule, no bag of accessories fixes the budget. |

Uber is the rideshare app in the most Canadian cities. Lyft exists in some markets and not others. The in-app list is the one that counts. Uber’s Canada pages describe a four-door car, about 10 model years or newer for UberX, in your name, not salvaged, and inspected. Cities can be stricter. Uber’s Toronto-area page has used a seven-year cap in Toronto, Mississauga, Oakville, and Brampton, and ten years elsewhere in the GTA, plus a full G licence and age 21. Read your city before you buy mats.

If the car is too old, the honest startup cost is “a different car,” which is not a side-hustle starter budget. Stop there.

## Who this budget fits

**It fits** drivers who already like a clean car, can work nights or weekends, and will show the decal and the insurance papers without improvising. Passengers are not parcels. Do not spend this money to enter a job you already dislike.

**It is a poor fit** in a one-car household that needs the car every evening, on a vehicle you would not put a stranger in, or where the app will not accept your city or your car. Winter night driving raises both demand and crash risk.

If you only wanted to move meals or boxes, the cheaper vehicle tests are [food delivery startup costs](/side-hustles/food-delivery-startup-costs-canada), [grocery shopping startup costs](/side-hustles/grocery-shopping-startup-costs-canada), and [package courier startup costs](/side-hustles/package-courier-startup-costs-canada). The GST/HST rule below is the part those jobs do not share.

## Ongoing costs, and a simple break-even

**Fuel and wear per kilometre.** Use your consumption and the price you paid, plus a per-kilometre set-aside for oil, tires, and brakes. That set-aside is planning, not the tax deduction. Self-employed drivers usually claim actual expenses with a log.

**Insurance.** Often the largest monthly line once passenger use is declared. Read the platform’s current Canadian insurance page for your province, then ask your insurer what remains yours when the app is off, waiting, or en route to the first pickup. The marketing summary is not your policy.

**Cleaning.** Car washes and a bottle of cleaner. A weekly habit might be $10–$30. A neglected car ends the shift early because riders cancel.

**Tolls, airport fees, and parking.** Only when a trip creates them. Airport queues can be an hour of unpaid standing. Local rules decide where app drivers may wait. The unpaid hour belongs in your hourly math.

**Phone data.** $0–$30 a month if you need an add-on. The app and navigation are not optional.

**GST/HST remittances.** For commercial passenger ride-sharing, registration is not optional below $30,000. You charge the tax and you remit it. Money in the account is not yours if it is tax. Details are in the Canada note.

### How to calculate break-even

1. One-time startup: mount, mats, clean, inspection, and any repair the inspection required.
2. Monthly fixed: extra insurance, extra data, a wash habit you always pay.
3. Variable cost of one shift: fuel, wear set-aside, tolls, parking, and the GST/HST portion of fares that you will remit if it was included in what you collected.

Contribution = what you keep after variable costs, not the fare screen alone.

Shifts to recover gear = startup ÷ contribution per shift.

Shifts to cover the month = monthly fixed ÷ contribution per shift. Count hours online, including empty waiting.

### Illustrative example

**Round numbers for arithmetic only. Not a city average and not a promise.**

Inspection $140. Mats and mount $70. You already had winter tires. Startup = $210. Insurance endorsement in this sketch is $120 a month. Data add-on $0.

One Saturday the screen shows $180. You drove 120 km. At 8 L/100 km and $1.60/L, fuel is 120 × 0.08 × 1.60 = $15.36. Wear at $0.12/km is $14.40. A $15 wash is in this shift. Variable costs before tax = $44.76.

This sketch uses 5% GST, as in Alberta, and treats $180 as GST-included. The GST slice is about $180 × 5/105 = $8.57, which you do not spend. Contribution = $180 − $44.76 − $8.57 = $126.67. Five hours online, including an airport wait, is about $25 an hour in this sketch, before the $120 insurance.

Insurance takes about one of these Saturdays. The $210 of gear takes about two more. A 13% or 15% HST province changes the tax slice. Replace the inputs. Do not budget rent on this paragraph.

## Where you can spend less

Pass the inspection in the car you already own before you buy accessories. Wash the car yourself until riders mention the smell. Skip the cabin camera until you have read your province’s recording rules and the app’s camera policy.

Do not finance a car on someone else’s weekend. Measure two weeks, subtract fuel, wear, insurance, and tax you must remit, then look at a payment. The app pays you, so skip invoicing software.

## Where not to cut corners

Do not drive passengers on a personal policy you have not shown the insurer. Do not skip winter tires where they are required, or on nights you already find frightening. Do not skip GST/HST registration because the fare looks smaller once tax is removed. Brakes, tires, and a phone mount fail with a person in the back seat.

## Canada note

This is general information, not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Fares are self-employment income. Keep summaries, receipts, and a kilometre log: date, where you went, kilometres, and why. Business use is business kilometres divided by total kilometres. Apply that percentage to fuel, insurance, maintenance, and to any capital cost allowance (CCA) on the car. You generally pay both shares of CPP on the net. The usual form with your T1 is T2125. A T4A does not replace the log. You cannot deduct a personal vehicle in full, or the whole purchase in year one. CCA spreads a lasting asset over time, on the business portion, in the class the CRA assigns. Rates change. Look them up for the year you file.

**GST/HST from the first fare.** The CRA says self-employed taxi operators and commercial ride-sharing drivers must register for GST/HST even if they are small suppliers, and must charge GST/HST on those fares. Registration is effective when you start supplying the rides, not when you cross $30,000. The sources are the CRA’s page for [taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) and info sheet [GI-196](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-196/gst-hst-commercial-ride-sharing-services.html). Commercial ride-sharing, in that sheet, is passenger transportation arranged with an electronic platform. Food and parcel delivery are not that rule.

GI-196 also says that if you are otherwise a small supplier, the mandatory registration can apply only to the ride-sharing services. You do not automatically charge GST/HST on unrelated side work, and you do not claim input tax credits on that other work, unless you ask to register more broadly or your combined taxable supplies exceed $30,000. The CRA’s own example: $20,000 of ride-sharing plus $15,000 of other taxable supplies is $35,000, you are no longer a small supplier, and you charge tax on all of it.

**Input tax credits.** On the ride-sharing work you are registered for, GST/HST paid on expenses for that commercial activity can often be recovered as an input tax credit. Receipts need to show the tax. Do not claim ITCs on unrelated hustles if your registration is the ride-sharing-only kind described above.

Charge the provincial rate. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST beside GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025. Confirm on the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and move the tax out of spending money the week you collect it.

Vehicle age, inspections, and municipal licences are local. Winter-tire dates are provincial. Use the page for your city and your province, not a national average.
