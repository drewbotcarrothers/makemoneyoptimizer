A Shopify product price in Canada has to cover the product, packaging, the shipping you absorb, the card fee and your ads, then leave a margin. On October 9, 2026, Shopify's Canadian Basic plan cost $49 a month (or $37 billed yearly) with online card rates from 2.8% + 30¢. Canadian brands selling online set the market you're compared with: Frank And Oak's Essential Slim Tee was $39, tentree's graphic tees $45, Province of Canada's Canada Tee $58 and Duer's Host City Tee $69. Shipping is the cost that sinks small orders: Chit Chats listed a 142 g T-shirt from Toronto to a Toronto address at $5.33, against $15.29 with a national carrier. A $39 tee costing $13 to make and pack leaves about $19 before ads on Basic.

## Which pricing approaches work for a Shopify store?

Price every product from cost first, then check it against the Canadian anchors above.

| Approach | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost-plus to a target margin | Price = (costs + 30¢) ÷ (1 − card % − target margin) | Every product, as a floor | Leaving out shipping or ads |
| Competitor-based | Price near similar Canadian products | Tees, mugs, candles | Matching a brand with lower unit costs |
| Value-based | Price on design, story or scarcity | Handmade or limited goods | Needs strong photos and copy |
| Free shipping built in | Shipping folded into the product price | Light items under 500 g | Heavy items and far destinations |
| Free-shipping threshold | Free over a basket size, flat fee under | Stores with several low-price items | Threshold set below your break-even |
| Bundles | Two or three items at one price | Raising order value | Bundles below your margin floor |

Markup and margin aren't the same. Markup is profit as a share of cost, margin is profit as a share of price. A $12 product sold for $24 has a 100% markup but a 50% gross margin.

## How do you calculate a Shopify product price?

Add every cost per order, choose a target margin and solve for price.

```text
Costs per order = product + packaging + shipping you absorb + ad cost per order
Price           = (costs per order + $0.30) ÷ (1 − card rate − target margin)
Profit          = price − costs per order − (card rate × price + $0.30)
Orders to cover = monthly plan + apps ÷ profit per order
```

1. **Cost the product.** Use your supplier invoice or materials list, not a guess.
2. **Add packaging and postage.** Weigh the packed parcel and price it with your carrier.
3. **Add ad cost per order.** Ad spend for the month ÷ orders the ads brought in.
4. **Pick a margin.** The examples below use about 50% before ads, which leaves room for ads, discounts and returns.
5. **Check the anchors.** If the formula says $28 and Canadian brands sell the same thing at $45, you can charge more. If it says $80 against $45, change the product or the supplier.

**Illustrative:** a $12 tee, $1 mailer and $5.33 postage cost $18.33. For a 50% margin on Basic: ($18.33 + $0.30) ÷ (1 − 0.028 − 0.50) = $18.63 ÷ 0.472 = $39.47. Round to $39, Frank And Oak's slim tee price.

## What do worked Shopify pricing examples look like?

**Illustrative.** Product and packaging costs and ad spend are assumed. Shopify rates, shipping rates and the market prices are published.

### Example 1: Hand-poured candle shipped to Vancouver

- Costs: wax, wick, jar and fragrance $9; box and padding $1.50; 425 g to Vancouver $6.07 (Chit Chats' mug rate). Total $16.57.
- Price $38 with free shipping. Card fee 2.8% × $38 + $0.30 = $1.36.
- Profit $38 − $16.57 − $1.36 = **$20.07**, a 52.8% margin.
- Market check: Province of Canada's candles are $48, so $38 leaves room for a 10% launch discount.

### Example 2: Printed tee to a Toronto customer, with ads

- Costs: blank and printing $12; mailer $1; 142 g to Toronto $5.33. Total $18.33.
- Price $39. Card fee $1.39. Profit before ads **$19.28**, a 49.4% margin.
- With $8 of ad spend per order, profit falls to **$11.28**.
- On Basic billed yearly ($37), 2 orders a month cover the plan before ads: $37 ÷ $19.28 = 1.92.

### Example 3: Same tee to a US customer paying by Amex

- Costs: $12 + $1 + $8.60 to New York (Chit Chats, CUSMA-approved) = $21.60.
- Price $39 with free shipping. Card fee at the international rate: 3.5% × $39 + $0.30 = $1.67.
- Profit **$15.73**, a 40.3% margin, $3.55 less than the Toronto order. Charge US shipping or set a higher US price if most orders go south.

### Example 4: When Grow pays for itself

- Grow costs $132 a month versus $49 for Basic, $83 more, and saves 0.2 points on online card sales (2.6% versus 2.8%).
- Break-even: $83 ÷ 0.002 = **$41,500 a month** in online card sales on monthly billing.
- On yearly billing the gap is $99 − $37 = $62, so $62 ÷ 0.002 = **$31,000 a month**.
- Below that, stay on Basic unless you need Grow's features.

A costing card for each product:

```text

Supplier cost per unit ......................... $[12.00]
Packaging ...................................... $[1.00]
Postage (weight [142] g, zone [ON]) ............ $[5.33]
Ad cost per order (last 30 days) ............... $[8.00]
```

## How do you raise prices on a Shopify store?

Raise prices when supplier or shipping costs rise, when ad cost per order climbs, or when a product sells out at its current price.

1. **Re-run the costing card** every time a supplier, carrier or Shopify rate changes.
2. **Change one product at a time** and compare profit per week, not order count.
3. **Use the free-shipping threshold.** Set it just above your best-seller's price, as Frank And Oak does at $99 with $10 shipping below it.
4. **Bundle instead of discounting.** Two tees at $72 in one parcel share a single postage charge, while 20% off one $39 tee cuts its $19.28 profit to $11.70.
5. **Tell your email list first:**

```text
Subject: Prices change on [date]
Hi [first name], on [date] our tees move from $[39] to $[42]
because shipping and blank costs went up this year. Anything
ordered before then ships at today's price. — [Your name]
```

The [guide to raising side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/) covers timing and wording for repeat customers.

## What Shopify pricing mistakes are common?

- **Confusing markup with margin.** A 50% markup is a 33% margin.
- **Free shipping on everything.** A $15.29 carrier rate erases most of the profit on a $25 item.
- **Ignoring Amex and international orders.** They cost 0.7 points more on Basic.
- **Upgrading too early.** Grow only saves money above about $31,000 a month in card sales.
- **Pricing far below Canadian brands.** A $25 tee next to $39 to $70 anchors looks like lower quality, not a deal.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect Shopify pricing?

Shopify store profit is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both [CPP shares](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net income. GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

Once registered, you charge tax at the rate for where the customer is, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). Decide before you register whether your displayed prices include tax. Set aside income tax as you go with the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/), and see the [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) before you pass the threshold.
