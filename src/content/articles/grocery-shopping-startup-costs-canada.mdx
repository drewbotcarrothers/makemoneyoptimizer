## Who should use a grocery shopping startup budget in Canada?

The grocery shopping startup budget in Canada fits organized shoppers who can lift, text a stranger about a substitution, and work when other people shop: weekday evenings, Saturday mornings, and the day before a holiday. The lean kit fits a hatchback that is already empty and orders that go to houses with driveways. The standard kit fits a regular schedule with cases of water. The walk-up-ready kit fits apartment-heavy zones.

Grocery shopping is a poor fit when you cannot lift, when downtown meters will eat every batch, or when the only car is full of child seats.

A long order with stairs is a body problem and a parking problem. Restaurant bags are a different kit. So are pre-packed parcels. ## How do pay floors change the break-even?

Ontario and B.C. now set a minimum for app work time. Ontario requires at least the general minimum wage, $17.95 an hour from October 1, 2026, for time spent on an assignment, with tips not counted, per [Ontario's digital platform workers page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers). B.C. requires $21.89 an hour of engaged time plus $0.35 per km for delivery by any means other than walking, from June 1, 2026, per [B.C.'s minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage). Waiting for a batch isn't engaged time in either province.

## What are the ongoing costs?

Ongoing grocery shopping costs in Canada are fuel, wear, parking, bag replacements, the phone plan, cash-out fees and any insurance change. Parking is the line shoppers forget.

**Fuel and wear.** Use your consumption, today's price, and the kilometres from home to store to door to home. A cents-per-kilometre set-aside for tires and oil is a planning number, not the tax deduction. A log of actual expenses is what a vehicle claim usually needs.

**Parking.** Some stores do not validate. Downtown meters and parkades are a cost centre. Write the amount on the batch.

**Cash-out fees.** $0.50 per Instacart instant cash-out or $1.49 per Uber Instant Pay adds up if you cash out daily. The weekly deposit is free.

**Bags you replace.** A torn insulated bag is a $28.99-to-$37.99 repeat purchase. Do not replace it weekly.

**Insurance.** A monthly endorsement or a higher premium is a fixed cost. If your insurer confirms the current policy covers delivery, write that confirmation down. If the quote is painful, the gear budget was never the decision.

**Unpaid time.** Out-of-stock substitutions, a gate code that does not work, a customer who wants the shop redone in the chat. Log hours from arrival at the store.

Pay batches into an account you do not use for rent, so the week's fuel stays visible. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian spending account for that split.

## Where can grocery shopping startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Start with the lean kit** and one real insulated bag. Skip a matching set of six.
- **Borrow a cart or hand truck** for a week before buying one. Houses with driveways don't need it.
- **Shop stores you already know.** Unpaid time in a strange superstore costs more than a better bag.
- **Cluster batches** from the same store. Dead kilometres are the leak.
- **Cash out weekly.** The fee is small; the habit isn't.

## Where should grocery shopping spending not be cut in Canada?

Do not skip an insulated bag for frozen food, the insurer, or the lift limit. Delivering frozen food in an open tote to save $28.99 is how a complaint costs the batch and the rating.

The car is still being used for pay when the cargo is groceries. This is not insurance advice. Ask before the first batch. Instacart's public Canadian standard is a lift of up to 40 pounds (18 kg). A back injury is not a line you can shop around. That's why the hand truck is in the standard kit, not the pro one.

## Which tax rules apply to a grocery shopping budget in Canada?

Grocery shopping income in Canada is generally self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole Canada Pension Plan contribution, both the employee and employer portions, as described under [Contributions to the Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). Bags and chargers are ordinary expenses, while a vehicle is [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep batch summaries and receipts. An app may send a T4A. You still report the income if the slip is missing.

**Vehicle log and CCA.** Claim the car only for the business portion. The log is the date, the destination, the kilometres, and the reason. Business kilometres divided by total kilometres is the percentage you apply to fuel, insurance, and maintenance. Do not deduct the purchase of a car in full because you started grocery shopping apps.

**GST/HST.** Grocery shopping and delivery are not commercial ride-sharing. The first-dollar GST/HST rule is for [taxi and passenger ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html). Ordinary shopping services use the [small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in one calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and your other hustles. If you also carry passengers, read [GST/HST and commercial ride-sharing services (GI-196)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-196/gst-hst-commercial-ride-sharing-services.html).

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases such as bags and fuel can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, if you have the receipts. If you are not registered, you do not charge the tax and you do not claim the credit.

Confirm the rate for the province, including Quebec, with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Search your city for a business licence rather than assuming couriers are exempt everywhere.
