## What offer gets a first junk removal client in Canada?

A first junk removal client in Canada usually buys removal of a few named items, with labour priced separately from the tipping fee. "We take anything" is hard to survive. "A dresser and six boxes, no paint, no fridge, fee at the gate passed through" is a purchase.

Look at the pile before you price it. Commercial loads are often priced differently from a resident's carload. This guide does not state a tipping fee. The number is whatever that facility posts that day.

[Product Care's paint page](https://www.productcare.org/products/paint/) describes free drop-off at listed sites in British Columbia, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, Nova Scotia, and Newfoundland and Labrador. Alberta, Quebec, and the territories are not on that list. If your province is missing, use the city's household hazardous waste page. Do not mix paint into a landfill load the site refuses.

## What should a first junk removal note say?

A first junk removal note in Canada should name the items, the labour price, the fact that the tipping fee is extra and posted, and what you will leave behind.

**Example message.** This is a generic example you can adapt. It is not a note that produced a client.

> Hello. I can take the dresser and the boxes I saw, in one trip, in a vehicle I already drive. Labour is a number we write down before I load. The facility's fee that day is a separate line, and I confirm it that morning. I do not take paint, propane, or a fridge on this visit. If the pile is larger when I arrive, we stop and reprice. Does that match what you need hauled?

Do not promise a price that includes a dump fee you have not opened. A resident flyer and a commercial rate are often different. Batteries, propane, electronics, tires, and appliances that contain refrigerant often have their own programs. Read the facility's banned list before you say yes. A mattress fee or an appliance rule you discover at the gate is a second conversation, not a surprise you absorb to be polite. If the item is still usable, a reuse centre is a different stop from the landfill, and only if that centre will take it that day.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first junk removal client look like?

A 14-day plan for a first junk removal client in Canada should produce a refuse-list, a facility page you have opened, and a few offers on piles you have seen. A paid load is a bonus. Silence is not a reason to buy a trailer.

### Days 1 and 2: Open the facility page

Note hours, what it refuses, and whether a business load is priced differently. Write paint, propane, and fridges on your no-list until you have a legal second stop.

### Days 3 to 5: Fit the vehicle

Gloves, straps, a tarp. Decide what actually fits. Ask for a liability quote that mentions entering homes. This is not insurance advice.

### Days 6 to 9: Look at real piles

Five conversations with people who can show you the items. If you email, follow the consent page. Quote labour only after you see the pile, and leave the tipping fee as "posted that day."

### Days 10 to 14: Haul what you named, or wait

If someone books, confirm the fee that morning, keep the lines separate, and do not add the paint. If nobody books, do not widen the no-list to sound busier.

## What mistakes block a first junk removal client in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first junk removal client in Canada are quoting a dump fee from another city, taking hazardous items to look helpful, and pricing a basement from a doorway photo. One visible load is enough.

Leaving a pile in a ravine or a stranger's bin is not a business model. A commercial rate you did not read becomes your loss. A purchased email list skips anti-spam consent.

Days 15 to 30 turn the first 14 days into a 30-day plan: one more round of asks, one finished job or a clear reason there was none, and numbers you can review on day 30.

After the first load, invoice the same day with a photo of the cleared space. Ask whether a neighbour or family member has a garage or basement to clear. Offer the same pickup to five new people.

## Which tax rules apply to a first junk removal client in Canada?

Junk removal income in Canada is self-employment income, including labour you keep, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. A tipping fee you pay and are repaid needs a receipt on both sides. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.
