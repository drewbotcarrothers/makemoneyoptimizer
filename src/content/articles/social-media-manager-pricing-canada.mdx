Charging as a social media manager in Canada usually means a monthly retainer priced by the number of platforms, posts per month, and community management hours, with setup audits, content shoots, and ad management quoted separately and ad spend paid by the client. Job Bank lists employed social media specialists at $20.50 (low), $35.58 (median), and $57.44 (high) an hour nationally. A freelancer prices above that employee wage because tools, unpaid pitching, reporting, and both halves of CPP come out of the same rate. ## Which pricing models work for social media management in Canada?

Social media management in Canada is usually sold as a monthly retainer by platform and posting volume, with one-off setup or audit projects, hourly content work, and ad management priced separately from ad spend.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly retainer | Platforms × posts per month + community hours + a report | Small businesses wanting consistency | Extra requests outside the package |
| Setup or audit project | Fixed price to set up profiles, a content plan, and templates | New clients | Turning setup into free ongoing work |
| Content creation add-on | Shoots, short video editing, graphics | Clients without their own content | Underpricing video |
| Ad management fee | Management fee separate from the ad budget | Clients running paid campaigns | Paying ad spend on your card |
| Hourly | Rate × hours | Ad hoc help, training | Open-ended scope |

Write how many posts, which platforms, response times for comments, how many revision rounds on content, and that the client pays ad spend directly to the platform.

## How do you calculate a social media management rate in Canada?

Calculate a social media management rate in Canada from a target hourly pay, your billable share, tool costs, and the hours a client's month takes (planning, creating, scheduling, engaging, reporting). Then build packages from those hours.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

The Job Bank table is your floor check.

### Step 2: Estimate billable share

Pitching, your own marketing, and learning platform changes are unpaid.

### Step 3: Add tools

Scheduling, design, and analytics tools, divided across billable hours or billed to the client.

### Step 4: Estimate monthly hours per client

Plan, create, schedule, engage, and report. Track a real month before locking a price.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $40 an hour, between Job Bank's national median of $35.58 and high of $57.44. Suppose 70% of your working time is billable. The billable rate is $40 ÷ 0.70 = $57.14, which you might round to $57 an hour.

Suppose a two-platform package with 12 posts a month takes 6 hours to plan, create, and schedule, 2.5 hours of community management, and 1.5 hours for a monthly report and call: 10 hours. The retainer is 10 × $57 = $570, which you might quote as $575 a month before tax.

Adding a third platform with 8 more posts adds about 4 hours, so 14 × $57 = $798, quoted as $800. Ad management is extra: if setting up and monitoring campaigns takes 3 hours a month, that is 3 × $57 = $171, billed separately from whatever the client spends on ads.

## When should you raise social media management prices in Canada?

Raise social media management prices in Canada when a client's scope grows past the package, when your calendar is full, or at contract renewal. Change new-client prices first and give retainer clients notice before the next term.

Track actual hours per client monthly. A client who sends daily extra requests belongs in a bigger package.

## What social media management pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common social media management pricing mistakes in Canada are unlimited posts or revisions, mixing ad spend with your fee, and promising follower numbers. Each creates work or risk you are not paid for.

Separate ad spend from your fee. Do not guarantee results. If a client pays creators or offers free products for posts, Canada's Competition Bureau says influencers should [disclose all material connections](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) with the business they promote; build that check into your process.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect social media management prices in Canada?

Social media management income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

When registered, the rate depends on the place of supply, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers registration.
