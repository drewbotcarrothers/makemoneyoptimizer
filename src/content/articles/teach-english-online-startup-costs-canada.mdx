## How much does teaching English online cost to start in Canada?

Teaching English online from Canada starts at about $0–$40 when the computer, internet, and a video tool you already have are enough, about $25–$120 when a headset is the purchase so consonants survive the call, and about $80–$250 when you add a simple backup of your materials and you have asked about insurance if a contract requires it. The gear ranges are typical Canadian retail prices to verify locally. They are not quotes. A new laptop is outside all three columns. A TESL Canada certificate, if an employer requires one, is that organization's fee on its own page, not a number this guide invents.

TESL Canada's [certification FAQ](https://tesl.ca/certification/tesl-canada-professional-certification/certification-faq) says TESL Canada professional certification is intended for ESL or EFL instructors teaching in adult learning environments, and that provincial teachers' colleges and provincial regulatory bodies govern the certification of K–12 teachers. Do not advertise a certificate you do not hold. The RCMP's page on [vulnerable sector checks](https://rcmp.ca/en/criminal-records/criminal-record-checks/vulnerable-sector-checks) says there is no federal legislation that requires any organization to conduct those checks, and that the hiring organization makes the request for a position of trust or authority over children or vulnerable persons. Prefer adult learners for the first offer.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for teaching English online in Canada?

Ongoing costs for teaching English online from Canada are the headset you replace, a platform fee you have read on that platform's page, and no-shows you did not write a rule for. Teaching break-even in the sketch is a method, not an hourly rate. A 45-minute conversation and a packaged exam course are different products.

**Platform fees.** Direct lessons have no marketplace cut. A marketplace publishes its own fee and payout currency. Read that page before you subtract a percentage you heard second-hand. This guide does not state one.

**Cancellations.** The note should say how much notice you need and what a no-show costs. A rule you invent after the learner misses the hour is a dispute.

**Prep.** The minutes you spend choosing a text are part of the hour even if the invoice says 45 minutes. Count them in the sketch.

**Unpaid promises.** An immigration outcome, a guaranteed exam score, and a child's lesson without a parent are not discounts. They are offers this budget does not include.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of Canadian lesson rates.**

Suppose the computer is already yours and a headset costs $35. Suppose the lesson is direct, so this sketch includes no marketplace fee. Suppose an adult pays an assumed $40 for a 45-minute conversation, and prep takes another 25 minutes, so the block is about 1.2 hours. That $40 is an assumption for the arithmetic, not a market rate.

One lesson: $40 − $0 platform = $40 before the headset. The headset is not earned back on one lesson. Suppose four lessons at the same assumed price: fees = $160. Remainder after the headset = $160 − $35 = $125. Four blocks at 1.2 hours are 4.8 hours, and $125 ÷ 4.8 is about $26 an hour in this sketch before a tax set-aside.

## Which tax rules apply to a teaching-English budget in Canada?

Teaching income from Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit and a marketplace payout, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) for taxable supplies, not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

Some tutoring is exempt. GST/HST Memorandum [20-6, Tutoring and Equivalent Services](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-6/tutoring-equivalent-services.html), explains that section 9 of Part III of Schedule V exempts tutoring or instructing an individual in a course approved for credit by, or that follows a curriculum designated by, a school authority, and certain equivalents and immediate prerequisites. The memorandum says that exemption does not apply to post-secondary courses. A private adult conversation is not automatically that school-credit supply. Do not tell a learner the hour is tax-free unless it meets the memorandum. This is not tax advice.

Keep the agreements and the e-transfers. Report the income on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add tutoring and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Language teaching is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST. If the supply is exempt, the memorandum is the reading, not a blog.

Once registered for taxable supplies, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set any tax you must collect aside.
