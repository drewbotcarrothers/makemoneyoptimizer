Teaching English online from Canada, meaning paid video lessons for adults who want to speak, write or test better in English, costs little in gear. What you're budgeting for is a headset so consonants survive the call, a webcam and light so learners can see your mouth for pronunciation work, insurance if a contract asks for it, and invoicing once you have direct students. The bigger cost is the share a platform keeps on each lesson. Amazon.ca prices below were checked on October 9, 2026. Platform rates are from the pillar guide, checked the same day.

How the platforms work, and how to find direct students, is the [teach English online side hustle guide](/side-hustles/teach-english-online-side-hustle-canada/). This page is the cash. A new laptop is outside every kit.

## How much does it cost to start teaching English online in Canada?

Teaching English online from Canada costs $39.55 for a USB headset, $152.52 adding a webcam, a ring light and a laptop stand, $552.52 with a starting tutor insurance policy, or $1,012.42 with a second monitor and a year of invoicing software. Goods and software include 13% Ontario HST. Insurance is added without HST. Use your province's rate.

| Kit | What's in it | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | Logitech H390 USB headset ($35.00) + the computer, internet and free video tool you already have | $39.55 with HST |
| Standard | Lean + Logitech Brio 100 1080p webcam ($52.99) + NEEWER Basics BR60 5-inch ring light ($26.99) + ivoler aluminum laptop stand ($19.99) | $152.52 with HST |
| Standard with insurance | Standard + professional liability at Zensurance's starting tutor estimate (from $400/year, $1 million limit) | about $552.52 |
| Pro | Standard with insurance + Samsung 24-inch FHD monitor, LS24D300G ($94.99) + FreshBooks Lite for 12 months ($26/month, $312) | $1,012.42 |

The arithmetic: lean is $35.00 × 1.13 = $39.55. Standard adds $52.99 + $26.99 + $19.99, so ($35.00 + $99.97) = $134.97 × 1.13 = $152.52. With insurance, $152.52 + $400 = $552.52. The pro additions are $94.99 + $312 = $406.99, × 1.13 = $459.90, on top of $552.52, for $1,012.42.

A certificate isn't in any kit. TESL Canada's [certification FAQ](https://tesl.ca/certification/tesl-canada-professional-certification/certification-faq) refers to a full application fee of $90.00 and a $45.00 streamlined fee for some provincial certificants, each plus tax. That's the fee to be certified, not the cost of the training program, which is the program's own price. Marketplaces and direct adult students don't all ask for one.

## Who should use a teach-English-online startup budget in Canada?

A teach-English-online budget fits people who speak clear English, can hold a 45- or 60-minute conversation with a stranger, and will write a lesson plan before buying gear. The lean kit fits someone testing a marketplace with a laptop they already own. The standard kit fits someone teaching most weekdays. The pro kit fits someone with regular direct students and invoices to send.

It's a poor fit if the plan is children's lessons without a parent present, or a promise about immigration or exam results.

Tutoring school subjects is [online tutoring startup costs](/side-hustles/online-tutoring-startup-costs-canada/). Writing for clients instead of teaching them is [freelance writing startup costs](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-startup-costs-canada/).

## Do you need insurance, a certificate or a check to teach English online in Canada?

**Insurance.** Zensurance's [tutor insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/tutor-insurance) says tutors can anticipate premiums starting from $400 a year for professional liability with a $1 million limit, and that requirements vary. That's an estimate, not a quote. Platforms don't generally require it for adult conversation lessons. A school or agency contract may. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance advice.

**Certificates.** TESL Canada's certification FAQ describes professional standards and fees for certification. An employer, a school board or a settlement-language program may require one. A marketplace profile for adults often doesn't. Read the platform's or employer's requirement before you pay for a course.

**Checks for children's lessons.** The RCMP's [vulnerable sector checks](https://rcmp.ca/en/criminal-records/criminal-record-checks/vulnerable-sector-checks) page explains that a vulnerable sector check is requested for a position of trust with children or vulnerable people, through the organization asking. For direct lessons, start with adults. If the learner is under 18, have a parent book and stay nearby.

### A first-month sketch

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of Canadian lesson rates.**

Suppose you buy the lean kit for $39.55 and teach four direct adult lessons at an assumed $40 for 45 minutes each, with 25 minutes of prep, so each block is about 1.2 hours. No platform fee applies.

Fees = 4 × $40 = $160. After the headset: $160 − $39.55 = $120.45. Four blocks at 1.2 hours are 4.8 hours, so $120.45 ÷ 4.8 is about $25.09 an hour before a tax set-aside. A no-show with no written rule turns one $40 line into $0. Do not copy $40.

## What are the ongoing costs?

- **Platform commission** on every marketplace lesson, or Cambly's fixed rate instead of your own.
- **Payout fees and currency conversion** on US-dollar earnings.
- **Insurance,** if you carry it, renewed yearly.
- **Invoicing software** for direct students.
- **Prep time.** The minutes you spend choosing a text are part of the hour even if the invoice says 45 minutes.
- **No-shows** without a written cancellation rule.

## Where can teaching English online startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Start with the headset only.** A laptop camera and window light work for the first learners.
- **Use the platform classroom** instead of paying for a video tool.
- **Skip the monitor** until switching windows during lessons slows you down.
- **Don't buy a certificate course** until a specific employer or program asks for one.
- **Skip invoicing software** while every student books through a platform.

## Where should teaching English online spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't cut the headset. Don't skip the written cancellation rule. Don't teach a child without a parent present and without checking your province's rules.

## Which tax rules apply to a teach-English-online budget in Canada?

Teaching income from Canada is self-employment income, including marketplace payouts, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. A headset is generally a current expense. A monitor or webcam that lasts for years may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) depending on its cost. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) for taxable supplies, not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Don't assume English lessons are tax-exempt. [GST/HST Memorandum 20-7](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-7/second-language-instruction.html) exempts instruction in English or French as a second language only when the courses form part of a program of second-language instruction supplied by a school authority, vocational school, public college, university or a business established and operated primarily to provide language instruction. Conversation practice, interview coaching and test prep may fall outside that exemption, and the CRA says each case is a question of fact. An earlier version of this page pointed to the tutoring memorandum (20-6); 20-7 is the one about second-language instruction.

Keep the agreements, payout reports and e-transfers. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add tutoring and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) once you are.
