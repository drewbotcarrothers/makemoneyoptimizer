<QuickAnswer>

- **Federal rules are the same everywhere:** report profit on Form T2125, and register for GST/HST once taxable sales pass $30,000 (rideshare from the first fare).
- **Provinces add three things:** business name registration (only for names other than your own in most places), any provincial sales tax (B.C., Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Quebec), and minimum wage rules.
- **Only Ontario and B.C.** have app-worker minimum pay rules for delivery and ride-hail, as of October 5, 2026.
- **Cities add licences:** some, like Vancouver, Regina and Whitehorse, license home-based businesses; Winnipeg does not.

</QuickAnswer>

Side hustle rules in Canada come from three levels. Federally, every side hustler reports profit on Form T2125 and registers for GST/HST once taxable sales pass $30,000. Each province or territory then decides whether you register a business name, whether a provincial sales tax applies, and what minimum wage employees and, in two provinces, app workers must receive. Cities decide licences. This hub compares all 13 provinces and territories and links to a detailed guide for each, so you can find your own rules in a minute.

The federal basics are covered in [how to report side hustle income](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/), [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/), and [registering a sole proprietorship](/side-hustles/register-sole-proprietorship-canada/), which lists every registry's fee.

## Which side hustle rules are the same in every province?

Income tax reporting and the GST/HST threshold. You report self-employment profit on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) (Quebec residents also file TP-80 provincially). You do not register for GST/HST until worldwide taxable sales exceed $30,000 in a calendar quarter or four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)), except [taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html), who register from the start. The rate you charge depends on the place of supply, shown on the CRA's [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

## Which provinces have their own sales tax for side hustlers?

Four: British Columbia (7% PST), Saskatchewan (6% PST), Manitoba (7% RST) and Quebec (9.975% QST). Their registration rules differ sharply.

- **B.C.** exempts [small sellers](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/taxes/sales-taxes/publications/pst-003-small-sellers.pdf) with $10,000 or less in eligible retail sales, among other conditions.
- **Saskatchewan** says [all businesses operating in the province](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/taxes-licensing-and-reporting/provincial-taxes-policies-and-bulletins/provincial-sales-tax) must be licensed or registered for PST.
- **Manitoba** does not require registration under [$30,000 in annual taxable sales](https://www.gov.mb.ca/finance/taxation/pubs/bulletins/noticerst2401.pdf), with conditions.
- **Quebec** uses the same $30,000 test for [GST and QST](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/consumption-taxes/gsthst-and-qst/registering-for-the-gst-and-qst/), both registered with Revenu Québec.

The Atlantic provinces and Ontario fold the provincial portion into HST, and Alberta and the territories have no provincial sales tax.

## Which provinces have a minimum wage for gig app workers?

Ontario and British Columbia. Ontario's [Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rights-and-protections-digital-platform-workers) guarantees ride share, delivery and courier platform workers at least the general minimum wage for work assignments, tips excluded. B.C.'s [minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) sets $21.89 per hour of engaged time from June 1, 2026, plus per-kilometre allowances. Elsewhere, employee minimum wages apply to employees only; app couriers are paid on the app's terms. See [food delivery pay](/side-hustles/food-delivery-pricing-canada/) to measure your own hourly rate.

## Do you need a city licence for a side hustle in Canada?

Sometimes, and it varies more by city than by province. Vancouver, Regina, Saskatoon, Edmonton and Whitehorse expect home-based businesses to be licensed; Winnipeg says you do not need a licence to work from home; Toronto licenses specific activities. The federal-provincial-municipal [BizPaL](https://bizpal-perle.ca/) search lists permits by location and business type, and each provincial guide links its biggest cities.

### Illustrative example

A teaching sketch with assumed facts. The same online bookkeeping side hustle, run under the owner's own name with $20,000 a year in sales, registers nothing provincially and charges no sales tax in Ontario, Alberta or Manitoba. In Saskatchewan it would still contact the Ministry of Finance about a PST vendor's licence or registered consumer number, because the province says all businesses must hold one, and in Regina it would need a $195 residential business licence because the business runs from home.

## What are the common mistakes when reading provincial side hustle rules?

- **Treating a name registration as tax registration.** They are separate everywhere.
- **Assuming PST follows GST.** Each PST province has its own test.
- **Expecting a gig minimum outside Ontario and B.C.**
- **Using a neighbour province's HST rate.** Nova Scotia is 14%, the other Atlantic provinces 15%, Ontario 13%.

## Which tax rules apply to side hustles in every province?

T2125, CPP (or QPP in Quebec) on net self-employment earnings, provincial income tax on the same return, and GST/HST once registered.
