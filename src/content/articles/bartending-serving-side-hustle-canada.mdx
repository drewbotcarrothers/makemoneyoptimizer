<QuickAnswer>

- **What it is:** weekend or evening shifts serving or bartending, usually as an employee of a bar, restaurant, caterer, or banquet hall. Holiday parties are the busy stretch.
- **Certificate first:** Ontario's Smart Serve full course is listed at $44.95 with no HST and is valid for five years. B.C. requires Serving It Right before you work. Alberta's ProServe is $25 plus GST. Other provinces use their own programs.
- **Wages are not a promise.** Job Bank's bartender median for Canada is $20.00 an hour (low $15.00, high $30.00), reference period 2023–2024, updated 19 November 2025. Your shift pays what the employer posts. Tips are extra income to report.
- **Many of these jobs are employment, with a T4.** A private bartender you invoice yourself is a different status. The CRA decides from the working relationship.

</QuickAnswer>

Weekend bartending and serving in Canada is paid shift work pouring and serving drinks, usually for an employer, on evenings you can stand through. It suits people who can get the provincial alcohol-service certificate, arrive when the call time says, and treat the job as employment unless the facts say otherwise. It is a poor fit if you cannot work the hours a venue actually rosters, or if you want a quiet desk.

Short shifts at weddings and corporate parties, including roles that never touch alcohol, are in [event staffing](/side-hustles/event-staffing-side-hustle-canada/). Retail and warehouse holiday hiring is in [seasonal holiday jobs](/side-hustles/seasonal-holiday-jobs-canada/). If the work you want is a whistle instead of a bar, see [sports refereeing](/side-hustles/sports-referee-side-hustle-canada/).

## Who does weekend bartending and serving in Canada suit?

Weekend bartending and serving in Canada suits adults who can be on their feet, stay polite when a room is loud, and complete the certificate their province requires before the first alcohol shift. It suits students and people with a weekday job who can give up Friday or Saturday night. It does not suit someone who needs daytime-only hours, or someone who wants to set their own rate on a venue's schedule.

Minimum age to serve liquor is a provincial rule. This guide does not invent one national age. Confirm it with the provincial liquor regulator and the employer. In one specific B.C. setting, Responsible Service BC says staff making a sale in a special wine store, and the manager supervising that sale, must be at least 19 and must hold Serving It Right. That sentence is about that licence. It is not a national rule for every server.

## What do bartenders and servers earn in Canada?

Employed bartenders in Canada have a national median of $20.00 an hour, according to [Job Bank's bartender wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/16455/ca) (updated 19 November 2025, reference period 2023–2024, Labour Force Survey). Those are prevailing wages for the occupation, not a quote for your next shift and not a figure that includes a tip you have not received.

| Area (Job Bank, bartender) | Low | Median | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada | $15.00 | $20.00 | $30.00 |
| Ontario | $17.95 | $20.00 | $30.00 |
| British Columbia | $18.25 | $20.00 | $30.59 |
| Alberta | $15.00 | $20.00 | $35.00 |
| Quebec | $16.60 | $22.44 | $40.00 |

[Job Bank's food and beverage server page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/6641/ca), same update date and reference period, lists a national low of $15.00, a median of $18.50, and a high of $30.00 an hour. Serving and bartending are different rows. Read the row that matches the posting.

Tips and gratuities that are not already on a T4 are reported as other employment income. The CRA explains that on its [line 10400 page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/line-10400-other-employment-income.html). A self-employed person includes tips in business income instead. Do not treat a Job Bank median as the cash you will take home after tax.

## Which server certificate does your province require in Canada?

The server certificate in Canada is provincial. A course that is valid at home is not automatically what the next province wants, except where that province publishes an equivalency. The table uses each regulator's own page. Fees change. Confirm the live price before you pay.

Smart Serve's full course meets the requirement for liquor licensees and employees, including bars and restaurants. Certificates are renewed every five years. | $44.95, no HST, on the AGCO page. Special Event Server training is a shorter program for many special-event permits. | Not stated on the "who needs it" page. Confirm the current fee on Responsible Service BC. |

Responsible Service BC also says employers in B.C. can still require a Serving It Right certificate even when an out-of-province certificate would exempt you. Carry proof. Inspectors ask.

Coat check, setup, and other roles that never sell or serve liquor often do not need the certificate. Many employers still prefer it. If your hand touches a drink order, assume you need the course until the regulator's page says you do not.

## How do you get a first weekend serving shift in Canada?

Getting a first weekend serving shift in Canada means the certificate, a short availability note, and applications to places that already staff weekends, before you buy a uniform they did not ask for.

### Step 1: Finish the provincial course

Use the table. Screenshot or download the certificate. Ontario's five-year clock and Alberta's five-year clock mean a card from years ago may be expired. Check the date.

### Step 2: Write the hours you will actually work

Friday close, Saturday brunch, and a Sunday private event are three different offers. Holiday weeks fill early. [Seasonal holiday jobs](/side-hustles/seasonal-holiday-jobs-canada/) covers the employee side of November and December hiring. Do not promise a close you cannot reach by transit or a safe ride home.

### Step 3: Apply where the licence already exists

Restaurants, banquet halls, caterers, and stadium concessions hire servers. You are not applying for your own liquor licence. [Event staffing](/side-hustles/event-staffing-side-hustle-canada/) is the agency and wedding path. [Flash staffing apps](/side-hustles/flash-staffing-apps-side-hustle-canada/) are another way into short shifts, with their own employee-versus-contractor questions.

### Step 4: Shoes after the dress code, not before

A long shift on a wet floor is a footwear problem. Buy what the employer requires. A <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="pair of slip-resistant shoes" search="slip resistant work shoes" /> is a category search once you know the colour and the policy. A listing is not a safety certification. Confirm the standard the venue wants.

### Step 5: Keep the first shifts small enough to repeat

One venue that will roster you again beats five trials you cannot staff. Ask what "close" means in hours, not in a vibe.

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a pay prediction.

Suppose Job Bank's national bartender median of $20.00 an hour is the rate on a posting, and you work an assumed 8-hour Saturday. Gross wages in the sketch are 8 × $20 = $160 before tax, CPP, and EI, which an employer would withhold if you are an employee. Suppose the posting's tips are an assumed $40 that are not on the T4. Replace $20 and $40 with the posting and the tips you actually receive.

## Are you an employee or self-employed when you serve in Canada?

Most weekend serving and bartending in Canada is employment: the venue sets the shift, the methods, and the price of the drinks. The CRA explains how it decides status on its page on [determining employment status](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/canada-pension-plan-cpp-employment-insurance-ei-rulings/employee-self-employed/determine-employment-status.html). A private event where you bring your own service, set your own price, and invoice the host can look like a business. The label in the text message is not the test.

Employees do not charge GST/HST on wages. A genuinely self-employed server follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) and reports business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), including both shares of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on the net. The questions people mix up, including tips and the $30,000 figure, are in the [side hustle tax FAQ](/side-hustles/side-hustle-tax-faq-canada/).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

## What mistakes are common in weekend serving in Canada?

Common weekend serving mistakes in Canada are working a bar shift without the certificate, treating Job Bank's median as the offer, and assuming every night is a contractor invoice. Expired cards are the same mistake as no card. Alberta gives 30 days from the employment start date for ProServe. Saskatchewan wants SIRS before the first shift. Do not split the difference.

Other misses: buying blacks and shoes before the dress code, arriving at guest time instead of call time, and having no way home after close. The [event staffing guide](/side-hustles/event-staffing-side-hustle-canada/) lists the same late-night problem.
