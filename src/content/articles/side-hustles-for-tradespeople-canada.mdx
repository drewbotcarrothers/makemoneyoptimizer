## What are the best side hustles for tradespeople in Canada?

The best side hustles for tradespeople in Canada stay inside work you're allowed to do privately and use tools you already own. The groups below follow that logic.

### Handyman and odd jobs

Small repairs, mounting, assembly, and patching are in steady demand. [Handyman work](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada/) covers setup, and [handyman tools](/side-hustles/handyman-tools-canada/) lists what you might still need. Apps are covered in [TaskRabbit vs Kijiji vs Facebook](/side-hustles/taskrabbit-vs-kijiji-facebook-handyman-canada/). Keep work that falls inside a compulsory trade's scope for when you're authorized for it.

### Seasonal outdoor work

[Snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/), [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada/), [Christmas light installation](/side-hustles/christmas-light-installation-side-hustle-canada/), and [fall leaf cleanup](/side-hustles/fall-leaf-cleanup-side-hustle-canada/) use a truck and equipment many tradespeople already have.

### Exterior cleaning and hauling

[Pressure washing](/side-hustles/pressure-washing-side-hustle-canada/), [window cleaning](/side-hustles/window-cleaning-side-hustle-canada/), [junk removal](/side-hustles/junk-removal-side-hustle-canada/), and [moving help](/side-hustles/moving-help-side-hustle-canada/) all reward people comfortable with physical work and equipment. ## Which trade rules apply to side jobs in Canada?

Trade rules are provincial. Ontario's [compulsory trades page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/compulsory-trades-and-enforcement) is a clear example:

- **144 prescribed trades, 23 compulsory.** Compulsory trades include electrician (construction and maintenance, and domestic and rural), plumber, hairstylist, and automotive service technician.
- **Authorization.** To work in a compulsory trade, you must hold a valid Certificate of Qualification or Provisional Certificate of Qualification in that trade, or be an apprentice under a valid registered training agreement. Exemptions are set out in regulation.
- **Scope of practice.** Each trade's scope is set by regulation, so the work you can do privately depends on your certificate.

Other provinces have their own compulsory and voluntary trades lists. Electrical, gas, and plumbing work can also need permits or licensed contractors, so check before quoting.

Your employer may have rules too. The federal [conflict-of-interest FAQ](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/values-ethics/conflict-interest-post-employment/frequently-asked-questions.html) for public servants is one example: outside work should be on your own time and not use the employer's property. Private employers and unions may restrict side work for the company's customers or the use of company vehicles and tools, so read your agreement.

## How should tradespeople price side jobs?

Quote by the job where you can, include materials separately, and build in travel time. The [handyman pricing guide](/side-hustles/handyman-pricing-canada/) explains the approach.

### Illustrative example

Assumed numbers, not market rates: a tradesperson quotes a weekend job at an assumed 4 hours at $70 per hour plus $120 in materials, and drives an assumed 30 km round trip.

- Labour: 4 × $70 = $280
- Total invoice: $280 + $120 = $400
- After materials: $400 − $120 = $280, before vehicle costs and tax

## What mistakes do tradespeople make with side jobs?

- **Doing compulsory-trade work without authorization.** Check your certificate and scope.
- **Skipping permits.** Some jobs need them even when they're small.
- **Using your employer's truck or tools.** Ask first, in writing.
- **No liability insurance.** Property damage claims can be large.
- **Cash jobs without records.** All income is taxable.

## How is a trades side hustle taxed in Canada?

Report it on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Tools, materials, and the business share of vehicle costs can be deductible; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/) and [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/). If net self-employment earnings exceed $3,500, you generally pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) outside Quebec. Register for GST/HST once you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html).
