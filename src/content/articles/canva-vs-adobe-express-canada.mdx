Canva and Adobe Express are both browser-based design tools that Canadian freelancers use for social media graphics, flyers, slide decks, and short videos, and both have a free plan. The practical differences are price, where the tool fits with the rest of your software, and the licence rules on stock content. Canva's Canadian pricing page showed Canva Pro at $142 CAD a year for one person before tax; Adobe Express Premium is sold on its own and is also included with Creative Cloud Standard, Creative Cloud Pro, and most single-app plans. Canva suits template-heavy client work; Adobe Express suits people already inside Adobe's apps.

The service side is covered in [how to start graphic design in Canada](/side-hustles/graphic-design-side-hustle-canada/), [graphic design startup costs](/side-hustles/graphic-design-startup-costs-canada/), and [graphic design pricing](/side-hustles/graphic-design-pricing-canada/).

## How do Canva and Adobe Express compare for Canadian freelancers?

Both start free and charge for premium content and features; the licence fine print matters as much as the price. Facts below are from each company's pages as read on October 4, 2026.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Free plan | Yes | Yes; Adobe says premium features such as remove background and bulk resize need a premium plan ([Adobe Express pricing](https://www.adobe.com/ca/express/pricing)) |
| Paid plan for one person | Canva Pro, shown at $142 CAD a year on Canva's Canadian pricing page, before GST and provincial tax ([Canva pricing](https://www.canva.com/pricing/)) | Adobe Express Premium, priced by region on Adobe's pricing page; confirm the CAD amount there |

## What can you sell or trademark with Canva or Adobe Express content?

You can generally use stock content inside an original design for client work and merchandise, but not as a standalone item for sale, and not as a trademark. Read the licence before you hand a logo to a client.

- **Logos and trademarks.** Canva says you cannot use any Free or Pro content from its library in a trademark, except fonts, basic shapes, and lines, because other users can use the same content ([Canva Help](https://www.canva.com/help/trademarks-logo/)). Its logo templates are non-exclusive, so a logo built from one cannot be registered.
- **Merchandise.** Canva allows designs printed on products for sale if you comply with its licence, but its [Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/) bars displaying content on a standalone basis on print-on-demand sites, such as a plain stock photo on a t-shirt.
- **Adobe Express premium content** carries a limited licence; read Adobe's terms for the asset type before using it on products or logos.

If a client needs an exclusive mark, draw it from scratch or commission it, then use either tool for the brand kit around it.

## How much does each tool cost a Canadian freelancer per year?

The free plans cost nothing; the paid tier is a business expense you can price into your rates. Below is labelled arithmetic, not a quote.

### Illustrative example

Assume you bill design work and pay for Canva Pro at the $142 CAD yearly price shown on Canva's page, plus 13% HST in Ontario as an example.

- **Canva Pro:** $142 × 1.13 = $160.46 a year, or about $13.37 a month.
- **Adobe Express Premium:** if you already pay for a Creative Cloud plan that includes it, the extra cost is $0. If not, take the CAD price from Adobe's page and do the same sum.
- **Cost per client:** with an assumed 8 clients a year, $160.46 ÷ 8 = about $20.06 per client.

If you are registered for GST/HST, you can generally claim input tax credits for the tax on business subscriptions; if you are not registered, the tax is part of the cost.

## Who should pick Canva or Adobe Express in Canada?

Pick by the rest of your toolkit and the type of client work. This is a decision rule, not a ranking.

| If you… | Lean toward | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Already pay for Creative Cloud | Adobe Express | Premium is included with most Creative Cloud plans |
| Make many social posts and templates for small clients | Canva | Template-first workflow and shareable templates |
| Need logos clients can trademark | Neither on its own | Stock content is non-exclusive; draw the mark yourself |
| Are just testing demand | Either free plan | Upgrade only when a paid feature saves time |
| Work with clients who already use Canva | Canva | Easier hand-off of editable files |

## What mistakes do freelancers make with Canva and Adobe Express?

The common mistakes are licence mistakes, not design mistakes.

- **Selling a template-based logo as exclusive.** Canva says its logo templates cannot be registered as trademarks.
- **Putting raw stock on merch.** Standalone stock on print-on-demand products breaks Canva's licence.
- **Paying for two suites.** Check whether your Creative Cloud plan already includes Express Premium.
- **Not pricing the subscription in.** Spread the yearly cost across clients.
- **Losing the receipts.** Keep the invoice for each subscription for your tax return.

## Which tax rules apply to design tools for Canadian freelancers?

Design-tool subscriptions used for client work are generally deductible business expenses on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and design income is generally business income. You must register for GST/HST once you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) over four consecutive calendar quarters.
