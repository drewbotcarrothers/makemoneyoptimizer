Audiobook narration in Canada is performing and producing a retail-ready recording of a book, usually from a home setup, then delivering it under a contract that pays either a finished-hour fee, a share of royalties, or both. ACX, Amazon's Audiobook Creation Exchange, is the marketplace this guide focuses on because its own pages talk about Canada. It suits someone who can read accurately for a long session, take direction, and edit to a spec. It is a poor fit if you need the money before the book is finished. Royalty-share projects pay later, if the book sells. A per-finished-hour project pays when the rights holder approves the audio, which is still after the hours are done.

Other paid voice work, with a different client, is [voiceover as a side hustle](/side-hustles/voiceover-side-hustle-canada/). Editing someone else's show is [podcast editing](/side-hustles/podcast-editing-side-hustle-canada/). The author's side of a book is [ebook publishing](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-side-hustle-canada/).

## What do royalty share and per finished hour mean on ACX?

Per finished hour means the rights holder pays a rate for each hour of finished audio when they approve it. ACX's narrator page says that if you chose to be paid per finished hour, you get paid then. Royalty share means you are paid from later sales instead of, or in addition to, that cheque.

ACX's [Royalty Share Plus announcement](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/negotiate-your-perfect-deal-with-royalty-share-plus) describes a hybrid: the rights holder negotiates a per-finished-hour amount, sends that fee when production is complete, and ACX also splits royalties after the book is on sale. The announcement does not set the finished-hour amount. That number is the negotiation.

A 2013 ACX post, [Money Talks](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/money-talks-paying-and-getting-paid-for-your-audiobook), describes royalty share as paying the producer nothing up front and splitting the rights holder's royalties 50-50, and it says that option was only available with exclusive distribution. That post is old. It also describes royalty math that later pages replaced. Use it for the shape of the deal, not for the percentage you will be offered in 2026. Confirm on the offer whether royalty share still requires exclusive distribution.

The same 2013 post calls roughly $200 per finished hour for narration and another $200 for post-production a "good estimate of industry standard rates," and it says about four hours of work can produce one finished hour (about two hours reading and another two to three hours editing). It also estimates about 9,300 words per finished hour. Those figures are ACX's 2013 description. They are not a 2026 Canadian rate card and not a survey. At the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4254 on October 5, 2026, US$200 is about $285.08 CAD and US$400 is about $570.16 CAD, if you treat the old post's dollars as US dollars, which the post does not label. Do not quote a client those numbers as "the going rate." Negotiate the offer in front of you, and keep a log of your own hours per finished hour.

## Which ACX royalty rates apply now?

Two official descriptions are live at the same time, so read the one attached to your offer.

The [KDP help page on ACX](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G201014330), written for authors, says exclusive distribution to Audible, Amazon, and iTunes earns 40%, royalty share earns 20%, and non-exclusive distribution earns 25%. In context, "you" is the rights holder. A 50-50 split of a 40% exclusive royalty is one way to read the 20% royalty-share line together with the 2013 post. Do not assume that arithmetic still governs a new contract.

ACX's own post, published April 28, 2026 and [updated May 26, 2026](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/audible-new-royalty-model), says creators can select exclusive distribution for a 50% royalty rate or non-exclusive distribution for 30%. It says the legacy model will be discontinued by the end of 2026, and that for marketplace titles, including pay-for-production, royalty share, and Royalty Share Plus, the new model applies to offers sent to producers after May 26, 2026. Enrollment takes effect on the 1st of the following month. The post explains member listening with a "Member Value" share, and it says cash-purchase calculations keep their old method while the contractual rate is higher than the legacy 40% and 25%. It does not, on that page, restate how the rights holder and the narrator divide a royalty-share percentage. The offer is where that split has to be written.

The KDP page also says you receive a royalty payment when sales are more than $50 in the period, and it mentions an ACX bounty program of $50 on certain eligible sales. Confirm both on the current help page before you count them. Amounts on US platforms need a Canadian-dollar record. See [earning in USD from US platforms](/side-hustles/usd-platform-income-tax-canada/).

## What does a home studio cost for audiobook narration in Canada?

A home studio for audiobook narration in Canada starts with a quiet, soft room and a microphone you can leave in one place for the length of a book. The ranges match the approach in the [voiceover guide](/side-hustles/voiceover-side-hustle-canada/): typical Canadian retail prices to verify locally, not quotes. ACX publishes audio requirements for files it will accept. Use the checklist inside your ACX account before you deliver. This guide does not copy a spec from memory.

| Item | Typical CAD range | How to think about it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USB microphone, or an XLR mic plus an interface | About $100–$400 | A USB mic is enough to learn. Consistency across chapters matters more than a new mic halfway through a book. |
| Closed-back headphones | About $50–$150 | You need to hear noise, breaths, and mismatched volume. |
| Pop filter, stand, and a way to keep the mic still | About $20–$60 | Handling noise shows up in a long session. |
| Room treatment | $0–$200 | A closet of clothes is a real starting booth. Blankets beat an untreated kitchen. |
| Editing software | $0 to start | Free editors can assemble a chapter. The time is the cost. |
| ACX account | The KDP page says it is free to try ACX | The cost is the hours, not a membership fee on that page. |

## How do you start audiobook narration in Canada?

Starting audiobook narration in Canada means proving the room, opening the account only if the eligibility screen accepts your details, and auditioning for a length you can finish.

### Step 1: Record a ten-minute test in the real room

Read a public-domain page or your own words. Listen back on headphones. Note the fridge, the street, and the echo. Fix the room before you make an audition.

### Step 2: Confirm ACX will take a Canadian account

The [KDP help page](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G201014330) says Canada is included, with a mailing address, a valid local TIN, and Canadian banking details (or details in the US, UK, or Ireland if that is the country you qualify through). The signup screen is the live check. If it refuses the address, stop and read the message. Do not borrow someone else's tax identity.

### Step 3: Complete the tax interview with your own information

US payers often ask a non-US person for [Form W-8BEN](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-8-ben). Use whatever ACX's interview actually requests, in your own name. That form does not replace a Canadian return.

### Step 4: Learn one clean chapter, not a whole book

Edit breaths, keep the volume even, and export in the format ACX's current requirements ask for. ACX's narrator page says you are responsible for the finished product, from recording through mastering, and that auditions should sound like the finished work.

### Step 5: Audition for a book you can stand to finish

Read the excerpt, the deadline, and the pay line. A royalty-share offer on a book with no audience can be months of work for a small cheque. A per-finished-hour offer pays sooner and ends when the audio is approved. Royalty Share Plus is the middle, if the finished-hour amount is enough for your hours. Write your own hour count. The 2013 "about four hours per finished hour" line is a starting suspicion, not your log.

### Step 6: Get the pay structure in the offer before you record the book

Per finished hour, royalty share, or both. Exclusive or non-exclusive. The percentage from the May 26, 2026 post or the older KDP figures, whichever the offer cites. If the offer does not say how the royalty is split with you, ask before you accept. [A simple freelance contract](/side-hustles/freelance-contract-canada/) is the habit even when the platform generates the form. Read the form.

### Step 7: Deliver on a schedule you can keep beside a day job

A side hustle book dies when chapter 10 slips by a month. Block the sessions. Tell the rights holder the date of the next check-in, which ACX's narrator page says is part of the process.

### Step 8: Track the statement in Canadian dollars

When a payment arrives, record the CAD amount and the fee the bank or payout service took. The KDP page describes monthly statements. A month under the payment threshold is still a month you worked, if you were on royalty share.

## What audiobook narration mistakes are common in Canada?

Common audiobook narration mistakes in Canada are accepting royalty share on a book you have not checked, changing microphones halfway through, and treating a 2013 blog rate as a 2026 quote.

- **Royalty share as "passive income."** You do the hours up front. Sales are uncertain. The [passive income reality check](/side-hustles/passive-income-reality-canada/) is the right caution.
- **An echoey audition.** Rights holders hear the room. Treatment is cheaper than a new microphone.
- **Ignoring the new rate date.** Offers after May 26, 2026 are under the model ACX described that day. Read the offer anyway.
- **Union and non-union mixed without reading the rules.** [ACTRA](https://www.actra.ca/) covers professional performers in recorded media in Canada. If you join, follow its rules before you take a non-union ACX title. Many beginners are not members. That is allowed. It is not a reason to ignore a contract you later sign.
- **A US VPN or a borrowed tax form.** Eligibility is about where you reside and whose TIN you have. Use your own.

## Which tax rules apply to audiobook narration in Canada?

Narration fees and royalties in Canada are generally self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), in Canadian dollars. A microphone you use for the work is generally an expense or a capital item under the usual rules. See [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/). Self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). A US form for ACX is not the Canadian filing.
