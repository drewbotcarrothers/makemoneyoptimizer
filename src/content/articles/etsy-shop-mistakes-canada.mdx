## Which fee mistakes do Etsy sellers make in Canada?

The main fee mistake Etsy sellers make in Canada is pricing like a craft fair, with no allowance for per-order fees.

### Mistake 1: Pricing without fees

Etsy's [fees page](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014483627-What-are-the-Fees-and-Taxes-for-Selling-on-Etsy) lists a US$0.20 listing fee and a 6.5% transaction fee on the order total, including shipping. Its [payment processing page](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015628847-What-are-Payment-Processing-Fees-for-Selling-on-Etsy) lists 3% + CA$0.25 for Canadian sellers on domestic and US orders, and its [Regulatory Operating fee page](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500011073202-What-is-a-Regulatory-Operating-Fee) lists 0.50% for Canadian sellers.

### Mistake 2: Forgetting Offsite Ads

Etsy's fees page describes an Offsite Ads fee of 15% on orders from those ads (12% for shops past $10,000 in sales over 365 days), capped at $100 per order. Check that an ad-driven order still makes a profit at your price.

## Which tax mistakes do Etsy sellers make in Canada?

The main tax mistake Etsy sellers make in Canada is misunderstanding what happens when a GST/HST ID is added to the shop.

### Mistake 3: GST/HST ID added, prices unchanged

Etsy's [Canadian tax page](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/6633345416215-What-Does-Etsy-Remit-to-Canadian-Tax-Authorities) says that since July 1, 2022, Etsy collects GST/HST at checkout on physical goods shipped from Canadian sellers to Canadian buyers when the seller has not added a GST/HST ID. Once you add an ID, Etsy stops collecting it on those sales, and you should adjust listing prices to collect the tax and remit it yourself. Etsy also charges GST/HST on non-order fees, such as listing and ads fees, regardless of registration. Registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html).

## Which product rule mistakes do Etsy sellers make in Canada?

The main product rule mistakes Etsy sellers make in Canada involve handmade cosmetics and textile labels.

### Mistake 4: Unnotified handmade cosmetics

Health Canada's [Cosmetic Notification Form page](https://health.canada.ca/apps/radar/CPS-SPC-0007.08.html) says manufacturers and importers must notify Health Canada within 10 days after first selling a cosmetic in Canada, and lists "handmade" cosmetics sold through home-based businesses or craft sales among the products covered. Soaps marketed as cosmetics, balms, and bath products need checking.

### Mistake 5: Missing textile labels

The Competition Bureau's [textile labelling summary](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/labelling/textile-labelling/labelling-textile-requirements-nutshell) says fibres at 5% or more of the total fibre mass must be shown by generic name and percentage, in English and French, along with dealer identification, either a name and postal address or a CA identification number.

## Which shipping and record mistakes do Etsy sellers make in Canada?

The main shipping mistake Etsy sellers make in Canada is guessing postage instead of weighing and measuring each product in its packaging.

### Mistake 6: Guessing shipping costs

Weigh and measure each packed item and price shipping from real rates. Remember the 6.5% transaction fee applies to shipping too.

### Mistake 7: No records in CAD

Etsy charges listing fees in USD. Export monthly statements, convert USD amounts to CAD for your records, and keep material receipts. Income goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not an average. Suppose a domestic order is $40 for the item plus $10 shipping: $50. The transaction fee is 6.5% × $50 = $3.25. Processing is 3% × $50 + $0.25 = $1.75. The Regulatory Operating fee is 0.50% × $50 = $0.25. Fees total $3.25 + $1.75 + $0.25 = $5.25, plus the US$0.20 listing fee. If that order came from Offsite Ads, add 15% × $50 = $7.50, for $12.75 before the listing fee.
