## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for automation freelancing in Canada?

Ongoing automation costs in Canada are the task plan the live zap needs, rework when a field name changes, and monitoring if you included it. Automation break-even in the sketch is a method, not a national project fee.

**Who pays Zapier.** Your practice account is yours. The production account should be the client's, in their billing, so a task spike hits the person who owns the list.

**Two-step versus multi-step.** Free stops at two steps. A filter or a second action is a paid plan on Zapier's comparison. Quote the plan the build actually needs.

**Email the zap sends.** If the automation emails people, [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) is part of the design, not a footnote. This is not legal advice.

### Step 1: Build the sample on Free

Fake data, two steps, a screenshot. If Free cannot do it, you have learned the limit for $0.

### Step 2: Price the task tier the live zap needs

Use the annual figure only if the client will still want it in month six. Otherwise quote the monthly card.

### Step 3: Put the tool invoice in the client's name

Your fee is the build and the runbook. Their Zapier bill is theirs.

### Step 4: Write the pause and the error email

A zap nobody can stop is a liability with a login. The runbook is a deliverable.

### Step 5: Log testing hours, not just the happy path

Testing is longer than the demo. If the fee does not cover the test, the next quote changes. Honour the written scope for the current client.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a survey of automation fees.**

Suppose the sample fits Zapier Free, so the practice month is $0. Suppose the client's live zap needs multi-step actions, so they will pay Professional at the 750-task monthly price, about $43 CAD, on their own card. That $43 is not your revenue and not your cost in this sketch. Suppose your fee for the build is an assumed $400. That $400 is an assumption, not a market rate. Suppose testing and the runbook take eight hours, and you buy a headset at $30 because the scoping call was in a noisy room. Suppose FreshBooks is still unnecessary.

Remainder = $400 − $30 = $370. Hour sketch = $370 ÷ 8 = $46.25 before tax set-aside.

If you mistakenly put the $43 plan on your card and forget to invoice it, the remainder falls to $327, and a task overage could take more. A yearly Zapier commitment on your card, for a client who might leave, is the version of this sketch that hurts in month four. Do not copy $400. If you are registered, tax on your invoice is not extra profit.

## Where should automation spending not be cut in Canada?

Automation spending in Canada should not skip a test with fake data, a written pause, or the question of who pays the task bill. A live customer list on the first try is how a field maps wrong and a hundred people get the wrong email. The free plan is the place to be clumsy.

Do not automate a message the business is not allowed to send. Canada's anti-spam law is the check before an email step, not after a complaint. Do not leave your login as the only owner. Access should end.

Insurance is a quote when the zap touches payment data or a customer list. This is not insurance advice. A headset is optional. A runbook is not.

Keep your fee separate from any balance the tool holds for the client. A thin fixed price that ignored testing hours is how the next build gets worse, not cheaper.

## Which tax rules apply to an automation freelancing budget in Canada?

Automation freelancing income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. A Zapier plan you pay for your own practice, and a headset, are generally current expenses. A computer that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the scope, the invoice, and the receipts. A Zapier bill the client paid is not your income. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over four consecutive quarters. Add other side hustles. This work is not ride-sharing.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, if you were a registrant and the receipt shows the tax. A USD subscription may show tax depending on how the vendor bills Canada. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST and do not claim the credit. Confirm the current rules with the CRA or a tax professional.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set the tax aside.
