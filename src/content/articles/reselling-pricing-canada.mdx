Price a resale item in Canada from recent sold prices for the same item and condition, then subtract fees, shipping and what you paid. eBay.ca takes 13.6% of the total sale in most categories, including shipping and sales tax, plus $0.30 or $0.40 an order. Poshmark Canada takes C$3.95 under C$20 and 20% from C$20, with the label included. Depop charges Canadian sellers 10% plus PayPal's 2.9% + $0.30. Chit Chats ships a T-shirt to Toronto for $5.33. A $45 jacket bought for $9 clears about $26.50 on Poshmark. Set a floor price before you list and mark down on a schedule.

eBay's rate depends on the category, and the percentage applies to the total amount of the sale, including the sales tax eBay collects.

| eBay.ca category | Final value fee (up to $7,500) | Per-order fee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Most categories, including clothing | 13.6% | $0.30 (≤ $10) / $0.40 |

## How do you calculate a resale price?

Find what the item actually sells for, then check the net clears your target. If it doesn't, change the platform, the shipping or whether you buy the item.

```text
Net (Poshmark ≥ C$20) = price × 0.80 − item cost − supplies
Floor (Poshmark)      = (item cost + supplies + target profit) ÷ 0.80
Net (eBay.ca)         = (price + shipping charged)
                        − [rate × (price + shipping charged) × (1 + sales tax) + per-order fee]
                        − label − supplies − item cost
Net (Depop, own label)= (price + shipping) × 0.90
                        − [2.9% × amount received + $0.30] − label − item cost
```

1. **Sold comps.** Use sold listings for the same item, size and condition, not asking prices.
2. **Fees.** Use the platform rule, including what the percentage applies to.
3. **Shipping.** Weigh it, then quote the label.
4. **Item cost and supplies.** What you paid, plus the mailer.
5. **Floor.** The lowest price that keeps your target profit. Don't accept offers below it.

**Illustrative floor:** a jacket bought for $9, a $0.50 mailer and a $20 target profit on Poshmark: ($9 + $0.50 + $20) ÷ 0.80 = $36.88, so list at $45 and accept offers down to $37.

## What do worked resale prices look like?

**Illustrative.** Items, sold comps, purchase costs and mailers are assumed. Fees and shipping rates are the published ones above.

### Item 1: jacket on Poshmark

- Listed at **$45**, bought for $9, mailer $0.50.
- Fee 20% × $45 = $9.00. Label included. Net $45 − $9 − $9 − $0.50 = **$26.50**.
- On eBay.ca to an Ontario buyer at $40 + $12 shipping: fee 13.6% × $58.76 + $0.40 = $8.39 before you pay the label.

### Item 2: athletic sneakers on eBay.ca

- Sold at **$160** with free shipping to an Ontario buyer. Total sale $160 × 1.13 = $180.80, so the 8% sneaker rate applies and there's no per-order fee.
- Fee 8% × $180.80 = $14.46. Assumed label $20, bought for $60. Net $160 − $14.46 − $20 − $60 = **$65.54**.
- At the 13.6% rate the fee would have been 13.6% × $180.80 + $0.40 = $24.99, so check whether your shoes fall in the $150+ band.

### Item 3: sports card on eBay.ca

- Sold at **$25 + $5 shipping** to an Alberta buyer. Total sale ($30) × 1.05 = $31.50.
- Fee 13.25% × $31.50 + $0.40 = $4.57. Chit Chats 85 g anywhere in Canada $3.83, top-loader and mailer $0.60, card bought for $5.
- Net $30 − $4.57 − $3.83 − $0.60 − $5 = **$16.00**.

### Item 4: T-shirt on Depop

- Sold at **$28 + $6 shipping**, your own Chit Chats label to Toronto ($5.33), bought for $4.
- Depop 10% × $34 = $3.40. PayPal 2.9% × $34 + $0.30 = $1.29.
- Net $34 − $3.40 − $1.29 − $5.33 − $4 = **$19.98**. On Poshmark at $28 the fee is $5.60 and the label is included: $28 − $5.60 − $4 = $22.40.

## What should a resale pricing sheet look like?

Keep one row per item. The floor column stops you taking a bad offer, and the date column drives markdowns.

```text
ITEM PRICING SHEET — [month]
SKU | Item / size / condition | Bought $ | Sold comps (3) | Platform
List $ | Floor $ | Weight g | Label $ | Listed on | Markdown dates
Sold $ | Fees $ | Net $ | Days to sell

Example row:
J-014 | Wool jacket, M, excellent | 9.00 | 42 / 45 / 50 | Poshmark
45 | 37 | 900 | incl. | Oct 9 | Nov 8 (−10%), Dec 8 (floor)
— | — | — | —
```

```text
OFFER REPLY (below floor)
Thanks for the offer! The lowest I can take on this one is $[floor].
If you bundle a second item, I can do $[bundle price] for both.
```

## How do you raise resale prices or mark down slow stock?

Raise prices when the data says buyers want the item, and cut them on a schedule, never below the floor.

1. **Reprice to rising comps.** Check sold prices again before relisting older stock.
2. **Read fast interest.** Many likes, watchers or offers in the first days means the price is low.
3. **List seasonally.** Coats and boots before winter; buyers search for them then.
4. **Compare platforms after shipping.** In Item 4, the $28 T-shirt nets $22.40 on Poshmark, where the label is included, against $19.98 on Depop with your own label.
5. **Bundle low-priced items.** Poshmark's C$3.95 flat fee is 26% of a $15 sale; two items at $30 pay 20%.
6. **Mark down on dates.** Example rule: 10% off after 30 days, floor price at 60 days, then donate or bundle.

## What reselling pricing mistakes are common?

- **Pricing from asking prices.** Only sold listings show what buyers pay.
- **Forgetting eBay's fee base.** It includes shipping you charge and sales tax.
- **Low-price items with fixed fees.** A $5 item on Poshmark loses $3.95 to the fee.
- **Guessing weight.** Weigh it; the label can exceed the fee.
- **No floor.** Without one, offers set your price.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect reselling prices?

Regular reselling for profit is generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both [CPP shares](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net income. Selling your own used belongings occasionally is treated differently; the [platform income tax guide](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/) explains the line.

GST/HST registration follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all your side hustles. eBay.ca's own fee example adds 13% HST to a $400 sale, making a $452 total and a $61.87 fee, so the tax a marketplace collects raises your fee. For sales you make yourself, see the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and the [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).
