Pricing items to resell in Canada starts with recent sold prices for the same item in similar condition, then working backwards from fees, shipping, and what you paid to see your profit. On eBay.ca, most categories pay a 13.6% final value fee on the total amount of the sale, including shipping charged to the buyer and sales tax, plus C$0.30 or C$0.40 per order. On Poshmark.ca, sales under C$20 pay a flat C$3.95, and sales of C$20 or more pay 20%. Low-priced items can leave little profit after fixed fees and shipping, so source with a target margin in mind. This guide suits resellers who will price from sold comparables rather than asking prices.

## What fees do eBay.ca and Poshmark.ca charge resellers?

eBay.ca charges most categories a 13.6% final value fee on the total amount of the sale up to C$7,500, plus a per-order fee, according to [eBay.ca's selling fees page](https://www.ebay.ca/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822). [Poshmark.ca](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/get_started_on_poshmark) charges C$3.95 on sales under C$20 and 20% on sales of C$20 or more.

| Rule | eBay.ca | Poshmark.ca |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listing | First 250 listings a month free, then C$0.30 per listing | Free to list |
| Sale fee, most items | 13.6% of the total amount of the sale up to C$7,500 | C$3.95 flat under C$20; 20% at C$20 or more |
| Per-order fee | C$0.30 for orders of C$10 or less; C$0.40 above C$10 | None listed |
| Books, movies, music | 15.3% | Same as other items |
| What the percentage applies to | Item price plus handling, shipping charged to the buyer, and sales tax | Listing price |
| Card processing | Included in the final value fee | Included, per Poshmark |
| Currency conversion | 3% if eBay converts your funds, rising to 3.25% from October 14, 2026 | Not applicable for CAD sales |

eBay also charges extra final value fees to sellers below its performance standards, and has different fees for Store subscribers. All eBay.ca fees are in Canadian dollars.

## Which pricing approaches work for reselling in Canada?

Reselling pricing approaches in Canada are fixed price from sold comparables, fixed price with offers, auctions for items with uncertain value, bundle discounts, and scheduled markdowns for slow stock.

| Approach | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed price from sold comps | Price near recent sold prices for the same item and condition | Most items | Using asking prices instead of sold prices |
| Fixed price with offers | List a little above your target and accept offers | Clothing, collectibles | Accepting offers below your floor |
| Auction | Start low and let bidders set the price | Rare or hard-to-value items | Selling below cost with few bidders |
| Bundle discount | Lower price for several items to one buyer | Clothing lots, books | Shipping costs on heavy bundles |
| Markdown schedule | Lower prices on a set schedule after weeks unsold | Seasonal stock | Cutting prices on items that were simply new |

Set a floor price for each item before listing: the lowest price that still leaves your target profit after fees and shipping.

## How do you calculate a resale price in Canada?

Calculate a resale price in Canada by starting from sold comparables, subtracting platform fees, shipping, and your item cost, and checking that the profit is worth your time. If it is not, change the price, the platform, or whether you buy the item.

### Step 1: Check sold comparables

Use recent sold listings for the same item, size, and condition.

### Step 2: Calculate platform fees

Use the platform's own rules, including what the percentage applies to.

### Step 3: Subtract shipping and supplies

Postage, packaging, and labels you pay for.

### Step 4: Subtract item cost

What you paid at the thrift store, sale, or auction.

### Step 5: Set aside tax

Resale profit is business income if you resell regularly; see the [reselling and platform income tax guide](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast, average, or survey. Suppose you bought a jacket for C$8, sold comparables suggest C$40, and you sell it on eBay.ca for C$40 plus C$12 shipping to a buyer in Ontario, where 13% HST applies.

The total amount of the sale is (C$40 + C$12) × 1.13 = C$58.76. The final value fee is 13.6% × C$58.76 + C$0.40 = C$8.39. Assuming eBay collects and remits the HST as the marketplace, you receive C$52 before fees. Suppose the label costs you C$12. Profit is C$52 − C$8.39 − C$12 − C$8 = C$23.61.

The same jacket on Poshmark.ca at C$40 pays 20%, C$8, leaving C$32 before your C$8 item cost: C$24. This row leaves shipping out; check Poshmark's shipping terms for who pays the label.

Now a cheaper item. A C$15 shirt on Poshmark.ca pays the flat C$3.95, leaving C$11.05. If you paid C$3, profit is C$8.05, or 54% of the sale price, compared with 60% on the C$40 jacket (C$24 ÷ C$40).

## When should you raise or lower resale prices in Canada?

Raise resale prices in Canada when sold comparables rise, when an item gets many watchers or offers quickly, or when the season turns in its favour. Lower them on a set schedule when items sit unsold well past the usual time for that category.

Do not lower below your floor price; donate or bundle the item instead.

## What reselling pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common reselling pricing mistakes in Canada are pricing from asking prices instead of sold prices, forgetting that eBay's fee includes shipping and tax, and buying low-value items that cannot carry fixed fees and postage. Each shrinks profit you counted on.

For the product safety, fee, and record mistakes resellers make most, and how to fix them, see the [reselling mistakes guide](/side-hustles/reselling-mistakes-canada/).

Weigh items before pricing shipping. Track profit per item, not just sales. Count your time listing and packing.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect reselling prices in Canada?

Regular reselling for profit is generally business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both [CPP shares](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net income. Selling your own used belongings occasionally is treated differently; the [platform income tax guide](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/) explains the line.

GST/HST registration follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all your side hustles. eBay.ca's own fee example shows HST included in the total amount of the sale, so the tax a marketplace collects can raise your fee. For sales you make yourself, see the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and the [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).
