## Which pricing models work for Zapier automation in Canada?

Zapier automation work in Canada is usually priced as paid discovery, a fixed price per automation, a project price for a bundle of workflows, a monthly maintenance retainer, or hourly.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paid discovery | Fixed fee to map the workflow and write a plan | New clients | Free planning that gets built by someone else |
| Per automation | Fixed price by steps, apps, and logic | Defined workflows | Hidden edge cases and messy data |
| Project bundle | One price for several related workflows | Onboarding or sales pipelines | Scope that grows mid-build |
| Maintenance retainer | Monthly hours for fixes, monitoring, and small changes | Clients relying on automations | Unlimited "quick fixes" |
| Hourly | Rate × hours | Troubleshooting, training | Clients surprised by totals |

The client should own the Zapier account and pay for their plan; [Zapier's pricing page](https://zapier.com/pricing) shows plans priced by task volume. Write who owns accounts, the testing period, and what counts as a change.

## How do you calculate a Zapier automation rate in Canada?

Calculate a Zapier automation rate in Canada from a target hourly pay, your billable share, tool costs, and the hours each workflow takes to map, build, test, and document. Then check the price against the time the automation saves the client.

### Step 1: Set a target hourly pay

The Job Bank tables are a broad floor check.

### Step 2: Estimate billable share

Scoping calls, proposals, and learning app changes are unpaid.

### Step 3: Add tools

Your own Zapier and app test accounts, divided across projects.

### Step 4: Estimate build hours

Count steps, apps, filters, and error handling. Add testing and documentation.

### Step 5: Hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; see [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) and the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $50 an hour, above Job Bank's web developer median of $38.46 and near the integration analyst median of $46.15. Suppose 70% of your working time is billable. The billable rate is $50 ÷ 0.70 = $71.43, which you might round to $71 an hour.

Suppose discovery takes 3 hours: 3 × $71 = $213, quoted as $215. A simple two-step automation takes 2 hours: 2 × $71 = $142, quoted as $140. A multi-step workflow with filters and error handling takes 6 hours: 6 × $71 = $426, quoted as $425.

Now check value. Suppose the multi-step workflow saves an admin employee 4 hours a week. At Job Bank's [administrative assistant](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/24789/ca) national median of $26.44 an hour, that is 4 × 52 × $26.44 = $5,499.52 of staff time a year, before payroll costs. A $425 build is easy to justify against that, which supports pricing on value rather than hours alone.

## When should you raise Zapier automation prices in Canada?

Raise Zapier automation prices in Canada when your calendar is full, when you have case studies with measured time savings, or when builds keep running over their hours. Change new quotes first; keep signed projects at their agreed price.

Track hours per automation type and reprice tiers that run over.

## What Zapier automation pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common Zapier automation pricing mistakes in Canada are free discovery, building in your own account, and no maintenance plan. Each leaves you with unpaid work or support you never priced.

Charge for discovery. Build in the client's account. Offer maintenance, or state that later fixes are billed hourly.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect Zapier automation prices in Canada?

Automation income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

When registered, the rate depends on the place of supply, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html).
