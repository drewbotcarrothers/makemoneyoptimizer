Charging for Zapier automation in Canada usually means a paid discovery session, a fixed price per workflow based on the apps and steps it connects, and an optional monthly maintenance fee, with the client paying for their own Zapier plan. Canadian firms publish prices from $1,500 CAD for one workflow at Ontario's Scobert to $25,000 CAD for large custom builds at Aurora Designs, and charge $490 to $750 for a discovery review. A freelancer starting out prices below those firms but above Job Bank's $46.15 median hourly wage for employed system integration analysts, because tools, unpaid scoping, testing and both halves of CPP come out of the same rate.

## Which pricing models work for Zapier automation?

The firms above use the same three pieces: a paid review, a fixed price per workflow, and optional support. Hourly billing is for troubleshooting and training.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paid discovery | Fixed fee to map the workflow and write a plan, credited toward the build | New clients | Free planning that gets built by someone else |
| Per workflow | Fixed price by apps, steps and logic | Defined workflows | Hidden edge cases and messy data |
| Project bundle | One price for several related workflows | Onboarding or sales pipelines | Scope that grows mid-build |
| Maintenance | Monthly hours for fixes, monitoring and small changes | Clients relying on automations | Unlimited "quick fixes" |
| Hourly | Rate × hours | Troubleshooting, training | Clients surprised by totals |

Build in an account the client owns and pays for. Write down who owns the accounts, the testing period and what counts as a change.

## How do you calculate a Zapier automation price?

Start from your target pay, divide by the share of your time that's billable, then multiply by the hours each workflow takes.

```text
Billable rate  = target hourly pay ÷ billable share
Build price    = (mapping + build + testing + handover) hours × billable rate
Discovery      = mapping hours × billable rate, credited toward the build
Maintenance    = monthly hours × billable rate
Value check    = hours saved per week × staff hourly cost × 48 weeks
                 − build price − client's software cost
```

**Set target pay near the Job Bank median.** A $50 target sits above the $38.46 web developer median and near the $46.15 integration analyst median. **Estimate your billable share honestly.** Scoping calls, proposals and keeping up with app changes are unpaid. At 70% billable, $50 ÷ 0.70 = $71.43, rounded to $71 an hour. **Count steps, apps and error handling** before you quote; Scobert says its price depends mainly on the number of apps and steps. The value check uses the 48-working-week method from Scobert's guide.

## What do worked Zapier automation quotes look like?

**Illustrative.** The $71 billable rate, hours and time savings are my assumptions. GST/HST lines apply only once you're registered, at the rate for the client's province.

### Quote 1: paid discovery for an Ottawa property manager

- 3 hours to map the tenant-request process and write a one-page plan: 3 × $71 = $213, quoted as **$215**.
- Credited in full if the client books the build within 30 days, the same approach Scobert and Toronto AI Consulting publish for their $750 reviews.

### Quote 2: one three-step automation for a Toronto cleaning company

- Website form → new contact in the CRM → alert in the team chat.
- Mapping 1 h + build 1.5 h + testing 1 h + handover notes 0.5 h = 4 hours × $71 = $284, quoted as **$285**.
- With 13% HST: $285 × 1.13 = $322.05.
- The client needs a paid Zapier plan because the free plan is limited to two-step Zaps.

### Quote 3: four-workflow lead-to-invoice bundle for a Calgary contractor

- 18 hours × $71 = $1,278, quoted as **$1,280**, including the $215 discovery already paid.
- Balance due: $1,280 − $215 = $1,065. With 5% GST: $1,065 × 1.05 = $1,118.25.
- Value check: if the bundle saves the office manager 5 hours a week at Job Bank's [administrative assistant](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/24789/ca) national median of $26.44, that's 5 × $26.44 × 48 = $6,345.60 of staff time a year. Minus the $1,280 build and Zapier Professional at US$19.99 × 12 = US$239.88 (about $341.59 at 1.4240), the first-year saving is about $4,724.
- Scobert's published price for one workflow starts at $1,500, so a $1,280 four-workflow bundle is a beginner's price.

### Quote 4: maintenance after the build

- 2 hours a month for monitoring, fixes after app changes and small edits: 2 × $71 = $142, quoted as **$140 a month**.
- Extra hours at $71. Unused hours don't roll over.
- Without a plan, fixes are billed hourly with a 1-hour minimum.

## How do you raise Zapier automation prices?

- **Track hours per workflow type.** If simple automations keep taking 5 hours instead of 4, reprice the tier.
- **Collect measured time savings.** A case study showing hours saved supports value-based quotes like the one in Quote 3.
- **Move toward the published range.** Scobert's $1,500 starting price per workflow and $750 review are reference points once you have case studies.
- **Change new quotes first;** keep signed projects at their agreed price. For the scripts, see [how to raise your side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/).

## What Zapier automation pricing mistakes are common?

Common Zapier pricing mistakes are free discovery, building in your own account, and no maintenance plan. Each leaves you with unpaid work or support you never priced.

Charge for discovery and credit it toward the build. Build in the client's account. Offer maintenance, or state that later fixes are billed hourly.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect Zapier automation prices?

Automation income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net earnings; see the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/). GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your side hustles.

When registered, the rate depends on the place of supply, per the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers registration.
