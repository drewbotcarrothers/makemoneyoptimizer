A first print-on-demand sale in Canada usually comes from a few designs for one clearly defined audience, a sample you have ordered and held, and a price that covers the fulfilment cost plus the selling platform's fees. Print-on-demand removes inventory, not the need for a buyer to find and trust the product.

The margin is thin because the printer is paid on every order. That makes the arithmetic before the first sale more important than the number of designs. ## What should you design for a first print-on-demand sale in Canada?

Design for one audience you can name for a first print-on-demand sale in Canada, such as a hobby, a trade, or a local reference, and put three to five designs on one or two products. A narrow audience gives a buyer a reason to pick your shirt over a blank one.

Use only artwork, text, and fonts you have the right to use commercially. Team logos, characters, and song lyrics belong to someone else, and a takedown ends the listing. Read the font licence before you print a word.

Order a sample before you list. Printful's [Canada page](https://www.printful.com/print-on-demand-canada) mentions a sample discount without giving the percentage, so check the current offer in your account. A sample lets you photograph a real item and check colour, placement, and size.

## Where do Canadians find a first print-on-demand buyer?

Canadians find a first print-on-demand buyer through marketplace search, mostly Etsy, through their own store with traffic they bring themselves, and through people in the audience they designed for. A store with no visitors does not sell, however good the design.

Etsy brings search traffic and charges for it on every order. A Shopify store, at the [Shopify pricing](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing) above, keeps more of each order but needs you to send buyers there. For a first sale, Etsy search plus a link shared with the audience is the simpler test.

## What should a first print-on-demand listing say?

A first print-on-demand listing in Canada should say the product, the print method if known, the sizing, the production and shipping time, and that it is made to order. Buyers who expect next-day shipping leave bad reviews.

**Example listing text.** This is a generic example. It is not a listing that produced a sale.

> Unisex cotton tee with a printed trail-map design. Made to order: printing usually adds a few business days before shipping, and the order page shows the estimate. Size chart in the photos, measured flat. Wash inside out in cold water.

Do not call a print "hand-made" or claim a material the product page does not state. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) covers claims made to promote a product.

## How do you build trust without fake reviews?

You build trust on a new print-on-demand listing with photos of the sample you actually received, a size chart measured from that sample, and accurate production times. Mock-ups are fine when labelled as mock-ups. A mock-up presented as a customer photo is a false claim.

Reviews come from real orders. Buying your own product through another account, or swapping reviews with other sellers, is a false signal that can put the shop at risk.

## How should you price the first print-on-demand sale in Canada?

Price the first print-on-demand sale in Canada from the printer's cost for that exact product, size, and shipping destination, plus platform fees, plus what you need left. This guide does not state a Printful product price; check the live catalogue in your account.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Etsy listing | Etsy fees on each order | Thin margins once all four fees are added |
| Shopify store | A monthly plan plus card fees | Paying the plan before any traffic arrives |
| Both | Both | Keeping prices and production times consistent |

### Illustrative example

Suppose a Canadian buyer orders a tee on Etsy for $35 with shipping built into the price, and ignore sales tax for simplicity.

- Transaction fee: 6.5% × $35 = $2.28
- Payment processing: 3% × $35 + $0.25 = $1.30
- Regulatory operating fee: 0.50% × $35 = $0.18 (assumed on the same total; confirm the base on Etsy's page)
- Listing fee: $0.20 USD ≈ $0.28 CAD at the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4246 on 2 October 2026 ([daily exchange rates](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/))
- Fees total: $2.28 + $1.30 + $0.18 + $0.28 = $4.04

Suppose the printer charges an assumed $22 for the shirt, printing, and shipping. What is left is $35 − $4.04 − $22 = $8.96 before tax and your design time. At $30, fees are $1.95 + $1.15 + $0.15 + $0.28 = $3.53, which leaves $30 − $3.53 − $22 = $4.47. These figures are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first print-on-demand sale look like?

A 14-day plan for a first print-on-demand sale in Canada should produce a sample in hand, three to five listings for one audience, and a price checked against fees. A sale inside 14 days is not guaranteed.

### Days 1 and 2: Pick the audience and read the terms

Name the audience in one sentence. Read the Printful Canada page and the three Etsy fee pages, and put the percentages in a sheet.

### Days 3 to 5: Design and order a sample

Make three to five designs with assets you can license. Order one sample to your address.

### Days 6 to 9: Photograph and list

Photograph the sample. Measure it for the size chart. Publish listings with real production times.

### Days 10 to 14: Share and adjust

Share the listing with the audience you designed for, in places where sharing is allowed. If nothing sells, rewrite titles in buyer words before you design more.

## What mistakes block a first print-on-demand sale in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first print-on-demand sale in Canada are designing for everyone, using artwork you do not have rights to, and pricing without adding every fee. Each one either gets the listing removed or erases the margin.

Skipping the sample means photos and sizing you cannot vouch for. Listing hundreds of designs adds listing fees without adding buyers. Promising fast shipping on a made-to-order item invites refunds.

## Which tax rules apply to a first print-on-demand sale in Canada?

Print-on-demand income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep order exports, printer invoices, platform fee statements, and sample receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Selling print-on-demand products is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
