Local SEO freelancing in Canada means fixing how a real local business shows up in Google Search and Maps: its Business Profile, its reviews process, its citations on other directories and the service pages on its own site. Surveys of the trade put the typical freelancer at about US$75 an hour and the average local SEO retainer at about US$1,150 to US$1,557 a month, and you can start with free Google tools and a checklist. What you sell is a documented cleanup and a monthly routine, never a guaranteed ranking.

## Who does local SEO freelancing suit?

Local SEO suits careful, organized people who like fixing details and explaining them in plain language: hours, categories, a service area, a matching phone number, a review request that actually gets sent. It fits a side schedule well because most work is asynchronous audits, edits and a monthly report. It is a poor fit if you want to sell guaranteed leads, if you are careless with logins or if your clients are online-only brands.

Google's [policy overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en) says a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours to qualify for a Business Profile. Online-only businesses, lead-generation agents and ongoing classes at a location you do not own are listed as ineligible. That rule shapes your client list: plumbers, dentists, cafés, physio clinics and mobile services that visit customers qualify; a dropshipping store does not.

You work as the owner's editor. Google's [ownership guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en) say an authorized representative must not claim a profile without the owner's express consent, must not make false or misleading claims, and should work with the owner on verification and transfer ownership back on request. Build that into every agreement.

## What does the math look like?

The formula is simple: **monthly income = clients × monthly fee**, and **effective hourly rate = fee ÷ hours spent**. Plug in the published benchmarks and an assumed time budget.

**Illustrative.** The hours per client and the beginner fee below are assumptions; the US$1,150 and US$1,228 retainers are survey averages converted at 1.4240.

| Scenario | Clients | Monthly fee (CAD) | Hours per client | Monthly revenue | Effective hourly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner (assumed fee) | 2 | $400 | 6 | $800 | $66.67 |
| Freelancer average (Ahrefs US$1,150) | 3 | $1,637.60 | 12 | $4,912.80 | $136.47 |
| Freelancer average (BrightLocal US$1,228) | 3 | $1,748.67 | 12 | $5,246.01 | $145.72 |

A one-time cleanup works the same way: at the BrightLocal freelance median of US$75 (C$106.80) an hour, an assumed eight-hour cleanup is 8 × $106.80 = $854.40. At the Job Bank median of $38.46, the same eight hours is $307.68. That gap is why a portfolio of before-and-after audits matters: it moves you from the employee wage toward the freelance rate.

Subtract tools. One BrightLocal Track location at US$41 a month is about C$58.38 at 1.4240; a Whitespark ranking grid starts at US$10 (about C$14.24). With two beginner clients at $800 a month, tools are 2% to 7% of revenue.

## What local SEO services can you sell? 18 ideas

Package services around outcomes an owner can see. These are the services local SEO clients ask for most, with a reason each one sells.

1. **Business Profile cleanup.** Name, categories, hours, phone, website and service area aligned with [Google's representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en); the easiest first sale.
2. **Category and services audit.** Primary category drives which searches a profile appears in; owners rarely revisit it.
3. **Photo refresh.** Real photos of the storefront, team and work replace stock images.
4. **Review request system.** A short link, a printed card and a follow-up text; BrightLocal found 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.
5. **Review reply service.** 89% of consumers expect owners to respond, and 81% expect it within a week (BrightLocal, 2026).
6. **Google Posts calendar.** Weekly offers or updates; pairs with the review reply plan as a monthly retainer.
7. **Citation audit and cleanup.** Fix the name, address and phone on Yelp, YellowPages.ca, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places and industry directories.
8. **Bing Places and Apple Business Connect setup.** Many owners only ever touched Google.
9. **Service-area page writing.** One page per real service and city the business serves, written with a writer if that is not your skill.
10. **On-page fixes.** Title tags, headings, NAP in the footer, a click-to-call button; BrightLocal says on-site optimization is the most in-demand service.
11. **LocalBusiness schema markup.** Structured data that repeats the profile facts; test with Google's Rich Results Test.
12. **Search Console setup and monthly report.** Free data on which searches bring clicks.
13. **Ranking grid reports.** A map grid shows where a profile appears across a city; simple to explain to owners.
14. **Competitor gap report.** Compare categories, review counts and photos with the top three map results.
15. **Multi-location consistency.** Franchises and clinics with several sites need the same facts everywhere.
16. **Bilingual listings for Quebec and New Brunswick clients.** French descriptions and posts where the business serves French speakers.
17. **Suspension and duplicate cleanup.** Merge duplicates and prepare reinstatement requests with real documents.
18. **AI search visibility check.** BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 45% of consumers used ChatGPT or similar for local recommendations, up from 6%; checking what AI tools say about a client is a new add-on.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

You can go from zero to a first paid cleanup in about four weeks: learn the rules, build sample audits, write an offer, then pitch businesses with a specific gap.

### Week 1: Learn the rules and the tools

- Read Google's [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en), [ownership guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en) and [policy overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en). Write the eligibility rule, the name rule and the consent rule in one sentence each.
- Read Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors report and BrightLocal's consumer review survey for talking points.
- Install Screaming Frog (free) and start a BrightLocal 14-day trial at the end of the week, not the start, so it covers your audits.

### Week 2: Build three sample audits

- Pick three public businesses in your town in different trades.
- For each, record categories, hours, review count and most recent review date, photo count, website title tag and whether the phone number matches across Google, Bing, Apple and Yelp.
- Turn each into a one-page audit using the template below. Do not log in or claim anything.

### Week 3: Write the offer and pitch ten businesses

- Write a fixed-scope cleanup offer and a monthly plan (template below).
- Pick ten businesses with a visible gap: wrong hours, no replies to reviews, a keyword-stuffed name, no photos in a year.
- Send a short note naming the gap, attach the relevant part of a sample audit and offer a 20-minute call. [How to get your first local SEO client in Canada](/side-hustles/local-seo-first-client-canada/) has more outreach angles.

### Week 4: Deliver and convert to monthly

- Get written consent and manager access from the owner, never their password.
- Make only the changes in the agreed scope, screenshot before and after, and send the report.
- Offer the monthly plan with a 30-day report date. Ask for a testimonial and a referral.

## What should you never sell?

Never sell fake reviews, keyword-stuffed business names, virtual-office addresses or guaranteed rankings. Google's representation guidelines require the real-world name and an accurate address or service area, and its policy overview says businesses using a P.O. box are not permitted and can be suspended. In Canada, the Competition Bureau [warned in January 2024](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2024/01/online-reviews-posted-by-employees-businesses-could-be-liable.html) that anyone who writes or permits reviews that give a false or misleading impression could be liable under the Competition Act, so writing reviews for a client exposes both of you. Ask customers for honest reviews, and never offer a gift or a discount for one; see [how to get your first Google reviews in Canada](/side-hustles/first-google-reviews-side-hustle-canada/).

## What mistakes do new local SEO freelancers make?

- **Promising rankings.** Google decides what to show; sell the checklist and the report.
- **Taking the owner's password.** Ask to be added as a manager so the owner stays the primary owner.
- **Stuffing the business name.** "Joe's Plumbing Toronto Emergency Plumber" breaks the representation guidelines and risks suspension.
- **Creating listings for ineligible businesses.** Online-only stores and lead-gen sites can't have profiles.
- **Underpricing the monthly plan.** 71% of freelancers bill under US$1,000 per client (BrightLocal); start low if you must, but write a review date into the agreement.
- **Skipping the report.** Owners renew what they can see; screenshots and a one-page summary are the product.

## Which tax rules apply to local SEO freelancing in Canada?

Local SEO income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Tool subscriptions, a share of internet and phone, and a home-office share can be deductible; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing, social media and other side-hustle revenue to the same total. Once registered, use the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) for the rate that applies to each client. The [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule does not apply to SEO work. Fees for U.S. tools are paid in USD, so record the CAD amount your card was charged.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered. Quebec businesses generally need French listing descriptions and posts, so bilingual freelancers have an edge.
- **Alberta:** GST only; Job Bank's Alberta SEO specialist median is $40.23.
- **British Columbia:** Job Bank's median is $37.50; PST is separate from GST, so check whether a B.C. client's purchase is PST-taxable.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table; the other Atlantic provinces charge 15%.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

The rate and retainer figures come from surveys of BrightLocal's and Ahrefs' audiences, which skew toward U.S. practitioners and are reported in U.S. dollars; Canadian small-business budgets, especially outside big cities, are often lower. Job Bank wages describe employees, not freelancers, and use 2023–2024 data. Tool prices are U.S.-dollar list prices on October 9, 2026, and the CAD conversions use the Bank of Canada rate for October 8, 2026, so your card statement will differ. The consumer survey covers U.S. adults. Rankings depend on Google, competitors and location, so no figure here predicts where a client will appear. The tax section is general information, not advice for your situation.
