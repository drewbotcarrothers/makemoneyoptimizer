## Who does local SEO freelancing in Canada suit?

Local SEO freelancing in Canada suits people who like accurate details: hours, service area, categories, and a website sentence that matches the door. Trades, clinics, and shops with a real visit or a real trip to the customer are the clients. Local SEO freelancing is a poor fit if you want to sell guaranteed leads, if you are careless with logins, or if the business is online-only.

Google's [policy overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en) says that to qualify for a Business Profile, a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours. The same page says online-only businesses, lead-generation agents, and ongoing classes at a location you do not own are examples of businesses that are not eligible. If your client does not meet that bar, do not create a profile to force it.

You are an editor with permission. Google's [ownership guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en) say an authorized representative must not claim a profile without the owner's express consent, must not make false or misleading claims, and should work with the owner on verification. The phone number and website on the profile should be the business's own.

## How do you start local SEO freelancing in Canada?

Starting local SEO freelancing in Canada means learning the public rules, auditing a listing you do not control, and selling a written checklist. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Read the three Google pages

Read the [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en), the [ownership guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en), and the [policy overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en). Write down, in your own words, who is eligible, what the name must be, and that you need consent. If you cannot explain those three points, you are not ready to log in for someone else.

### Step 2: Audit three public listings in one city

Pick businesses you could describe, such as a cleaner, a lawn route, or a clinic. Note missing hours, a keyword-stuffed name, or a website that disagrees with the profile. Do not claim the profiles. The audit is practice.

### Step 3: Write a one-time offer

Example shape: confirm eligibility, align name, categories, hours, phone, and website, list what you will not change, and deliver a one-page report. Monthly posting can be a second offer. Ranking guarantees are not an offer you should sell.

### Step 4: Say what you will not do

No fake reviews. No borrowed addresses. No profiles for online-only brands. No keyword lists stuffed into the business name. Google's guidelines and the policy overview are the reason. Write the refusals into the proposal so the client hears them before they pay.

### Step 5: Pitch five businesses with a specific gap

Use the public audit. "Your hours on the profile do not match the door" is a pitch. "I will get you to number one" is not. One follow-up is enough.

### Step 6: Get consent and a path to verification

The owner adds you, or sits with you while you work. Google's ownership page says to work directly with the owner on verification and to transfer ownership back when they ask. Save that sentence in the agreement.

### Step 7: Change only what the report listed

Name, categories, hours, phone, website, service area, and a short description that is true. Photograph the real location if you are there and the owner agrees. Stop when the list is done. Extra edits "while you are in there" are how a profile gets messy.

### Step 8: Match the website to the same facts

The phone number and the city should agree. A thin service page can be a writing task. If you are not the writer, say so and point the client at a writer, or learn that piece before you include it.

### Step 9: Review after the first two clients

Note how long verification took and which edits Google rejected. Update your checklist. Keep the price for the scope you wrote. Do not add a rank promise because a client asked for one in the invoice email.

## What local SEO mistakes are common in Canada?

Common local SEO mistakes in Canada are promising a rank, creating a profile for a business that is not eligible, and editing without the owner in the loop. The checklist is the product. The map is Google's.

Stuffing the city and a keyword into the business name to "help SEO" breaks the representation guidelines. Use the name on the sign.

Fake reviews are not a service. Ask the owner to invite real customers through the process Google provides. Do not write the reviews yourself.

A P.O. box is not a workaround. Google's policy overview says businesses with a P.O. box address are not permitted and can be suspended. If the business visits customers, use the service-area option the guidelines describe, and confirm you are allowed to.

## Which tax rules apply to local SEO freelancing in Canada?

Local SEO income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for this work follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement, the report, and the payments. Report the income on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing, social, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Local SEO work is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.

The profile rules are not tax, but they are the professional boundary. Re-read Google's three pages when a client asks you to bend a name, an address, or a review. The invoice is easier to defend when the report lists only changes those pages allow.
