Making a first Shopify store sale in Canada means taking one real order for a product you can deliver, after a test checkout, with shipping and a refund rule you have already tried. A Shopify store suits people who know the product because they make it, already sell it locally, or can support a digital file they use themselves.

The first sale is one product and one buyer, not a catalogue and an ad account. Opening the store is covered in [how to start a Shopify store in Canada](/side-hustles/shopify-store-side-hustle-canada/). Selling the same kind of object on someone else's site first is [reselling](/side-hustles/reselling-side-hustle-canada/).

| Fact | Detail for a first Shopify sale in Canada |
| --- | --- |
| Catalogue | One product you can ship or deliver, with your own photos |
| Plan price | Basic is $49 CAD a month, or $37 CAD a month billed yearly, on Shopify's [Canada pricing](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing) page. Confirm it |
| Card rate | The same page shows card rates from 2.8% + 30¢ CAD on the Basic description |
| Intro price | The page describes 3 days free, then $1 a month for 3 months, on many plans. That is not the ongoing cost |
| Claims | Product claims must not mislead. [Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) |
| GST/HST | Small-supplier threshold of $30,000. [When to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) |

A digital file with a lighter checkout is a [Notion template](/side-hustles/notion-templates-side-hustle-canada/). Repeating order chores can later be [Zapier automation](/side-hustles/zapier-automation-side-hustle-canada/).

## What should you sell for a first Shopify order in Canada?

The first Shopify order in Canada should be one product you can hand to a buyer, with a ship-from place and a delivery window you have seen. "Something trending" is not a product. A thing you make, a thing you already sell locally, or a file you have tested is a product.

Start with a promise you can keep inside Canada. Other countries add duties the buyer may not expect. If you import stock to sell, the CBSA [guide to importing commercial goods](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/import/guide-eng.html) is the path, not a personal shopping story.

Dropshipping from a supplier you have never tested is a common way to miss the date on the first order. This guide does not treat an unseen supplier as the way to make that sale.

## Where do Canadians find a first Shopify buyer?

Canadians find a first Shopify buyer among people who already know the product: local customers, a class, or buyers from a marketplace listing you already fulfilled. Paid ads are optional and easy to spend before the checkout works.

Tell the people who have already held the item or used the file. A note to a past local buyer, if they asked to hear about a store, is a different message from a purchased list. Put the link where those people already look, including the marketplace listing that points at the product you can still supply.

A public launch post is fine when the community allows commerce. Read that group's rules. Do not create fake buyer comments under the post.

If the message is a commercial email or text, [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies. ISED's [getting consent to send email](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page says you need consent before those messages. Express consent means the person agreed. Implied consent is limited, including some existing business relationships, and that page says it may last up to two years, or six months after an inquiry. The message needs your name, a mailing address, contact details valid for at least 60 days, and an unsubscribe mechanism acted on within 10 business days at no cost. This is not legal advice.

Place a test order yourself before you send anyone the link. Pack it as you would for a stranger. Fix the hole the test shows. Then tell people.

## What should a first product listing say?

A first Shopify listing in Canada should state what the buyer receives, the price, where it ships from, how long your own test took, and how a refund works. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) is the standard for those claims.

**Example listing.** This is a generic example. It is not a listing that sold.

> [Product name]. Handmade in [city], ships from [province] to addresses in Canada. Price is [amount] before tax at checkout. A test parcel to my own address took [the days you measured]. Refunds: contact me within [your window] if the item arrives damaged. Photo 2 shows the real colour in daylight. This page does not promise a delivery date the carrier has not given you.

Skip borrowed lifestyle photos. A phone picture of the object you will ship is the listing.

## How do you prove the store is real without fake reviews?

You prove a new Shopify store in Canada with photos of the actual product, a test order you placed, and a refund rule you can perform. Imported reviews, a star widget with no buyers, and a "as seen in" line you cannot support are misleading.

Show the packed parcel from the test, without pretending a customer wrote about it. If a person you know buys and later agrees you may say they bought it, say that. Do not write their sentence for them.

The order export is your record: price, card fee, shipping, product cost, and any refund. The dashboard revenue is not profit.

## How should you price the first Shopify sale in Canada?

Price the first Shopify sale in Canada after the plan cost, the card fee on Shopify's pricing page, the product, and the mailer. Shopify's [Canada pricing](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing) page lists Basic at $49 CAD a month, or $37 CAD a month billed yearly, and shows card rates from 2.8% + 30¢ CAD on that Basic description. It also describes 3 days free, then $1 a month for 3 months. Write the standard price into your sketch. The intro months are not the ongoing cost. Confirm the live page, because it changes.

| Path | What has to be true | What the first order must cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One shipped good | You can pack it this week | Card fee, mailer, product cost, and a plan that continues after the intro price |
| Digital file | You have duplicated it on a fresh account | Card fee and the plan. No mailer, and still no promise of ten orders |

One order often does not cover a month of Basic. That is a reason to know the price, not a reason to hide it.

**Illustrative example.** Suppose you are past the introductory months and you use Basic at the yearly rate on the pricing page, $37 for the month. Suppose one order arrives at an assumed $40 product price. Card rates from 2.8% + 30¢ CAD mean 0.028 × $40 = $1.12, plus $0.30, which is $1.42. Suppose a mailer at $2. The sketch is $40 − $1.42 − $2 − $37 = −$0.42 before the cost of the product itself and before your time. Suppose instead the product is a digital file with no mailer and you assume three orders at $40: card fees are 3 × $1.42 = $4.26, and $120 − $4.26 − $37 = $78.74 before any other fee, refund, or hours. These totals are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result. Confirm the live card rate.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first Shopify sale look like?

A 14-day plan for a first Shopify sale in Canada should produce one listed product, a test order, and a few messages to people who already understand the product. A paid order from a stranger is not guaranteed. A large ad spend is not part of this plan.

### Days 1 and 2: Prove delivery

Write what the product is, who it is for, and whether you ship it. Send a sample to yourself, or duplicate the digital file. Time it. Read the pricing page and write Basic's standard monthly or yearly figure next to the intro offer's end.

### Days 3 to 5: List one product

Photograph the real item. Draft the refund and shipping lines. Open the store and list that product. Skip extra apps. Place a test order and note the card fee.

### Days 6 to 9: Tell people who already know it

Fix what the test showed. Message buyers or peers who asked to hear when the product was ready, using the consent rules if the note is commercial email. Point a local listing at the store only if you can still fulfill both.

### Days 10 to 14: Take the order or pause

If an order arrives, pack it the way you packed the test, and log the fee. If no order arrives, decide whether the next step is another round of people who know the product, or cancelling before the standard price is a surprise. Shopify's pricing page says you can cancel. Do not "fix" a quiet store by importing unseen stock.

## What mistakes block a first Shopify sale in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first Shopify sale in Canada are subscribing before the product exists, advertising a broken checkout, and listing goods you have not held. The intro price is temporary. The test order is the point of week one.

Borrowed photos and a page of lookalike products are hard to support. Fake reviews are a misleading claim. Promising two-day delivery from a supplier in another country creates the refund.

Forgetting duties on imported stock is a cost problem and a border problem. Use the CBSA guide before you treat a personal exemption as inventory.

## Which tax rules apply to a first Shopify sale in Canada?

Shopify store income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep order exports, fee reports, and receipts for stock and postage. Report the store on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. A store is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside. Shopify can calculate tax when you configure it. The configuration is not a legal opinion. Check the result against the CRA table.

Digital products add complexity. The CRA's [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) describes rules, in effect since 1 July 2021, for certain non-resident suppliers and distribution platform operators. A platform collection line does not replace your small-supplier test or Quebec's QST. Importing goods to sell follows the CBSA [commercial import guide](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/import/guide-eng.html).
