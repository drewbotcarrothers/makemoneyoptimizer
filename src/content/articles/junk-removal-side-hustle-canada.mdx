Junk removal in Canada means hauling furniture, appliances, renovation scraps and garage clutter away for households and small businesses, then paying the posted fee at a landfill, depot or recycler and keeping the difference. HomeStars lists typical Toronto prices of $150 to $350 for a small mixed household load and $80 to $150 for a single item, while a city scale ticket runs from a $19 to $25 minimum. The margin is your labour, your trip and your sorting, so the people who do well quote from photos, separate the fee from the labour and know what the facility refuses before they load.

## Who does junk removal suit?

Junk removal suits people who can lift a dresser safely, have (or can borrow) a pickup, van, SUV with a roof rack or a trailer, and can work Saturday mornings when most clean-outs happen. It rewards organized people: the money is made by sorting reuse, scrap metal and recycling away from the landfill scale.

It's a poor fit if you have a back injury, no legal place to tip, or a plan that starts with financing a dump trailer before you have a single client. It's also hard in a dense downtown with no parking, where every job means a long carry.

Good fits:

- Students with a summer and a pickup (Scudamore's start).
- Tradespeople and renovators who already pass the landfill weekly.
- Anyone already doing moving help, lawn care or handyman work who keeps hearing "can you take this too?"
- Rural and small-town operators where no franchise covers the area (Dunk Disposal's base in Caledon and Dufferin).

## How does junk removal pay?

You get paid by the job, usually by how much of your truck or trailer the load fills, with flat prices for single items and a minimum charge. HomeStars' city guides say most companies charge by volume, some offer flat fees for common jobs, and tipping fees vary by municipality. Out of that price you pay the disposal fee, fuel, any helper, and your time.

| Job type | Typical price, Toronto (HomeStars, checked October 9, 2026) |
|---|---|
| Single item (chair, small appliance) | $80–$150 |
| Furniture (sofa, mattress, dining set) | $120–$300 |
| Small mixed household load | $150–$350 |
| Yard waste or seasonal debris | $100–$350 |
| Construction or renovation debris | $200–$600+ |

HomeStars' older Canada-wide guide, with costs as of November 2020, put average job totals at $517.87 in Toronto, $428.72 in Ottawa, $417.99 in Hamilton, $825.08 in Vancouver, $655.81 in Edmonton and $525 in Winnipeg. Those averages include big commercial jobs (Toronto's maximum was $24,000), so the median small job is well under them.

## What does it cost to dump a load in Canada?

Disposal is priced by weight at most municipal sites, with a minimum charge per visit, and higher rates for loads mixed with recyclables. Two published 2026 examples:

## How much can you make doing junk removal?

A part-timer doing a few small loads on weekends can clear a few hundred dollars a week after disposal and fuel, but take-home depends on load size, travel, disposal rates and how often you need a helper. Published Canadian figures that frame it:

- **Price per job:** $150 to $350 for a small household load and $80 to $150 for a single item in HomeStars' Toronto guide.
- **Labour value:** Job Bank's furniture-mover wages are $16.55 low, $22.00 median and $30.29 high per hour across Canada (reference period 2023–2024). If your profit per hour is under that range, a moving-company job pays better.
- **Founder example:** Scudamore told CNBC he broke even within the first couple of weeks of 1989 and made about $1,700 profit that first summer, working alone with a $700 truck.

This guide doesn't state a Canada-wide average income for junk removal operators, because no public source collects one. Your own number comes from a simple job log: price charged, disposal paid, kilometres and hours.

## What does the math look like on a load?

The formula is: price − disposal − fuel and vehicle − helper = your pay, then divide by your hours.

**Illustrative.** The prices below come from HomeStars' Toronto ranges, the disposal from Calgary's and Toronto's posted rates, and the helper wage from Job Bank's median. The load weights, fuel cost and hours are assumptions.

| Scenario | Price | Weight and disposal | Fuel/vehicle (assumed) | Helper (assumed hours × $22) | Your hours (assumed) | Your pay | Per hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single sofa, solo, Calgary | $120 | 60 kg → $25 minimum | $10 | none | 1.0 | $85 | $85.00 |
| Small mixed load, solo, Calgary | $250 | 300 kg → $33.90 | $15 | none | 2.0 | $201.10 | $100.55 |
| Small mixed load, solo, Toronto depot | $250 | 300 kg → $56.96 | $15 | none | 2.0 | $178.04 | $89.02 |
| Basement clean-out with helper, Toronto depot | $600 | 900 kg → $170.87 | $30 | 4 h → $88 | 4.0 | $311.13 | $77.78 |

Check one row: $250 − $33.90 − $15 = $201.10, and $201.10 ÷ 2 hours = $100.55 an hour. The per-hour figure drops sharply when you count quoting time, driving between jobs that didn't book, and days with no work. If you book two small loads in a weekend, that's about $350 to $400 of pay in this sketch, before tax and before saving for vehicle wear.

Recyclables mixed into a Calgary load change the answer: at the $180 surcharge rate, the 300 kg load costs 0.3 × $180 = $54.00 instead of $33.90. Sorting cardboard and metal out before the scale pays you directly.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

### Week 1: Rules and a practice load

- Look up your landfill or transfer station page. Write down the per-tonne rate, the minimum, the hours, and the refused list.
- Find the paint drop-off (Product Care in the eight listed provinces, the city's hazardous-waste depot elsewhere), a scrap yard and one charity that takes furniture.
- Take a load of your own junk. Pay the real fee, keep the scale ticket, and time the round trip.

### Week 2: Gear, insurance and an offer

- Buy gloves and ratchet straps. Practise securing a dresser so it doesn't shift.
- Get an insurance quote and ask about vehicle use.
- Write a one-page offer: three load sizes, single-item price, minimum, and your "won't take" list.
- Check whether your city requires a business licence for hauling.

### Week 3: First clients

- Tell 20 people you know, and post once in local community groups with a photo of your vehicle and your price for a single item.
- Message five realtors and two property managers with the realtor template below.
- Quote every job from photos. Book two.

### Week 4: Review

- Compare each job's price with disposal, fuel and hours. Raise your minimum if single items lose money.
- Ask each client for a review and a referral.
- Decide whether a helper, a dolly or a trailer is justified by actual jobs.

## How should you run each job?

The repeatable workflow is: photos → quote → confirm in writing → arrive and re-check → load → sort → dump and recycle → send receipt and invoice.

## What mistakes do new junk haulers make?

- **Quoting without photos.** A "few boxes" becomes a basement. Quote from photos and reprice on site if the pile grew.
- **Ignoring the minimum.** A single chair can cost a $19 or $25 minimum plus the trip; set your single-item price above that.
- **Loading unsorted.** Calgary's recyclable surcharge is $180 a tonne versus $113. Sort first.
- **Taking banned items.** Paint, propane, batteries, tires and refrigerant appliances often have separate rules. A load the site refuses is a wasted trip.
- **Overloading the vehicle.** Your payload limit is on the door sticker and in the manual. Two trips beat one unsafe one.
- **Buying a trailer first.** Use the vehicle you have until your job log shows you're turning down work.
- **Keeping scrap value quietly.** If you plan to keep copper or metal, say so in writing.
- **Skipping workers' compensation questions.** If you pay a helper, check your province's rules before the first job.

## Which tax rules apply to junk removal in Canada?

Junk removal is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. Disposal fees, fuel, the business share of vehicle costs, gloves, straps, insurance and a helper's pay are deductible business expenses. Keep scale tickets, receipts and a kilometre log.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count moving help and other side-hustle revenue in the same total. Junk removal isn't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. Once registered, charge GST/HST on the whole invoice, including the disposal portion, at the rate for the province where the work happens; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered. Toronto's drop-off depot rate is $189.86 a tonne.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only. Calgary's landfill page says no GST is charged on its rates, but you charge GST on your invoice once registered.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered. Product Care's paint program doesn't list Quebec, so use the municipal hazardous-waste depot.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** provincial sales tax runs separately from GST; check whether your services are PST-taxable in your province.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Disposal rates are set by each municipality and usually change every January; the Calgary and Toronto figures are 2026 rates checked on October 9, 2026, and your city's may be higher or lower. HomeStars' price ranges describe what customers typically pay professional companies in a city, not what a new operator will be able to charge, and its Canada-wide averages date from November 2020. Job Bank wages describe employees, not self-employed haulers. Amazon.ca prices move daily. The worked math is illustrative, with assumed weights, fuel costs and hours. Insurance coverage, workers' compensation and licensing differ by province and city, and nothing here is tax, legal or insurance advice.
