## Who does junk removal in Canada suit?

Junk removal in Canada suits people who can carry a dresser, who have a vehicle they are allowed to load, and who will drive to the facility the same day. A Saturday, a pair of gloves, and the patience to sort paint out of a "misc" pile are the real tools. Junk removal is a poor fit if you have nowhere legal to tip, if you will not refuse hazardous items, or if the plan is a trailer payment before the first paid load.

Commercial loads are often priced differently from a resident's carload. Read the facility's page for the words "commercial" or "business" before you promise a fee you saw on a resident flyer.

## How do you start junk removal in Canada?

Starting junk removal in Canada means learning one facility's rules, then selling small loads you can lift. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Pick the vehicle and the facility

Use a car or SUV you already own. Open the facility's web page. Note hours, accepted items, banned items, and whether a business pays a different rate. Save the link in the note you give clients.

### Step 2: Write what you will not take

Paint, propane, unknown chemicals, needles, and anything the facility bans. Refrigerators and other refrigerant appliances stay off the list until you have read the rule that applies. The no-list is how you avoid a car full of material you cannot tip.

### Step 3: Build a quote that separates labour and the fee

You look at the pile, you check today's posted fee, you add stairs and the drive. The client sees both numbers. If the pile grows when you arrive, you stop and reprice before you load.

### Step 4: Buy gloves, straps, and a tarp

Practise tying a bulky item so it does not shift. A dolly is optional until the work proves you need it.

### Step 5: Ask for an insurance quote

Say you enter homes, carry furniture, and haul to a disposal site. Ask whether a personal auto policy covers this use before you rely on it. This is not insurance advice.

### Step 6: Do one practice load of your own unwanted items

Drive it to the facility. Pay the real fee. Time the round trip. That number replaces the guess in your quote.

### Step 7: Tell five households

People who are moving, renovating, or cleaning a garage. Offer a look at the pile, not a flat price sight unseen. Mention the no-list in the same message.

### Step 8: Run the first paid load with photos

Photograph the pile before you touch it. Ask what the bags contain. Load only what you priced. Get a receipt at the facility. Send the client the receipt line and the invoice the same day.

### Step 9: After three loads, decide if a dolly or a helper belongs

If every job was a dresser, buy the dolly from the earnings, not from a credit card in week one. If you bring a second person, check that province's workers' compensation rules before you direct their work.

## What is easy to miss on a junk-removal load in Canada?

Junk removal in Canada goes wrong when bags are not opened, when a "small pile" includes a shed's worth of bricks, and when the facility closes in an hour. Ask what is in the bags. Count the awkward pieces. Check the clock against the posted hours.

Weight limits on your vehicle are in the manual and on the door sticker. A car is not a dump truck. Two trips at a fair price beat one overloaded trip.

Metal that a scrap yard will pay for is still the client's material until your note says you may keep it. Write that line if you want it. Do not quietly peel the copper out of a job you priced as disposal.

## Which tax rules apply to junk removal in Canada?

GST/HST for junk removal follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule.
