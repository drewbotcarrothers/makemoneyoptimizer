## How much does moving help cost to start in Canada?

Moving help in Canada starts at about $70–$160 when you already own boots you trust on stairs, about $150–$380 when boots and a furniture dolly are still to buy, and about $400–$900 when a liability quote you accepted and a larger blanket set wait until a few jobs are real. The ranges are typical Canadian retail prices to verify locally. They are not quotes. They assume the client supplies the vehicle for the first jobs.

| How you start | One-time cash, about | What you can carry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean labour kit | $70–$160 | Work gloves, a small set of moving blankets, and tie-down straps. Enough for a sofa and a table, not a three-bedroom house. You have asked about liability insurance even if the quote is still in your inbox. |
| Typical helper start | $150–$380 | The lean kit, closed-toe boots if you needed them, and a furniture dolly once you know the jobs include more than boxes. Still no truck. |
| Doing it properly | $400–$900 | The typical kit, more blankets, a liability quote you accepted, and a dolly you have already used. Supplying your own truck is a separate decision, with whatever plate and insurance that province requires for that use. |

In Ontario, the province's [Hiring a mover](https://www.ontario.ca/page/hiring-mover) page says moving companies are not licensed by the province, and that consumer contracts for moving services must be in writing if they are worth more than $50. The page says those contracts include the mover's name and contact information, a description and itemized prices, the total, start and end dates, and payment terms. It also says a mover cannot charge more than 10 percent above the estimate unless the customer needs additional supplies or services and agrees to the increase. Other provinces publish their own pages. Ontario is the example this guide can cite, not a national code. Write the note even when the job is under that threshold.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for moving help in Canada?

Ongoing moving help costs in Canada are gloves you replace, fuel only if you drove, parking, and insurance that does not shrink between moves, and the break-even sketch further down keeps this guide's assumed fees. Retail ranges are typical Canadian retail prices to verify locally, not a study. Moving help break-even in the sketch is a method, not an hourly rate.

**Gloves and blankets.** Gloves wear out. Blankets get torn. Replace them when they stop doing the job. A ripped blanket is not a saving.

**Stairs and time.** A studio with an elevator and a third-floor walk-up are different hours. The note should name flights before you start.

**Vehicle.** $0 if the client supplies it. If you drive your own car to the job, log the kilometres. Supplying the truck is a later column, with its own insurance question.

**Damage.** A dresser that does not fit a landing is a conversation before you force it. The Ontario page's 10 percent rule, where it applies, is not a licence to invent extras at noon.

**Unpaid scope.** "While you are here, the piano" and a second address that was never on the note are how a morning becomes a loss.

### Step 1: Add the one-time gear

One-time gear means gloves, blankets, and straps, plus boots or a dolly only if you had to buy them.

### Step 2: Add the fixed costs

Fixed costs mean insurance you accepted. Invoicing software waits until a second job would otherwise live in a text.

### Step 3: List the variable cost of the jobs you did

Variable cost means parking, fuel if you drove, gloves you replaced, and the hours, including the piece that would not turn.

Contribution equals fees collected minus variable costs. Gear is earned back when contribution covers the startup. A truck payment on two labour-only mornings is not in this sketch because the truck is not the offer.

A piano, a second address, and a truck payment are not in the $385. Do not copy $200. If Ontario's writing rule applies because the job is over $50, the note still has to exist before the first box.

## Which tax rules apply to a moving help budget in Canada?

Moving help income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. Gloves and a torn blanket you replace are current expenses, while a dolly or a vehicle that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion if you bought it for the work. GST/HST for moving help uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the written notes, the e-transfers, and the receipts. Report moving help on Form T2125 with the T1. If you drive, keep a kilometre log and claim the business portion only. Do not claim a truck as 100 percent business because you helped one friend move.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add junk removal, handyman, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Moving help is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform. This guide does not explain input tax credits. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the work happens. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Set any tax you must collect aside.

Ontario's hiring-a-mover page says the province does not licence moving companies. A municipal licence can still exist. This guide does not state a city's fee. If you pay a helper, check that province's workers' compensation rules before you call the person a favour.
