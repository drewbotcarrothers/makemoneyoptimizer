## What do car detailers earn per hour in Canada?

Employed auto detailers in Canada earn a median of $19.23 an hour, according to [Job Bank's auto detailer wage page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/14380/ca) (NOC 65311, reference period 2023–2024, updated November 19, 2025). Those are employee wages from the Labour Force Survey. They are not freelance rates, and they are the floor to beat, not the price to copy.

| Area (Job Bank, auto detailer) | Low | Median | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada | $15.50 | $19.23 | $27.00 |
| Ontario | $17.95 | $19.25 | $25.00 |
| British Columbia | $18.25 | $20.00 | $30.00 |
| Alberta | $16.00 | $20.00 | $30.00 |
| Quebec | $16.67 | $21.00 | $26.00 |

An employee at a car wash does not buy the towels, drive to the car, answer messages, or pay the employer half of CPP. You do. That is why a self-employed hourly target usually sits above the employee median, and why the published table is a reference point rather than a price list. This guide does not publish a national per-car detailing price because no public source we could verify collects one.

## Which pricing models work for car detailing in Canada?

Car detailing in Canada is usually sold per car as a named package, with add-ons priced separately and an optional repeat plan for regular clients. Hourly billing is honest but makes clients nervous on a long interior. Pick one model per service so the quote stays fast.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per-car package | Fixed price for "interior", "exterior", or "full", by vehicle size | Most first clients | A dog-hair minivan priced like a clean sedan |
| Add-on menu | Pet hair, salt stain extraction, engine bay, headlight polish | Turning a basic package into a full job | Doing add-ons for free because you were already there |
| Hourly with estimate | Rate × timed hours, with a written range | Heavily soiled or unusual vehicles | Clients who want one number |
| Repeat plan | Same car every 4 or 8 weeks at a set price | Neighbours and small fleets | A maintenance price applied to a car that skipped three visits |

Vehicle size is the first multiplier. A two-door coupe, a mid-size SUV, and a three-row minivan are three different jobs. Condition is the second. Write both into the package name so the client chooses before you arrive.

## How do you calculate a car detailing rate in Canada?

Calculate a car detailing rate in Canada from your target hourly pay, the share of your hours that are unpaid, the minutes the car actually takes, and the supplies used on that car. Then round, and compare with local listings for the same package.

### Step 1: Pick a target hourly pay

Use the Job Bank table as a sanity check. A target well under the provincial employee low means you are paying clients to let you work.

### Step 2: Count the unpaid hours

Quoting, driving, loading, messaging, and invoicing are not detailing time. If one hour in four is unpaid, the billable rate has to cover all four.

### Step 3: Time the package on a real car

Time a full interior and a wash on your own or a friend's vehicle, then the same on a larger one. Guessing time is how prices end up wrong.

### Step 4: Add per-car supplies and travel

Product, towels that wear out, water and power if you bring them, and the fuel to get there. Bigger equipment is a startup cost, not a per-car line.

### Step 5: Set aside tax and CPP, then round

Self-employed people pay both halves of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net income. The [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) covers how to hold that money back.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target is $25 an hour, a little under Job Bank's national high of $27.00. Suppose one working hour in four is unpaid, so 75% is billable. The billable rate is $25 ÷ 0.75 = $33.33 an hour.

Suppose a full interior on a mid-size SUV times at 2.5 hours. Labour is 2.5 × $33.33 = $83.33. Suppose product and towel wear on that car is $8, and the drive costs $4 in fuel. The total is $83.33 + $8 + $4 = $95.33, which you might round to $95 before tax.

Check it backwards: $95 − $12 = $83 for 2.5 billable hours is $33.20 an hour billable, or about $24.90 per working hour once the unpaid quarter is counted. Replace every number with your own timing.

Rows round labour plus an assumed $8–$12 of supplies and travel. Write "prices before tax" if you are not registered for GST/HST, and "plus GST/HST" if you are. Say what is not included: paint correction, ceramic coatings, and biohazard cleanup are separate trades or separate quotes.

## When should you raise car detailing prices in Canada?

Raise car detailing prices in Canada when your calendar is full two weeks out, when supply costs rise, or when your timing shows a package runs longer than priced. Give repeat clients written notice before the next visit, and change new-client prices first.

A once-a-year review in late winter, before spring salt cleanups, keeps the change predictable. Raise by package, not by a vague percentage, and show what each package now includes. If a client leaves over a modest increase, that slot can go to someone on the waiting list.

## What car detailing pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common car detailing pricing mistakes in Canada are quoting before seeing the car, pricing every vehicle the same, and giving away add-ons. Each one turns a three-hour job into a five-hour one at the same price.

Ask for photos of the interior before quoting. Charge for pet hair and heavy salt in writing. Do not match a car-wash tunnel price; you are selling a different service. Do not discount "to get started" so far that you cannot raise prices later without losing everyone. Track the actual time on every car for the first month and fix the sheet.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect car detailing prices in Canada?

Car detailing income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). You only add GST/HST once you are registered; registration is required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, counting all your taxable side hustles together.

When registered, charge the rate for the province where the service is supplied, using the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) explains voluntary registration and input tax credits. Quote "before tax" clearly so a later registration does not feel like a price hike.
