## What are the best side hustles for bilingual French-English speakers in Canada?

The best side hustles for bilingual Canadians are the ones where speaking both languages is the main selling point, not just a bonus.

[Freelance translation](/side-hustles/freelance-translation-side-hustle-canada/) covers websites, product listings, and documents for businesses that need French and English versions. Start with the direction you write best in, usually into your first language, and a subject you know.

## Which translator titles are protected in Canada?

Translator titles are regulated by provincial associations:

- **Ontario.** [ATIO](https://atio.on.ca/membership/f-a-q/) says Ontario granted a reserved title to its certified members through the Association of Translators and Interpreters Act, 1989. ATIO says only certified members, not candidates for certification, can use the association's seal.
- **Quebec.** [OTTIAQ](https://ottiaq.org/en/future-members) members have the exclusive legal right to the title "certified translator (C. Tr.)".
- **Other provinces.** Other provincial associations offer certification too; the [freelance translation guide](/side-hustles/freelance-translation-side-hustle-canada/) covers the national CTTIC route.

Uncertified translators can still work for clients who don't need certified documents, such as website copy or marketing materials. Translations for official purposes can come with their own requirements, so ask what the receiving organisation needs before you quote, and don't offer certified translations until you hold the title.

## How should bilingual freelancers price their work?

Translation is often priced per word, and tutoring or VA work per hour. Charge more for specialised subjects and tight deadlines. The [translation guide](/side-hustles/freelance-translation-side-hustle-canada/) covers pricing models.

### Illustrative example

Assumed numbers, not market rates: a translator quotes an assumed $0.15 per word for a 2,000-word website and takes an assumed 6 hours including review.

- Fee: 2,000 × $0.15 = $300
- Effective hourly rate: $300 ÷ 6 = $50

## What mistakes do bilingual freelancers make?

- **Calling yourself certified too early.** The title is protected in Ontario and Quebec.
- **Translating into your weaker language.** Quality shows.
- **Quoting official documents without checking requirements.** Ask what the receiving organisation accepts.
- **Pricing bilingual work the same as unilingual.** Bilingual service is the value you're selling.
- **Machine translation without review.** Clients pay for accuracy.

## How is a bilingual side hustle taxed in Canada?

Report freelance income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Translation and tutoring are services under the $30,000 GST/HST small-supplier test ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)); in Quebec, QST registration follows a similar test, so see [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/). Read [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
