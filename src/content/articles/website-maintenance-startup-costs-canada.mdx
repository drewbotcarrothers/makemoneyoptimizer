## How much does website maintenance cost to start in Canada?

Website maintenance in Canada costs $39.55 for a headset on free tool tiers, $472.46 adding a prepaid test site, a password manager and paid uptime monitoring, $822.46 with a starting professional liability policy, or $1,495.24 with a paid multi-site dashboard and a year of invoicing software. Goods and software include 13% Ontario HST. Insurance is added without HST. Use your province's rate. None of these include the computer.

| Kit | What's in it | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | Logitech H390 USB headset ($35.00) + free local WordPress install + free tiers of ManageWP or MainWP, UptimeRobot and Bitwarden | $39.55 with HST |
| Standard | Lean + Hostinger Premium web hosting, 48 months prepaid (CAN$201.12) + Bitwarden Premium, 12 months (US$19.80, about $28.20 CAD) + UptimeRobot Solo, 12 months billed annually (US$9 × 12 = US$108, about $153.79 CAD) | $472.46 with HST |
| Standard with insurance | Standard + professional liability at Zensurance's starting freelancer estimate (from $350/year, $1 million limit) | about $822.46 |
| Pro | Standard with insurance + MainWP Pro, 12 months (US$199, about $283.38 CAD) + FreshBooks Lite for 12 months ($26/month, $312) | $1,495.24 |

The arithmetic: lean is $35.00 × 1.13 = $39.55. Standard is ($35.00 + $201.12 + $28.20 + $153.79) = $418.11 × 1.13 = $472.46. With insurance, $472.46 + $350 = $822.46. The pro additions are $283.38 + $312 = $595.38, × 1.13 = $672.78, on top of $822.46, for $1,495.24.

US-dollar conversions use the Bank of Canada [daily exchange rate](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) of 1.4240 on October 8, 2026: $19.80 × 1.4240 = $28.20, $108 × 1.4240 = $153.79, and $199 × 1.4240 = $283.38. Your card issuer's conversion fee isn't included.

## Who should use a website maintenance startup budget in Canada?

A website maintenance budget fits people who already run WordPress comfortably, will test a restore before they promise one, and can explain an update to a non-technical owner. The lean kit fits someone with one or two clients on free tools. The standard kit fits someone who wants a live sandbox and paid monitoring. The pro kit fits someone managing many sites from one dashboard.

It's a poor fit if you've never restored a backup, or if the plan is rebuilding sites rather than looking after them.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for website maintenance in Canada?

**Insurance.** An update that breaks a checkout page is a claim about your work. Zensurance's [freelancer insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/freelancer-insurance) said on October 9, 2026 that insurance for freelancers generally starts from $350 to $500 a year for professional liability with a $1 million limit. That's an estimate, not a quote. Ask about errors and omissions and cyber coverage for client sites. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance advice.

**Security and privacy.** Patching applications is one of the Cyber Centre's [Top 10 IT security actions](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/top-10-it-security-actions), which is the core of a care plan. Contact forms collect personal information, and [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) covers personal information in commercial activity. Keep client logins in a password manager, not a spreadsheet.

**Business licence.** There's no website maintenance licence. Some cities licence home-based businesses. Register a business name with your province if you trade under one.

### A first-month sketch

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical rate.**

Suppose you buy the lean kit for $39.55, use free tool tiers, and carry insurance at $350 a year, or $29.17 a month. Three local businesses each pay an assumed $59 a month, and per-site tools are $5.97 each.

Revenue = 3 × $59 = $177. Tools = 3 × $5.97 = $17.91. After tools, insurance and the headset: $177 − $17.91 − $29.17 − $39.55 = $90.37 before a tax set-aside. The first month per client takes longer than the rest: audit, access setup, first backup and a test restore. Do not copy $59.

- **Your own subscriptions:** password manager, monitoring, dashboard.

- **Insurance,** renewed yearly.

## Where can website maintenance startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Use free tiers** of ManageWP or MainWP, UptimeRobot and Bitwarden until you have several clients.
- **Practise locally** instead of paying for a test site.
- **Bill per-site add-ons to the client** instead of absorbing them.
- **Pay monthly** for monitoring until you know you'll keep it.
- **Skip premium plugin bundles** you'd install on one site.

## Where should website maintenance spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't skip a tested restore. A backup you've never restored is a hope, not a backup. Don't keep client passwords outside a password manager. Don't skip insurance once a business depends on a site you update.

Keep monthly plan fees apart from household money. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account that can hold fees until the tax set-aside and next month's subscriptions are covered.

## Which tax rules apply to a website maintenance budget in Canada?

Website maintenance income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. Subscriptions, hosting and insurance are generally current expenses. A computer may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep signed care-plan scopes, monthly reports, invoices and tool receipts. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html). If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST. Once registered, use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
