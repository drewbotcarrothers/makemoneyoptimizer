## How much does website maintenance cost to start in Canada?

Website maintenance in Canada starts at $0 with a local practice install and free tool tiers, about $200–$320 with a prepaid test site, and about $320–$800 with paid plugins and software. The client pays for their own hosting and domain, so those are not your startup costs.

| How you start | First-year cash, about | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $0 | A free local WordPress install for practice, free tiers of a backup plugin and an uptime monitor, and a computer you own |
| Typical | $200–$320 | A prepaid hosting term for your own live test site (the Hostinger example is CAN$201.12 for 48 months), plus free tools |
| Fuller | $320–$800 | The typical column plus a paid backup or security plugin licence, a password manager, and invoicing software |

## Who should use a website maintenance startup budget in Canada?

A website maintenance startup budget in Canada fits people who are comfortable in a WordPress or site-builder dashboard and want recurring monthly work. The lean column fits someone still practising updates and restores. It is a poor fit if the offer is "I will stop all hackers," or if you would rather build new sites than look after existing ones.

## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for website maintenance in Canada?

Website maintenance break-even in Canada is your monthly retainers minus tool licences, divided by the hours each site takes. Paid licences often cover several sites, so per-site cost falls as clients are added.

### Step 1: Practise a full restore before selling backups

A backup you have never restored is a guess.

### Step 2: Use free tiers for the first client

Upgrade a tool when the free tier cannot do something the checklist requires.

### Step 3: Keep logins in the client's name

The client owns the domain, hosting, and admin account. You get your own user. The Cyber Centre's [Top 10](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/top-10-it-security-actions) includes managing administrative privileges.

### Step 4: Test the contact form every month

Forms collect names and emails. [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) covers personal information handled in commercial activity.

### Step 5: Divide licences by sites

A licence that covers five sites costs one-fifth per site when you have five clients.

### Illustrative example

**A teaching sketch, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical retainer.**

Suppose one local business pays an assumed $60 a month for a care plan: monthly updates, a backup check, a form test, and a short report. Tools are free tiers. You keep a live test site on the Hostinger term above, about CAN$4.19 a month spread over 48 months. Monthly remainder: $60 − $4.19 = $55.81 before a tax set-aside. Suppose the site takes 1.5 hours a month: $55.81 ÷ 1.5 = about $37.21 an hour inside these assumptions. With three clients at the same fee and time, the test site cost is shared, and the remainder is $180 − $4.19 = $175.81 for 4.5 hours. These totals are assumptions. Do not copy $60.

## Where can website maintenance startup spending be lower in Canada?

Website maintenance startup spending in Canada stays lower when you practise on a free local install, use free tiers of backup and uptime tools, and buy licences only when a client needs a feature the free tier lacks. Skip agency software built for dozens of sites until you have more than a few.

## Where should website maintenance spending not be cut in Canada?

Website maintenance spending in Canada should not cut tested backups, a password manager, or time to read update notes before clicking update on a client's live site. A broken site after an untested update is the opposite of what the client paid for.

## Which tax rules apply to a website maintenance budget in Canada?

Website maintenance income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep signed care-plan scopes, monthly reports, invoices, and tool receipts. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Website maintenance is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Supplies, platform fees, and subscriptions used for the business are generally current expenses. Equipment that lasts for years, such as a computer or a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion rather than a one-year deduction. If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, with receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not charge GST/HST and you do not claim the credit.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
