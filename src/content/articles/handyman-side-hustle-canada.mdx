A handyman side hustle in Canada means doing small home repairs and installations for a fee: hanging shelves and TVs, patching drywall, replacing screens, assembling furniture, fixing doors and caulking tubs. You need reliable tools, a vehicle or a way to carry them, liability insurance and a clear sense of which jobs legally belong to licensed trades. HomeStars puts typical handyman rates at $85 to $110 an hour in Canada as of August 2026, with small jobs at $130 to $250.

<QuickAnswer>

- **What clients pay (HomeStars, August 2026):** $85 to $110 an hour on average, up to $175–$180 for specialized skills. Small jobs average $130–$250, medium jobs $250–$500, and large jobs $500 and up.
- **Know the legal line:** in Ontario, only Licensed Electrical Contractors (with some exemptions) can do electrical work in someone else's home, according to the Electrical Safety Authority. In Quebec, the RBQ says certain types of construction work must be done by a licence holder. Plumbing, gas and structural work are licensed trades across Canada.
- **Real examples:** Frank Davignon has run Frank's Handyman Services in Elliot Lake, Ontario since 2001. Handy Girlz has done residential handywork in Winnipeg since 2003.
- **Startup:** a cordless drill/driver, level, stud finder, hand tools, ladder, and liability insurance. Most people start with tools they already own.
- **Tax:** report on Form T2125, pay 11.9% CPP on net earnings above $3,500, and register for GST/HST after $30,000 in taxable revenue.

</QuickAnswer>

## Who handyman work suits

Handyman work suits people who already fix things at home and enjoy solving small problems. It's a good fit if:

- You own basic tools and know how to use them safely.
- You're comfortable on a ladder and in tight spaces.
- You can estimate how long a job will take and say no to work beyond your skills.
- You're good with customers. Many clients are seniors, busy parents or new homeowners who need someone trustworthy in their home.
- You have a vehicle, or you serve a dense area where you can carry a small kit.

It's a poor fit if you want to do electrical, plumbing or gas work without a licence. That's illegal in many provinces and dangerous for you and your clients.

## What you can and can't do legally

Every province regulates trades differently, so check yours before you advertise. The key rules:

- **Electrical (Ontario):** the Electrical Safety Authority says only Licensed Electrical Contractors, with some exemptions such as HVAC installers, are legally authorized to do electrical work in a home. Homeowners or occupants can do work in their own homes. As a handyman, that means swapping a light fixture, outlet or switch in a client's Ontario home is not your job unless you hold the licence. Other provinces have similar rules; check with your provincial electrical safety regulator.
- **Quebec:** the Régie du bâtiment du Québec says certain construction work must be carried out by a licence holder, and you may need a licence or need to hire a licensed contractor. Check the RBQ's guidance before you take renovation jobs.
- **Plumbing and gas:** gas fitting and most plumbing work are regulated trades. Check your provincial regulator before you touch either, and refer anything beyond a simple fixture swap.
- **Permits:** structural changes, new walls and decks often need municipal permits. Those jobs belong with a contractor.

When a client asks for work you can't legally do, refer them to a licensed trade. Clients remember the honest answer, and you avoid liability.

During the pandemic he shifted away from renovation work toward home and property maintenance and repairs that let people stay safe at home. "By offering services that allowed people to stay safe at home, I was able to keep my business afloat," he said.

Both show common paths: one built on referrals from a seniors' residence, the other on a single trade (painting) that expanded into general repairs.

## Pricing and the math on a day of jobs

Most handymen charge a minimum call-out (often one or two hours), then hourly or a flat price per task. Flat prices are easier for clients to say yes to; hourly protects you on unpredictable jobs.

**Illustrative example.** A Saturday with three jobs from HomeStars' time table: hanging a heavy mirror (1.5 hours), mounting shelves (2.5 hours) and replacing a torn screen (1.5 hours). You charge $85 an hour, the low end of HomeStars' average range, and assume $40 for fuel and consumables (an assumption).

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Billable hours | 5.5 |
| Revenue at $85/hour | $467.50 |
| Fuel and consumables (assumed) | –$40.00 |
| Profit before tax | $427.50 |
| Effective per billable hour | $77.73 |

Travel between jobs isn't billable, so cluster jobs by neighbourhood or add a trip charge. If you did one such Saturday a month for a year, revenue would be about $5,610, well under the $30,000 GST/HST threshold. Pricing frameworks are in [how to price handyman jobs](/side-hustles/handyman-pricing-canada/).

### Materials: supply them or not?

HomeStars notes that some handymen supply materials while others ask clients to buy them first. Supplying materials saves the client a trip and lets you choose hardware you trust, but it ties up your cash and adds shopping time. If you supply them, keep receipts, bill materials as a separate line, and charge for the time it takes to pick them up. If the client supplies them, send an exact list with model numbers so you don't arrive to find the wrong anchors or hinges.

## Your launch plan: the first 30 days

### Week 1: choose your services

- List 10 jobs you can do well and legally. Cross off anything electrical, gas, plumbing beyond simple fixture swaps, or structural.
- Set a minimum charge and flat prices for your top five jobs using the HomeStars ranges as a reference.
- Do two or three jobs for family or friends and time them.

### Week 2: get set up

- Get liability insurance quotes; see [APOLLO vs Zensurance](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/).
- Set up quoting and invoicing, and a separate account.
- Start a [CRA-ready mileage log](/side-hustles/mileage-log-cra-side-hustle-canada/) for every work trip.
- Take before-and-after photos of every job from now on.

### Week 3: find first clients

- Tell neighbours, friends and coworkers what you do, with a short list of services and prices.
- Post in local community groups and neighbourhood apps.
- Create a Google Business Profile with your service area and photos.
- Contact seniors' residences, property managers and real estate agents, the kinds of referral sources Davignon's business started with.
- More scripts in [how to get your first handyman clients](/side-hustles/handyman-first-clients-canada/).

### Week 4: deliver and collect reviews

- Arrive on time, protect floors, clean up, and walk the client through the finished work.
- Ask for a review the same day.
- Offer a fall or spring maintenance package to every client.

## A seasonal calendar for Canadian handymen

Demand for small repairs follows the weather. Plan your marketing a few weeks ahead of each wave.

| Season | What clients ask for | What to promote |
|---|---|---|
| Spring (March–May) | Screen repairs, fence and gate fixes, deck board swaps, caulking, gutter cleaning after winter | A "spring fix-up list" visit priced as a half day |
| Summer (June–August) | Painting, shelving, furniture assembly, landlord turnovers around July 1 moves | Move-in packages for renters and new owners |
| Fall (September–November) | Weatherstripping, door sweeps, hose bib shutoffs, gutter cleaning, Christmas light hanging | A winter-prep package with a checklist |
| Winter (December–February) | Indoor jobs: drywall patches, TV mounting, closet systems, baby-proofing, grab bars | Indoor project bundles and gift certificates |

Winter is slower for outdoor work, which is why many handymen pair it with [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/) or indoor-only jobs.

## How to quote from photos

Most clients will send photos if you ask. A good photo request saves you unpaid site visits.

1. Ask for a wide photo of the area and a close-up of the problem.
2. Ask what the wall is made of (drywall, plaster, brick or tile) and how heavy the item is for anything you'll mount.
3. Ask for measurements: door width, shelf length, window size.
4. Ask whether they have the parts or want you to supply them.
5. Quote a range if you can't be sure, and say what would push it to the top of the range.
6. For anything that looks electrical, plumbing-related or structural, say so up front and offer a referral.

## Templates and checklists

**Quote template**

> Hi [Name], thanks for the photos. Here's my quote for [job list]:
>
> - [Task 1]: $[price]
> - [Task 2]: $[price]
> - Materials: [included / client supplies / at cost with receipt]
>
> Total: $[total]. This includes cleanup and disposal of small debris. If I find hidden damage (rot, wiring or plumbing issues), I'll stop and talk with you before doing anything extra. I can come [date/time]. Does that work?

**Job-site checklist**

- [ ] Confirm scope and price before starting
- [ ] Check for wires and pipes before drilling (stud finder with AC detection)
- [ ] Lay drop cloths and protect floors
- [ ] Use a level and correct anchors for the wall type and weight
- [ ] Photograph before and after
- [ ] Clean up, remove packaging and small debris
- [ ] Walk the client through the finished work
- [ ] Invoice on the spot or the same day

**Refer-out list.** Keep contacts for a licensed electrician, plumber and general contractor. Referring work you can't do builds goodwill and often brings referrals back.

## Insurance and safety

Liability insurance protects you if you damage a client's property or someone is injured. A single flooded basement or cracked countertop can cost more than a year of side-hustle profit. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

Safety basics: use eye and hearing protection, check for wires and pipes before drilling, follow ladder safety rules (three points of contact, no standing on the top step), and never work on anything energized.

## Taxes for handymen

- **Income tax:** report revenue and expenses (tools, insurance, materials you resell, vehicle use, phone) on Form T2125. Larger tools may need to be depreciated through capital cost allowance rather than deducted in one year.
- **CPP:** 11.9% of net earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026 (QPP in Quebec).
- **GST/HST:** you're a small supplier until your taxable supplies pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. The rideshare first-dollar rule doesn't apply. Once registered, charge the rate where the work happens, such as 13% HST in Ontario or 14% in Nova Scotia.
- **Mileage:** keep a log from day one; see [mileage logs for CRA](/side-hustles/mileage-log-cra-side-hustle-canada/).

## Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** electrical work in clients' homes is for Licensed Electrical Contractors (ESA). Check the ESA's contractor lookup if you subcontract.
- **Quebec:** check the RBQ's rules before taking renovation work; QPP replaces CPP and QST applies once registered.
- **Manitoba:** Handy Girlz describes itself as licensed and insured; check your municipality's business licence rules.
- **Hamilton and other Ontario cities** have strong demand for small repairs in older housing; see [the best side hustles in Hamilton](/side-hustles/best-side-hustles-hamilton-canada/).
- **Everywhere:** municipal permits apply to structural work, decks and some renovations. When in doubt, refer to a contractor.

## What to watch out for

- **Doing licensed work without a licence.** It's illegal in many provinces, may void a client's insurance and puts people at risk.
- **Underquoting.** Hidden rot, crumbling plaster or old wiring can double a job's time. Build in a "stop and talk" clause.
- **Unpaid travel.** Charge a minimum and cluster jobs.
- **Injury.** Ladders and power tools cause most handyman injuries. You have no sick pay as a self-employed worker.
- **No insurance.** One accident can cost more than the business earns.
- **Scope creep.** "While you're here…" jobs should get a price before you start.
