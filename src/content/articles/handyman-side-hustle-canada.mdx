After this guide you can offer a short list of repairs you can actually finish, turn down the jobs that belong to a licensed trade, and get through a first week of paid work with photos, a scope, and a price that includes the drive.

“Handyman” is a dangerous word because it sounds unlimited. The useful version is small, repeatable household work: furniture, caulking, a door that sticks, a shelf in a stud, a fence board, a faucet aerator, the latch the lawn client mentioned in May. The unsafe version is a panel, a gas line, a structural wall, or a bathroom rebuild you learned on their house. If you want the app to send you the first assembly jobs, start with [odd-job apps](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-side-hustle-canada) and keep the same limits. The outdoor clients who will ask you to fix a gate are in [lawn care](/side-hustles/lawn-care-side-hustle-canada) and [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada).

## Who it fits

**This can fit if** you already fix things in your own home, you can explain what you will do before you do it, and you own or can borrow a basic kit. It fits people who like a finished photo more than a long relationship with drywall dust. It fits evenings and Saturdays, which is when homeowners are actually home.

**This is a poor fit if** you need to learn on a paying customer’s only bathroom, you cannot drive or haul a small kit, or you hate writing things down. It is a poor fit if you will say yes to anything with a dollar sign. The first expensive mistake in this trade is usually a job you were not allowed to do, or a job you were allowed to do and underpriced by half.

You do not need a van and a wrap. You need three skills you can demonstrate and a sentence you can say at the door: “I don’t do electrical beyond a device a homeowner is allowed to change, and I don’t touch gas.”

Adjust that sentence to your province. The line moves.

## The line between handy and licensed

Provinces reserve parts of construction for qualified people. The labels differ. The idea does not.

Leave these to a ticket holder unless you are one:

- Electrical work beyond what a homeowner may legally do in that province, and often not even that when it is someone else’s house. Replacing a customer’s panel, running new circuits, or “just adding an outlet in the bathroom” is how unlicensed work starts. In Ontario, the Electrical Safety Authority is the body people mean. Other provinces have their own regulators.
- Gas appliances, fireplaces, and lines. A flexible connector is not a personality test.
- Plumbing that is more than a surface fix. A washer in a tap is different from opening a wall and moving a drain. If you do not know which one you are looking at, stop.
- Structural changes: removing a wall, cutting a joist, building a deck that needs a permit.
- Refrigerant, asbestos, and anything that already looks like mould remediation.

Permits are municipal. A “small deck” and a “small shed” can still need one. Ask the client who pulls the permit. If the honest answer is “nobody,” decline the job or shrink it until no permit is required. Quebec’s construction rules are their own system and are often stricter about who may do paid renovation. Do not import an Ontario forum post into Montreal.

What a careful beginner can offer, if they have practiced:

- Furniture assembly and anchoring to studs
- Shelves, curtain rods, and pictures, with the right anchors
- Caulking a tub or a drafty trim joint
- Adjusting a door, replacing weatherstripping, swapping a hinge
- Fence boards, a latch, a gate sag you actually understand
- Filling a small drywall dent, not rebuilding a ceiling
- Replacing a toilet seat, a showerhead, or a supply line you can shut off and test
- Smart-home gadgets that plug in, not gadgets that need a new circuit

Write that list. Publish that list. When someone asks for item eight and you only do items one through four, you say so and, if you know a licensed person, you refer them. Referring is a reputation tool.

## Costs in CAD

Buy tools as jobs demand them. A truck full of unused specialty tools is a debt, not a brand. The itemized CAD budget is in [handyman startup costs](/side-hustles/handyman-startup-costs-canada).

| Item | Typical CAD range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drill or driver, bits, Robertson and Phillips | $80–$180 | Robertson (square) bits matter in Canadian homes. |
| Level, tape, stud finder, pencil | $30–$70 | Mounting without a stud finder is a gamble. |
| Hand tools: screwdrivers, pliers, adjustable wrench, utility knife | $40–$90 | Buy ones you will keep, not a crushing 200-piece case. |
| Assorted anchors, screws, caulk, painter’s tape | $25–$50 | You will use these on job two if not job one. |
| Work lights or a headlamp | $20–$40 | Closets and basements are dark. |
| Shoe covers, drop cloth, vacuum attachment or a small brush | $20–$40 | The mess is part of the job you were hired to not leave behind. |
| Basic liability insurance | Get a quote | A falling TV or a leak you caused is the scenario to describe. |
| Fuel or transit | Your cost | A $90 shelf across the region is a bad shelf. |

## Pricing the job in front of you

Hourly rates feel easy and punish you for being fast. A fixed price for a defined scope rewards skill. Use hourly only when the scope is genuinely unknown, and then cap it: “up to two hours to diagnose the door, then I stop and we agree on a price.”

For a fixed price, include:

- Travel
- The task, in one sentence
- What is not included
- Who supplies the materials
- The assumption you are allowed to rely on (“wall is drywall over wood studs, not concrete”)
- A price to stop if the assumption is wrong

Materials should be a separate line or a store run you are paid for. Do not bury a $60 faucet in a $70 labour price.

Look at local handyman ads for a sanity check, then ignore the cheapest one if the photos look reckless. Your number has to pay for insurance, bits, and the hour you spent quoting. Quoting is work. A short paid diagnostic is reasonable for vague requests. A free look at a neighbour’s hinge is also reasonable. A free evening of estimates across the city is not.

There is no guaranteed weekly income. A side route of one evening job and one Saturday morning is a real start. An empty calendar in week one is normal.

## Step-by-step

### 1. Pick three offers you have already done

Not three you have watched. Assemble furniture. Hang a shelf. Fix a door or recaulk a tub in your own place first. If you cannot do it calmly at home, you cannot do it calmly for pay.

### 2. Write the no-list beside the yes-list

Put both in the message you send people. The no-list prevents the awkward kitchen conversation later. Include gas, panel work, structural changes, and roof work. Add plumbing or electrical limits in the words your province would recognize. If you are unsure, the limit is “I don’t open walls or touch wiring and gas.”

### 3. Photograph practice work

Before, during, after, and a close detail. Bright rooms. No faces, no mail on the counter. These photos are your portfolio until paid jobs replace them.

### 4. Price one sample of each offer

A shelf, an assembly, a small exterior repair. Write the assumptions. This becomes the reply you paste. You will still visit or ask for photos before you promise a number on anything hidden inside a wall.

### 5. Get the insurance quote

Describe residential handyman work, no structural, no gas, the tools you use, and that you work alone. Ask what is excluded. If a policy excludes the exact job you wanted to advertise, change the advertisement, not the truth you tell the insurer.

### 6. Tell a small circle and one local place

Neighbours, a community group, your lawn or cleaning clients if you have them. Offer the three services and the no-list. Ask for a photo of the job before you drive. A photo saves you from a “small patch” that is a hole the size of a football.

### 7. Quote, then do the job you quoted

At the door, restate the scope. If they add the closet rod and the curtain rod, price the add before you start. Shoe covers on. Find studs. Test the faucet shutoff before you disconnect anything that can leak. Clean up. Show the result. Get paid.

### 8. Write what you found

A two-line note: what you did, what you refused, what a licensed person should look at later. That note is how you stay out of “you were the last person to touch it” arguments about a problem you did not create.

### 9. Ask for the next small thing, or a name

Handyman routes grow by the next minor annoyance in the same house and the house next door. They do not grow by advertising that you remodel kitchens.

## Your first week

- **Day 1:** Yes-list and no-list. Check your city’s business-licence page and, if the work sounds like construction, your province’s workers’ compensation site.
- **Day 2:** Practice one skill if you are rusty. Take photos. List the tools you truly lack.
- **Day 3:** Insurance quote. Buy the missing ordinary tools, not a new saw you do not need.
- **Day 4:** Tell fifteen people who live near you. Include the no-list so the right jobs show up.
- **Day 5:** Quote one job from photos. If it is outside the list, refer it.
- **Day 6:** Do one paid job inside the list. Collect the e-transfer before you romanticize the relationship.
- **Day 7:** Log time, travel, materials, and net. Decide whether the offer is too broad. Narrow beats impressive.

## Tips that are easy to miss

Shutoffs fail. Before you disconnect a supply line, know where the main water shutoff is and whether the valve at the fixture actually stops the water. A “five-minute” faucet job becomes a ceiling stain when the old valve spins and does nothing.

Anchors are a skill. Drywall, plaster, tile, and concrete are different. The anchor in the package is a suggestion. If you cannot name the wall, do not hang the television.

Caulk is only as good as the gap you cleaned. Old, mouldy caulk has to come out. A bead on top of rot is a photo that looks fine for a week.

Canadian winters show up in the work: weatherstripping, storm doors, and outdoor screws that will rust if you use the indoor ones. Exterior fasteners are a different bin. Use them.

The [odd-job app](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-side-hustle-canada) path is a fine way to practice assembly with a paper trail. Keep your no-list there too. The app will not save you from a job you should have declined.

## Common mistakes

Advertising “no job too small” and also doing jobs that are too licensed. The slogan is the problem.

Quoting a firm price from a text that says “minor leak.” Look, or price a diagnostic only.

Leaving a mess and a half-working door because you ran out of time. Finish or stop and reschedule before you start. A house with tools still in it overnight is a trust problem.

No photos of existing damage. A cracked tile you did not cause will be described as yours. A polite “this was already cracked, I’ll work around it” in the thread is enough.

Hiring a friend and hoping they are not your employee. If you direct the work, workers’ compensation and employment rules may see a worker. In Ontario, construction has its own WSIB gravity. Other provinces’ boards are different and still real. Check before you scale.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Handyman income is self-employment income. Invoice it, keep receipts for materials and tools, and report it even if nobody issues a T4A. The T2125 is the usual form with your T1. Both shares of CPP generally apply to the net. If you drive to jobs, a kilometre log supports a vehicle claim. Guessing “about half my gas” is how those claims fall apart.

Small-supplier GST/HST: worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and your other hustles. Lawn, snow, and repairs are one pot for that test if they are all you. The CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page explains the timing when you cross in a single quarter versus over four quarters. This is not the ride-sharing rule that requires GST/HST from the first fare. Once you are registered, charge the rate for the province, set it aside, and remit it. Quebec adds QST on top of GST. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025. Confirm on the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Licences and permits are the legal half. Business licences are often municipal. Trade restrictions are provincial. Permits for decks, structural work, and many plumbing and electrical jobs are a city or a technical-authority question. A platform profile or a magnetic car sign does not replace any of them. If a job needs a ticket you do not hold, the professional move is to decline it.

Contracts do not have to be long. Scope, price, assumptions, and “I stopped because the wall was not what we thought” will prevent more disputes than a logo. For larger jobs, a local legal clinic or a lawyer is worth it. This page is not that document.
