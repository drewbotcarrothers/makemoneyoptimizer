## Who is handyman work a fit for in Canada?

Handyman work in Canada fits people who already fix things at home, can say what the visit includes before they start, and own or can borrow a basic kit. Handyman work is a poor fit if you need to learn on a paying customer’s only bathroom, you cannot haul a small kit, or you will accept any job that comes with a dollar sign.

Handyman work also fits people who like a finished photo more than a long day of drywall dust, and who can work when homeowners are actually home. The first expensive mistake is usually a job reserved for a licensed trade, or a job you were allowed to do and underpriced by half.

You do not need a van and a wrap. You need three skills you can demonstrate and a sentence you can say at the door: you do not take electrical work reserved for a licensed trade, and you do not touch gas. Adjust that sentence to your province. Check the provincial trade authority for where the line sits.

## Which handyman jobs are reasonable in Canada, and which are often licensed?

Handyman work in Canada should stay with small household repairs you have practised, because electrical, gas, plumbing, and structural work are often reserved for licensed trades and the rule is provincial. Check the provincial trade authority before you advertise those jobs, and treat another province’s forum post as someone else’s rule.

Leave these to a ticket holder unless you are one:

- Electrical work such as replacing a panel, running a new circuit, or adding an outlet. Check the provincial trade authority before you treat any wiring as a handyman task, including work a homeowner might do on their own house.
- Gas appliances, fireplaces, and lines.
- Plumbing that opens a wall or moves a drain. A washer in a tap is a different job from opening a wall. If you cannot tell which one you are looking at, stop.
- Structural changes: removing a wall, cutting a joist, or building a deck that needs a permit.
- Refrigerant, asbestos, and anything that already looks like mould remediation.

Permits are municipal. A small deck or a small shed can still need one. Ask the client who pulls the permit. If the honest answer is nobody, decline the job or shrink the job until no permit is required.

| | Reasonable if you have practised | Leave to a licensed trade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inside the house | Furniture assembly, shelves and curtain rods on the right anchors, pictures, caulking, a door adjustment, weatherstripping, a hinge, a small drywall dent, a toilet seat, a showerhead, a supply line you can shut off and test, plug-in gadgets | Panels, new circuits, gas appliances and lines, drains inside a wall, structural changes, refrigerant, asbestos, mould remediation |
| Outside | A fence board, a latch, a gate sag you understand | Roof work, a deck or shed that needs a permit nobody will pull |

Write the yes-list. Publish the yes-list. When someone asks for a job outside the list, say so and refer a licensed person when you know one. Referring is a reputation tool.

## How should you price handyman work in Canada?

Price handyman work in Canada as a fixed amount for a defined scope, and use a capped hourly diagnostic only when the problem is still hidden. There is no guaranteed weekly income from handyman work. One evening job and one Saturday morning is a real start, and an empty calendar in week one is normal.

Hourly rates punish you for being fast. For a fixed price, include travel, the task in one sentence, what is not included, who supplies the materials, the assumption you are allowed to rely on (“wall is drywall over wood studs, not concrete”), and a price to stop if the assumption is wrong.

Use hourly only when the scope is genuinely unknown, and then cap it: “up to two hours to diagnose the door, then I stop and we agree on a price.”

| | Fixed price | Capped hourly diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use it when | The task and the wall type are defined | You cannot see the problem yet |
| Include | Travel, one-sentence scope, exclusions, who buys materials, the assumption | A time cap, then a stop |
| If the assumption is wrong | Stop and reprice | The cap ends the visit until you agree on a new price |

Materials should be a separate line or a store run you are paid for. For example, do not bury a $60 faucet in a $70 labour price. That pair of figures is an illustration of the mistake, not a price survey.

Look at local handyman ads for a sanity check, then ignore the cheapest one if the photos look reckless. Your number has to pay for insurance, bits, and the hour you spent quoting. Quoting is work. A short paid diagnostic is reasonable for vague requests. A free look at a neighbour’s hinge is also reasonable. A free evening of estimates across the city is not.

## What does an illustrative handyman visit look like in Canada?

An illustrative handyman visit in Canada uses assumed figures so materials and a short drive are visible inside a fixed price. Those figures are not a typical price and not an earnings claim for handyman work in Canada.

**Illustrative example.** The fuel use, fuel price, and wear set-aside below are assumptions, not CRA vehicle rates.

Assume a quote of $140 to hang one shelf and one curtain rod, materials included, on drywall over wood studs. You spend $16 on anchors and brackets. The round trip is 10 km. At an assumed 8 L/100 km and an assumed $1.60/L, fuel is 10 × 0.08 × 1.60 = $1.28. An assumed wear set-aside of $0.12/km is $1.20. Variable cost = $16 + $1.28 + $1.20 = $18.48. Amount left after those costs = $140 − $18.48 = $121.52. On site for 1.25 hours and door to door for 1.75 hours, this sketch is $69.44 per door-to-door hour before insurance ($121.52 ÷ 1.75). If the wall is concrete, stop and reprice. Do not absorb a different anchor system to protect the $140 figure.

## How do you start handyman work in Canada?

Start handyman work in Canada by naming three repairs you have already finished, writing the jobs you will refuse, and quoting only the scope in the photos. Handyman work grows from the next small annoyance in the same house, not from advertising kitchen remodels.

### 1. Pick three offers you have already done

Not three you have watched. Assemble furniture. Hang a shelf. Fix a door or recaulk a tub in your own place first. If you cannot do the repair calmly at home, you cannot do the repair calmly for pay.

### 2. Write the no-list beside the yes-list

Put both in the message you send people. The no-list prevents the awkward kitchen conversation later. Include gas, panel work, structural changes, and roof work. Add plumbing or electrical limits in the words your province would recognize. If you are unsure, the limit is “I don’t open walls or touch wiring and gas.” Check the provincial trade authority when you are unsure which words apply.

### 3. Photograph practice work

Before, during, after, and a close detail. Bright rooms. No faces, no mail on the counter. These photos are your portfolio until paid jobs replace them.

### 4. Price one sample of each offer

A shelf, an assembly, a small exterior repair. Write the assumptions. This sample becomes the reply you paste. You will still visit or ask for photos before you promise a number on anything hidden inside a wall.

### 5. Get the insurance quote

Describe residential handyman work, no structural, no gas, the tools you use, and that you work alone. Ask what is excluded. If a policy excludes the exact job you wanted to advertise, change the advertisement.

### 6. Tell a small circle and one local place

Neighbours, a community group, and your lawn or cleaning clients if you have them. Offer the three services and the no-list. Ask for a photo of the job before you drive. A photo saves you from a “small patch” that is a hole the size of a football.

### 7. Quote, then do the job you quoted

At the door, restate the scope. If the client adds the closet rod and the curtain rod, price the add before you start. Shoe covers on. Find studs. Test the faucet shutoff before you disconnect anything that can leak. Clean up. Show the result. Get paid.

### 8. Write what you found

A two-line note: what you did, what you refused, and what a licensed person should look at later. That note is how you stay out of arguments about a problem you did not create.

### 9. Ask for the next small thing, or a name

Handyman routes grow by the next minor annoyance in the same house and the house next door.

## What is easy to miss on a handyman visit in Canada?

Shutoffs, wall type, and exterior fasteners are the details that change a small handyman visit in Canada from a finished photo into a callback. The [odd-job app](/side-hustles/odd-jobs-apps-side-hustle-canada) path is a way to practise assembly with a paper trail, and the same no-list applies there.

Shutoffs fail. Before you disconnect a supply line, know where the main water shutoff is and whether the valve at the fixture actually stops the water. A five-minute faucet job becomes a ceiling stain when the old valve spins and does nothing.

Anchors are a skill. Drywall, plaster, tile, and concrete are different. The anchor in the package is a suggestion. If you cannot name the wall, do not hang the television.

Caulk is only as good as the gap you cleaned. Old, mouldy caulk has to come out. A bead on top of rot is a photo that looks fine for a week.

Canadian winters show up in the work: weatherstripping, storm doors, and outdoor screws that rust if you use the indoor ones. Exterior fasteners are a different bin.

## What mistakes do new handyman workers make in Canada?

The expensive mistakes in handyman work in Canada are advertising licensed jobs, pricing a hidden leak from a text message, and leaving a house unfinished. Photograph existing damage before you start, and check provincial workers’ compensation rules before you direct someone else’s work.

Advertising “no job too small” and also taking jobs reserved for licensed trades. The slogan is the problem.

Quoting a firm price from a text that says “minor leak.” Look, or price a diagnostic only.

Leaving a mess and a half-working door because you ran out of time. Finish, or stop and reschedule before you start. A house with tools still in it overnight is a trust problem.

No photos of existing damage. A cracked tile you did not cause will be described as yours. A polite “this was already cracked, I’ll work around it” in the thread is enough.

Hiring a friend and hoping the friend is not your employee. If you direct the work, workers’ compensation and employment rules may see a worker. Those rules are provincial. Check the provincial workers’ compensation board before you scale. This guide is not legal advice.

## How do taxes and trade rules apply to handyman work in Canada?

Handyman income in Canada is self-employment income: invoice the income, keep receipts, report the income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with the T1 even if nobody issues a T4A, and budget for both shares of [Canada Pension Plan contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on the net. GST/HST for handyman work follows the small-supplier test of $30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, including associates and other hustles, which is a different rule from [GST/HST for taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html). After registration, charge Nova Scotia’s 14% HST ([charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html)), add Quebec’s QST on top of GST, confirm other rates on the CRA [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and check the provincial trade authority before electrical, gas, plumbing, or structural work.

This guide is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

The CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page explains the timing when taxable supplies cross the threshold in a single quarter versus over four quarters. Lawn, snow, and repairs are one pot for that test if they are all you. Set aside the tax you collect. Confirm the current QST rate before you add QST to a Quebec invoice.

If you drive to jobs, a kilometre log supports a vehicle claim. Guessing a share of fuel is how those claims fall apart.

Business licences are often municipal. Trade restrictions are provincial: electrical, gas, plumbing, and structural work are often reserved for licensed trades. Check the provincial trade authority. Permits for decks, structural work, and many plumbing and electrical jobs are a city or technical-authority question. A platform profile or a magnetic car sign does not replace either check. If a job needs a ticket you do not hold, decline it.

Contracts do not have to be long. Scope, price, assumptions, and a line that you stopped because the wall was not what you both thought will prevent more disputes than a logo. For larger jobs, a local legal clinic or a lawyer is worth it. This page is not that document.
