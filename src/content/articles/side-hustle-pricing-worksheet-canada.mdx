<QuickAnswer>

- **Work backwards from what you want to keep** per hour, not from the lowest price you've seen.
- **Gross it up for tax and CPP.** Nothing is withheld from self-employment income, and self-employed CPP is 11.9% combined in 2026 on pensionable earnings above $3,500.
- **Add unbilled time and job costs:** travel, quoting, messaging, supplies, fees.
- **Then check the rules:** advertised prices must be attainable, sales tax is the only add-on, and GST/HST registration starts after $30,000.

A side hustle pricing worksheet for Canada starts with the hourly amount you want to keep, grosses it up for the income tax and CPP you'll owe, adds the unbilled time and costs each job carries, and only then compares the result with what others charge. Skip any one of those lines and the price comes out too low. Self-employed people pay both the employee and employer CPP portions, 5.95% each in 2026 ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/payroll-deductions-contributions/canada-pension-plan-cpp/cpp-contribution-rates-maximums-exemptions.html)), and nothing is withheld for you, so the price has to carry it.

This worksheet is the general method. ## How do you fill in a side hustle pricing worksheet in Canada?

Work down the lines once, then revisit them after a month of real jobs.

### Step 1: Line A, the take-home you want

Pick an hourly amount that makes the work worth your evening. Your province's minimum wage is a floor to beat, not a target.

### Step 2: Line B, the set-aside rate

This covers income tax at your marginal rate plus self-employed CPP. [How much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) shows how to estimate it. If you have a job, side income is added on top of your salary, so it's taxed at your marginal rate.

### Step 3: Line C, gross it up

Divide A by (1 − B). At a 25% set-aside, keeping $20 means charging about $26.67 per hour of work.

### Step 4: Line D, real hours per job

Add travel, setup, quoting, messages and invoicing to the billable time. Leaving this line out quietly lowers your real hourly rate.

### Step 5: Line E, costs per job

Supplies used, fuel, parking, platform or payment fees. Only business costs count, and the business share of mixed costs ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses.html)). Startup purchases go in the plan, not each job; see your hustle's startup costs guide, such as [lawn care startup costs](/side-hustles/lawn-care-startup-costs-canada/).

### Step 6: Line F, the price

(C × D) + E. Round to a clean number clients understand.

### Step 7: Line G, the market check

Compare with what similar offers charge. If yours is far higher, look for hours or costs you can cut, or a more specific offer. If it's far lower, you've probably left something out.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch using the table's assumed numbers. A car detailer wants to keep $20 an hour and sets aside 25%. Gross hourly is $26.67. An interior detail takes 1.5 hours plus 0.5 hours of driving and messaging, so 2 hours. Supplies and fuel are $6. Price: $26.67 × 2 + $6 = $59.33, rounded to $60. If local detailers advertise interiors well above that, there's room to charge more; if they're below, the detailer looks at cutting travel by grouping jobs by neighbourhood.

## What pricing rules apply to side hustles in Canada?

Three rules shape how you present the number.

- **All-in pricing.** The Competition Bureau says advertising a price that can't be attained because of mandatory added fees is against the law; only government charges such as sales tax can be added ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/drip-pricing)). Build fees into the price.
- **Written estimates in Ontario.** If a written estimate is part of a consumer contract, the final price can't be more than 10% above it unless the client agrees and signs a change ([Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-when-signing-or-cancelling-contract)).
- **GST/HST.** Once total taxable sales from all your businesses pass $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters, you must register and charge GST/HST on top ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). See [GST/HST registration](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).

## What pricing mistakes do side hustlers make in Canada?

- **Pricing from the cheapest competitor.**
- **Leaving out travel and admin time.**
- **Forgetting the tax and CPP gross-up.**
- **Adding a mandatory "service fee" at checkout** instead of building it into the price.
- **Never revisiting the worksheet** after real jobs show the true hours.

## How does your price affect your side hustle taxes in Canada?

Your price determines revenue, which goes on Form T2125 along with your expenses. The set-aside line keeps the tax money in your account until you file.
