Paid focus groups in Canada are market-research sessions: you answer questions about a product, a service, or a habit, either in a room or on a call, and the research company pays an incentive if you attend and qualify. They suit someone who can speak clearly, show up on time, and accept that screeners reject most people. They do not suit someone who wants daily tasks. This guide does not publish a Canadian average session fee, because the companies below do not publish one national rate. Each study shows its own incentive.

Shorter polls are [survey sites](/side-hustles/survey-sites-canada/). Recorded website tests are the [UserTesting review](/side-hustles/usertesting-review-canada/) and the [user testing comparison](/side-hustles/user-testing-sites-canada/). Academic-style studies are [Prolific](/side-hustles/prolific-review-canada/). The hub is [beermoney apps](/side-hustles/best-beermoney-apps-canada/).

## What counts as a paid focus group in Canada?

A focus group is a moderated discussion with a small number of people, or a one-to-one interview that uses the same screening and incentive model. A five-minute panel survey is not a focus group, even when the panel sometimes invites you to a longer session. An in-person group usually asks you to travel to a facility in a large city. An online group asks for a camera, a quiet room, and the length printed on the invite.

The incentive is the point of reading the invite before you book the time off. Respondent lists incentives in US dollars and then deducts a fee. User Interviews shows the incentive type on the study page before you apply. A facility email that does not name the length, the city, and the incentive is not ready to accept.

## How do you find paid focus groups in Canada?

### Step 1: Join the panels that publish a Canada page

Open User Interviews and Respondent from their own sites. Add Ipsos iSay, Leger Opinion, or AskingCanadians if you will also do short surveys. Three honest profiles beat ten half-finished ones. Use your real city. Screeners exist to find a specific person.

### Step 2: Read the incentive and the fee before the screener

On Respondent, subtract 5%, or US$1 if that is larger. The company’s own examples: US$20 pays US$19, and US$95 pays US$90.25. On User Interviews, check whether you will receive a digital gift card through Tremendous or a payment the researcher sends. A wrong-currency gift card is not reissued. Details are in the [user testing comparison](/side-hustles/user-testing-sites-canada/).

### Step 3: Book only what you can attend

A no-show wastes the incentive and the facility’s slot. Online sessions need a quiet room and a headset. A basic <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="USB headset" search="USB headset microphone" /> is enough if the laptop mic echoes. You do not need a studio.

### Step 4: Treat an unsolicited cheque as a scam

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre’s [job scam page](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/scams-fraudes/job-emploi-eng.htm) describes mystery-shopper letters that include a cheque, a request to wire most of it onward or to buy gift cards, and a customer-service survey that is only there to make the job look real. The same shape shows up as a "you have been selected for a paid focus group" email. You did not apply. The cheque is the trap. The survey is the prop. Report it to local police and the Anti-Fraud Centre. The wider list is [side hustle scams](/side-hustles/side-hustle-scams-canada/) and [fake cheque scams](/side-hustles/fake-cheque-overpayment-scams-canada/).

### Step 5: Confirm the company on its own domain

Search the name, open the site yourself, and call a phone number printed on that site if you are unsure. A webmail address and a registration fee are warning signs on the Anti-Fraud Centre’s page. Legitimate panels in the table above say joining is free.

### Step 6: Keep a record of what you were paid

Write the date, the platform, the currency, and the amount that actually arrived. USD gift cards and PayPal transfers are converted for your tax return using [earning in USD from US platforms](/side-hustles/usd-platform-income-tax-canada/).

## What does a legitimate invite look like next to a fake one?

| Legitimate session | Fake offer |
| --- | --- |
| You applied, or a panel you joined matched you | You were "selected" out of nowhere |
| Incentive, length, and place are stated before you accept | A cheque arrives first |
| You attend, then you are paid | You must wire money or buy gift cards |
| No fee to join | A certification, registration, or unlock fee |
| The company site matches the email domain | A free webmail address |

### Illustrative example

Assumed session, using Respondent’s published fee. A study advertises US$80 for a one-hour interview. The fee is 5% × 80 = US$4, so you receive US$76. At the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4254 CAD per US dollar on October 5, 2026, US$76 is about $108.33 CAD before the gift card’s own conversion. If you are screened out, you receive nothing for that application. If the session is in another city and you spend $40 on transit, the sketch’s net is lower by that $40, and the US$80 was still an assumption inside Respondent’s allowed range. It is not a typical focus group.

## What mistakes do Canadians make with focus groups?

- **Paying a directory for "exclusive" studies.** The panels above are free to join. A paid list is not a research company.
- **Missing Respondent’s fee.** The headline number is not the payout.
- **Travelling on an invite that never stated the incentive.** Ask before you book the train.
- **Inventing screener answers.** You will be removed, and the study that did match you is gone.
- **Depositing the cheque.** The Anti-Fraud Centre’s description is the whole scam. The survey at the end does not make it real.

## Which tax rules apply to focus group pay in Canada?

Session pay is income. Report it in Canadian dollars. Business income goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). A gift card is still value you received. How to think about small rewards versus business pay is in [are rewards and bonuses taxable](/side-hustles/are-rewards-and-bonuses-taxable-canada/). The GST/HST small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000, explained in [the $30,000 myth](/side-hustles/gst-hst-30000-threshold-myth-canada/). A handful of sessions will not reach it. Add your other side hustles before you assume that.
