Goods include 13% Ontario HST. Use your province's rate. None of these include the car, an insurance premium change, the loan, parking or a detail shop.

## Who should use a Turo host budget in Canada?

A Turo host budget fits people with a paid-off or lightly financed car that sits idle most days, a lender and insurer who say yes in writing, and the time to clean and hand over keys between trips. The lean kit fits a car that books a few weekends a month. The standard kit fits a car that books most weeks, in a province with winter. The pro kit fits a host who can't meet every guest in person.

It's a poor fit if the car payment only works when the car books every week, or if your lender or insurer says no.

A stall instead of a car is [renting out a parking space](/side-hustles/rent-out-parking-space-canada/). Cleaning cars for other people is [car detailing startup costs](/side-hustles/car-detailing-startup-costs-canada/).

## What does the protection plan change in a Turo budget?

The protection plan changes how much of the trip price you keep and how much damage responsibility you accept. Turo's [February 27, 2026 Canada post](https://turo.com/blog/canada/news/host-protection-plans-update-april-2026/) sets three plans:

| Plan | You keep | Damage responsibility | Replacement vehicle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| More earnings | 85% | $2,000 | Not included |
| Balanced | 75% | $250 | Not included |
| More peace of mind | 65% | $0 | Included |

A higher percentage isn't a higher profit if one claim wipes out several trips. On an assumed $60 daily price, the difference between 85% and 75% is $6 a day; one claim at the $2,000 responsibility is 334 of those days. The list-your-car page says the public summary is incomplete and terms apply. The plan in your account is the one to budget. Earlier versions of this page called the post "April 2026"; it's dated February 27, 2026.

The same post adds two changes that move revenue: a standardized 10% non-refundable option for trips booked four or more days ahead, and minimum baseline discounts for 3-day, 1-week and 30-day trips. Longer trips mean fewer turnarounds, at a lower daily rate.

British Columbia hosts should also budget the delivery period as uncovered time. Turo's hosting guide says B.C. coverage is active during the reservation period only.

## What should you ask your insurer and your lender before you list?

Ask whether the personal policy allows peer-to-peer car sharing, what you pay if it does, and what's excluded while the car is on a trip and while it isn't. Turo says you must carry your own insurance and that the insurer must approve participation. This page doesn't state a premium. Ask for the change in writing, and put that number in the sketch below.

Ask the lender or lessor whether the contract allows you to rent the car to other drivers. A finance payment doesn't pause because a week has no booking. If the answer is no, the startup cost of hosting is a different car, or it's zero because you don't list. This is not insurance or legal advice.

### A monthly sketch

**A teaching sketch with assumed numbers. It isn't a quote, a forecast, or Turo's median.**

Suppose guest trip prices in a month add up to $800. You're on the 75% plan, so your share is $600. Your insurer adds an assumed $40 that month because you disclosed car sharing. Washes and supplies are $60. You set aside $80 for wear. The stall would have rented for $150, so you count $150 as a cost of keeping the car there. Cash left before the loan and depreciation: $600 − $40 − $60 − $80 − $150 = $270.

Suppose the car payment is $450. Then $270 − $450 = −$180 before depreciation and the hours you spent on handoffs. A paid-off car skips the $450 line. Change any input and the result changes.

Turo's list-your-car page says hosts earn "an average of $657 per month"; its footnote says that's the median monthly earnings of Canada hosts from January 1 to October 31, 2025, rounded down, and not reduced for vehicle ownership costs like maintenance and insurance. Use the footnote. Don't paste $657 into this sketch and call it profit.

## What ongoing costs do Turo hosts in Canada pay?

- **The plan share** on every trip: 15%, 25% or 35% of the trip price.
- **Insurance** at whatever your insurer charges for car sharing.
- **The loan or lease,** booked or not.
- **Cleaning** between trips: supplies, washes, and the occasional detail.
- **Wear and repairs.** Brakes, tires, windshields and winter tires are ownership costs whether or not Turo reimburses a guest-caused incident.
- **Parking.** A stall you could have rented is a cost even when you don't write a cheque.
- **Depreciation.** Extra kilometres can reduce what the car is worth when you sell it. Track kilometres and keep the purchase papers.
- **Your time.** Handoffs and messages are unpaid unless you build them into the daily price.

## Where can Turo hosting startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **List a car you already own,** not one bought for Turo.
- **Start with cleaner and cloths.** Buy the vacuum when trips are back to back.
- **Meet guests in person** before buying a key safe.
- **Skip the detail shop** except between long trips.
- **Let longer trips do the work.** Fewer turnarounds mean fewer cleans.

## Where should Turo hosting spending not be cut in Canada?

Don't list without your insurer's and lender's answers. An unapproved listing can be a coverage problem that costs more than a month of trips. Don't skip winter tires or brakes to make a month look profitable. Don't pick the 85% plan without $2,000 you can actually pay.

Keep trip payouts apart from household money. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account that can hold payouts until repairs, insurance and the tax set-aside are covered.

## Which tax rules apply to Turo host costs in Canada?

Net hosting income, if the activity is a business, goes on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Cleaning supplies are generally current expenses. A vehicle used to earn income may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion, under the class and first-year rules for that year. This guide doesn't assign a class. Personal kilometres stay out. Self-employed net income is also subject to both shares of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) for taxable supplies across all your side hustles. Guests drive the car themselves, so don't assume the ride-sharing "register from the first fare" rule applies to a vehicle rental. Once registered, the rate depends on the province; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table. Confirm the treatment for your facts. Keep the trip records with [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).
