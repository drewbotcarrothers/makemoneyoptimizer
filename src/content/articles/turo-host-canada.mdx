Hosting on Turo in Canada means listing an eligible car you control, on Turo's marketplace, so a screened guest can book it and drive it. Turo's list-your-car page says listing is free, with no sign-up charge and no monthly fee, and that payouts go by direct deposit within three days after a trip. It suits someone who can spare the car, meet guests or arrange a handoff, and will call their insurer and their lender before the first listing.

The dollar side of cleaning, idle days, and a car payment is [Turo host costs in Canada](/side-hustles/turo-host-costs-canada/). Driving passengers yourself is a different product, covered in [rideshare driving](/side-hustles/rideshare-driving-side-hustle-canada/).

## Where can you host a car on Turo in Canada?

You can book a car on Turo in the provinces and territory Turo lists, and you should confirm the host-listing page still includes your province before you photograph the car. Turo's [How Turo works](https://turo.com/ca/en/car-rental/canada/how-turo-works) page says you can book a car with a host in Alberta, British Columbia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec, and Yukon. The [hosting guide](https://turo.com/ca/en/car-rental/canada/hosting-your-first-trip) lists eligible vehicles in Ontario, British Columbia, Quebec, Alberta, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, and Newfoundland and Labrador. Saskatchewan and Manitoba are not on either list. Availability changes, so read both pages on the day you list.

## Who does Turo hosting in Canada suit?

Turo hosting in Canada suits people who already have a qualifying car, a legal place to keep it, and time to clean it and hand it over. It is a weak fit if you need the car every weekday, if the insurer will not approve car sharing, or if the loan or lease forbids renting the vehicle to others. A second car that sits still is the usual setup people describe. A single car you also use for work is a calendar problem, not a listing problem.

## Which cars can you list on Turo in Canada?

Turo's hosting guide says a listable vehicle is an eligible passenger vehicle, registered to you or to an owner who authorized you to list it, with permanent plates, a clean title, and a market value up to $150,000. It must meet provincial legal requirements, be no more than 12 years old except for specialty or classic vehicles Turo allows, and meet Turo's maintenance rules. The same guide says that once a car is listed, a vehicle that reaches or exceeds 200,000 km may stay listed if it is in excellent condition and still meets legal, safety, and mechanical standards.

## What protection plans does Turo offer hosts in Canada?

Turo's current list-your-car page says you earn 65%, 75%, or 85% of the trip price depending on the protection plan, with up to $2,000,000 of liability from Economical or, in British Columbia, ICBC. An April 2026 post on Turo's Canada blog names three plans: More earnings at 85% of the trip price with $2,000 damage responsibility and no replacement vehicle; Balanced at 75% with $250 damage responsibility and no replacement vehicle; More peace of mind at 65% with $0 damage responsibility and a replacement vehicle included. The list-your-car page says that summary is not complete and that terms, exclusions, and conditions apply.

Turo's pages do not all use the same table. The hosting guide still describes a 75 plan and an 85 plan, including a $2,000 deductible during delivery and no deductible during the reservation period on the 75 plan. Read the plan the app actually offers you. The blog and the list-your-car page are the newer three-plan description. The insurance page says physical-damage reimbursement is subject to terms, exclusions, and any deductible, for the lesser of repair cost up to actual cash value or $150,000 in the provinces where Turo operates.

British Columbia is different on timing. The hosting guide says protection for hosts in Ontario, Quebec, Alberta, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, New Brunswick, and Newfoundland and Labrador is active during the reservation period and delivery periods, and that British Columbia coverage is active only during the reservation period. The vehicle-protection page says there is no third-party liability coverage during the delivery period in British Columbia. If you deliver the car in B.C., ask what is covered while you are driving it to the guest.

## Do you still need your own auto insurance and lender's permission?

Yes. Turo's hosting guide says protection plans cover trips booked on Turo only, so you still need your own insurance to comply with the law. It says your personal insurance will not be used during a Turo trip, and that you must contact your insurer before you list because your insurer must approve peer-to-peer car sharing. The vehicle-protection page says the same, names examples such as Intact, Desjardins, Aviva, Wawanesa, and Co-operators, and points you to Turo's list. That list is Turo's statement about insurers, not a promise that your policy allows hosting. Read your policy, then ask the insurer in writing.

A loan or a lease is a separate contract. Turo can accept a car that your lender or lessor does not allow you to rent out. Ask the lender or lessor, in writing, whether peer-to-peer rental is permitted. Personal auto insurance and financing terms can each block hosting even when the app will let you publish a listing. This is not insurance or legal advice. A plain-language overview of side-hustle policies is [side hustle insurance](/side-hustles/side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

The hosting guide also says to keep the vehicle registration, proof of personal insurance, a certificate of Turo insurance, and the Turo incident information card in the car. For hosts outside British Columbia, Turo names Economical Insurance Company. In British Columbia it names ICBC.

## How do you start hosting on Turo in Canada?

You start by confirming the province, the insurer, and the lender, then listing only a car that meets Turo's vehicle rules.

### Step 1: Confirm the province and the car

Open Turo's how-it-works page and the hosting guide. If your province is missing, do not list. Check age, title, plates, kilometres, and the value cap against the hosting guide.

### Step 2: Ask the insurer and the lender

Call the insurer on the policy and the lender or lessor on the contract. Save the answers. "The app allowed it" is not approval from either of them.

### Step 3: Choose a protection plan you can afford to use

A higher share of the trip price comes with more of the damage responsibility, on the plans Turo's April 2026 post describes. Pick the plan before you set a daily price. The price guests see is not the amount that lands in your account.

### Step 4: List the car and set rules you will keep

Turo's list-your-car page says you set availability and a daily price, or you can let Turo adjust the price. Set the handoff place, the advance notice you need, and what you will not allow. A rule you cannot enforce at 11 p.m. is not a rule.

### Step 5: Plan the handoff, the clean, and the photos

Guests book a car that looks like the photos. Budget time to vacuum, wipe, and photograph before every trip, including trips that end late. A basic kit of towels and cleaner is enough to start. <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="Microfibre towels and interior cleaner" search="microfiber car cleaning towels" /> are ordinary supplies, and a bucket you already own is enough if the car is not filthy.

### Step 6: Track the payout against the costs

Turo says you are also reimbursed for fuel and for mileage beyond your limit. Those reimbursements are not profit. Put trip deposits in an account you do not spend from household bills, such as <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" />, so a repair is visible before you treat the deposit as pay. Compare account fees before you open one.

## What work sits between Turo trips in Canada?

The work between trips is cleaning, messages, key handoff, and the hours the car is unavailable to you. Turo's marketing line about sitting back describes the booked hours, when the guest has the car. You still reply, meet or unlock, inspect, fuel if that is your rule, and deal with a late return that collides with your own Monday. Winter tires, salt, and a longer clean are part of hosting in most of the country. None of that is priced on the trip-price percentage.

Turo's list-your-car page publishes a monthly figure: it says hosts earn an average of $657 per month, and the footnote says that number is the median monthly earnings of Canada hosts from January 1, 2025 through October 31, 2025, rounded down, and not reduced for maintenance and insurance. The headline says "average" and the footnote says "median." Use the footnote. The figure is Turo's, it is not a forecast for your car, and it is not profit after a loan, parking, or depreciation. The cost sketch is in [Turo host costs](/side-hustles/turo-host-costs-canada/).

## What mistakes do new Turo hosts in Canada make?

- **Listing before the insurer and the lender answer.** Turo's own guide tells you to contact the insurer. A finance contract can forbid the rental even when the insurer agrees.
- **Treating 85% of the trip price as income.** Damage responsibility, cleaning, and days with no booking sit outside that percentage.
- **Ignoring the British Columbia delivery gap.** The hosting guide limits B.C. coverage to the reservation period.
- **Using one car you also need for work.** A booking is a block on your calendar.
- **Quoting Turo's $657 median as what you will clear.** Turo says that figure is not reduced for maintenance and insurance.

## Which tax rules apply to Turo hosting in Canada?

Turo hosting is renting a vehicle, which is a business if you carry it on for profit, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). It is not real-estate rent on Form T776. The comparison is [T2125 vs T776](/side-hustles/t2125-vs-t776-canada/). Self-employed hosts pay both shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on the net.

Commercial ride-sharing has its own GST/HST rule. [GI-196](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-196/gst-hst-commercial-ride-sharing-services.html) is about passenger transportation arranged through an electronic platform, the kind of work in the [rideshare guide](/side-hustles/rideshare-driving-side-hustle-canada/). Turo guests drive the car themselves. Do not assume the "register from the first fare" ride-sharing rule applies to a vehicle rental. Taxable supplies of a rental business still count toward the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all of your side hustles. Confirm the treatment for your facts. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
