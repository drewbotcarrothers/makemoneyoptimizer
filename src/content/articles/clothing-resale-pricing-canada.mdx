## What fees do clothing resale platforms charge in Canada?

Poshmark.ca and Depop take a share of each sale, and the fee changes how low you can price. Poshmark's [getting started guide](https://poshmark.ca/posh_guide/get_started_on_poshmark) says sales under C$20 pay a flat C$3.95 and sales of C$20 or more pay 20%, and you keep 80%. Depop's [seller fees page](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001791127-Seller-fees-and-charges) says sellers outside the UK, US, and Australia pay a 10% selling fee on the total transaction plus payment processing, which varies, and an 8% fee on sales from boosted listings outside the UK and US.

| Platform | Fee on a C$15 sale | Fee on a C$40 sale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poshmark.ca | C$3.95 flat | 20% = C$8.00 |
| Depop (sellers in Canada) | 10% = C$1.50 + processing | 10% = C$4.00 + processing |
| Local pickup sale | None from a platform | None from a platform |

Fees change; check each page before you set prices for a batch.

## How do you find the market price for used clothing in Canada?

Find the market price for used clothing in Canada from sold listings, not active ones, for the same brand, item type, size, and condition. Active listings show what sellers hope for; sold listings show what buyers paid.

Most resale apps let you filter to sold items. Look at the last few comparable sales, ignore outliers, and note whether they sold quickly. Condition matters: pilling, stains, missing tags, and stretched collars all push the price down, and should be photographed and stated.

## How do you calculate a clothing resale price in Canada?

Calculate a clothing resale price in Canada by starting from sold comps, then checking that what you keep after fees covers your cost and the time it takes.

### Step 1: Record your cost

What you paid, plus cleaning or repairs.

### Step 2: Find two or three sold comps

Same brand, type, size, and condition.

### Step 3: Set the list price

Near the comps, with room above your floor for offers.

### Step 4: Work out what you keep

List price minus the platform fee at that price.

### Step 5: Set the floor

The lowest offer you will accept: what you keep must still exceed your cost by a margin you choose.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not an average or a market price. Suppose you buy a wool sweater for $8 and spend $2 cleaning it: cost $10. Sold comps (assumed) cluster around $40. You list on Poshmark.ca at $45. If it sells at $45, the fee is 20% × $45 = $9 and you keep $36, a profit of $36 − $10 = $26.

You set a $30 floor. An accepted $30 offer pays 20% × $30 = $6 and you keep $24, a profit of $14. At $18, under C$20, the fee is the flat $3.95, you keep $14.05, and profit falls to $4.05. That is why low-priced items often go into bundles instead.

## When should you lower or raise clothing resale prices in Canada?

Lower clothing resale prices in Canada when an item gets views but no offers for a few weeks, or when the season is ending. Raise prices when similar items sell within days or when you keep getting full-price offers.

Mark down in steps, such as 10% at a time, and never below your floor. Seasonal items, such as winter coats in spring, may sell better if held until autumn than if cut deeply.

## What clothing resale pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common clothing resale pricing mistakes in Canada are pricing from active listings, ignoring the flat fee on small sales, and not recording cost, which hides whether items make money.

Children's clothing has safety rules. Health Canada's [guide to second-hand products](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html) says the Canada Consumer Product Safety Act applies to sellers of second-hand products, and covers children's products and recalls; read it before pricing kids' items, because some cannot be sold at any price. Counterfeit items should never be listed.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect clothing resale in Canada?

Regular clothing resale for profit in Canada is business income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html); occasionally selling your own used clothes at a loss is generally not. Keep receipts for what you buy, as the CRA's [keeping records page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records.html) explains. GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters.
