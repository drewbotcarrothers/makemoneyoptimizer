## Which bylaw mistakes do snow removal workers make in Canada?

The main bylaw mistake snow removal workers make in Canada is moving snow off the client's property onto public space.

### Mistake 1: Pushing snow onto sidewalks or roads

The City of Toronto's [snow clearing page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-maintenance/winter-maintenance/clearing-snow-and-ice-from-your-property/) says residents must not push snow from their property onto sidewalks, roads, or bike lanes, that it is dangerous and illegal, and that it can bring a $500 fine and a $110 victim surcharge. Check your own city's bylaw, and plan where each client's snow will go before the first storm.

### Mistake 2: Burying hydrants

Toronto also says it is important to clear snow around fire hydrants so firefighters can reach them quickly. Never pile snow on one.

## Which insurance and contract mistakes do snow removal workers make in Canada?

The main insurance mistake snow removal workers make in Canada is assuming personal coverage applies to paid work on other people's property.

### Mistake 3: No liability insurance

Ask a broker about liability coverage for snow clearing, including slip-and-fall claims. The FCAC's [home insurance page](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/insurance/home.html) says home insurance isn't business insurance and generally won't cover business claims.

### Mistake 4: Vague contracts

Write down the trigger depth (for example, after a set number of centimetres), when you will clear by, whether salting or sanding is included, what happens with ice, and how many visits a seasonal price covers. The [snow removal contract checklist](/side-hustles/snow-removal-contract-checklist-canada/) covers the clauses.

## Which safety mistakes do snow removal workers make in Canada?

The main safety mistakes snow removal workers make in Canada are clearing snow blower clogs with the machine running and overexerting while shovelling.

### Mistake 5: Unsafe snow blower habits

The CCOHS [snow thrower page](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/landscaping/snow_throwers.html) says abnormal vibrations and clogged blowers are a warning sign: stop the machine, then inspect and clean it following the owner's manual. It also says to handle fuel with care and wait until the engine has cooled before refuelling.

### Mistake 6: Overexertion

The CCOHS [snow shovelling page](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/snow_shovelling.html) says shovelling is strenuous: moving 5 kg of snow every 5 seconds adds up to over 70 kg a minute. It advises a proper lightweight snow shovel, a steady pace, and pushing snow rather than lifting it.

## Which record mistakes do snow removal workers make in Canada?

The main record mistake snow removal workers make in Canada is not logging routes and visits, which weakens vehicle claims and leaves billing disputes unresolved.

### Mistake 7: No mileage or income log

The CRA's [motor vehicle records page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/motor-vehicle-records.html) says to keep a logbook with each business trip's date, destination, purpose, and distance. Record each visit's time and snow depth too; it helps if a client disputes a charge.

## How do you set up snow removal to avoid these mistakes in Canada?

Set up snow removal in Canada to avoid these mistakes by sorting insurance, contracts, and a snow plan for each property before the first storm.

### Step 1: Read your city's snow bylaw

Note where snow may and may not go.

### Step 2: Get insurance quotes

Ask about slip-and-fall and property damage coverage.

### Step 3: Write the contract

Trigger depth, timing, salting, ice, and visits included.

### Step 4: Walk each property

Mark where snow will be piled, hydrants, and hazards such as garden edges.

### Step 5: Set up a visit log

Date, time, depth, and distance driven.
