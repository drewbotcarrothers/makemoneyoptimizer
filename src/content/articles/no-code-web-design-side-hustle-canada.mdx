No-code web design in Canada is building a simple site for a client with a hosted builder: usually one page with the service, the phone number, and the area they cover, without writing a custom application. No-code web design suits people who can follow a builder's screens, write plain sentences, and put the domain in the client's name.

The money, when this work pays, comes from a page a customer can find and call. People who do this well ship a small site and hand over the login. People who stall promise a brand, a shop, and search rankings, then disappear inside the settings.

## Who does no-code web design in Canada suit?

No-code web design in Canada suits people who can turn a kitchen-table explanation into a page a stranger can use on a phone. Local trades, clinics, and studios that still have only a social profile are the first clients. No-code web design is a poor fit if you want to write custom software, if you will hold the domain hostage, or if you will promise a spot on the first page of Google.

You do not need to invent a brand. You need the client's real phone number, spelled correctly, and a sentence about what they refuse to do.

## What should the client own on a Canadian small-business site?

The client should own the domain, the hosting login, and the copy on a no-code site in Canada, because the site is their shop window. CIRA's [Canadian Presence Requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/legal-policy-and-compliance/canadian-presence-requirements/) explain who may register a .ca name. Categories include a Canadian citizen and a permanent resident, among others. The client, if they qualify, should be the registrant. A site registered only in your personal account is a dispute waiting for the day you stop answering.

If the page collects names or emails, privacy law may apply to that commercial collection. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner's [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/) page is the federal starting point. Alberta, British Columbia, and Quebec have their own private-sector privacy statutes. Do not keep a copy of the client's customer list after the handoff. If the client will email people from the page, point them at [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en). You are not their lawyer.

A business that operates in Quebec may have French-language duties for public commercial content. The [Office québécois de la langue française](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/) is the place to send that client. Do not promise that a template is compliant.

## How do you start no-code web design in Canada?

Starting no-code web design in Canada means publishing one sample page and offering that same shape to businesses that do not have a site. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Pick the page and the client

One page for one trade: cleaners, tutors, or a mobile detailer. Write the sections: headline, services, area, phone, hours. If the list grows past one page, you are designing a different product.

### Step 2: Build a sample with invented details

Use a fictional shop name. Do not copy another business's text or photos. Make the page readable on a phone, with real headings and text that is not only an image. That is a quality bar. It is not a claim that every bylaw requires a particular standard.

### Step 3: Read the domain rule

If the live site should be a .ca, read CIRA's [Canadian Presence Requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/legal-policy-and-compliance/canadian-presence-requirements/). Plan for the client to be the registrant. Write the handoff steps while the sample is still fictional.

### Step 4: Write the agreement

Pages, revision rounds, date, price, who pays hosting, who owns the domain, and what you will not do: no ranking promise, no store, no legal policies you are not qualified to draft. If a form collects emails, say the client is responsible for that list and for anti-spam rules. This is not legal advice.

### Step 5: List fifteen businesses with no working site

A social page and a broken link count. Note the phone number you would put on the page, so the pitch is specific.

### Step 6: Send ten short notes

Name the gap and the sample. If the note is a commercial electronic message, read [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) first. Do not buy a list.

### Step 7: Take one paid build

Deposit, then draft. Use the client's words. One revision. Put the login in their email before you call it done.

### Step 8: Hand off in writing

Domain registrant, hosting login, and how to edit the phone number. Delete your copy of any customer information. Send the invoice the same day.

### Step 9: After two sites, raise the price or cut the scope

If both clients asked for a shop, the next conversation starts with Shopify, priced separately. If both stalled on copy, the next agreement says the text is due on a date or the clock stops.

## What no-code web design mistakes are common in Canada?

Common no-code web design mistakes in Canada are registering the client's domain in your name, promising search rankings, and building a store because the builder had a cart button. The domain is the asset. Your memory of the password is not a business continuity plan.

Copied text from a competitor's site is a copyright problem, not a shortcut. Write from the client's facts or leave a bracket they must fill.

Quebec clients are not an afterthought. If the business faces the public in French, send them to the [Office québécois de la langue française](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/) before you publish a single-language page and call it finished. This is not legal advice.

## Which tax rules apply to no-code web design in Canada?

No-code web design income in Canada is self-employment income, including a deposit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for this work follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreements and the e-transfers. If you pay for hosting and bill it back, keep that receipt separate from your fee so the markup is visible. Report the work on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add design, writing, and other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Web design is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.

Privacy, anti-spam, language rules, and .ca eligibility sit beside the invoice. Search your city for a business licence if you advertise locally. This guide does not state a licence fee.
