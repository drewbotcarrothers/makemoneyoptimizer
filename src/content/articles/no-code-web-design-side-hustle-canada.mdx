No-code web design in Canada means building websites for small businesses on Squarespace, Wix, Webflow, Framer or Hostinger's builder, without writing code, and charging a one-time project fee plus optional monthly care. Canadian freelancers and studios publish packages from $500 to $4,997, and Job Bank's median wage for employed web designers is $33.65 an hour. The client should own the domain and platform account from day one.

## Who does no-code web design suit?

It suits people with a good eye for layout, clear writing and patience with clients, who can learn a builder's features and explain them in plain English. It's evening-friendly work that sells well to local businesses you can meet in person.

It suits:

- **Graphic designers** adding websites to logos and branding; see [graphic design](/side-hustles/graphic-design-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Marketers and copywriters** who already write the words a site needs.
- **Developers** who want faster projects; see [side hustles for software developers](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-software-developers-canada/).
- **Students** with a portfolio and time in the summer (Ontario's Summer Company funds youth businesses).
- **People with local business networks** in trades, salons, restaurants or clinics.

It doesn't suit people who dislike client revisions, who want to avoid sales conversations, or who promise SEO rankings they can't control (see [local SEO](/side-hustles/local-seo-side-hustle-canada/) for that service done properly).

Match the platform to the person who will edit the site after launch, not to your favourite tool.

## How should you structure your offer?

Three tiers make it easy for clients to say yes and for you to avoid custom quoting every time. The published Canadian packages above all use tiers by page count, and Lead Web Studio pairs each tier with a support window (30 days on its starter package and 60 days of priority support on the next) and says most sites launch in 5–7 days.

|---|---|---|
| Starter | 1–3 pages, contact form, mobile check, 1 revision round | $500–$800 |
| Standard | 5–7 pages, booking or gallery, SEO basics, 2 rounds, training video | $1,000–$1,800 |
| Premium | 8–15 pages, copywriting, brand kit, 30 days' support | $2,000–$3,500 |

**Add-ons that sell well:** copywriting per page, a logo and colour palette, Google Business Profile setup, a French version, extra revision rounds, and product uploads for small stores.

**Payment terms:** a 50% deposit before work starts and 50% at launch protects you from projects that stall. For larger projects, split into thirds: deposit, design approval and launch.

## How long does a project take?

A small brochure site usually takes two to four weeks of calendar time, even when the build itself is only a few evenings. Most of the delay is waiting for the client's content, photos and approvals. Set content deadlines in the contract, and say that the launch date moves if content arrives late. Track your actual hours on every project for the first six months so you can tell which tier is underpriced.

## What should the client own?

The client should own the domain, the platform account and the content, because the site is their business asset. You get access as a contributor or collaborator.

- **Domain:** registered in the client's name. CIRA's [Canadian Presence Requirements](https://www.cira.ca/en/legal-policy-and-compliance/canadian-presence-requirements/) set who can register a .ca.
- **Platform account and billing:** in the client's name with their card; you're invited as a contributor.
- **Copy and images:** licensed for their use; avoid images you can't prove rights to.
- **Contact forms and email signups:** privacy law (federal [PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/), plus Alberta, B.C. and Quebec statutes) applies to collecting personal information, and [CASL](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies to marketing email signups. Add a privacy policy and an unticked consent box.
- **Accessibility:** Ontario's AODA requires designated public sector organizations and businesses or non-profits with **50 or more employees** to make public websites meet **WCAG 2.0 Level AA** (with two exceptions), per Ontario's [accessibility guide](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible). Smaller clients aren't covered by that rule, but accessible design (contrast, alt text, headings) is good practice for everyone.
- **Quebec:** businesses operating in Quebec have French-language obligations for commercial content; refer clients to the [Office québécois de la langue française](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/) rather than promising a template is compliant.

This is not legal advice.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

### Week 1: Learn and build your own site

- **Days 1–2:** Pick one platform and complete its free tutorials.
- **Days 3–5:** Build your own portfolio site with three sample projects (redesign real local businesses' sites as mock-ups, clearly labelled as concepts).
- **Days 6–7:** Write your service menu: starter site, standard site, care plan.

### Week 2: Systems

- Write a client questionnaire and a contract (templates below).
- Set up invoicing with a 50% deposit.
- Make a launch checklist you'll reuse on every project.

### Week 3: First clients

- List 30 local businesses with no site, a broken site or a non-mobile site.
- Contact 10 a day with a short, specific note (template below).
- Offer one discounted "portfolio project" in exchange for a testimonial and case study.

### Week 4: Deliver

- Run the first project: questionnaire, sitemap, build, two revision rounds, launch.
- Record a 10-minute handoff video showing the client how to edit.
- Offer the care plan at launch.

### Days 31–90

- **Weeks 5–8:** Two projects a month; track hours per page.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Raise prices for new clients if you're booked out.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Add an upsell: local SEO setup, booking, or [website maintenance](/side-hustles/website-maintenance-side-hustle-canada/).

**Launch checklist**

- [ ] Domain connected, SSL active, www and non-www redirect

- [ ] Contact form tested; privacy policy linked
- [ ] Page titles and descriptions written
- [ ] Alt text on images; colour contrast checked

## Which tax rules apply?

Web design income is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on net profit (QPP in Quebec).

This is not tax advice.

- **GST/HST:** registration is required once worldwide taxable supplies pass **$30,000** in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)); the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule doesn't apply.
- **Rates once registered:** generally based on where the client is: Ontario 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% (since April 1, 2025), New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I. 15%, 5% GST elsewhere, with QST in Quebec. Confirm with the [CRA rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Pass-through costs:** if you pay for a client's platform plan and rebill it, record it properly; it's cleaner for the client to pay the platform directly.
- **Deductions:** software, templates, fonts, stock images, a share of home office, internet and a computer (through capital cost allowance).

## Common no-code web design mistakes

- **Registering the domain in your own name.** It causes disputes when the relationship ends.
- **Unlimited revisions.** Put the number of rounds in the contract.
- **Starting without content.** Waiting on the client's text is the top cause of delays; make it a deposit condition or write it for an added fee.
- **Promising Google rankings.** Offer SEO basics, not guarantees.
- **No care plan.** Recurring revenue is what makes the business stable.
- **Ignoring accessibility and privacy** on forms and signups.
