A car detailing side hustle in Canada means cleaning, restoring and protecting vehicles inside and out, usually at the customer's home or workplace (mobile detailing) or in your own driveway or garage. You need a vacuum, buckets, microfibre, cleaners and a way to deal with wash water. Published 2026 prices from Canadian detailers start around $109 to $149 for an interior detail on a sedan and $170 to $249 for a full interior and exterior, and Canadian winters add a steady stream of salt-stained carpets.

<QuickAnswer>

- **What customers pay (2026 price lists):** Toronto interior detail from $149 and full detail from $170 for a small car, plus $20 for mobile service (Detailing Pro); Calgary basic interior $120 sedan / $140 mid-SUV (SunWash); Vancouver interior from $109 and full interior + exterior from $249 (Auto Alchemy).
- **Add-ons that sell:** pet hair removal and salt stain removal at $25 each, and headlight restoration at $75 (SunWash, Calgary).
- **Real examples:** Toronto high school student James Campbell launched Leaside Auto Spa at 16 with basic gear from Canadian Tire and an Ontario Summer Company grant; his prices start around $190. Saanich, B.C. student Owen Klassen earned more than $800 in his first weeks of mobile detailing at 15.
- **Water rules:** many cities restrict what can go into storm drains. Toronto has a page on washing cars outdoors under its sewers bylaw; check your city before washing on the street.
- **Tax:** report on Form T2125, pay 11.9% CPP on net earnings above $3,500, and register for GST/HST after $30,000.

## Who car detailing suits

Detailing is physical, satisfying work with visible before-and-after results. It suits you if:

- You enjoy cleaning cars and notice small details.
- You can kneel, bend and work outdoors for three or four hours at a stretch.
- You have a vehicle to carry gear, or access to a driveway or garage where customers can drop off cars.
- You're comfortable marketing with photos and short videos.

It's a weaker fit if you have no water or power access and no budget for a mobile setup, or if you want year-round outdoor work in a cold province without planning for winter.

## Real Canadian detailers

Both kept startup costs low, went to the customer, and priced by vehicle condition, which is how most detailing side hustles begin.

## The math on one detail

**Illustrative example.** You do a mobile interior detail on a mid-size SUV and charge $140, the same as SunWash's Calgary basic interior price for a mid-SUV. Assume the job takes 2.5 hours, products cost $8 and driving costs $6 (assumptions; track your own).

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Price | $140.00 |
| Products (assumed) | –$8.00 |
| Travel (assumed) | –$6.00 |
| Profit before tax | $126.00 |
| Per hour at 2.5 hours | $50.40 |

Add a $25 pet hair or salt stain add-on that takes 30 minutes and the job becomes $165 for three hours, or about $50.33 an hour after the same costs, while giving the customer a better result.

**Illustrative weekend.** Four details like this on a Saturday and Sunday would bring in about $504 in profit before tax. Do that most weekends from April to October and you'd still be well under the $30,000 GST/HST threshold. Pricing frameworks are in [how to price car detailing](/side-hustles/car-detailing-pricing-canada/).

## Your launch plan: the first 30 days

### Week 1: build your package and skills

- Write two or three packages (interior, exterior, full) with clear checklists.
- Practise on your own car and two friends' or family members' cars. Time every job.
- Take before-and-after photos from the same angles.

### Week 2: set up

- Buy your basic kit; confirm where you'll get water and power for mobile jobs.
- Check your city's rules on wash water and storm drains.
- Set up booking, payments and a separate account.
- Look into liability insurance that covers damage to customers' vehicles.
- Students in Ontario can check whether the Summer Company program is open, as Campbell did.

### Week 3: first customers

- Post before-and-after photos in local community groups and on social media.
- Offer an introductory price to neighbours and coworkers.
- Leave flyers at nearby condos and workplaces with lots of parked cars (with permission).
- Scripts in [how to get your first detailing client](/side-hustles/car-detailing-first-client-canada/).

### Week 4: deliver and collect reviews

- Walk each customer around the finished car.
- Ask for a review and permission to post photos.
- Offer a monthly maintenance wash at a lower price.

### Days 31 to 90

| Weeks | Focus | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | Add pet hair, salt stain and odour add-ons | Higher ticket per job |
| 7–8 | Start monthly maintenance clients | Recurring income |
| 9–10 | Cluster jobs by neighbourhood or workplace | Less travel |
| 11–12 | Decide on extractor, steamer or polisher upgrades | Faster, better results |

## Mobile, driveway or rented bay?

| Setup | Pros | Cons | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile (you go to the customer) | Convenient for customers; you can charge a travel or valet fee (Detailing Pro adds $20, Auto Alchemy $10) | Need water and power access or your own supply; weather cancellations | Most side-hustlers starting out |
| Your driveway or garage | Your own water and power; no travel time | Customers must drop off; neighbour and bylaw considerations | Interior-heavy work and winter jobs in a heated garage |
| Rented bay or shared shop | Heated, lit, year-round space | Monthly rent before you have steady demand | Detailers expanding into ceramic coating and paint correction |

Most people start mobile or in their own driveway and only rent space once winter demand and higher-end services justify it.

## Selling maintenance plans

One-time details pay well, but recurring clients smooth out your income. After a full detail, offer a maintenance plan:

- **Monthly exterior wash and interior tidy** at a lower price than a full detail, because the car never gets as dirty.
- **Seasonal resets** in spring (salt removal) and fall (protection before winter).
- **Workplace days**, where you detail several cars in one office lot on a set day each month, with the property manager's permission.

SunWash, for example, describes its $50 exterior maintenance wash as ideal for customers on recurring maintenance plans. Price your own plan so each visit still covers your time, products and travel.

## Photos that sell detailing

Before-and-after photos are the best marketing a detailer has.

1. Shoot the "before" in the same spot and angle as the "after."
2. Get close-ups of the worst areas: salt-stained carpet, pet hair, dirty cup holders, brake dust on wheels.
3. Use natural light, and avoid harsh midday glare on paint.
4. Post a short video of the transformation; extraction and pet-hair removal are especially satisfying to watch.
5. Ask permission before posting, and hide licence plates.

## Checklists and templates

**Interior detail checklist**

- [ ] Remove mats and personal items (bag valuables for the customer)
- [ ] Dry brush and vacuum seats, carpets, trunk and crevices
- [ ] Shampoo or extract stains and salt marks
- [ ] Clean dash, console, vents, door panels, cup holders
- [ ] Clean and condition leather
- [ ] Clean interior glass and mirrors
- [ ] Wash and dry mats; reinstall
- [ ] Final walk-around with the customer

**Exterior detail checklist**

- [ ] Rinse; pre-treat bugs, tar and wheels
- [ ] Two-bucket hand wash, top to bottom
- [ ] Clean wheels, tires and wheel wells
- [ ] Dry with clean microfibre
- [ ] Apply sealant or wax
- [ ] Dress tires; clean exterior glass
- [ ] Check door jambs

**Vehicle check-in form (before you start)**

- [ ] Customer name, vehicle, licence plate
- [ ] Existing scratches, dents, chips and stains noted and photographed
- [ ] Valuables removed or bagged with the customer's knowledge
- [ ] Special requests (fragrance-free, child seats, pet hair)
- [ ] Customer signature or text confirmation

**Booking reply template**

> Hi [Name]! For a [year/make/model], our [package] is $[price] and takes about [x] hours. I come to you; I'll need [access to an outdoor outlet / a parking spot]. Add pet hair or salt stain removal for $[price]. I have openings on [days]. Want me to book you in?

## Water, chemicals and the law

Wash water carries soap, oil and grime. Many municipalities prohibit discharging anything other than clean water into storm drains, which flow to creeks and lakes. Toronto has a dedicated page on washing cars outdoors under its sewers bylaw. To stay compliant:

- Use rinseless or waterless wash products for mobile exterior work.
- Use mats or containment to collect runoff where required.
- Work on grass or gravel where allowed, never next to storm drains.
- Ask condo and workplace property managers for permission before working in their lots.

## Insurance and safety

Liability insurance protects you if you damage a customer's paint, interior or electronics. Ask insurers specifically about care, custody and control coverage for vehicles you work on. Wear gloves and eye protection with chemicals, keep products away from children, and use extension cords rated for outdoor use.

## Taxes for detailers

- **Income tax:** report revenue and expenses (products, tools, insurance, vehicle use, phone) on Form T2125.
- **CPP:** 11.9% of net earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 in 2026 (QPP in Quebec).
- **GST/HST:** you're a small supplier until taxable supplies pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Once registered, charge the rate where the work happens, such as 13% HST in Ontario (Detailing Pro quotes its prices before HST).
- **Equipment:** larger tools may be depreciated through capital cost allowance rather than deducted in one year.

## Provincial and seasonal notes

- **Ontario:** Summer Company grants have helped student detailers start; HST is 13% once registered.
- **Alberta:** GST only once registered; SunWash in Calgary lists salt stain removal as a standard add-on.
- **B.C.:** a milder coastal climate makes year-round exterior work more practical; Auto Alchemy charges a fixed $10 travel fee.
- **Prairies, Quebec and Atlantic Canada:** outdoor washing stops in deep winter, but interior and salt removal work continues if you have a heated space. See [winter car detailing](/side-hustles/winter-car-detailing-salt-removal-canada/).
- **Spring** is the busiest season almost everywhere, as owners clean off winter salt.

## What to watch out for

- **Damaging a customer's car.** Test products on hidden spots and learn before offering paint correction or ceramic coating.
- **Water runoff fines.** Know your city's storm drain rules.
- **Weather.** Rain and cold cancel outdoor jobs; keep a flexible schedule.
- **Underpricing heavy jobs.** Price by condition, as Campbell does, and quote pet hair separately.
- **Physical strain.** Use kneeling pads and take breaks.
- **Equipment theft** from your vehicle; lock everything.
