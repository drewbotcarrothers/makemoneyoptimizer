<QuickAnswer>

- **App shoppers:** Instacart pays batch pay + promotions + 100% of tips, with heavy pay of at least $2 and tip protection up to $10. On an illustrative 3-hour Ontario Saturday, that nets $17.43 an hour after car costs.
- **Floors:** Ontario $17.95 an hour of assignment time from October 1, 2026; B.C. $21.89 an hour of engaged time plus $0.35/km. Tips are excluded and waiting is unpaid.
- **Private shopping for seniors:** Toronto services list $25 to $35 an hour; BabaTap charges $35 for one hour or $65 for two.

Grocery shoppers in Canada earn two ways: per batch on an app such as Instacart, or per hour or per trip from households they find themselves. App pay is batch pay plus promotions plus tips, minus what the kilometres cost, divided by every hour logged in. Private shopping for older adults is priced like companion care: Toronto services list $25 to $35 an hour. App shopping suits someone who can log shifts near busy stores. Private clients suit someone who wants fewer, steadier trips.

Batch pay plus promotions plus tips. What the sources said on October 9, 2026:

## How do you calculate grocery shopping pay?

```text
App shift:     real hourly = (batch pay + promotions + tips + top-up − km × cost per km)
                             ÷ logged-in hours
Batch floor:   minutes ÷ 60 × target hourly + km × cost per km
Private trip:  trip price = hours (drive + shop + checkout + put away) × hourly rate
                            + km × cost per km
```

**Get a per-km cost.** Until you have receipts, CAA's figures for a 2025 Nissan Sentra in Ontario work out to $0.26/km with operating costs, from its [Driving Costs Calculator](https://carcosts.caa.ca/results/ontario/passenger_mainstream/ice). **Count checkout and the wait.** Heavy items and large counts take longer than the distance suggests. **For private clients, price the visit, not the cart.** The groceries are the client's purchase.

## What do worked grocery shopping examples look like?

**Illustrative.** Batch pay, tips, minutes, kilometres and targets are my assumptions; car cost is $0.26/km.

### Example 1: an Ontario Saturday on an app

- 4 batches at $9 batch pay and promotions plus $7 tips = $64. 3 hours logged in, 2.25 hours on assignments, 45 km.
- Car: 45 × $0.26 = $11.70. ($64 − $11.70) ÷ 3 = **$17.43 an hour**.
- Floor check: 2.25 × $17.95 = $40.39 against $36 of pay before tips. Ontario checks per assignment or pay period, so read the statement for a top-up.

### Example 2: the same shift in B.C.

- 2.25 engaged hours × $21.89 = $49.25, plus 35 engaged km × $0.35 = $12.25: floor $61.50 before tips. Pay before tips was $36, so a $25.50 top-up applies for that pay period.
- $61.50 + $28 tips − $11.70 = $77.80; ÷ 3 = **$25.93 an hour**.

### Example 3: a weekly shop for a senior in Toronto

- 1.5 hours (drive, shop, checkout, put away) and 14 km at a $30 target: 1.5 × $30 + 14 × $0.26 = $48.64.
- Anchors: BabaTap $65 for 2 hours; Tuktu $25–30 an hour.
- Quote **$50 a trip, up to 90 minutes**, extra time at $7.50 per 15 minutes. Groceries paid by the client or reimbursed at cost with the receipt. Net: ($50 − $3.64) ÷ 1.5 = $30.91 an hour.

### Example 4: four weekly trips, HST-registered in Ontario

- 4 × $50 = $200 a month. With 13% HST on your fee: $200 × 1.13 = **$226**.
- If you buy as the client's agent and pass the receipt on at cost, the groceries are a reimbursement, not part of your taxable fee. Not registered: $200, no tax line.

## How do you raise grocery shopping earnings?

- **On apps, decline below the batch card.** The 60-minute, 8 km batch needs $22.08 at a $20 target.
- **Learn two or three stores well.** A familiar layout cuts the shopping minutes in every batch.
- **Check statements against the floor.** In Ontario and B.C., the top-up is part of your pay.
- **On private clients, raise at the new year** with notice and the reason, as in the template. Scripts are in [how to raise your side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/).
- **Group private clients by street and day.** Two clients on one route share the drive.

## What grocery shopping pricing mistakes are common?

Counting gross pay as income, ignoring waiting and checkout time, and accepting heavy batches that underpay for the effort. For private clients: pricing as a percentage of the grocery bill, fronting groceries on your own card without a receipt process, and leaving out cancellation terms.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect grocery shopping income?

Shopper income is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both shares of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): register once taxable supplies pass $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, across all your businesses. The first-fare rule is for passenger rides, not grocery delivery. Once registered, use the rate from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html). The [platform income tax guide](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/) explains what platforms report.
