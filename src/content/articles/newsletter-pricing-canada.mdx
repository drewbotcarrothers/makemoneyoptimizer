Pricing a paid newsletter in Canada means setting a monthly price, an annual price that rewards commitment, and, if you take sponsors, a per-issue sponsor rate, then checking what each one leaves after platform and card fees. On Substack, the platform keeps 10% of subscription revenue and Stripe takes its card fees on top. There is no official Canadian price for a newsletter, so the work is in matching the price to the value of one issue and the size of your list. This guide suits writers who already publish on a schedule and want to charge for it.

## What do newsletter platforms and card processors charge in Canada?

Newsletter platforms either take a percentage of paid revenue or charge a plan fee, and the card processor takes a fee per payment on top. The figures below come from each company's own page; open them again before you set a price.

| Platform or processor | What it charges | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substack | Free to publish; keeps 10% of each subscription payment; optional one-time $50 custom domain | [Substack cost page](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037607131-How-much-does-Substack-cost) |
| Stripe via Substack | 2.9% + $0.30 per card payment (listed for US cards) plus 0.7% Stripe Billing on recurring payments | [Substack cost page](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037607131-How-much-does-Substack-cost) |
| Stripe in Canada | 2.9% + CA$0.30 per successful transaction for domestic cards | [Stripe Canada pricing](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing) |
| beehiiv | Free plan up to 2,500 subscribers; says it takes 0% of paid subscription revenue, minus Stripe's processing fee; paid subscriptions are listed on its paid plans | [beehiiv pricing](https://www.beehiiv.com/pricing) |

A platform with a 0% take rate and a monthly plan fee can cost more than a 10% platform at a small list size, and less at a larger one. Compare both at your actual subscriber count.

## Which pricing models work for a newsletter in Canada?

Most paid newsletters in Canada use a monthly price with a cheaper annual option, and some add a higher "supporter" tier. Sponsorships are a separate model that pays per issue and works on free lists.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly subscription | Readers pay each month | Testing a price | The fixed $0.30 card fee bites on small prices |
| Annual subscription | One yearly payment, usually discounted | Committed readers | Refund requests mid-year |
| Supporter tier | Higher price for the same issues or extras | Readers who want to back you | Promising extras you cannot keep up |
| Sponsorship | A business pays to be mentioned in an issue | Free lists with a clear audience | Disclosure, and fit with your readers |
| Paid product | A guide or template sold to the list | Practical subjects | Product work competes with writing time |

## How do you calculate a newsletter price in Canada?

Calculate a newsletter price in Canada from the value of one issue to a reader, your publishing cost per month, and the fees per payment. Then work out how many paying readers you need to reach your goal.

### Step 1: Decide what a paid reader gets

Extra issues, full archives, or the main issue itself. Write it in one sentence before you choose a number.

### Step 2: Choose a monthly test price

Look at newsletters on similar subjects and pick a price a reader can say yes to without a long think.

### Step 3: Subtract fees per payment

On Substack, take off 10%, then 2.9% + $0.30, then 0.7% for Billing.

### Step 4: Set the annual price

A common approach is a discount equal to one or two months. Annual payments also pay the fixed $0.30 card fee once instead of twelve times.

### Step 5: Work out how many paying readers you need

Divide your monthly goal by the net amount per subscriber. Keep that number in view when you write the paid offer.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose you charge $6 a month on Substack. Substack's 10% is $0.60. Stripe's card fee is 2.9% × $6 + $0.30 = $0.47. Billing is 0.7% × $6 = $0.04. Together that is $0.52 after rounding, so you net about $6.00 − $0.60 − $0.52 = $4.88 per monthly subscriber.

An annual price of $60 (two months free) nets $60 − $6.00 − ($1.74 + $0.30) − $0.42 = $51.54, or $4.30 a month. Annual subscribers pay less per month but are paid up front and less likely to cancel next month.

If your goal is $500 a month from subscriptions and everyone pays monthly, you need $500 ÷ $4.88 = about 103 paying readers.

## How do you price newsletter sponsorships in Canada?

Price a newsletter sponsorship in Canada as a flat fee per issue based on your list size, how many open, and how closely the sponsor matches your readers. There is no official rate card. Start with a number you would be comfortable saying out loud, and raise it as results come in.

**Illustrative example:** suppose you have 3,000 subscribers and choose to charge an assumed $50 per 1,000 subscribers for one placement. That is 3 × $50 = $150 per issue. This rate is a teaching assumption, not a market figure. Sponsored mentions are advertising: the Competition Bureau's [influencer guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says to disclose material connections, including payment, clearly.

## When should you raise newsletter prices in Canada?

Raise newsletter prices in Canada when readers keep paying after a few months, when you add issues or features, or when your costs go up. Many writers keep existing subscribers at their old price and raise it for new ones.

Tell readers ahead of a change and say what they get. Raise sponsor rates when your list grows or a sponsor rebooks.

## What newsletter pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common newsletter pricing mistakes in Canada are pricing so low the fixed card fee eats the payment, forgetting the platform's cut, and emailing people who never agreed to receive it. The last one is a legal issue, not just a pricing one.

Under [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en), commercial emails need consent, identification, and an unsubscribe option; a paid newsletter pitch is commercial. Do not buy lists. Do not promise "daily" if you can write weekly. The [newsletter side hustle guide](/side-hustles/newsletter-side-hustle-canada/) covers consent in more detail.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect newsletter prices in Canada?

Newsletter income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, across all your businesses.

Substack's page notes that some writers add sales tax on top of their price. Once you are registered, decide whether your price includes tax or shows it at checkout, and keep sponsor invoices with tax shown separately.
