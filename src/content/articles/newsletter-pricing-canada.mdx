Pricing a paid newsletter in Canada means a monthly price, an annual price, sometimes a founding or supporter tier, and a per-issue sponsor rate if you sell ads. Seven Canadian Substack newsletters checked on October 9, 2026 charge CA$6 to CA$19.99 a month, and four Canadian newsletter rate cards price one sponsor placement at $200 to $750. Platform choice changes what you keep: at 100 paying readers on CA$8, the gap between Substack and Kit's free plan is about $80 a month. A paid newsletter suits a writer who already publishes on a schedule. It is a slow way to earn if you have no list yet.

## What do newsletter platforms keep in Canada?

Substack takes a share, beehiiv charges a plan fee, and Kit charges a commerce fee. Figures from each company's page on October 9, 2026.

| Platform | What it charges | Card fees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Substack](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037607131-How-much-does-Substack-cost) | Free; keeps 10% of each subscription payment; optional $50 one-time custom domain | Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 per payment plus 0.7% Billing |
| [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/pricing) | Free up to 2,500 subscribers; paid subscriptions start on Lite at US$49 a month billed yearly (US$59 monthly); 0% of subscription revenue | Stripe's fee, which for [domestic Canadian cards](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing) is 2.9% + CA$0.30 |
| [Kit](https://kit.com/pricing) | Free plan up to 10,000 subscribers includes selling subscriptions; Creator US$33 a month | 3.5% + $0.30 per CAD sale, processing included |

Stripe adds 0.8% for international cards and 2% when currency is converted, so a US reader paying a CAD price costs more to process. At the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4240 on October 8, 2026, beehiiv Lite's US$49 is CA$69.78 a month.

## Which pricing models work for a newsletter?

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly subscription | Readers pay each month | Testing a price | The $0.30 card fee is a bigger share of a small price |
| Annual subscription | One yearly payment, one to three months off | Readers who already trust you | Refund requests mid-year |
| Founding or supporter tier | Voluntary higher price, same issues or small extras | Readers who want to back you | Extras you can't keep delivering |
| Sponsor placement | A business pays for a section of one issue | Free lists with a narrow audience | Disclosure, and fit with your readers |
| Dedicated send | One email about the sponsor only | Niche professional lists | Unsubscribes if you do it often |

## How do you calculate a newsletter price?

Start from the local price range, then check the net and the reader count.

```text
Net per payment     = price − platform share − card fees − billing fee
Paying readers      = monthly goal ÷ net per monthly payment
Annual price        = monthly × 12 − (1 to 3 months)
Sponsor rate        = (list size ÷ 1,000) × rate per 1,000
```

**Price from the table, not from a guess.** Pick the row nearest your subject. A personal finance newsletter for Canadians sits at CA$6 to CA$8; research a reader can act on is where CA$19.99 appears. **Then run the net.** The fixed $0.30 matters more at CA$5 than at CA$10. **Then count readers.** If the count is 10 times your current paid conversion, the price or the goal has to move.

## What do worked newsletter pricing examples look like?

**Illustrative.** Reader counts, the sponsor rate and the goal are my assumptions; fees are each platform's published rate, and prices are before any sales tax.

### Example 1: CA$8 a month on Substack, 40 monthly and 10 annual readers

- Monthly: $8 − $0.80 (10%) − $0.53 (2.9% + $0.30) − $0.06 (0.7%) = **$6.61** net. 40 × $6.612 = $264.48 a month.
- Annual at CA$80: $80 − $8.00 − $2.62 − $0.56 = **$68.82** net. 10 × $68.82 = $688.20 a year, or $57.35 a month.
- Total: about **$321.83 a month**. Gross is 40 × $96 + 10 × $80 = $4,640 a year, far below the GST/HST threshold.

### Example 2: the same CA$8 on three platforms

| Paying monthly readers | Revenue | Substack keeps you | beehiiv Lite keeps you | Kit Free keeps you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | $160 | $132.24 | $79.58 | $148.40 |
| 100 | $800 | $661.20 | $677.02 | $742.00 |

beehiiv is revenue − Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30 per payment − CA$69.78 plan fee; if Stripe's 0.7% Billing fee also applies there, subtract $5.60 more at 100 readers. Kit is revenue − 3.5% + $0.30 per payment. At 20 readers the beehiiv plan fee eats most of the money; at 100 it beats Substack by $15.82 a month.

### Example 3: a sponsor for a 3,000-reader regional newsletter

- Anchors: $22.73 to $160 per 1,000 in the rate cards above; the narrow, high-open lists are the expensive ones.
- At $50 per 1,000: 3 × $50 = **$150 per issue**. A four-issue pack at 10% off: 4 × $150 × 0.9 = **$540**.
- If you're registered for HST in Ontario: $150 × 1.13 = **$169.50**. Not registered: invoice $150 with no tax.

### Example 4: how many readers $1,000 a month takes

- Kit Free at CA$8: $8 − ($0.28 + $0.30) = $7.42 net. $1,000 ÷ $7.42 = 134.8, so **135 paying readers**.
- At CA$10: $10 − ($0.35 + $0.30) = $9.35. $1,000 ÷ $9.35 = 106.95, so **107 readers**.
- Gross at 135 × $8 × 12 = $12,960 a year, below $30,000 in any quarter or over four quarters, unless other business income pushes you over.

## What templates help you price a newsletter?

```text
PAID TIER PAGE — [Newsletter name]
Free: [one issue a week]
Paid, CA$8 a month or CA$80 a year (two months free):
  - [the extra issue / full archive / what readers act on]
  - [comments, Q&A, or community]

Prices before applicable sales tax.
```

```text
SPONSOR RATE CARD — [Newsletter name], [month year]
Readers: [3,000] subscribers, [__]% average open rate, [weekly]
Audience: [who they are, in one line]
Sponsored section (top of issue, 60 words + link)      $150
Four issues                                            $540
Dedicated email (one per month maximum)                $450
Every sponsored mention is labelled "Sponsored."
Prices in CAD, plus GST/HST if registered [# ______].
```

```text
Hi [Name], a note before [date]: the paid plan goes from CA$6 to
CA$8 a month for new subscribers. Since you joined early, your price
stays at CA$6 [until (date) / for as long as you stay]. Paid issues
went from two a month to four this year, and that's what the new
price pays for.
```

## How do you raise newsletter prices?

- **Raise for new readers first.** Keeping existing readers at the old price, as in the template, avoids a wave of cancellations.
- **Change the annual discount before the monthly price.** The Line's annual plan saves $6; Canadian Finance Girly's saves $17. A smaller discount is a quiet increase.
- **Add a founding tier.** Six of the seven newsletters above offer one, at CA$150 to US$400.
- **Raise sponsor rates by list size and opens.** Re-divide your rate by your list every quarter; a list that has grown 50% supports a higher sponsor price.
- Scripts for the conversation are in [how to raise your side hustle rates](/side-hustles/how-to-raise-side-hustle-rates-canada/).

## What newsletter pricing mistakes are common?

Common mistakes are a price so low the $0.30 fee eats the payment, choosing beehiiv's plan fee before you have enough paying readers, and selling sponsor slots without a label. Sponsored mentions are advertising: the Competition Bureau's [influencer guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says to disclose payment clearly. Under [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en), commercial emails need consent, identification and an unsubscribe link, so build the list from sign-ups and never buy one. The [newsletter side hustle guide](/side-hustles/newsletter-side-hustle-canada/) covers consent.

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect newsletter prices?

Newsletter income is self-employment income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). GST/HST registration is required once taxable supplies pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, across all your businesses. Once registered, charge the rate from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) on sponsor invoices.

Substack changes its role on December 1, 2026: its [tax page](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/53351588701588-Upcoming-changes-to-tax-on-Substack) says it will calculate, collect and remit sales tax on paid subscriptions for every publication with payments enabled, and Canadian publishers will have tax added at checkout, with no option to include it in the price. A CA$8 plan will cost readers more than CA$8. On other platforms, subscription tax stays your job once you're registered. Sponsor invoices are yours on every platform; <AffiliateLink program="freshbooks" label="FreshBooks" /> can send them with tax shown separately, and a spreadsheet works at the start.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
