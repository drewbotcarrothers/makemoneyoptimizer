After this guide you can decide if a mowing route fits your climate, buy the least equipment that does the job, and sign a few neighbours before you own a trailer you do not need.

Lawn care in Canada is a season, not a personality. In much of the country you are selling weekly cuts from sometime in spring until the grass stops, then you are either done or you switch to leaves and, later, snow. The operators who stay calm treat it that way. If you want the winter half of the same driveways, read [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada) before you buy only a mower. Small repairs along the same street often turn into [handyman work](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada). Indoor recurring work is [house cleaning](/side-hustles/house-cleaning-side-hustle-canada).

## Who it fits

**This can fit if** you can do physical work in the heat, you have a place to store a mower, and you live where people have lawns rather than only balconies. It fits early risers who can mow before municipal noise rules and before the day gets hot. It fits people who like repeating a route more than inventing a new offer every week.

**This is a poor fit if** you have nowhere to keep fuel and equipment, your body will not tolerate pushing a mower, or your “market” is a high-rise with a superintendent who already has a contract. It is a poor fit if you need income in January from grass. It is a poor fit if you plan to spray whatever is under the sink. Several provinces restrict cosmetic pesticides. More on that below.

A riding mower is not step one. A reliable push mower, a string trimmer, and a rake will tell you if you like the work.

## The season is the business model

Grass does not follow a national calendar.

- **Southern Ontario and southern Quebec:** a long enough season to matter, with a spring rush and a hot July that slows growth. Noise bylaws still limit early Sunday mornings in many cities.
- **Prairies:** a shorter, intense season. Drought can cut the route in half. Price water reality into your expectations. You do not control rain.
- **Atlantic Canada:** a decent season, more rain, and schedules that slip. Build slack into the week.
- **British Columbia’s south coast:** a longer green season and moss, not the same brown-August story. The interior is closer to a prairie calendar.
- **The North:** a short season. Do not buy southern-Ontario equipment debt for a six-week cut.

Leaves are a second product in many cities. Bagging or hauling them is heavier than mowing and may be banned or restricted by municipal yard-waste rules. Know whether you can leave clippings, whether the city takes paper bags only, and where you are not allowed to dump.

## Pricing that survives a dry July

Charge per cut for a defined lawn, not “whatever it takes.” Walk the property. Note width, hills, gates, and whether you must bag. A small, flat, fenced front yard is not a corner lot with a ditch.

Weekly service on a set weekday is easier to sell and easier to deliver than random calls. Biweekly only works when the grass cooperates. In a wet June, biweekly looks shaggy and you will be asked to come early. Put that in the conversation.

A simple seasonal mindset: you are selling about as many cuts as the climate gives you. If you need twelve equal months of income, pair this with [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada) or indoor work. Do not pretend July’s revenue happens in February.

There is no honest national price. Get three local flyers or posts and see what maintained lawns actually go for, then check the number against hours and fuel. If the winner’s price only works with unpaid help, it is not your price.

## Step-by-step

### 1. Walk your own block and count real lawns

Not parks. Not condos with a crew. Houses and small rentals whose owners are not already mowing happily. Five possible clients within a walk is a better start than a Facebook ad to the whole region.

### 2. Check noise, clipping, and pesticide rules

Cities set hours for mowers and leaf blowers. Some restrict blowers entirely. Ontario’s cosmetic pesticide rules, and Quebec’s rules, mean you should not show up with a weed killer from the hardware store and spray a client’s lawn. Assume cosmetic pesticides are restricted until you have read your province’s page and any municipal bylaw. Fertilizer has its own labelling and, near water, extra care. If you are not sure, sell cutting and trimming only. That is a complete service.

### 3. Buy or borrow the smallest working kit

Mower that starts, trimmer, rake, fuel, protection for ears and eyes, closed boots. Service the mower before the first paid cut: blade sharpness changes both the look and the strain on you.

### 4. Write a one-screen offer

Weekly cut and trim, clippings handled the way the city allows, price for a typical yard on your street, and the weekday you will come. Say you do not spray. Say what happens in a rain delay: you come the next dry day, not at 10 p.m.

### 5. Knock or post locally once

A short note through the door still works for lawns because the buyer can see the grass. Include your first name, the street you are on, the price range, and a phone number. Skip the five-paragraph origin story.

### 6. Do the first cut in daylight and leave edges

Blow or sweep clippings off the walk. That five minutes is the difference between “someone mowed” and “I will keep them.” Take a photo for your own record if the client is fine with it. Do not photograph their kids, plates, or open garages.

### 7. Lock a weekday

“I’ll be here Thursdays” is the product. Weather exceptions get a text the night before or the morning of. Silence is how you lose a lawn to the teenager next door.

### 8. Track time and fuel per lawn

After four cuts you know which properties are underpriced. Fix those prices for next month rather than subsidizing a corner lot all summer. Offer the client the new number with notice. Some will say no. That is allowed.

### 9. Add fall, or do not

If you want leaf cleanup, price it separately and learn the municipal bag rules. If you do not want it, say so in September before they assume you are their autumn plan. The same conversation is how you introduce snow without being cornered in November. The [snow guide](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada) is the one to follow if you go there.

## Your first week

This week can happen in spring, or in early fall if you are selling the rest of this season and next.

- **Day 1:** Count lawns on two streets. Read the noise bylaw and the pesticide rule for your province.
- **Day 2:** Secure a mower that starts. Price insurance with an honest description: residential cutting, no pesticides, no employees.
- **Day 3:** One-screen offer. Fuel, line, glasses, ears.
- **Day 4:** Ten doors or a local post aimed at those streets.
- **Day 5:** First paid cut. Time it. Sweep the walk.
- **Day 6:** Ask for the weekly slot and for the house two doors down.
- **Day 7:** Do the math per hour after fuel. Continue only if your body and the number both say yes.

## Tips that are easy to miss

Sharp blade, shorter visits. A dull blade tears grass and tugs the mower. Sharpening is a skill you can learn or a $15 shop job. Either is cheaper than a new mower.

Do not mow wet grass to “stay on schedule.” It clumps, it slips, and it looks worse. Text the client. A rain delay you explain is professional. A mud streak is not.

Edging and the sidewalk sweep are the visible quality. The middle of the lawn is the easy part.

Rocks, toys, and irrigation heads hide. Walk the lawn once before you mow a new property. A broken sprinkler head can erase several cuts of profit.

Fuel storage has rules and common sense: approved can, out of the sun, not in a stairwell, not beside the furnace. Your municipality and your insurer both have opinions.

## Common mistakes

Buying a zero-turn and a trailer for a hope. Storage, insurance, and payments arrive before the route does.

Spraying weeds because a client asked nicely. Provincial bans exist, and “the customer wanted it” is a weak defence.

Ignoring noise bylaws and starting at 7 a.m. on a holiday because you have a day job at 9. One complaint can end a street’s worth of goodwill.

Quoting a price from the sidewalk in March for a lawn you have never walked. Walk it.

No plan for July heat or for smoke days. Shorten the day, carry water, and do not be a hero at 34 degrees. The grass will wait until evening or tomorrow.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Lawn fees are self-employment income. Keep a list of clients, dates, and what you spent on fuel and repairs. Report it on the T2125 with your return. Both shares of CPP generally apply to the net. A truck or car used to haul the mower needs a kilometre log if you claim vehicle costs. The mower itself is equipment. The CRA’s capital-cost rules are picky. A simple first year with modest used gear is easier than a trailer you cannot explain. If the amounts get large, ask an accountant.

Small-supplier GST/HST: $30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, including associates, in one calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Under that, you usually do not have to register. Over it, you do. The CRA’s [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page has the timing, including the difference between crossing the line in one quarter and crossing it over four. Lawn care is not ride-sharing. Do not apply the taxi first-dollar rule here.

Charge the rate for your province once you are registered. That might be HST, or GST plus a provincial tax. Quebec is GST and QST. Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% from 1 April 2025. Confirm on the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Business licences are municipal. Pesticide rules are provincial plus municipal. Noise bylaws are municipal. Three different searches, one afternoon. Workers’ compensation may matter later if you hire. A buddy you pay in cash to help on Saturdays can still be your worker. Check the board in your province before you scale.
