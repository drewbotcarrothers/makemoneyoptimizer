<QuickAnswer>
A lawn care side hustle in Canada usually means weekly or biweekly mowing, trimming and cleanup for neighbours, sold by the visit or by the season. Published 2026 prices give a sense of the market: Ottawa Lawn Maintenance lists weekly mowing from $42–$65 a visit for lots up to 5,000 sq ft, and Happy Grass Calgary lists $48 a service for an infill lot and $59 for a 50' × 100' inner-city lot on its Pro plan. For comparison, Job Bank's median wage for employed landscaping labourers is $20.75 an hour. You can start with a mower, a trimmer and a blower. ## Who does lawn care in Canada suit?

Lawn care suits people who like physical outdoor work, can keep a weekly schedule, and have a way to move equipment, whether that's a trailer, a truck, a car trunk at first, or a route you can walk. It's one of the easiest service businesses for teens and students to start, as the examples below show.

It suits:

- **Students** with summers free (and Ontario students can apply for Summer Company funding).
- **Early risers and weekend workers** who want outdoor cash work.
- **People with a truck or trailer** who can also do [fall leaf cleanup](/side-hustles/fall-leaf-cleanup-side-hustle-canada/) and [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/) for year-round income.
- **Handy people** who can upsell small yard jobs (see [handyman](/side-hustles/handyman-side-hustle-canada/)).

It doesn't suit people with allergies to grass and pollen, people who can't work in heat, or anyone who needs income all year without adding a winter service.

## How long is the lawn care season in Canada?

The season follows the region. Grass doesn't follow a national calendar. Noise hours for mowing are set by each city's bylaw, so check yours.

- **Southern Ontario and southern Quebec:** a long enough season to matter, with a spring rush and a hot July that slows growth.
- **Prairies:** a shorter, intense season. Drought can shrink the route, and you don't control rain, so factor that into your expectations.
- **Atlantic Canada:** a decent season with more rain and schedules that slip. Build slack into the week.
- **British Columbia's south coast:** a longer green season, plus moss. The interior is closer to a prairie calendar.
- **The North:** a short season. Don't take on equipment debt for a few weeks of cutting.

Leaves are a second product in many cities. Bagging and hauling them is heavier work than mowing, and municipal yard-waste rules apply, so check whether your city takes paper bags only and where dumping isn't allowed. See also [the best spring side hustles](/side-hustles/best-spring-side-hustles-canada/) and [the best winter side hustles](/side-hustles/best-winter-side-hustles-canada/).

## How to quote a lawn

Quote on site, or from a satellite view with a quick visit before the first cut. Price on these factors:

| Factor | What raises the price |
|---|---|
| Lot size | Bigger lawns take longer; use the lot-size bands in published price lists |
| Obstacles | Gardens, trees, play sets and tight side yards slow mowing and trimming |
| Slope | Hills are slower and harder on you and the mower |
| Access | Locked gates, narrow side passages or stairs to the back yard |
| Frequency | Biweekly lawns grow longer between cuts and take more time per visit |
| Condition | Overgrown first cuts take far longer; charge a one-time first-cut fee |
| Extras | Edging, bagging clippings and hauling away waste |

Write the price per visit and what's included, and tell the client what happens on rain days. Re-quote if the lawn changes, for example a new garden or a fence. Keep a short note on each client's file with the lot details, gate codes, where to leave clippings and any problem areas, so a helper can cover the route if you're sick or away. Notes like these also make it easier to sell the route later if you move on.

## A lawn care season calendar

| Period | Main work | Sell now |
|---|---|---|
| Early spring | Cleanups, raking, debris | Season mowing contracts |
| Late spring | Peak growth, weekly mowing | Aeration and overseeding (where allowed) |
| Summer | Mowing slows in heat or drought; switch some lawns to biweekly | Hedge trimming, vacation mowing |
| Early fall | Mowing continues, growth picks up again | Fall leaf cleanup and snow removal contracts |
| Late fall | Leaf cleanup, final cut | Next spring's early-booking list |
| Winter | Snow removal (if offered) | Equipment service and blade sharpening |

## Worked math: a 15-lawn weekly route

**Illustrative.** The per-cut price and times are assumptions for the arithmetic. The price sits below the Ottawa and Calgary list prices above, as a newer operator might charge.

Assume 15 small lawns on a weekly route at $40 a cut, each taking 30 minutes on site, plus 10 minutes of driving or walking between lawns.

- Revenue: 15 × $40 = $600 a week.
- Time: 15 × 40 minutes = 10 hours a week.
- Gross per hour: $60.
- Suppose fuel, trimmer line, blades and maintenance take an assumed $50 a week: $550 ÷ 10 hours = **$55 an hour** before tax, CPP and equipment depreciation.

Over an assumed 20-week season, that's $11,000 before costs other than the weekly allowance. Rain-outs, drought weeks and customers who cancel all reduce it. Clustering lawns close together is the biggest lever, because travel time is unpaid. Adding a second lawn on the same street often takes only the on-site time, with almost no extra travel.

## Real Canadian lawn care businesses

These are publicly documented. Their results are not typical.

**L&L Lawn Care (Sarnia, Ontario).** The Sarnia Journal reported on April 14, 2025 that Luke Laevens, 17, and Luca Andali, 16, launched the business in April 2024 with grass cutting, packing their equipment into a car trunk. They found customers by knocking on doors, handing out flyers and posting on social media, and built up repeat customers and several rental properties. They expanded into snow removal, spring cleanups, junk removal, power washing and gutter cleaning. In March 2025 they received a $3,000 Summer Company grant, which they used for a power washer and a second truck. Their advice: "Start small, keep it simple, and don't try and do everything at once." **Lesson:** grow from one service to several for the same clients, and add winter work.

**Dynamic D Landscaping (Thorold, Ontario).** PelhamToday reported on July 22, 2026 that Dylan Wilkinson, 16, started with snow shovelling when he couldn't find a part-time job, then added mowing, spring cleaning and design work, and hired two classmates. Through Summer Company (up to $3,000, paid in two $1,500 instalments), he bought a new Toro mower for around $1,000. A separate $1,000 school-board pitch-contest prize paid for 3,000 door hangers, branded shirts and hats, and yard signs. **Lesson:** door hangers and yard signs at job sites are cheap local marketing, and funding programs can pay for your first real equipment.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

**Week 1: Plan the route**
- Day 1: Walk or drive your neighbourhood and count lawns that look overgrown or unkept. Note which streets are close together.
- Day 2: Check your city's noise bylaw, yard-waste rules and whether you need a business licence.
- Day 3: Check pesticide rules in your province before offering anything beyond cutting.
- Day 4: Gather or buy the minimum kit: mower, trimmer, blower, safety gear.
- Day 5: Set your prices by lot size, using the published prices above as a ceiling.
- Days 6–7: Make a simple flyer or door hanger with your name, services, price range and phone number.

**Week 2: Get the first clients**
- Hand out 100–200 flyers on the streets closest to home.
- Post in local community groups and on Facebook Marketplace services, following each group's rules.
- Claim a free Google Business Profile if you qualify; Google says you can [add or claim a Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778?hl=en) at no charge.
- Ask family, friends and neighbours for referrals. See [finding your first lawn care clients](/side-hustles/lawn-care-first-clients-canada/).
- Offer a first-cut quote on site, and leave a clean edge.

**Week 3: Lock the schedule**
- Assign each client a fixed day of the week.
- Set up Wave or Jobber for invoices and reminders.
- Track time and travel for every lawn. If you drive, the CRA's [motor vehicle records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/motor-vehicle-records.html) page says to log the date, destination, purpose and kilometres of each business trip.

**Week 4: Measure and expand**
- Calculate your real hourly rate per lawn, including travel.
- Ask happy clients for a review and a referral, and put up a yard sign (with permission) while you work.
- Flyer the streets around your current clients to tighten the route.

### Days 31–90
- **Weeks 5–6:** Add an edging or trimming upsell for every client.
- **Weeks 7–8:** Offer seasonal packages that include spring cleanup, weekly mowing and fall cleanup at one price.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Drop or reprice lawns far from your cluster.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Pre-sell fall leaf cleanup and winter [snow removal](/side-hustles/snow-removal-side-hustle-canada/) to your mowing clients.

## Which pesticide rules apply to lawn care in Canada?

Sell lawn care as cutting and trimming unless the provincial pesticide rule allows the product you intend to use. In Ontario, [Pesticides (home lawns and gardens)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/pesticides-home-lawns-and-gardens) says you cannot use certain pesticides, including products containing 2,4-D, diazinon or glyphosate, to kill weeds and insects on lawns, and cosmetic use is limited to active ingredients on the allowable list. In Quebec, the [Pesticides Management Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/P-9.3,%20r.%201) sets the provincial lawn pesticide rules, so read it before you apply anything.

A hardware-store weed killer doesn't automatically become a legal service in Ontario or Quebec. Other provinces and cities may have their own rules, and applying products for pay can require a licence. Fertilizer has its own label, and you should take extra care near water. If you're not sure a product is allowed, sell cutting and trimming only. That's a complete service.

## Which tax rules apply to lawn care in Canada?

Lawn care income is self-employment income. It's reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on their net income (QPP in Quebec). GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the first-dollar [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

- **Small-supplier test:** worldwide taxable supplies, including your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive quarters. Add snow removal and any other side income to the same total.
- **Rates:** once registered, charge the rate for where the service is performed. Ontario is 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% HST (since April 1, 2025, per the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table) and Alberta 5% GST. Confirm with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Equipment:** mowers, trimmers and trailers are capital property, claimed over time through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html), not deducted in full in year one.
- **Vehicle:** keep a kilometre log if you drive between lawns, so you can claim the business portion.
- **Grants:** Summer Company funding and similar grants can have tax consequences. Keep the paperwork and ask the program or an accountant how to report it.
- **Insurance:** general liability insurance protects you if a rock thrown by the mower breaks a window. Get a quote before taking on many clients.

## Common lawn care mistakes

- **Scattered routes.** Driving between distant lawns eats your profit.
- **Underpricing for the season.** Raising prices mid-season is harder than starting at a fair rate.
- **Skipping the walk-around.** Hidden rocks, toys and sprinkler heads damage equipment and property.
- **Spraying without checking the rules.** Ontario and Quebec restrict cosmetic pesticides.
- **Mowing at dawn.** Noise bylaws apply, and neighbours notice.
- **No winter plan.** Clients who use you for snow removal stay loyal for mowing too.

## What are the limits of lawn care as a side hustle?

Lawn care is seasonal and weather-dependent. Drought, rain and early frost all cut into the schedule, and equipment breakdowns stop the route until they're fixed. The published prices above come from established companies with insurance and marketing costs, and the student examples are documented cases, not typical results. Pesticide, noise and waste rules vary by province and city, so confirm them locally before you sell more than cutting and trimming.
