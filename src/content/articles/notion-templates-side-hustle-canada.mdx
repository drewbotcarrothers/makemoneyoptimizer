Selling Notion templates in Canada means turning a workflow you already use, such as a client tracker, study planner or content calendar, into a duplicate-ready Notion page that buyers pay to copy. Canadians can sell directly on Notion's Marketplace (8% + US$0.40 per sale) or through Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Payhip or Etsy, and you can build templates on Notion's free plan. B.C.-based creator Marie Poulin's course and templates bring in about $40,000 a month, but most sellers start with a single file and a few sales.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Selling fees (checked October 9, 2026):** Notion Marketplace takes 8% + US$0.40 per sale, plus 1% to convert payouts for creators outside the U.S. Gumroad takes 10% + US$0.50 on direct sales and 30% via its Discover marketplace. Lemon Squeezy takes 5% + 50¢. Payhip's free plan takes 5% plus Stripe or PayPal fees.
- **Canada is eligible** to sell directly on Notion Marketplace. Approval can take a few months, payouts need at least US$20, and buyers can request a refund within 14 days.
- **Build cost:** Notion's Free plan costs $0 and the Plus plan is US$10 a month per member, so a first template can cost nothing but your time.
- **Real numbers:** Thomas Frank (U.S.) reported about US$1 million in Notion template sales in 2022. Marie Poulin (B.C.) sells templates from $5 to $10 that lead buyers to a course with more than 2,100 students.
- **Tax:** report sales on T2125, pay CPP on the profit, and register for GST/HST once taxable sales pass $30,000. Merchant-of-record platforms collect sales tax at checkout, but you still report your income.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does selling Notion templates suit?

It suits people who already run a system in Notion, can explain it in plain language, and enjoy improving a product over time. Templates sell to people with the same job you have, so your day job or hobby is usually your best niche.

It suits:

- **Freelancers and consultants** with a client pipeline, invoicing tracker or project board they've used for months.
- **Students** with a study system that works; students and educators also get Notion's Plus plan free with a school email, according to [Notion's pricing page](https://www.notion.com/pricing).
- **Creators** with a content calendar or sponsorship tracker (see [YouTube](/side-hustles/youtube-side-hustle-canada/) for the audience side).
- **Small operators** (resellers, tutors, cleaners) with inventory, booking or client lists.
- **Designers** who can make a page look calm and clear; template design skills overlap with [graphic design](/side-hustles/graphic-design-side-hustle-canada/).

It doesn't suit people who've never used the workflow they want to sell, who dislike answering setup questions, or who need steady income right away. If you'd rather run the system for someone else, that's [virtual assistant work](/side-hustles/virtual-assistant-side-hustle-canada/), billed by the hour.

**Notion Marketplace details.** According to Notion's [selling page](https://www.notion.com/help/selling-on-marketplace) (checked October 9, 2026), you set up a creator profile, join a waitlist for payments, get approved and onboard with Stripe. Notion is the merchant of record and handles regional sales tax or VAT, but creators report their own income tax. Payouts go out every two weeks once your balance reaches US$20, funds are held 14 days, and buyers can request a refund within 14 days. A refund removes the template from the buyer's workspace. If you sell through Notion, the template's public link redirects to Marketplace checkout, so set up a separate copy for any third-party store.

A full storefront for several products is a [Shopify store](/side-hustles/shopify-store-side-hustle-canada/); Shopify's [Canada pricing](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing) page listed Basic at $49 CAD a month, or $37 CAD a month billed yearly, when checked on October 7, 2026. ## How to start: a 30-day plan

### Week 1: Pick the job and clean the template

- **Day 1:** Write one sentence: "This template helps [who] do [job] in [time]."
- **Day 2:** Duplicate your working system; delete all real client, student or personal data.
- **Day 3:** Cut it down to what a new person can set up in one sitting (most first templates need 2–4 databases, not 20).
- **Days 4–5:** Add obviously fake example rows, a "Start here" page and toggles explaining each view.
- **Days 6–7:** Ask two people who do the job to duplicate it and set it up without your help. Note every question.

### Week 2: Package it

- Write the setup guide (template below) and record a 2–3 minute video.
- Make 5–6 preview images: dashboard, each main view, mobile view.
- Write the sales page: who it's for, what's inside, what it doesn't do, refund rule.
- Decide on price and a free "lite" version for your email list.

### Week 3: Launch

- Publish the paid version on one checkout and submit to Notion Marketplace (approval may take time).
- Post the free version with an email opt-in.
- Share it where your buyers already gather: your social accounts, newsletters, communities whose rules allow it.
- Make one short video showing the template solving one problem.

### Week 4: Learn and improve

- Read every question and update the guide.
- Ask the first buyers for honest feedback (never write fake reviews).
- Track views, conversions and refunds.
- Plan the second template only if the first sells to people who aren't friends.

### Days 31–90

- Publish weekly content (a tip, a mini tutorial) that links to the free template.
- Add a bundle once you have two or three related templates; Thomas Frank reported his bundle outsold the separate editions 3 to 1.
- Update templates when Notion ships new features, and email buyers about updates.

## Common Notion template mistakes

- **Selling a system you don't use.** Buyers can tell; refunds follow.
- **Too many databases.** If setup takes more than one sitting, cut.
- **Leaving real data in.** Client names or emails in a template are a privacy problem.
- **No setup guide.** Every unanswered question becomes a support email or refund.
- **Copying another creator's template.** Build from your own workflow so you can explain and support it.
- **Fake reviews or buyer counts.** They mislead buyers; use only real feedback.
- **Not linking a separate copy for third-party stores.** Once you sell through Notion, the original link redirects to Marketplace checkout.
- **Ignoring the email list.** One platform change can cut discovery; your list stays yours.

## Which tax rules apply to Notion templates in Canada?

Template sales are self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on your net profit (QPP in Quebec).

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

- **GST/HST registration:** required once your worldwide taxable supplies, from all your businesses and associates, pass **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)). The [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule doesn't apply.
- **Platforms and digital tax:** Notion, Gumroad and Lemon Squeezy say they're merchants of record and collect sales tax at checkout. The CRA's [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) describes GST/HST rules, in effect since July 1, 2021, for platforms and non-resident suppliers, and the [cross-border digital products](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/cross-border.html) page explains who collects in different setups. Sales through your own Payhip or Shopify store work differently: once registered, you charge tax yourself.
- **Rates once registered:** based on the buyer's province: 13% HST in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia (since April 1, 2025), 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I., 5% GST elsewhere plus QST in Quebec. Check the [CRA rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **USD income:** most platforms pay in or convert from U.S. dollars; record income in Canadian dollars using a consistent rate such as the [Bank of Canada's daily rate](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/).
- **Deductions:** Notion Plus, checkout fees, design tools, a share of internet and home office, and a computer through capital cost allowance.

Keep a simple record per sale: date, platform, price, fee, refund, payout date. A Canadian account like <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> can keep template income separate from personal spending.

## Provincial notes

- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP; QST applies once registered. If you market templates to Quebec consumers, check French-language requirements with the [Office québécois de la langue française](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/) first.
- **HST provinces (Ontario and Atlantic Canada):** rates of 13%–15% apply once you're registered and selling through your own store.
- **Other provinces:** 5% GST once registered. B.C., Saskatchewan and Manitoba also run their own provincial sales taxes, so check the province's rules before selling directly from your own store.
