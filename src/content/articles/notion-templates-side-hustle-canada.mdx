## Who does selling Notion templates in Canada suit?

Selling Notion templates in Canada suits people who already run a repeatable system and can explain it without a call. Freelancers, students with a real study board, and small operators with a pipeline they have used for months are the natural makers. Selling templates is a poor fit if you have never used the workflow, if you dislike support email, or if you need rent money from the first upload.

Buyers are other people with the same job: a solo consultant, a creator, a student, a reseller tracking listings. Build for one of them. A "life operating system" with forty databases is hard to finish and harder to support. One job, done clearly, is the product.

Writing the instructions well matters as much as the database. If the words are the part you like, [freelance writing](/side-hustles/freelance-writing-side-hustle-canada) can sit beside the template. The template still has to work when the buyer duplicates it.

## How much can Notion templates earn in Canada?

Notion template earnings in Canada cannot be promised as a catalogue income, because one useful file and a library of unsold duplicates are different products. What you keep depends on the list below, and this guide does not publish an average template income.

- Whether a stranger can duplicate the file and finish the setup page
- The checkout fee on that checkout's own page
- Refunds when the instructions were thin
- Support time you did not price

## How do you start selling Notion templates in Canada?

Starting Notion template sales in Canada means using a workflow yourself, writing the setup steps, and selling one file before you design a brand. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Pick a job you have already done

A freelance pipeline, a student assignment tracker, a reseller inventory list, or a content calendar you have used. If you have to invent the columns, you are not ready to sell them. The buyer's job should be nameable in one sentence.

### Step 2: Duplicate a clean copy

Remove your clients, your notes, and any personal information. Leave example rows that are obviously fake, labelled as examples. A template that leaks a real customer's email is a privacy problem, not a bonus.

### Step 3: Write a one-page setup guide

How to duplicate, what to edit first, and what the template will not do. Three screenshots or a short recording. If the guide needs a call, the product is consulting, not a file.

### Step 4: Choose one checkout and read its fee page

Marketplace or your own link. Read the current fee, payout timing, and refund rule on that company's site. This guide does not freeze those numbers. If you use Shopify, use the [Canada pricing](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing) page, not a screenshot from a video.

### Step 5: Price one file, with support in the price

Pick a number you can say, for one file, with a defined support window, such as email questions for 14 days. Unlimited lifetime customization is a services business. Label it that way if that is what you sell.

### Step 6: Put the sales page in plain language

Who it is for, what is inside, what is not, and how to get a refund under the checkout's rule. Canadian buyers should see the price in CAD if you are charging Canadians. Do not invent reviews. A sentence from a friend is not a testimonial you can fabricate into a crowd.

### Step 7: Sell it to five people who do the job

People you know, or a relevant community whose rules allow a link. Ask what broke. Fix the guide. The first five sales are a test of the instructions, not a revenue target.

### Step 8: Record the payout

Date, price, fee, refund, and hours of support. That record is what you report. A platform dashboard is helpful. Your own line is what you will still have if the dashboard changes.

### Step 9: Decide on a second template only after the first one survives a month

Keep the file updated if the tool moves a button. Add a second product when the setup guide is no longer the reason people write to you. A catalogue of unfinished files is not a shop.

## What should the first week of selling Notion templates in Canada include?

The first week of selling Notion templates in Canada should produce one clean template, a setup page, a checkout you have read, and a handful of real users. A catalogue can wait.

- **Day 1:** Name the job. Duplicate a clean copy with fake example rows.
- **Day 2:** Write the setup page and record the duplicate step.
- **Day 3:** Read one checkout's fee and refund page. Price a single file.
- **Day 4:** Draft the sales page. Remove any review you did not receive.
- **Day 5:** Ask three people who do the job to try the duplicate and tell you what failed.
- **Day 6:** Fix the guide. Send the link to two more people, with the price.
- **Day 7:** Log any sale, fee, and question. If nobody could duplicate it, the file is not for sale yet.

Common Notion template mistakes in Canada are selling a system you do not use, hiding the setup work, and opening a store subscription before a single buyer exists. Use the file yourself for a week. If you abandon it, buyers will too.

Copying someone else's template and changing the cover is not a product. Build from a workflow you can explain. Fake scarcity and fake buyer counts are misleading. Say what is true: one file, a price, a guide.

## Which tax rules apply to Notion templates in Canada?

Notion template income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST for your own taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep payout reports and refund notes. Report the income on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Template sales are not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Confirm Quebec and every other province on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.

Digital products and platforms that sell to people in Canada add a layer the small-supplier test does not erase. The CRA's [digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) describes GST/HST rules, in effect since 1 July 2021, for certain non-resident suppliers and distribution platform operators that facilitate digital products and services to Canadians. The [cross-border digital products](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/cross-border.html) page explains how collection can fall on the supplier or on the platform, depending on who is registered and how the sale is made. A platform that collects tax at checkout is not a substitute for your own registration test, your records, or Quebec's QST. Read those pages before you assume the checkout finished the tax work.
