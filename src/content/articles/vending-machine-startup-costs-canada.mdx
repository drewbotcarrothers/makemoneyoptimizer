Starting a vending machine business in Canada costs whatever a written quote says for the machine, plus a card reader if you add one, the first inventory, delivery, and any permit or insurance the location requires. This guide does not publish a national price for a new or refurbished machine. Dealers change prices, and a "route for sale" often bundles locations you have not verified. Get two quotes. Then decide if the location is real.

## How much does a vending machine business cost to start in Canada?

It costs the quote for one machine you have seen work, the product to fill it, and the cost of getting it into a building that has agreed in writing. A lean start is one used machine at a site you already know, such as a workplace that invited you. A larger start is several machines, a reader on each, and a vehicle that can carry them. Neither has a standard Canadian sticker price.

| Piece | How to price it | What not to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Machine, new or refurbished | Two written quotes from sellers who will show the unit powered on | A single "package" price that hides the locations |
| Card reader and monthly service | The processor's current fee schedule | Assuming coins are enough |
| First inventory | The wholesale invoice for a full machine | Retail grocery prices as your cost forever |
| Delivery and install | The quote to move it through the door, including elevators | Assuming you can carry a drink machine alone |
| Location payment | The commission or flat fee in the signed agreement | A verbal "we'll take 10 percent" |
| Permits and insurance | [BizPaL](https://bizpal-perle.ca/) plus the location's certificate request | Copying another city's fee |
| Vehicle and a hand truck | What you already drive, plus a truck if cases are heavy | A trailer before the first machine earns |

## What is the difference between a new machine and a refurbished one?

A new machine has a warranty you can read and a higher quote. A refurbished machine can be the right first unit if you see it refrigerate or vend, and if the seller will say what was replaced. Age matters less than whether the compressor, the validator, and the card kit work. Ask who repairs it in your city. A cheap machine with no technician within a day's drive is an expensive ornament.

## What ongoing costs sit under vending sales?

**Product.** You replace what sold and you throw out what expired. Spoilage is a cost even when the machine looked full.

**Location commission or rent.** Whatever the agreement says. Renegotiate in writing. A percentage of sales and a flat monthly fee are both common structures. This page does not pick a "normal" percentage.

**Card fees.** The reader company takes a schedule you signed. Cash still needs counting and a bank deposit.

**Power.** Some locations ask you to pay a share of electricity. Put that in the agreement.

**Repairs and downtime.** A dark machine sells nothing and still occupies the spot. Budget a repair call as a real invoice, not as zero.

**Your kilometres.** The trip from home to the site is part of the margin. Log them if you will claim vehicle expenses later.

## How do you sketch break-even for one vending machine?

### Step 1: Write the cash you need before the first sale

Machine quote, reader, delivery, first fill, permit, and insurance deposit. Use invoices.

### Step 2: Write the monthly costs that exist even in a slow month

Any location rent that is flat, reader subscription, insurance monthly share, and the fuel for one visit.

### Step 3: Write product cost as a share of sales from your invoice

If a case costs you half of what the machine charges, say so. Do not use a margin you hope for.

### Step 4: Subtract commission

Apply the signed percentage to sales, not to profit.

### Illustrative example

Assumed numbers. Not a quote and not a survey.

Suppose the machine quote is $2,500, the reader is $300, and the first fill is $200. Cash to start is $3,000. Suppose a month of sales is $400, product cost is $200, and commission is 20 percent of sales, which is $80. Amount left before your time, fuel, and repairs is $400 − $200 − $80 = $120. At $120 a month, recovering the $3,000 takes $3,000 ÷ $120 = 25 months, and that ignores spoilage, a repair, and the hours you drove. If sales are $200, product is $100, and commission is $40, the amount left is $60, and the recovery stretches further. Change the quote and you change the months. If a seller will not let you plug your own sales guess into this sketch, walk away.

## What startup mistakes show up in vending budgets?

- **Paying for locations you have not visited.** The machine is the smaller risk. An empty hallway is the larger one. See [is vending worth it](/side-hustles/vending-machine-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Forgetting commission.** Gross sales are not your sales.
- **Buying a food machine before the health unit answers.** Ontario's [Food Premises Regulation](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/170493) is the provincial starting point. Your health unit applies it locally.
- **One machine, three cities.** Fuel will erase a thin margin.
- **Treating the purchase as a same-year tax write-off.** A machine that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html).

## Which tax rules apply to vending startup costs in Canada?

Sales are business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Inventory you still hold at year-end is not all an expense of the year you bought it. Follow the CRA inventory rules in the business guide rather than deducting every case on the shelf. Equipment is capital cost allowance on the business portion. Self-employed net income draws both shares of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). GST/HST registration follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) on taxable supplies. Toronto's business table mentions HST beside coin-operated machines, and the CRA page is the test that matters. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
