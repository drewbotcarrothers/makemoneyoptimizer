Dealers list these prices before tax. Apex Vending Canada's [sales policy](https://apexvending.ca/sales-policy/) says its prices exclude taxes, freight, liftgate service, installation and card-reader activation fees, and that sales are final with non-refundable deposits. Snackphoria lists its machines "+ HST". Vendtech's page does not say, so the table adds 13% to every line. With 5% GST only, the totals are $2,843.20, $6,224.21 and $11,263.06.

Lines left out on purpose:

- **Freight.** Apex says shipping is calculated by postal code and is not in the machine price. The APEX 3 weighs 490 lb and the BevMax 764 lb. Get the delivery quote, including whether the truck has a liftgate and whether anyone will take the machine through the door, before you pay a deposit.
- **Insurance.** $300 to $600 a year for liability on one to three machines is a planning range, covered below.
- **Location payment.** Commission or rent is in the agreement you sign, so it is a monthly cost, not a startup cost.
- **A business name.** Selling under your own legal name needs no registration in most provinces. A trade name in Ontario is $60 to register online, per [Ontario's fee page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit).

### New or refurbished?

A new machine buys a longer warranty and current payment hardware. Apex's new combo carries a 1-year manufacturer's parts warranty; its refurbished BevMax carries 90 days, and its warranty page excludes labour and technician time on both. A refurbished coin-only machine is the cheapest entry, but every sale needs exact change or a bill validator, and many people no longer carry coins. Budget the $550 reader for any refurbished machine you plan to keep. See the refurbished unit vend and cool before you pay, and ask who repairs that brand in your city.

## What does a card reader cost for a vending machine?

A vending card reader costs $550 to buy at Apex Vending Canada, plus $9.95 a month per device and 5.95% of each card sale. That applies to both the [Nayax VPOS Touch](https://apexvending.ca/product/nayax-vpos-touch-card-reader/) and the [Cantaloupe ePort G11](https://apexvending.ca/product/cantaloupe-eport-g11-4g-lte-credit-debit-card-reader/), which Apex lists at the same price and service fees, with a two-tier pricing option on inquiry. A bill validator and a coin mechanism are $399 each.

At a $2.50 vend, 5.95% is about 15¢ a sale. On $500 of card sales in a month, the processor keeps $29.75 plus the $9.95 service fee, $39.70 in all. A card reader usually earns its fee by catching sales from people with no coins, but put the percentage into your margin rather than treating cards as free.

## What does vending machine insurance cost in Canada?

Liability insurance for one to three vending machines is about $300 to $600 a year as a planning figure, and $500 to $1,200 with equipment cover for a small route, according to a [September 2026 guide](https://feelgoodsnacks.ca/blog/vending-machine-insurance-in-canada/) from Feel Good Snacks, a Canadian vending supplier. The guide calls these planning estimates, not quotes. It says no law requires the insurance, but most good locations ask for a certificate, often at a $2 million limit and naming them as an additional insured.

[Zensurance's vending page](https://www.zensurance.com/restaurant-insurance/vending-machine) does not publish a price. It lists the parts of a vending policy: general and product liability, equipment breakdown for compressor and electrical failure, property cover for stock and machines, and commercial auto. It also warns that a personal car policy does not cover a vehicle used for business, which matters if you restock several sites a week. Ask the location what limit it requires before you get a quote. More on matching cover to the work is in [side hustle insurance in Canada](/side-hustles/side-hustle-insurance-canada/).

## Do you need a licence or permit for a vending machine?

Most cities do not issue a vending-machine licence for packaged snacks and drinks, but zoning, business registration and HST still apply. Toronto's [business regulations table](https://www.toronto.ca/business-economy/new-businesses-startups/business-regulations/) lists four requirements for coin-operated vending machines: zoning, sign permits, business registration and HST. It shows no municipal operating licence for that row. Check your own municipality with [BizPaL](https://bizpal-perle.ca/), because rules differ city by city.

Food is different. Ontario's [Food Premises Regulation](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/170493) is the provincial starting point, and the local public health unit applies it. Ask the health unit before you buy a fresh-food or refrigerated meal machine.

## How many vends does it take to break even?

The amount to recover is the equipment with HST plus $450 of insurance plus a year of reader fees ($119.40 per reader). The first fill is not in it, because each item's cost is already subtracted when it sells. Standard: $6,338.16 + $450 + $119.40 = $6,907.56, and $6,907.56 ÷ $0.7163 = 9,643.3, so 9,644 vends. Lean: $2,699.55 + $450 = $3,149.55, and $3,149.55 ÷ $0.8651 = 3,640.9.

Two dealer claims are worth testing with the same numbers. Apex's home page says machines can make $300 to $1,500 or more a month and "pay for themselves within 6 to 18 months".

- **$300 of sales a month** is 120 vends. The standard machine keeps 120 × $0.7163 − $9.95 = $76.01, so payback takes about 91 months.
- **$1,500 a month** is 600 vends, which keeps $419.83 and pays back in about 16.5 months.
- **Six months** would need about 1,621 vends a month, roughly $4,053 in sales.

The low end of the dealer's range does not pay a new machine back in 18 months at these margins. The high end does. Your location decides which one you get.

If you register for GST/HST, the posted price includes the tax. A $2.50 vend is $2.21 after 13% HST, product cost drops to about $1.23 because you claim the purchase tax back as [input tax credits](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html), and the card machine keeps about $0.59 a vend. Raise prices, or pick lower-cost products, before you cross the $30,000 threshold.

## Where can vending startup spending be lower, and where should it not be cut?

Spending is lowest with one refurbished machine at a site that invited you, bought from a dealer who will show it running and service it locally. Fill only the best-selling columns at first rather than buying a case of everything. Put a reader on as soon as card sales justify the $9.95 a month.

Do not cut the delivery quote, the signed location agreement, or insurance the location asks for. Do not pay for a "route" or a list of locations you have not visited and confirmed in writing. Several dealers advertise locations for sale. An empty hallway is the most expensive mistake in vending, and the machine is still yours after the location falls through.

## Which tax rules apply to vending startup costs in Canada?

Vending profit is business income on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). A machine that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html), not a same-year deduction, and stock still in the machine or your garage at year-end goes through cost of goods sold. Self-employed net income draws both shares of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html).

GST/HST registration follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). Vending has its own rule for what is taxable. The CRA's [Memorandum 4.3](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/4-3/basic-groceries.html) says food and beverages sold through a vending machine are taxable, even items that are zero-rated in a grocery store, such as a single serving of white milk or an apple. The exception is an item sold for a single coin of 25 cents or less. [Info Sheet GI-022](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-022/application-gst-hst-sales-water.html) adds that all bottled water sold from a vending machine is taxable. Once you are registered, every vend carries HST. More on the threshold is in [GST/HST registration for side hustles](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/).
