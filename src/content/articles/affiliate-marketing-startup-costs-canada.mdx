Starting affiliate marketing in Canada costs very little up front. The programs are free, and the real costs are a place to publish, the products you buy so your reviews are real, and the photos that show you actually used them. US-dollar amounts use the [Bank of Canada's](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) October 8, 2026 rate of 1.4240.

## How much does affiliate marketing cost to start in Canada?

Affiliate marketing in Canada costs $0 on a channel you already have, $227.27 for four years of hosting, $462.28 with photo gear and Canva Pro, or $1,051.61 with lights and a year of a keyword tool. Totals add 13% Ontario HST to Hostinger, the Amazon.ca gear and Canva; Ahrefs is added in CAD without tax. Use your province's rate.

| Kit | What's in it | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $0 start | A blog, newsletter, or video channel you already run; free program sign-ups | $0 |
| Lean | Hostinger Premium, 48 months (CAN$201.12), free first-year domain | $227.27 with HST |
| Standard | Lean + EUCOS phone tripod ($29.99) + Lightdow 12" light box ($35.99) + Canva Pro ($142/year) | $462.28 with HST |
| Pro | Standard + NiceVeedi 2-pack LED lights ($82.99) + Ahrefs Starter for 12 months (US$348, about C$495.55) | $1,051.61 |

None of these include the products you review. Budget those separately, and only buy what you'd use anyway.

## Who should use an affiliate marketing startup budget in Canada?

An affiliate marketing budget fits people who already use the products they'll recommend, or can afford to buy and test them, and who can wait months for traffic. The $0 start fits someone with an existing audience. The standard kit fits product reviewers who need their own photos. The pro kit fits someone treating this as a site they'll run for years.

It's a poor fit if you need income soon, or if the plan is a page of buttons with no explanation. Your own site without commission links is [SEO blogging startup costs](/side-hustles/seo-blogging-startup-costs-canada/). ## How does Amazon.ca Associates pay a Canadian affiliate?

These rules are from Amazon.ca Associates Central, checked October 9, 2026.

| Rule | What Amazon.ca says |
| --- | --- |
| Commission rates | Fixed by category, 0% to 10%. Examples: Luxury Beauty 10%; Pet Food & Supplies 9%; Furniture, Home, Clothing 6%; Camera, Cell Phones, Office 5%; Books, Kitchen & Dining, Sports & Fitness 4%; Health & Household, Toys 3%; Computers 1%; Gift Cards, Grocery 0%. |
| Approval | After you apply, you have 180 days to refer three qualifying sales. Amazon then reviews the application, usually within a day or two. Personal orders don't qualify. |
| If you miss it | The application can be withdrawn after 180 days without enough qualifying sales, and a rejected Associates ID can't be reinstated. You can reapply later. |
| When you're paid | Monthly, about 60 days after the end of each month, by direct deposit, gift certificate or cheque. |
| Minimum payout | One help page says amounts under C$10 (direct deposit or gift certificate) or C$100 (cheque) roll into the next month. Other Associates Central pages list higher minimums, so check the figure in your own account. |
| Dormancy | With no earnings in three years, Amazon may charge an account maintenance fee of the lesser of C$10 or your unpaid balance, and close the account. |

Sources: [commission rates](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ), [account dormancy](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/node/topic/G7MJTPEP9NC3YKMG), [minimum payment threshold](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/node/topic/GJCKWFEECYW25FGJ), [payment methods](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/node/topic/GKDG94FQSRXSJCGK).

**Correcting a common claim.** Many guides say Amazon pays "4% to 10%" across the board. Amazon.ca's own table runs from 0% (gift cards, grocery) to 10% (luxury beauty), with 1% on computers and tablets. A laptop roundup earns a quarter of what a kitchen roundup earns on the same order value.

Retail networks and software programs set their own rates, cookie windows and payout thresholds per advertiser. The [affiliate commission rates and earnings guide](/side-hustles/affiliate-marketing-pricing-canada/) compares them.

## How many orders does it take to break even?

**Illustrative. Assumes every referred order is C$100 of qualifying items and the commission is the fixed Amazon.ca rate for that category. Order values, conversion and returns vary, and returned items don't earn. Each figure is the kit cost divided by the commission per order, rounded up. Verify the arithmetic.**

| Kit | Cost to cover | C$100 orders at 4% (kitchen, books) | at 5% (camera, office) | at 6% (home, furniture) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0 start | $0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Lean | $227.27 | 57 | 46 | 38 |
| Standard | $462.28 | 116 | 93 | 78 |
| Pro | $1,051.61 | 263 | 211 | 176 |

For example, $462.28 ÷ $4 = 115.6, so 116 orders. The lean kit's hosting covers four years, so its year-one share is about $56.82 with HST, or 15 orders at 4%.

### A first-year sketch

**Illustrative example. A teaching sketch, not a forecast.** Suppose you buy the standard kit ($462.28) and an assumed $120 of products to test and keep: $582.28 in year one. ## What does the Competition Bureau require for affiliate links?

Disclosure is free, and skipping it is the expensive mistake. The Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) lists commissions as a material connection, says disclosures should be clear and visible, and says linking to an affiliate page is unlikely to be enough on its own. It also says reviews should reflect actual experience with the product, which is the reason to budget for products you buy and test. This is not legal advice.

No affiliate-specific licence applies in Canada. If you trade under a business name that isn't your own legal name, most provinces require you to register it; in Ontario that's $60 online, per the province's [fee page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit).

## Where can affiliate marketing startup spending be lower in Canada?

- **Start on a channel you already have** and write about products you already own.
- **Use your phone, a window and Canva Free** before you buy lights or a light box.
- **Use Ahrefs Free and Search Console** instead of a paid keyword tool.
- **Skip paid traffic to affiliate links,** bought reviews, and courses that promise a monthly number. Some programs restrict paid ads; read the agreement first.

## Where shouldn't you cut affiliate marketing spending?

Don't cut honest testing, a clear disclosure, or backups. A review written from a product page is easy to spot and doesn't earn trust. A missing disclosure risks a Competition Act problem and program removal. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe thin affiliate pages as a tactic that can lower or remove a site. A site you can't restore loses every page.

Keep commission payouts apart from household money. <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian account that can hold payouts until a tax set-aside is moved.

## Which tax rules apply to an affiliate marketing budget in Canada?

Affiliate commission income in Canada is self-employment income when you're carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Self-employed people pay both portions of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net income. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule.

The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same total. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). Commissions from foreign programs can raise their own questions, so ask a tax adviser.

Hosting, tools and products bought for review are generally business expenses to the extent they're used for the business; a product you keep for personal use is a judgment call to discuss with an adviser. Equipment that lasts for years, like a camera, may be [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion. If you're registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.
