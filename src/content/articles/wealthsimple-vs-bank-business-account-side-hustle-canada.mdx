A Canadian side hustle needs a separate account, not necessarily a business account. Most side hustlers are sole proprietors, and a sole proprietorship, as Wealthsimple's help centre puts it, is a business with no separate legal identity from you. The real choice is between a no-fee account at Wealthsimple, either a second personal chequing account or the new sole proprietor business chequing account if it is open to you, and a small business account at a big bank that charges $5 to $6 a month and adds branches, cash deposits and a business name on the account. Every fee below is from the provider's own page or fee schedule, checked October 9, 2026, in Canadian dollars.

## What does each account cost over a year?

Monthly fees are the easy part: $0 at Wealthsimple, $60 at BMO ($40 in the first year with four free months), and $72 at TD and RBC. What separates the banks is how you get paid.

| Activity | Wealthsimple | TD Business Digital | RBC Digital Choice | BMO Essential |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly fee | $0 | $6 | $6 | $5 |
| e-Transfers included | No fee charged | 10 | 10 outgoing | 6 |
| Each extra e-Transfer | $0 | $1.50 | $1.50 | $1.50 |
| Cash deposits | Canada Post locations; $9,000 per 24 hours | $5 per $1,000 | $5 per $1,000 branch, $2.25 per $1,000 ATM | $2.50 per $1,000 |
| Cheque deposits | Photo in the app, about 6 business days | Unlimited | Unlimited | $0.25 per item |
| Teller help | None | $2.50 per transaction | $2.50 per paper transaction | $5 per transaction |
| Interest | 1.25% to 2.25% | Not listed | Not listed | Not listed |

### Illustrative example 1: a freelancer paid by e-Transfer and direct deposit

**Assumed inputs:** clients send payment (you do not request it), you send 4 e-Transfers a month to pay a subcontractor or supplier, no cash, and an average balance of $3,000 kept for tax and expenses. **Sourced inputs:** the fees and rates above.

- **Wealthsimple:** fees $0. Interest at 1.25%: 0.0125 × $3,000 = **$37.50** a year, which is taxable.
- **TD or RBC:** 12 × $6 = **$72** a year. 4 sends a month are inside the 10 included.
- **BMO:** 12 × $5 = **$60** a year, or 8 × $5 = **$40** in the first year with the four-month offer. 4 sends are inside the 6 included.
- **Gap:** Wealthsimple is $72 + $37.50 = **$109.50** a year ahead of TD or RBC before tax, and $60 + $37.50 = $97.50 ahead of BMO after the first year.

### Illustrative example 2: a local service paid partly in cash

**Assumed inputs:** $800 of cash deposited a month, 4 outgoing e-Transfers, no Request Money. **Sourced inputs:** the cash deposit fees above.

- **TD:** $6 + (0.8 × $5) = $6 + $4.00 = $10.00 a month, **$120** a year.
- **RBC at a branch:** $6 + (0.8 × $5) = $10.00 a month, **$120** a year. **At an RBC ATM:** $6 + (0.8 × $2.25) = $6 + $1.80 = $7.80 a month, **$93.60** a year.
- **BMO:** $5 + (0.8 × $2.50) = $5 + $2.00 = $7.00 a month, **$84** a year, before any $0.25 deposited-item charges.
- **Wealthsimple:** no monthly fee; the cash goes in at a Canada Post counter, up to $9,000 in a rolling 24 hours across all your Wealthsimple accounts.

### Illustrative example 3: invoicing with Request Money at TD

**Assumed inputs:** you invoice 15 clients a month with Interac Request Money and all accept, plus 4 outgoing sends. **Sourced input:** TD's fee schedule charges $1.50 for each send and each accepted request after the 10 included, charged to the account that receives the money.

- e-Transfers counted: 15 + 4 = 19. Over the limit: 19 − 10 = 9.
- Extra: 9 × $1.50 = $13.50 a month, **$162** a year, on top of the $72 monthly fees, for **$234** a year.

Asking clients to send the e-Transfer to your email, instead of sending them a request, keeps those 15 payments out of the count at TD. RBC lists Request Money and Autodeposit as services outside its 10 included transfers, with fees shown in online banking rather than on the account page, so check them before you choose RBC for invoicing.

## Which account fits a Canadian side hustle?

- **Wealthsimple personal chequing** fits most unincorporated side hustlers starting out: freelancers, tutors, online sellers and gig workers paid by direct deposit or e-Transfer. Open a second account, name it for the business, and keep a third for tax. It costs nothing, pays interest on the tax money, and the statements satisfy the CRA's records rule.
- **Wealthsimple sole proprietor business chequing** fits the same people once it is offered to them, especially a registered sole proprietor with a business number who wants cheques in the operating name and to pay GST/HST from the account. Check the app; the help article says it is not available to all clients yet.
- **TD Business Digital or RBC Digital Choice** fits a registered business that takes cheques, wants branch access, needs the business name on the account for clients or a payment processor, or plans to borrow from the bank later. Between those two, RBC's $2.25 per $1,000 ATM cash rate suits a cash-heavy local service better.
- **BMO Essential** fits a business that is almost entirely electronic and sends few e-Transfers, and is cheapest of the three banks over a first year with the four free months. It is the wrong account for anyone who visits a teller or deposits many cheques.
- **A corporation** banks in the corporation's name. That is when the main Wealthsimple business chequing account, or any bank's business account, becomes the only proper choice.

If you register a business name, the steps and documents banks ask for are in [how to open a business bank account](/side-hustles/business-bank-account-side-hustle-canada/), and registration fees by province are in [how to register a sole proprietorship](/side-hustles/register-sole-proprietorship-canada/). Other no-fee options, including KOHO and EQ Bank, are compared in [KOHO vs Wealthsimple vs EQ Bank](/side-hustles/koho-vs-wealthsimple-vs-eq-bank-side-hustle-canada/).

## Is side hustle money in Wealthsimple protected like a bank deposit?

Yes, through partner banks. Wealthsimple says it is not a bank or a CDIC member, but holds personal chequing balances in trust with CDIC-member institutions, extending coverage to a combined amount of up to $1 million. Its business chequing accounts, including the sole proprietor account, list $100,000 of CDIC coverage per business entity. At TD, RBC and BMO, eligible deposits are covered directly. [CDIC](https://www.cdic.ca/depositors/whats-covered/) insures each eligible category separately up to $100,000 per member institution, including principal and interest. A side hustle's tax set-aside rarely comes near either limit.

## What mistakes do side hustlers make with their business money?

- **One account for everything.** The CRA's [business records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html) guidance requires records of income and expenses, bank statements, deposit slips and receipts, generally kept for six years. Rebuilding a year from a mixed account takes hours. The simple setup is in [separating business and personal money](/side-hustles/separate-business-personal-money-canada/).
- **Paying for a business account you do not use.** $72 a year buys branch access and a business name on the statements. A side hustle paid by direct deposit uses neither.
- **Invoicing by Request Money on a capped account.** At TD, every accepted request counts toward the 10 included transfers.
- **Spending the tax set-aside.** Keep it in its own account; see [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Missing instalments.** If net tax owing is more than $3,000 ($1,800 in Quebec) in the current year and either of the two previous years, the CRA may ask for [quarterly instalments](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/payments/payments-cra/individual-payments/income-tax-instalments.html). Dates and options are in [CRA tax instalments for side hustlers](/side-hustles/cra-tax-instalments-side-hustle-canada/).

## What could change this comparison?

Fees and access change more often than the account names. TD's current fee schedule took effect March 16, 2026, and BMO's new plan fees on March 9, 2026. Wealthsimple's interest rates follow the Bank of Canada and its sole proprietor account is in beta, so availability and features can change month to month. ## Which tax rules apply to side hustle bank accounts in Canada?

Side hustle income is reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). Monthly fees and transaction charges on an account used for the business are generally deductible business expenses, so a $72 TD or RBC fee costs less than $72 after tax. Interest earned on the tax set-aside is investment income, reported separately from business income and taxable at your marginal rate.

Once worldwide taxable supplies pass [$30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), you register for GST/HST and collect it; keep that money in its own account too.
