## What should you look for in a shipping label printer in Canada?

Look for 4-inch print width, direct thermal printing, a connection that fits how you create labels (phone, laptop or both) and support for fanfold stacks. Speed and resolution matter less than whether the printer reliably feeds the labels you buy.

| Feature | Why it matters | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Print width | Shipping labels are 4 × 6 in. | A 4-inch printer. 2-inch models such as the Zebra ZD411 are for barcodes, not shipping labels |
| Print method | Direct thermal needs no ink or ribbon | "Direct thermal" in the listing |
| Connection | Where you buy postage | Bluetooth for phone apps; USB for a laptop; Wi-Fi/AirPrint for several devices |
| Label feed | Fanfold stacks are cheaper per label than rolls | An open back slot or an external feed so a stack sits behind the printer |
| Software | Label auto-sizing | The ability to crop a letter-size PDF to 4 × 6, or a 4 × 6 option at your carrier |
| Resolution | Barcode sharpness | 203 dpi is standard for shipping; 300 dpi helps for small product labels |

Direct thermal labels darken with heat and fade in strong sunlight over time, which doesn't matter for a parcel that's delivered in days but does for long-term inventory tags.

## Which label printers suit Canadian resellers?

Pick by how you buy postage. If you create labels on your phone at Canada Post or a marketplace app, Bluetooth is easiest; if you work at a laptop, a USB or Wi-Fi desktop printer is faster and sturdier.

## How fast does a label printer pay for itself?

A thermal printer pays back through minutes saved per parcel rather than ink saved, so the answer depends on volume. With the assumptions below, the MUNBYN 130B pays back after about 178 parcels, or about 9 weeks at 20 parcels a week.

**Illustrative example.** The label costs are the Amazon.ca prices above. The 1.5 minutes saved per parcel (no trimming paper, no taping over the label), the $20 an hour value of your time and the 10¢ of paper, ink and tape saved per paper label are assumptions, not measured averages. The 13% HST is Ontario's rate from the [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html).

Formula: **parcels to pay back = printer cost with tax ÷ (time saved × hourly value + paper costs saved − thermal label cost)**

Savings per parcel = (1.5 ÷ 60 × $20) + $0.10 − $0.04 = $0.50 + $0.10 − $0.04 = **$0.56**

| Printer | Cost with 13% HST | Parcels to pay back | Weeks at 20 parcels/week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MUNBYN 130B | $87.99 × 1.13 = $99.43 | $99.43 ÷ $0.56 = 177.6, so **178** | 9 |
| Rollo USB | $293.99 × 1.13 = $332.21 | **594** | 30 |
| Zebra ZD220 | $612.25 × 1.13 = $691.84 | **1,236** | 62 |

The bigger printers earn their price through speed and reliability on high-volume days, not through the label maths. Under about 20 parcels a week, the budget Bluetooth printers are the better buy.

## What should Canadian resellers check before buying?

Check that your carrier and marketplace produce a 4 × 6 label, and plan how you'll print the ones that don't.

- **Canada Post.** Canada Post sells blank 4 × 6 thermal labels to commercial customers ([catalogue](https://origin-www.canadapost.ca/cpc/doc/en/support/customer-guide/product-catalog-commercial.pdf)) and its [label specifications](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/doc/en/support/label-specifications.pdf) say to print at "Actual size", not "fit to page". Shrinking a barcode can make it unreadable.
- **Letter-size PDFs.** If a platform gives you a full page, crop the label area to 4 × 6 in your PDF viewer or the printer's software rather than scaling the page down.
- **Discount carriers.** [Chit Chats](https://chitchats.com/pricing) and [Stallion Express](https://stallion.ca/pricing/) publish their own rates; compare them in [Stallion Express vs Chit Chats vs Canada Post](/side-hustles/stallion-express-vs-chit-chats-vs-canada-post-canada/).
- **Cold garages.** Label adhesive grips poorly on cold cardboard. Keep the station and your box stock indoors in winter.

## How do taxes treat a label printer and supplies?

Supplies such as labels, mailers and tape are business expenses in the year you buy them ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses.html)). A printer or scale is equipment claimed through capital cost allowance; CRA's [classes of depreciable property](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance/classes-depreciable-property.html) page explains the classes, and notes that Class 12 (100%) excludes electronic data processing equipment, so a printer generally doesn't qualify for the small-tools class. Once your taxable sales pass $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters, you must register for GST/HST ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html)); see [GST/HST registration for side hustles](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/). This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
