With print-on-demand (POD), you upload a design and a printer makes and ships each item only after a customer orders it. In Canada, the main decision is where the item is printed. ## Who does print-on-demand in Canada suit?

Print-on-demand suits people who can make designs a specific group wants and who are willing to market them. The printer handles production and shipping. You handle everything else: design, listings, photos, customer service and promotion.

It suits:

- **Designers and illustrators** with a style or a niche following (see [side hustles for graphic designers](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-graphic-designers-canada/) and [graphic design](/side-hustles/graphic-design-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Creators with an audience**, such as a YouTube channel, a club or a community, who want merch without holding stock.
- **Niche experts:** hobbies, professions, hometown pride, sports leagues, pets or language jokes.
- **Etsy or Shopify sellers** adding a product line without inventory (see [Etsy shop](/side-hustles/etsy-shop-side-hustle-canada/) and [Shopify store](/side-hustles/shopify-store-side-hustle-canada/)).

It doesn't suit people hoping for passive income from generic designs. POD is often oversold as easy money; see [overhyped side hustles](/side-hustles/overhyped-side-hustles-canada/) for why. Margins are thin, and the products anyone can upload compete only on design and marketing.

- **Many products.** One blank in a few colours is enough to test a design.

## Which products should you start with?

T-shirts are the default, but they're also the most crowded. Match the product to the niche and the season:

| Product | Why it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| T-shirts | Cheapest test of a design; every niche wears them | Most competition; thin margin at low prices |
| Hoodies and crewnecks | Fall and winter demand in Canada; higher price points | Higher base cost and heavier shipping |
| Toques and embroidered hats | Very Canadian gift; embroidery reads as premium | Embroidery needs simple designs with few colours |
| Mugs | Gifts, office humour, profession pride | Breakage risk; check the printer's packaging |
| Stickers | Low price and impulse buys; good add-ons | Small order values; shipping can exceed the item price |
| Tote bags | Markets, book clubs, eco-minded buyers | Seasonal; plain designs blend in |
| Posters and wall art | Maps, illustrations, hometown art | Framing options change the price a lot |

Before you add a product type, order one sample, because fabric weight, print placement and colour can differ from the mockup. Compare base prices and shipping for each product on the printer's own catalogue and shipping pages before you list. A hoodie and a sticker carry very different shipping costs, and that changes the price you can charge.

## Worked math: one T-shirt sold on Etsy to a Canadian buyer

**Illustrative.** The $45 retail price is an assumption for the arithmetic. Every cost comes from the sources above (checked October 9, 2026). Assume the buyer pays $45 for the shirt plus $11.29 shipping, so the order total is $56.29.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Customer pays (shirt + shipping) | $56.29 |
| Printful T-shirt | −$16.58 |
| Printful shipping to Canada | −$11.29 |
| Etsy transaction fee (6.5% of $56.29) | −$3.66 |
| Etsy processing (3% of $56.29 + $0.25) | −$1.94 |
| Etsy listing fee (US$0.20, about C$0.28) | −$0.28 |
| **Left before tax, ads and returns** | **≈ $22.54** |

Etsy's regulatory operating fee, sales tax on the printer's invoice, currency conversion, Offsite Ads and returns are not included. Price the same shirt at $30 and the margin drops to about $9. That's why niche designs that justify a higher price matter more than volume.

**On Shopify:** with no Etsy fees, but card processing and a monthly plan, the margin per shirt is higher. The $37 monthly fee only pays off once you're selling a few shirts a month from your own traffic. See [print-on-demand pricing](/side-hustles/print-on-demand-pricing-canada/).

## 18 print-on-demand niche ideas for Canada

1. Hometown and neighbourhood pride (street names, area codes, local landmarks)
2. Provincial jokes and regional slang
3. French and bilingual wordplay
4. Hockey-league and beer-league team designs (with permission, and no NHL marks)
5. Cottage, cabin and lake-life designs
6. Winter humour on toques, hoodies and mugs
7. Profession pride: nurses, tradespeople, teachers, paramedics
8. Pet breeds with funny captions
9. Hiking, paddling and trail designs (without park logos)
10. Hand-drawn maps of your own (the iLikeMaps example below)
11. Book-club and reading designs
12. Running clubs and race-day shirts for local events, with the organiser's permission
13. Gaming and tabletop jokes that don't use any trademarked names
14. Gardening and homesteading
15. Family reunions and group trip shirts
16. Small business merch for local cafés and gyms
17. Language-learning jokes (ESL, French immersion)
18. Your own channel or podcast merch

Avoid team logos, band names, characters and brand names. Check the [Canadian Intellectual Property Office trademarks](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/trademarks) database before using a phrase. Marketplaces remove infringing listings and can close shops that repeatedly post them.

## Real print-on-demand examples

These are publicly documented, vendor-published case studies. Their results are not typical.

**iLikeMaps (Montreal).** In a 2018 Printful customer story, Olivier Gratton-Gagné said he launched his online map-art store in 2012 while working in marketing, got his first sale on Etsy that year, and later went full-time. Printful reported his revenue as about US$120,000 a year from all channels. He runs the store on Shopify, has more than 2,000 Etsy listings, and described the income after expenses as middle-class for Montreal. **Lesson:** one recognisable style (maps), many variations, and several sales channels.

**itsjusta6.** A 2022 Printful interview describes itsjusta6 as an automotive video brand started by friends. They built a merch store alongside the channel, and it averaged 6,000–7,000 orders a month. **Lesson:** POD works best when the audience comes first. The merch sold because the videos had already built demand.

## How to start print-on-demand: a 30-day plan

**Week 1: One design, one blank**
- Day 1: Pick one niche from the list and write down who wears it and where they hang out.
- Day 2: Check the CIPO database and marketplace searches for conflicts with your phrase or image.
- Day 3: Make three versions of one design in Canva or your own tool.
- Day 4: Open Printful (or Printify) and choose one blank, for example a Bella + Canvas 3001 in three colours.
- Day 5: Order a sample to your own address. Note the real shipping time.
- Days 6–7: Write your listing copy and plan your photos.

**Week 2: Open one storefront**
- Open an Etsy shop, or a Shopify trial if you already have an audience. Connect the printer.
- Photograph the real sample in natural light. Use mockups only alongside real photos.
- Write a listing that names the buyer, the occasion and the size guide.
- Set shipping and processing times that match what Printful's page and your sample showed.

**Week 3: Show it to real people**
- Share it with five people who fit the niche, and ask what they'd change.
- Post the design where your niche gathers: a club newsletter, local groups or your own social accounts.
- Add the same design to a second product type (a mug or a toque) only after you get feedback.

**Week 4: Measure**
- Check views, favourites and the conversion rate per listing.
- Fix titles and the first photo before adding new designs.
- Calculate real profit per order with the worked-math table above.

The [first sale guide](/side-hustles/print-on-demand-first-sale-canada/) goes deeper on the launch week.

### Days 31–90
- **Weeks 5–6:** Add two or three designs in the same niche. Consistency builds a recognisable shop.
- **Weeks 7–8:** Plan the seasons: Canada Day, back-to-school, Halloween and winter holidays. Holiday orders need lead time.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Try one paid promotion with a fixed budget, and stop if it doesn't pay back.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Drop designs with no views. Expand the ones that sell into new colours and products.

## A seasonal calendar for Canadian POD

POD is made to order, so seasonal designs need to be listed well before the date. Leave room for production plus shipping time, as shown on your printer's shipping page.

| Season | Design ideas | When to list |
|---|---|---|
| Winter holidays | Gifts, family shirts, winter humour, toques | Early fall, so marketplace search has time to pick them up |
| Valentine's Day | Couples, pets, "galentine" designs | Early January |
| St. Patrick's Day and spring | Local pub and running-club designs, gardening | Late winter |
| Canada Day | Red-and-white designs, hometown pride (no government logos) | Late spring |
| Summer | Cottage, camping, paddling and road-trip shirts | Spring |
| Back to school | Teacher shirts, school-club merch (with permission) | Midsummer |
| Halloween | Costumes, pet costumes, spooky puns | Late summer |

Keep evergreen niche designs live all year. Treat seasonal designs as a bonus on top.

## Photos and mockups that sell

Mockups are fast, but buyers trust real photos. A simple routine:

1. **Photograph the real sample** in daylight near a window, on a plain background and on a person if you can.
2. **Show the print up close** so buyers can see the texture and colour.
3. **Add a size-guide image** using the measurements from the printer's product page, not your own guesses.
4. **Use one lifestyle mockup** that shows the use case: a toque on a ski hill, a mug at a cottage dock.
5. **Keep colours honest.** If the mockup's colour looks different from the sample, use the sample photo.

The first photo does most of the work in marketplace search results. Test two versions of it, keep the one with more clicks, and change one thing at a time.

## Marketing that fits POD

- **Start where the niche already gathers:** club newsletters, local groups and hobby forums, where self-promotion rules allow it.
- **Partner with local organisers:** a running club, a community event or a hockey league may want a custom shirt. Agree on the design and price in writing.
- **Build an email list** from day one on Shopify, so repeat buyers hear about new designs.
- **Short videos** of unboxing a real sample or the design process can work on TikTok, Reels and Shorts (see the [YouTube guide](/side-hustles/youtube-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Don't buy ads until listings convert.** Paying to send traffic to a listing nobody buys only speeds up the loss.

## Customs and the made-in-Canada question

Printful describes its Ontario facility as offering "faster shipping across the country, no surprise customs or duty fees for your customers." Products printed outside Canada can face duty and taxes when they cross the border. Printful offers a Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) setting for some routes, which is covered in its help centre. If you sell items printed outside Canada to Canadian buyers, read the CBSA's [guide to importing commercial goods](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/import/guide-eng.html) and state clearly in the listing who pays any duty.

Be honest about origin. "Designed in Canada" isn't the same as "Made in Canada," and the Competition Bureau's guidance on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) applies to listings.

## Which tax rules apply to print-on-demand in Canada?

Print-on-demand income is self-employment income. It's reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on their net income (QPP in Quebec). GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the first-dollar [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

- **Small-supplier test:** worldwide taxable supplies, including your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive quarters. Count all your side-hustle revenue together.
- **Rates:** once registered, charge the rate for the buyer's province. Ontario is 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% HST (since April 1, 2025, per the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table) and Alberta 5% GST. Quebec adds QST. Confirm with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **Printer invoices:** Printful explains how it handles tax on Canadian orders in its [sales tax help article](https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017039060-How-does-sales-tax-work-for-my-Canadian-orders). Tax the printer charges you isn't tax you collected from your customer, so keep both records.
- **Marketplaces:** check each order's tax line and your marketplace's tax help pages to see what was collected on your behalf before you file.
- **Deductions:** printer costs, samples, shipping, platform fees, design software and a share of internet can be business expenses.

Check your city for a home-business licence.

## Common print-on-demand mistakes

- **Uploading a whole catalogue.** One good design on one blank beats 50 untested ones.
- **Using trademarks.** Team names, characters and slogans get listings removed and shops closed.
- **Pricing from the base cost alone.** Shipping and fees can cost almost as much as the shirt.
- **Mockups without a sample.** You can't describe fit, colour or print quality honestly without seeing it.
- **Promising fast delivery.** POD is made to order; state the production time.
- **Calling it "Made in Canada"** when the item is printed elsewhere.

## What are the limits of print-on-demand?

Margins are thin. In the illustrative example above, the printer's base price and shipping take about half of a $56 order before fees. Marketplaces are crowded, and generic designs rarely sell without promotion. Quality and shipping times depend on the printer and provider, and you take the customer service hit when something goes wrong. The iLikeMaps and itsjusta6 results come from vendor-published case studies and aren't typical. Prices and fees change, so recheck the printer's product and shipping pages, plus your marketplace's fee page, before setting prices.
