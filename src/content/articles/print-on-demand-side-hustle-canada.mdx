Print-on-demand in Canada is selling a design you made, printed onto a blank such as a shirt or a poster only after a buyer orders, with a printer that ships the finished item. It suits people who can make one original design and read a shipping page before they promise a delivery date. It is a poor fit if the artwork is a team logo, a character, or another maker's file, or if you need the money before the first order.

Printful's [Canada page](https://www.printful.com/print-on-demand-canada) says it has a fulfillment centre in Ontario and that orders fulfilled and shipped within Canada from that facility do not incur customs or duty fees for the customer. That is Printful's description of its own network. It is not a rule that every print-on-demand order in Canada is duty-free. If a product ships into Canada from a facility outside the country, duties can be a real charge. Confirm the live page for the product you actually enable.

## Who does print-on-demand in Canada suit?

Print-on-demand in Canada suits people who can draw, letter, or photograph something original and who will order one sample before they advertise a fit. Evenings are enough for a single design. The work is a poor fit if you want a catalogue of two hundred listings in week one, if you will not handle a return, or if the design only works because it uses someone else's trademark.

You are selling a picture on a blank. The printer sells you the production. You still own the promise on the product page: what the item is, where it ships from, and how long you have actually seen it take.

## How much can print-on-demand earn in Canada?

Print-on-demand earnings in Canada cannot be promised as a margin per shirt, because the blank price, the shipping, and the duty path change by product and by facility. What you keep depends on the list below. This guide does not publish a national profit rate.

- The price you set, minus the printer's charge, minus shipping you promised to cover
- Duty, if the parcel crosses the border and nobody prepaid it
- Returns and reprints when the print is wrong
- Store plan and app fees
- Hours to make the design and to answer the buyer
- A sample you paid for and did not sell
- Other side-hustle revenue in the same GST/HST pot

A mockup is not a sold shirt. Count an order only when a buyer paid and the printer accepted the job.

## How do you start print-on-demand in Canada?

Starting print-on-demand in Canada means designing one original image, ordering a sample, and listing it only after you know where it ships from. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Make one design you own

Words you wrote, a photo you took, a shape you drew. Do not trace a logo, a character, or a city's official mark. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) is the standard if you later claim a shirt is "official" or "local" when it is not.

### Step 2: Pick one blank

A shirt or a poster. Read the size chart on the printer's page. Write the fabric in the listing from that page, not from memory.

### Step 3: Read where that blank is fulfilled

Use the printer's Canada or shipping page. Printful's Canada page describes Ontario fulfillment and no customs on orders it ships inside Canada. Its [DDP article](https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/15941136562332-What-is-the-Delivered-Duty-Paid-DDP-shipping-option) says a Delivered Duty Paid option can include duties and taxes in the shipping price for international orders to Canada and the UK when live rates are on, and that the address needs a phone number. If your product is not on that path, do not promise "no surprise fees."

### Step 4: Order the sample to yourself

Wear it or hold it. Photograph the real print. A mockup can stay as a secondary image. The first image should be the object if you have it.

### Step 5: Open one checkout

A store such as Shopify, or a marketplace that allows the product. Confirm the plan price. Connect one product. Place a test order if the platform allows it.

### Step 6: Write the listing in plain language

What it is, the ship-from region you confirmed, the window you will not beat, and how a buyer asks for a reprint. Do not write "ships tomorrow" because a video said so.

### Step 7: Tell the truth about tax

Printful's [sales-tax article](https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017039060-How-does-sales-tax-work-for-my-Canadian-orders) says orders fulfilled and shipped within Canada are subject to Canadian sales tax based on the destination province, and that you are responsible for deciding whether to register for GST/HST. The CRA's rate table is the authority for the rate, not a printer's summary. Read both.

### Step 8: Show the listing to five people who would wear it

Not a drop of ads. Ask whether the price still makes sense after you say the shipping line out loud.

### Step 9: After the first real order, fix the listing before you add a second design

If the shirt ran small, say so. If the print was darker than the mockup, change the photo. A second design does not repair a wrong first listing.

## What should the first week of print-on-demand in Canada include?

The first week should produce one design, one sample order, and a listing you understand. A stranger's purchase is a bonus.

- **Day 1:** Draw or letter the design. Remove anything you do not own.
- **Day 2:** Pick one blank. Read the fulfillment location.
- **Day 3:** Read the DDP page if the blank can ship from outside Canada. Write one sentence you could put on the listing.
- **Day 4:** Order the sample. Save the receipt.
- **Day 5:** Open the store. Do not import the whole catalogue.
- **Day 6:** Draft the listing. Leave it unpublished until the sample is in your hands, if the sample is still in transit.
- **Day 7:** When the sample arrives, photograph it. Publish only if you would wear it or gift it. If you would not, fix the design. Do not advertise a shirt you disliked.

## What print-on-demand mistakes are common in Canada?

Common mistakes in Canada are listing a mockup as if it were a photo of the real print, promising delivery from a facility you did not check, and using artwork that is not yours. Printful's own pages distinguish Ontario fulfillment from international orders. A customer who pays duty you never mentioned will ask for the order to be cancelled. You still may owe the printer.

Another mistake is importing a stack of blanks "to save money" and calling it print-on-demand. Goods you import to sell are commercial imports. The CBSA [import guide](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/import/guide-eng.html) is the starting point. A personal exemption is not a business plan.

Do not claim the shirt is made in a city unless the fulfillment page says that. "Designed in Canada" and "printed in Canada" are different sentences. Use the one that is true.

## Which tax rules apply to print-on-demand in Canada?

Print-on-demand income in Canada is self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice. Recheck printer tax pages and CRA rates together.

Keep order exports and the printer's invoices. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Print-on-demand is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside. A printer collecting tax on its invoice to you is not the same as you collecting tax from your customer. Read both documents.

Search your city for a business licence and use that city's fee if there is one. This guide does not state a licence fee from another city.
