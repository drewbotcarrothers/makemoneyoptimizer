Graphic design freelancing in Canada means making defined pieces (a logo, a flyer, a menu, a slide deck, a set of social posts) for clients who pay per project or per month. Employed graphic designers earn a national median of $31.25 an hour, according to Job Bank, so that is the floor a freelance rate has to beat once you add your own tax, software and unpaid admin time. The software can cost nothing: Affinity is now free, and Canva and Figma both have free plans. The designers who build real income tend to narrow to one kind of piece for one kind of client.

- **Tools (checked October 9, 2026):** Affinity is free for individuals with a Canva account; Canva's Canadian pricing page showed Canva Pro at $142 a year; Figma has a free Starter plan and a Professional full seat from US$16 a month.

- **Real example:** Toronto designer Linda Tran put $500 into a Google ad in 2022, niched into PowerPoint decks and earned $87,000 in her first year, according to DollarSprout.

## Who does graphic design freelancing suit?

It suits people who can take a messy brief and return a file a client can use, on a date, within an agreed number of revisions. You don't need a design degree to start selling small pieces, but you do need a portfolio that shows you can finish.

It suits:

- **Working designers** who want extra income evenings and weekends, or who want to test going solo before leaving a job. Linda Tran, below, had done design work on the side for years before turning it into a business.
- **Marketing and admin people** who already make the flyers and slides at work and can package that skill for small businesses.
- **Illustrators** who can sell custom work and licence it, alongside products such as [printables](/side-hustles/printables-side-hustle-canada/) or [print-on-demand](/side-hustles/print-on-demand-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Quiet workers.** Design runs on written briefs and email, which is why it's on the list of [side hustles for introverts](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-introverts-canada/).

It doesn't suit people who take feedback personally, who can't stop polishing, or who need guaranteed income next month. Most first clients come slowly, and the admin (quotes, contracts, invoices, chasing payment) is a real share of the work.

## How much do graphic designers earn in Canada?

Employee wages give a benchmark. Job Bank's wage report for graphic designers and illustrators (NOC 52120), based on Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey for 2023–2024, shows these hourly figures:

| Area | Low | Median | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canada | $20.00 | $31.25 | $52.88 |
| Ontario | $21.00 | $32.69 | $52.88 |
| Toronto region | $21.00 | $33.65 | $53.37 |
| British Columbia | $24.04 | $35.00 | $57.69 |
| Quebec | $20.00 | $30.00 | $51.28 |
| Alberta | $18.00 | $26.30 | $45.19 |

Source: [Job Bank wage report](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/5703), checked October 9, 2026.

These are employee wages. An employee doesn't pay for software, a computer, the employer's half of CPP, or the hours spent on quotes and unpaid pitches. A freelancer who charges the median hourly wage is earning less than an employee. A common way to set a floor is to start from the employee median and add a margin for those costs, then price by project so you aren't penalized for working fast. Our [graphic design pricing guide](/side-hustles/graphic-design-pricing-canada/) builds a full price sheet.

What moves your earnings most:

- **Niche.** A narrow offer ("pitch decks for startups") is easier to price high than "graphic design."
- **Repeat work.** Slide decks, social posts and menus come back monthly or seasonally; logos usually don't.
- **Scope control.** Revision rounds and file formats written down before you start.
- **Channel.** Marketplace fees take 20% on Fiverr and up to 15% on Upwork; direct clients cost only your time to find.

## What design services can you sell?

Start with one of these and add others once a client asks for them. Each is a defined deliverable with a clear "done."

1. **One-page flyers** for cleaners, tutors, trades and events.
2. **Restaurant and café menus**, printed and digital, updated each season.
3. **Social media post sets**, for example 9 or 12 branded templates the client can reuse.
4. **Pitch decks and sales presentations**, in PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides.
5. **Simple logos and wordmarks** for new small businesses.
6. **Brand starter kits**: logo, colours, fonts and a one-page usage sheet.
7. **Business cards and stationery**, printed through a service such as <AffiliateLink program="vistaprint" label="Vistaprint" />.
8. **Real estate listing graphics** and feature sheets for agents.
9. **Event materials**: posters, programmes, signage and name tags.
10. **Infographics and annual reports** for non-profits and associations.
11. **Book covers** for self-published authors; see [ebook publishing](/side-hustles/ebook-publishing-side-hustle-canada/).
12. **YouTube thumbnails and channel art**, often paired with [video editing](/side-hustles/video-editing-side-hustle-canada/).
13. **Email newsletter templates** for businesses using Kit, Beehiiv or Mailchimp.
14. **Packaging and labels** for small food, candle and cosmetics makers.
15. **Etsy shop banners and listing images** for makers.
16. **Website graphics** for people building on Wix or Squarespace; the full site is [no-code web design](/side-hustles/no-code-web-design-side-hustle-canada/).
17. **Trade-show banners and pull-up signs** for local companies.
18. **Wedding and party stationery** such as invitations and seating charts.
19. **Custom illustrations** for blogs, children's books or merchandise.
20. **Monthly design retainers**: a fixed number of pieces each month for one business.

Ideas 4, 13 and 20 are the strongest for repeat income because the client needs them again and again. Ideas 5 and 6 are what most beginners try first, and they're the most crowded.

## What does a Canva design licence let you hand to a client?

A design agreement in Canada should name the piece, the size, the revision rounds, the date, the price, and who may use the file. Canva's [Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/) says you may transfer a Canva design that contains Free or Pro content to a client for that client's own use if you have a written agreement that the client's use stays within those terms, you remain responsible for the client's compliance, and the design goes to a single client. The same section says this does not let you hand the client Canva content on its own, outside a design.

The agreement also says you may not copy, download, or distribute Pro content as a standalone item, and it restricts Branded Content, Education Content, and Editorial Use Only content. Disney content on Canva has its own personal-and-educational limits. Read the current agreement before you export, because it can change. The Canadian Intellectual Property Office explains [copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright) in plain language. Put ownership and sample rights in your client agreement so you aren't guessing after the flyer is in the window. This is not legal advice.

| | One flyer for one client | A template pack you sell to many people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canva path | Section 4A of the content licence, with a written client agreement | A different, tighter limit. Don't assume client-transfer rights cover a pack |
| Week one | A reasonable first offer | Wait until you have read the licence and made original art |
| Copyright | The agreement says who may publish it | You need rights in every element |

Logos are the classic trap: a logo built from Canva stock elements can't be trademarked or owned exclusively by your client, because the element isn't yours to assign. Draw logos from scratch (Affinity, Figma or Illustrator) and keep stock elements for flyers and posts. Template packs sold to many buyers are their own business with stricter rules; see [Notion templates](/side-hustles/notion-templates-side-hustle-canada/) and [printables](/side-hustles/printables-side-hustle-canada/).

## Real Canadian designers who built a design business

These are reported cases, chosen because their results are public. They aren't typical, and they aren't promises.

**Linda Tran, Toronto (presentation design).** DollarSprout reported in 2026 that Tran, a corporate graphic designer who had asked repeatedly for a raise, started freelancing from her Toronto apartment in 2022 after putting $500 into a Google ad for pitch-deck and presentation help. A client reached out within a week, paid for the ad and kept hiring her after changing companies. She narrowed to PowerPoint presentations, earned $87,000 in her first year and passed six figures the next, raising her prices 40% by year two. Most new clients find her through LinkedIn, and every client signs a lawyer-drafted service agreement and pays a deposit before work starts. She has since added digital products such as Squarespace templates and pitch-deck resources. Her site is [lindatran.com](https://www.lindatran.com/).

**Andrea Carter, B.C.-raised generalist (Andrea Carter Design).** HostPapa profiled Carter for National Small Business Week in 2026. She grew up in B.C.'s interior, moved to Edmonton, and now runs a one-person design business from Santiago, Chile, serving a largely international client list. Her work covers book covers, infographics, annual reports, illustration, identity design and long-form documents, mostly in Illustrator, Photoshop and InDesign, adapting to Figma, Canva or Adobe Express when clients need it. She doesn't rely on advertising or social media: her site works as a portfolio for credibility, and some clients have been with her for about 20 years.

What the two have in common: a clear reason clients come back (decks that clients need every week; reliable, long-term service) and a professional site that works as proof, not as the main sales channel.

## What does the money look like on a few projects?

**Worked example 1, a Fiverr gig (illustrative).** Assume you sell a US$200 social media template pack on Fiverr.

- Fiverr keeps 20%: US$200 × 0.20 = US$40, so you earn US$160.
- At the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4240 CAD per USD (October 8, 2026): US$160 × 1.4240 = C$227.84, before any withdrawal fee.
- If the job takes 5 hours: C$227.84 ÷ 5 = about C$45.57 an hour, before tax.

The order price and hours are assumptions for the arithmetic, not market rates.

**Worked example 2, a direct local client (illustrative).** Assume a café pays $450 for a menu redesign plus a matching set of 6 social posts, and the work takes 7 hours including one revision round and the invoice.

- $450 ÷ 7 = about $64.29 an hour, before tax.
- That's about twice the $31.25 national median employee wage, which is the gap that pays for software, CPP and unpaid pitching time.

**Worked example 3, a small retainer business (illustrative).** Assume three clients on a $400-a-month retainer, each for a fixed set of pieces.

- Revenue: 3 × $400 = $1,200 a month, or $14,400 a year.
- Canva Pro at $142 a year and a $150 drawing tablet (assumed price) bring first-year tool costs to $292.
- Profit before tax: $14,400 − $292 = $14,108.
- Revenue is under the $30,000 GST/HST small-supplier threshold, so registration is optional unless your other side hustles push you over.

All three are illustrative; the prices, hours and client counts are assumptions, not forecasts.

## How do you start? An 8-week plan

**Week 1: Pick the offer.** Write one sentence: "I make [piece] for [type of business]." List 25 businesses or people in that group. If you can't name 25, choose a broader group or a different piece.

**Week 2: Set up tools and read the rules.** Install Affinity or open free Canva and Figma accounts. Read Canva's Content License Agreement if you'll use Canva content. Write a one-page agreement (see the checklist below).

**Week 3: Make three samples.** Make three versions of your one piece for fictional businesses (or real ones you have permission to feature). Don't put a real company's name, reviews or prices on a sample they didn't approve.

**Week 4: Build a simple portfolio.** One page with the three samples, the offer sentence, a starting price and a contact form. A free Behance profile, a Canva-built site or a small site on <AffiliateLink program="hostinger" label="Hostinger" /> all work.

**Week 5: Outreach, round one.** Send 10 short personal notes to businesses on your list, each pointing at one visible gap. Post the offer on LinkedIn. If you'll use a marketplace, publish one gig on Fiverr with the same three samples.

**Week 6: Outreach, round two, and follow-ups.** Send 10 more notes and follow up once on week 5's. Ask friends and former colleagues for one introduction each.

**Week 7: Deliver the first paid job.** Send the agreement and take a deposit before you start. Deliver on the date, in the file types the client can open, and send the invoice the same day.

**Week 8: Review and ask for the next piece.** Log hours per job and calculate your real hourly rate. Ask the client for a testimonial and for the next piece. Raise your price for the next client if your rate is below your target.

For a detailed first-client playbook, see [how to get your first graphic design client in Canada](/side-hustles/graphic-design-first-client-canada/).

## Templates you can copy

### Client brief questions

Send these before you quote:

1. What exactly do you need (piece, size, quantity)?
2. Where will it be used (print, Instagram, website, slides)?
3. Who is it for, and what should they do after seeing it?
4. Do you have a logo, brand colours and fonts? Please send the files.
5. What text, photos and prices must appear? Who supplies them?
6. Share two or three examples you like, and one you don't.
7. What's your deadline, and who approves the final version?
8. What's your budget range?

### Outreach note (direct business)

> Subject: Your [menu / flyer / Instagram] at [Business]
>
> Hi [Name], I'm a designer in [city] who makes [piece] for [type of business]. I noticed [specific, polite observation, such as "the menu on your website is a phone photo"]. Here are three samples: [link]. I could do a [piece] for a fixed $[price] with one revision round, delivered in [X] days. Would that be useful?
>
> [Your name], [contact] (Reply "no thanks" and I won't follow up.)

Keep notes personal and relevant to the recipient's business, and read [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) before you send commercial email at scale or buy a list.

### One-page agreement checklist

- The deliverable, sizes and file formats
- Number of revision rounds, and the price of extra rounds
- Delivery date, and what happens if the client's content is late
- Price, deposit (often half up front) and payment terms
- Who owns the final files and source files, and whether you may show the work in your portfolio
- For Canva designs: the client's use stays within Canva's licence, and the design is for that single client
- Stock, font and image licences, and who pays for them
- Cancellation terms

The Association of Registered Graphic Designers ([RGD](https://rgd.ca/about)) publishes freelancing resources, including invoices and contracts, that are worth reading before you write your own. This is not legal advice.

## What mistakes do new designers make?

- **Selling everything at once.** "Logos, websites, branding, social" reads as no offer at all. One piece is enough until someone pays for a second.
- **Unlimited revisions.** Write the number of rounds into the agreement and price extra rounds.
- **Starting without a deposit.** Linda Tran, above, has every client sign an agreement and pay a deposit before work begins.
- **Logos from stock elements.** Canva stock elements in a logo can't be owned exclusively by the client. Draw logos from scratch.
- **Reselling Canva content.** A template pack, an icon bundle or a folder of Canva photos isn't the same permission as one flyer for one client.
- **Invented testimonials and copied work.** If you didn't make it and don't have a licence, leave it out of your portfolio.
- **Pricing at the employee wage.** $31.25 an hour as an employee is worth more than $31.25 an hour as a freelancer.

## Which tax rules apply to graphic design freelancing in Canada?

Graphic design income in Canada is self-employment income, including deposits, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with your T1 return. Self-employed people pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net self-employment income. Software subscriptions, a share of your computer, a drawing tablet, fonts, stock licences and marketplace fees used for the business are generally deductible; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/). Income paid in USD through Fiverr or Upwork is reported in Canadian dollars.

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add writing, social media and other side-hustle revenue to the same total. Under that test, registration is usually optional; over it, you must register. The first-dollar rule for [taxi and ride-sharing drivers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) doesn't apply to design. Once registered, the rate depends on where the supply is made, per the CRA's [charge and collect table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) and [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers voluntary registration. Tools such as <AffiliateLink program="wealthsimple-tax" label="Wealthsimple Tax" /> handle T2125.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** HST of 13% once registered. Job Bank's Toronto-region median for designers is $33.65 an hour, the highest of the regions shown above besides B.C.
- **British Columbia:** the highest provincial median in the table ($35.00). B.C. adds PST to some goods and services; check whether any printed products you resell are taxable.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and you charge GST and QST once registered. Quebec's Charter of the French Language affects signage and commercial materials, so French-language skills are a selling point.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered; the lower median ($26.30) makes niching and remote clients more important.
- **Atlantic Canada:** HST of 14% in Nova Scotia (since April 1, 2025) and 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and P.E.I., per the CRA table.

Search your city for a business licence requirement if you present yourself as a local business. This guide doesn't state a licence fee.

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

The Job Bank figures are employee wages from 2023–2024 data, not freelance rates; they're a benchmark for setting a floor, not a forecast of what you'll earn. Software prices were read from each company's site on October 9, 2026 (Canva's figure was shown on its Canadian pricing page) and can change, and Figma and the marketplaces bill in USD, so the CAD figures move with the exchange rate. The worked examples use assumed prices, hours and client counts. The designers profiled were chosen because their results were published, not because they're typical: most freelancers take months to land steady clients, and Linda Tran was already an experienced corporate designer when she started.
