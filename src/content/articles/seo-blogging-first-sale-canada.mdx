## What earns the first dollar from an SEO blog in Canada?

The first dollar from an SEO blog in Canada most often comes from an affiliate link in a post that compares or explains products a searcher is already deciding between. Display ads usually pay little until traffic is large, and a product of your own needs readers who already trust you.

Choose one topic you can write about with specifics, such as the gear, rules, or costs in a niche you know. A post that answers "which snow shovel for a townhouse driveway" with measurements and trade-offs is more useful than "best snow shovels" copied from a manufacturer list.

## Where does the first SEO blog reader come from in Canada?

The first SEO blog readers in Canada come from Google search for long, specific questions, from links you share where you already participate, and from other sites that cite a useful page. Big, competitive keywords rarely send traffic to a new site.

Google's [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) say it does not cost money to appear in results, and that meeting the requirements does not guarantee Google will crawl, index, or show a page. A paid "indexing" service is not a shortcut Google describes. Its [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) cover link spam, so buying links is a risk, not a strategy.

## What should a first money post on an SEO blog say?

A first money post on an SEO blog in Canada should answer the searcher's question first, show how you compared the options, state prices with dates and sources, and disclose affiliate links near the top. It should be useful with the links removed.

**Example opening.** This is a generic example. It is not a post that earned a commission.

> The lightest snow shovel that still clears a wet Ontario driveway is usually a 24-inch poly blade on a bent shaft. This page compares five shovels by blade width, weight, and price on Canadian retailer pages checked on [date]. Some links are affiliate links, which means this site may earn a commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you.

Google's spam policies describe thin affiliate pages: product descriptions copied from the merchant with no added value. Add what the merchant page does not say.

## How do you build trust on an SEO blog without fake reviews?

You build trust on an SEO blog in Canada by saying how you compared products, citing the pages you used, dating prices, and never claiming to have tested something you have not. "Based on manufacturer specifications" is honest. A line saying all five were tested is false if they were not.

The Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says reviews must be based on actual experience and that material connections, including commissions, must be disclosed prominently. A disclosure in the footer only is unlikely to be enough.

## How should you think about the first SEO blog income in Canada?

Think about the first SEO blog income in Canada as small and uneven: a commission when a reader buys, or ad revenue once traffic and eligibility line up. This guide does not state typical traffic, commission rates, or ad earnings.

| Path | Who pays | What you watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affiliate links | The merchant, after a qualifying purchase | Programme terms, cookie windows, and disclosure |
| Display ads | The ad network, by traffic | Eligibility and policy compliance |
| Own product | Readers directly | Building something readers have asked for |

Google's [AdSense eligibility page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724) says you need your own content that meets AdSense programme policies and must be at least 18. Being approved is not the same as earning meaningful revenue.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first SEO blog sale look like?

A 14-day plan for a first SEO blog sale in Canada should produce three useful posts answering specific questions, one money post with clear disclosure, and a list of programmes you have applied to. A first dollar inside 14 days is unlikely and not guaranteed.

### Days 1 and 2: Read Google's rules

Read Search Essentials and the spam policies. Write down what you will not do: buy links, copy merchant text, or claim tests you did not run.

### Days 3 to 5: Write two helper posts

Answer two narrow questions in your niche with specifics and sources.

### Days 6 to 9: Write the money post

Compare products a searcher is deciding between. Date the prices. Put the disclosure near the top.

### Days 10 to 14: Apply and share

Apply to one or two affiliate programmes whose terms you have read. Share the posts where you already participate and where sharing is allowed.

## What mistakes block a first SEO blog sale in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first SEO blog sale in Canada are targeting keywords dominated by big sites, publishing thin affiliate pages, and hiding disclosure. Each one keeps the post from ranking or costs reader trust.

Buying links risks the spam policies. Publishing dozens of AI-spun pages with nothing added is thin content. Claiming hands-on testing you did not do is a false review.

## Which tax rules apply to a first SEO blog sale in Canada?

SEO blog income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep affiliate statements, ad network payouts, hosting receipts, and payment records. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Running a blog is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
