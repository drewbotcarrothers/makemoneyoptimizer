Furniture flipping in Canada means buying (or collecting free) solid but tired furniture, cleaning, repairing or refinishing it, and reselling it locally for more. It's one of the few resale side hustles where your work, not just your sourcing, adds the value: Marcus Faccenda told The Globe and Mail that his first piece, a dresser left at the roadside, sold for $500 after he refinished it, and Ramara, Ont. refinisher Kaylee Brouwer turned a $10 coffee table into an $80 sale. The catch is time, storage and safety: old paint can contain lead, and a sectional you can't move is inventory you're stuck with.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Demand:** a 2025 DIG360 and Angus Reid Institute poll found 77% of Canadian adults bought at least one pre-owned item in the past year, and Facebook Marketplace was the most popular place to buy (reported August 11, 2025).

- **Safety:** Health Canada says homes built before 1960 probably contain lead paint and warns against sanding, scraping or burning it. Treat old painted furniture the same way.

## Who does furniture flipping suit?

It suits people who like working with tools, have somewhere to work that isn't a bedroom, and can move a dresser with a friend. You also need patience: a refinished piece can take a weekend to complete and a few weeks to sell.

Good fits:

- **DIYers and hobby woodworkers** who already own a sander and a drill.
- **People with a garage, shed or covered balcony** where dust and paint fumes won't reach living space.
- **Owners of an SUV, truck or trailer**, because pickup and delivery decide what you can buy.
- **Home stagers and decorators** who know which colours and styles sell. Brouwer is a certified home stager and says that helps her showcase her pieces.
- **Parents with flexible evenings**, since most of the work is done at home on your schedule.

It's a poor fit if you live in a small apartment without a balcony or storage, need fast cash, or would be tempted to sand old paint indoors.

## What kinds of flips are there?

Not every flip is a full refinish. Pick the lightest flip that gets the result:

1. **Clean and relist:** clean, tighten, photograph well and relist. Lowest effort; profit comes from buying well.
2. **Hardware swap:** new knobs and pulls. Faccenda's tips to The Globe and Mail include swapping hardware for a more modern look.
3. **Paint flip:** clean, scuff-sand a modern finish, prime if needed, paint and topcoat.
4. **Stain and refinish:** strip or sand to bare wood (only where the old finish is known to be safe), stain and seal. More work, higher prices for solid wood.
5. **Repair flip:** re-glue joints, fix drawers, replace backs. Good for mid-century and solid wood pieces.
6. **Reupholstery:** chairs, stools and benches. Start with a drop-in seat before attempting a sofa.
7. **Acquisition and relocation:** Tim MacLeod's couch business, described below, is mostly buying underpriced pieces and moving them, not restoring them.

## Is old paint safe to sand?

Often it isn't, and you can't tell by looking. Health Canada's [lead page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-garden-safety/reduce-your-exposure-lead.html) says a home built before 1960 probably contains lead-based paint, homes built between 1960 and 1990 may have it in smaller amounts, and consumer paints were virtually lead-free by 1990. It says lead paint in good condition is safer left alone or painted over, and that sanding, scraping or burning it creates harmful dust or fumes. Health Canada's [DIY sheet](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/hc-sc/documents/services/home-safety/chemical-diy-projects/healthy-homes-tips-diy-en.pdf) also warns that older paint may be hidden under newer coats.

Those pages are written for homes, but furniture of the same age can carry the same paint. Practical rules:

- If a painted piece may predate 1990 and you don't know what's under the paint, don't sand or strip it in a living space. Clean it and paint over a sound surface, or pass on it.
- Keep children and pregnant people away from the work.
- Ventilate when painting or varnishing; Health Canada's page on [pollutants from furniture and building materials](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-garden-safety/pollutants-furniture-building-materials.html) recommends it.
- Check [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) before reselling children's furniture, and read Health Canada's [guide to second-hand products](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/industry-guide-second-hand-products-including-children-products/guidance-document.html).

This is not medical advice.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

**Week 1: set up and learn the market.**
- Measure your workspace and storage. If you have no ventilated area, plan clean-and-relist flips only.
- Search sold and active listings near you for dressers, nightstands and side tables. Note asking prices for finished pieces in good condition.
- Read the Health Canada lead page and decide your rule for old paint.

**Week 2: buy and do one quick flip.**
- Buy one small, solid piece under $40 that only needs cleaning, tightening or new hardware.
- Use Faccenda's test: look at the back. Spongy-looking material is likely fibreboard; continuous grain usually means wood.
- Clean, photograph in daylight and list with measurements.

**Week 3: do one paint flip.**
- Buy a solid-wood nightstand or small dresser with a modern finish.
- Clean, scuff-sand, paint two coats and topcoat any surface that gets handled.
- Photograph before and after; post both.

**Week 4: price, sell and review.**
- Sell both pieces; log every cost and hour.
- Calculate profit per hour against the Job Bank wage.
- Write your "won't buy" list (water damage, unknown old paint you'd need to sand, anything you can't lift).
- Decide your niche (dressers, chairs, mid-century) for month two.

## What mistakes do new flippers make?

- **Sanding unknown old paint indoors.** It's the one mistake that can hurt people, not just profit.
- **Buying water-damaged particleboard.** Paint won't make it solid.
- **Skipping prep.** Faccenda calls cleaning and sanding the most important part of the job; paint peels off greasy surfaces.
- **No topcoat on tops.** Chalk-style paints on a dresser top scuff quickly without one, unless the product has a built-in topcoat.
- **Calling veneer "solid wood."** The Competition Bureau's guidance on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) applies to your listing.
- **Buying more than you can store.** Unsold pieces block your workspace.
- **Ignoring your hours.** Log them; a $200 profit over 20 hours is $10 an hour.

## Which tax rules apply to furniture flipping in Canada?

Buying furniture to resell is generally business income, even part time. Report it on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) and pay both the employee and employer shares of [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net income. The cost of pieces you sold, paint, hardware, sanding supplies, listing fees and the business share of vehicle costs can be deducted; tools usually go through capital cost allowance. See [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/). Selling your own used furniture is different: that's generally personal-use property, covered in the [reselling guide](/side-hustles/reselling-side-hustle-canada/).

GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Count other side-hustle revenue too. Furniture flipping isn't ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the CRA's [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) page doesn't apply. Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made; see the CRA's [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table and [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html). For a set-aside habit, see [how much tax to set aside for a side hustle in Canada](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/). <AffiliateLink program="wave" label="Wave" /> or a spreadsheet can track pieces, supplies and kilometres.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial and local notes

- **Ontario:** HST is 13% once registered. Some cities, including Toronto, license certain [second-hand shops](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/second-hand-salvage-yards-shops/second-hand-shop/); check your city before opening a storefront or regular market stall.
- **Nova Scotia:** HST is 14% as of April 1, 2025 on the CRA table.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST once registered; check Revenu Québec, including any rules for upholstered and stuffed articles if you reupholster.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** PST applies separately from GST; check whether PST applies to your sales of used goods and when you must register.
- **Alberta:** 5% GST only once registered.

For reupholstered pieces, also read the Competition Bureau's page on [federal labelling requirements for upholstered furniture](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/federal-labelling-requirements-upholstered-furniture).

## What should you keep in mind about these numbers?

Job Bank wages describe employees in each occupation for 2023 to 2024, not flippers. The operator figures come from news reports (OrilliaMatters 2025, The Globe and Mail 2025, Daily Hive 2024, Moose Money 2026) and are sales prices, not audited profits; Faccenda and MacLeod also earn from content and courses. Amazon.ca prices were read on October 9, 2026 and change often. Fuel, buy and sale prices, hours and paint usage in the worked examples are assumptions. Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji fees can change and vary by account and category.
