Furniture flipping in Canada is buying, or taking with permission, one piece of furniture, making it safe and honest to sell, and listing it for local pickup. It suits people who can lift with help, tell the truth about chips and wobble, and stop when the paint might be old enough to be a lead problem. It is a poor fit if you have nowhere to keep a dresser, or if the plan is to sand unknown paint in a room where people sleep.

The work here is the piece: whether it is worth moving, whether the finish is safe to disturb, and whether the listing matches the object.

## Who does furniture flipping in Canada suit?

Furniture flipping in Canada suits people who can look at a chair and see a stable joint, not a television transformation. A dry corner, a friend who can lift, and a vehicle or a buyer who will pick up are the real tools. The work is a poor fit if you live in a small apartment with no balcony or garage, if you cannot say no to a water-damaged sofa, or if you need the money before the piece sells.

Start with something that already functions: a side table, a stool, a bookshelf with all its shelves. A piece that needs new joinery is a repair project. Price it as one, or leave it.

## Where is old paint a problem on furniture in Canada?

Old paint is a problem when you disturb it, because lead dust is the hazard Health Canada describes for homes, and the same dust can come off a piece of furniture if the paint is old. Health Canada's [lead page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-garden-safety/reduce-your-exposure-lead.html) says a home built before 1960 probably contains lead-based paint, a home built between 1960 and 1990 may have it on the exterior and in smaller amounts indoors, and houses built after 1990 should not contain lead because consumer paints in Canada and the United States were virtually lead-free by then. The same page says that if lead paint is in good condition, it is safer to leave it or paint over it, and that sanding, scraping, or burning it creates harmful dust or fumes.

Health Canada's [DIY sheet](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/hc-sc/documents/services/home-safety/chemical-diy-projects/healthy-homes-tips-diy-en.pdf) says homes built before 1991 may have lead-based paint, including under newer coats, and tells people not to sand, scrape, or burn off leaded paint. Those pages are written for homes. A dresser from an unknown decade can still carry old paint. If you cannot tell, do not sand it in a living space. Keep children and pregnant people away from the work. This is not medical advice and not a renovation manual.

Fresh paint and varnish also off-gas. Health Canada's page on [pollutants from furniture](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-garden-safety/pollutants-furniture-building-materials.html) says to ventilate when painting or varnishing. Let a piece dry before it sits in a bedroom.

## How do you start furniture flipping in Canada?

Starting furniture flipping in Canada means choosing one small piece, deciding whether the finish is safe to touch, and listing it with measurements and flaws. The steps below are that sequence.

### Step 1: Choose a piece you can lift

A side table or a stool. Not a sectional. If you need two people and you do not have the second person, do not buy it.

### Step 2: Check the structure before you pay

Wobble, missing hardware, swollen particle board, smells. Particle board that has been wet does not become solid wood because you painted it. Walk away.

### Step 3: Decide the finish without sanding mystery paint

If the paint might be old, clean it and sell it as-is, or leave it. Read Health Canada's lead pages before you buy sandpaper. Ventilate if you use new paint or varnish.

### Step 4: Measure and photograph the flaws

Width, depth, height. A chip, a stain, a repaired leg. The Competition Bureau's page on [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) is the standard for "solid wood" and "like new." If it is veneer, say veneer.

### Step 5: Skip children's sleep products until you have checked recalls

Cribs and car seats are a poor first flip. Search [recalls and safety alerts](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en) if you are anywhere near baby gear. When in doubt, do not sell it.

### Step 6: List for local pickup

Use the reselling guide for Marketplace and Kijiji habits. Put the measurements in the first lines. Name the neighbourhood, not a promise of delivery across the city, unless you have a vehicle and a price for the drive.

### Step 7: Meet in a way you can cancel

Daylight, a public place or the door with another person home. Show the flaw again in person. If they want a discount for a scratch that was in the photo, you can say no.

### Step 8: Keep the receipt and the listing

What you paid, what you sold, what you spent on paint. One piece is easy to remember. Four pieces are not.

### Step 9: After two sales, decide what you will not buy

Water damage, unknown paint you feel tempted to sand, and anything you cannot lift. Write that list down. The third piece should match the list.

## What should the first week of furniture flipping in Canada include?

The first week should produce one piece in progress or a decision not to buy the one you saw. A sold table is a bonus.

- **Day 1:** Measure the space you can spare. If there is no dry corner, stop.
- **Day 2:** Read the Health Canada lead page. Decide you will not sand unknown paint.
- **Day 3:** Look at local listings. Note real asking prices for small tables, not sold-fantasy prices.
- **Day 4:** Buy or collect one piece that passes the wobble test, or buy nothing.
- **Day 5:** Clean it. Photograph flaws. Measure.
- **Day 6:** If it needs paint and the finish is safe to coat, paint it with ventilation. If not, list it clean.
- **Day 7:** Publish the listing with measurements. Tell two people. Do not buy a second piece the same day.

## What furniture flipping mistakes are common in Canada?

Common mistakes in Canada are sanding old paint, buying upholstered pieces you cannot clean, and writing "solid wood" for veneer. Health Canada's guidance is to avoid creating lead dust. A video that sands a "vintage" dresser in a kitchen is not a Canadian safety standard.

Another mistake is treating a city second-hand shop licence as either a national rule or a rule that never applies. Toronto's [second-hand shop page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/second-hand-salvage-yards-shops/second-hand-shop/) describes a licence for certain shops. A person selling one table from home may be outside that sentence. Read your own city's page before you open a stall or a storage unit full of goods. This guide does not state Toronto's fee and does not copy it onto another city.

Do not haul a piece to the dump and call the dump fee profit. If nobody wants it, the [junk removal](/side-hustles/junk-removal-side-hustle-canada/) problem is yours, including the disposal cost.

## Which tax rules apply to furniture flipping in Canada?

Furniture flipping income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on the net. GST/HST on your taxable supplies follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep purchase notes, supply receipts, and the sale record. A single personal item you no longer want is a different question from a series of pieces you buy to sell. If you are unsure which one you are doing, ask a tax adviser before you assume a garage sale is invisible. Report business activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Flipping furniture is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Confirm the rate on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html), and set the tax aside.

Search your city for a business licence and use that city's page. This guide does not state a licence fee from another city.
