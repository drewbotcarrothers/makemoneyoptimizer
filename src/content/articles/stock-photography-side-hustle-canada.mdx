## Who does selling stock photos in Canada suit?

Selling stock photos and videos in Canada suits people who already shoot often, can produce clean, well-lit, commercially useful images, and don't mind repetitive tasks like keywording. It's a poor fit if you want quick income, mostly shoot people you can't get releases from, or only shoot art photos with little commercial use. Canadian subjects such as winter scenes, Canadian cities, seasonal activities, and everyday Canadian life can fill gaps in agency libraries, but competition is high.

## How much do stock sites pay contributors in Canada?

Stock sites pay contributors a percentage of each licence, which is usually a small amount per download. Adobe's [royalty rates page](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/payments-earnings/royalties-pricing/royalty-rates-assets.html) says contributors earn 33% for photos, vectors, and illustrations and 35% for video, applied to the net price per licensed asset based on the US purchase price, including discounts. Adobe's own example: a plan that costs US$29.99 a month for 10 photo licences pays (US$29.99 ÷ 10) × 33% = about US$0.99 per licensed photo. Other agencies set their own rates; read each contributor agreement.

Adobe's [payment requirements](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/payments-earnings/payment-taxes/payment-requirements.html) say you can withdraw once you have US$25 in royalties and 45 days have passed since your first sale, using PayPal, Payoneer, or Skrill. At the [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/) rate of 1.4246 on Oct 2, 2026, US$25 is about $36 CAD. The same page says Adobe withholds tax if you don't have a valid tax form on file, so complete the tax documentation it asks for.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a forecast. Suppose a portfolio of 500 photos averages one licence per photo per year at Adobe's example royalty of about US$0.99. That's 500 × US$0.99 = about US$495 a year, or about 495 × 1.4246 = $705 CAD. Halve the download rate and the figure halves; build a portfolio of more useful images and it can rise. The point is that stock income scales with portfolio size and demand, not effort per photo.

## What releases do you need for stock photos in Canada?

You need a signed model release for any recognisable person. Adobe's [model release overview](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/content-policies-guidelines/model-property-releases/model-release-overview.html) says a release is required to license content with people, whether they're professional models or captured candidly in public, and that recognisable can mean by face, voice, tattoos, clothing, or surroundings. Property releases can be required for private property and some artwork or buildings; check each agency's rules before uploading.

Copyright starts with you. CIPO's [guide to copyright](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/guide-copyright) says a work is protected the moment it is created and fixed in a material form, and registration is voluntary. Stock agencies license your work; read whether a contract is exclusive before uploading the same image elsewhere.

## How do you start selling stock photos in Canada?

Starting stock photography in Canada means learning what buyers need, uploading consistently, and keywording well.

### Step 1: Study what sells

Search agency libraries for subjects you plan to shoot. Look for gaps: Canadian seasons, regional landmarks, everyday workplaces.

### Step 2: Sign up as a contributor

Create contributor accounts, complete tax forms, and read the contributor agreement.

### Step 3: Shoot for commercial use

Clean compositions with space for text, accurate colour, and no logos or trademarks unless editorial.

### Step 4: Get releases on the day

Have release forms ready on your phone for anyone recognisable.

### Step 5: Keyword and title carefully

Buyers find images by search. Describe what's in the image and its use, not just "beautiful."

### Step 6: Upload consistently

A steady upload schedule builds a portfolio faster than occasional bursts.

### Step 7: Review your stats

Shoot more of what sells and less of what doesn't.

## What mistakes are common in stock photography in Canada?

Common stock photo mistakes in Canada are skipping releases, ignoring trademarks, and expecting fast income.

- **No model release.** Recognisable people need signed releases.
- **Logos and brands.** Trademarks in frame can get images rejected.
- **Weak keywords.** Great images no one can find don't sell.
- **Uploading near-duplicates.** Agencies may reject them; vary angles and subjects.
- **Expecting quick money.** Royalties per download are small; portfolio size matters.

## Which tax rules apply to stock photo income in Canada?

Stock royalties in Canada are generally self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with expenses such as equipment, software, and storage. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable sales pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). Foreign agencies may ask for tax forms to set withholding; keep copies with your records.
