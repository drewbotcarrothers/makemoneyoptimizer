<QuickAnswer>
Affiliate marketing pays you a commission when someone buys through a tracked link on your site, video, newsletter or social post. In Canada, the best-known program is Amazon.ca Associates. Its fixed rates run from 0% to 10% by category: for example, 6% for home and furniture, 4% for kitchen and tools, 1% for computers, and 2% for categories not listed. Credit only applies to items added to the cart within 24 hours of the click. Commissions are small per sale, so this is a long-term content business, not quick cash. The Competition Bureau expects clear disclosure of paid relationships. Report the income on T2125, and track GST/HST once your total taxable revenue nears $30,000.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does affiliate marketing in Canada suit?

Affiliate marketing suits people who already make useful content, or will make it consistently for months before it pays. You earn when readers trust your recommendation enough to click and buy.

It suits:

- **Bloggers and SEO writers** who can rank helpful pages (see [SEO blogging](/side-hustles/seo-blogging-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **YouTubers and short-video creators** who show products in use (see [YouTube](/side-hustles/youtube-side-hustle-canada/) and [TikTok and Reels](/side-hustles/tiktok-reels-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Newsletter writers** with an engaged list (see [newsletter](/side-hustles/newsletter-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Pinterest creators** in home, food, travel and craft niches (see [Pinterest affiliate](/side-hustles/pinterest-affiliate-side-hustle-canada/)).
- **Experts** in a hobby or trade who can compare products honestly.

It doesn't suit people who need income this month. It also doesn't suit anyone tempted to publish thin, copied or auto-generated pages: Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) call out thin affiliate content, and search traffic is where most affiliate income starts.

## How much does affiliate marketing pay?

You earn a percentage of the sale (or a flat bounty), only on qualifying purchases, only within the program's tracking window, and minus returns.

| Pet Food & Supplies | 9% |
| Jewelry, Luggage | 7% |

| Baby, Camera/Photo, Cell Phones, Major Appliances, Musical Instruments, Office Supplies, Software | 5% |

| Automotive, Tires & Wheels, Blu-ray/DVD, CDs & Vinyl | 3.5% |

| Gift Cards, Grocery | 0% |

*Source: Amazon.ca Associates Central, Standard Commission Income Statement.*

Amazon.ca says you earn on qualifying items placed in the customer's cart within 24 hours of their arrival through your link, as long as the order is placed before the cart expires (usually after 90 days).

**Other models**
- **Retail networks** such as Rakuten Advertising, CJ, Impact and Awin host many Canadian and U.S. retailers, each with its own rate and cookie window. The Bargain Moose example below earned about 1% to 3% of sales from retailers.
- **Software and services** often pay a flat bounty or a recurring share. Rates are set in each program's terms, so read them before you write the page.
- **Referral programs** from Canadian fintech and services pay per sign-up under their own terms.

For a deeper look at rates and first-year expectations, see [affiliate marketing pricing](/side-hustles/affiliate-marketing-pricing-canada/).

## Worked math: what a page actually earns

**Illustrative.** The traffic, click-through and conversion rates below are assumptions for the arithmetic, not typical results. The commission rates come from Amazon.ca's table above.

Assume a page about kitchen gear gets 3,000 visits a month, 5% of visitors click an Amazon.ca link, and 8% of those clickers buy an average $60 order in Kitchen & Dining (4%).

- Clicks: 3,000 × 5% = 150
- Orders: 150 × 8% = 12
- Sales: 12 × $60 = $720
- Commission: $720 × 4% = **$28.80 a month**

The same traffic for a page about computers (1%) earns $7.20. A page about home furniture with a $200 average order at 6% earns 12 × $200 × 6% = $144. Category, order size and how many pages you have matter far more than any single page. Hosting and a domain cost money every month, so most affiliate sites take many pages and months of publishing before they cover their costs.

| YouTube | Product demos build trust; videos can rank in Google too | Filming and editing time; links sit in descriptions | Reviews and "best X for Y" videos |

Most durable affiliate businesses use a website as the hub and other channels to send traffic to it. Your site keeps working even if a platform changes its rules.

## How to find topics people are searching for

New sites rarely rank for broad terms like "best headphones." Look for specific questions instead:

- **Google autocomplete and "People also ask."** Type your niche plus "best," "vs" or "for," and note the suggestions.
- **Google Search Console.** Once your site is live, it shows the queries you already get impressions for, which are your easiest wins.
- **Canadian angles.** "Best winter boots for Calgary," "snow brush for an SUV" and "baby gear that ships within Canada" face less competition than U.S.-centred pages.
- **Comparison pairs.** "A vs B" searches come from buyers who are close to a decision.
- **Problems, not products.** "How to keep a garage warm in winter" can lead naturally to a heater comparison.
- **Your own questions.** Questions friends and family ask you about your hobby often turn out to be real searches.

Write down the exact wording people use, because that becomes your headings and FAQ questions. Check whether the current top results are big retailers or smaller sites like yours; if they're all major brands, pick a narrower question. Group related questions into one strong page rather than many thin ones, and link related pages together so readers and search engines can follow the topic.

## How to write a comparison page that earns trust

A comparison page is where most affiliate clicks happen. A structure that works:

1. **Lead with the answer.** Name the best choice for most readers in the first two sentences, and say who should pick something else.
2. **Explain your criteria.** Price range, size, durability, warranty and Canadian availability, the things the buyer actually weighs.
3. **Use a table.** Put the key specs and a "best for" line side by side so readers can scan.
4. **Be honest about downsides.** Every product has a drawback. Naming it makes the rest of the page believable.
5. **Show the product in use.** Your own photos, screenshots or a short video, where you can.
6. **Point to the live price.** Link to the retailer rather than quoting a price that will change next week.
7. **Answer follow-up questions.** Add a short FAQ with sizing, compatibility and returns.

Avoid fake urgency ("only today!"), invented ratings and ranking products you know nothing about. They break reader trust, and some of them can break the Competition Act's rules on misleading representations.

## Keeping pages up to date

Affiliate pages decay: products are discontinued, links break and better options appear. Set a quarterly routine:

- Check every outbound link on your top 20 pages.
- Replace discontinued products, and note the change in an "updated" line.
- Refresh comparison tables and criteria.
- Merge thin or overlapping pages into one stronger page, and redirect the old URL.
- Re-read your disclosure and make sure it still sits near the first affiliate link on every page.
- Check each program's dashboard for policy or rate changes that affect your pages.

## Real Canadian affiliate examples

These are publicly documented. Their results are not typical.

**Bargain Moose.** The Financial Post reported on January 30, 2012 that Anna Waters started the deals blog in August 2008 as a sideline while studying to become an accountant. By then it drew five million page views a year. When shoppers clicked through to a retailer, the blog earned an affiliate fee of about 1% to 3% of the sale, with retailers such as Chapters/Indigo. Affiliate revenue made up 83% of the business's revenue. Monetization was scarce in the first year. **Lesson:** low percentages only add up at large volume, and it took years.

**Traveling Mitch (Toronto).** The Globe and Mail reported on November 13, 2024 that Christopher Mitchell started his travel blog in early 2011 while studying in Oslo. At a blogging conference he realised he was well on the way to earning from the site through ads and affiliate marketing. He went on to create full-time work for himself and his wife, launching a newsletter and two more blogs. **Lesson:** affiliate income usually sits alongside ads and other revenue, built on years of content in one niche.

## How to start: a 30-day plan

**Week 1: Niche and site**
- Day 1: Choose a niche you know well and write down 20 questions buyers in it actually ask.
- Day 2: Check the main affiliate programs in that niche and read their terms, including link placement, disclosure and banned traffic sources.
- Day 3: Register a domain and set up hosting or WordPress.com.
- Day 4: Install a clean theme and create About, Contact and Privacy pages.
- Days 5–7: Outline five cornerstone pages: two roundups, two comparisons and one how-to.

**Week 2: Write without links**
- Write the first two pages with no affiliate links yet. Make each useful on its own.
- Add your own photos, comparison tables and clear pros and cons.

**Week 3: Apply and link**
- Apply to Amazon.ca Associates or one network. Amazon.ca's operating agreement sets conditions for keeping the account, so read them.
- Add links where they help the reader, plus a clear disclosure (see the rules section).
- Publish pages three and four.

**Week 4: Measure**
- Set up Google Search Console and an analytics tool.
- Publish page five, and start an email sign-up with Kit or beehiiv.
- Note which pages get impressions in search, and plan more pages around those topics.

For the first-commission milestone, see [your first affiliate sale](/side-hustles/affiliate-marketing-first-sale-canada/).

### Days 31–90
- **Weeks 5–6:** Publish two pages a week. Update the best-performing page with more detail.
- **Weeks 7–8:** Add internal links between related pages, and build a simple "start here" page.
- **Weeks 9–10:** Repurpose your top pages into Pinterest pins or short videos.
- **Weeks 11–12:** Review earnings by page and category. Cut or merge thin pages, and plan seasonal content (Black Friday, holidays, back-to-school) two to three months ahead.

## Rules: disclosure, spam and email

- **Disclosure:** the Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says material connections, including affiliate commissions, must be disclosed clearly and prominently. Readers should see the disclosure before they act on the link, and it applies to social posts and videos too.
- **Search quality:** Google's [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) and spam policies warn against thin affiliate pages that copy product descriptions without adding value.
- **Program rules:** many programs restrict links in emails or offline material and ban incentivised or misleading clicks. Read each operating agreement before you promote.
- **Email:** sending commercial messages falls under Canada's anti-spam legislation; the CRTC's [CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm) covers consent and unsubscribe rules.
- **Ads:** if you add display ads, check Google's [AdSense eligibility requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724).

## Which tax rules apply to affiliate marketing in Canada?

Affiliate commissions are business income. They're reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on their net income (QPP in Quebec). GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the first-dollar [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule.

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

- **Small-supplier test:** worldwide taxable supplies, including your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive quarters. Count all your side-hustle revenue together.
- **Foreign programs:** commissions from non-resident programs may be zero-rated exports once you're registered. Check the CRA's rules or ask an accountant, and keep each program's payment records.
- **Rates for Canadian clients:** Ontario 13% HST, Nova Scotia 14% HST (since April 1, 2025, per the CRA [charge and collect](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table) and Alberta 5% GST. Confirm with the [GST/HST calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
- **USD payments:** record income in Canadian dollars using a reasonable exchange rate, and use the same method consistently.
- **U.S. tax forms:** many U.S. programs ask for a tax form during signup. Fill it out accurately for a Canadian resident.
- **Deductions:** hosting, domains, software, products bought for reviews and a share of internet can be business expenses.

## Common affiliate marketing mistakes

- **Writing for the link, not the reader.** Thin pages don't rank or convert.
- **Hiding the disclosure.** Clear, prominent disclosure is what the Competition Bureau expects.
- **Hard-coding prices.** Prices change; link to the live page instead.
- **Picking only low-rate categories.** Check the commission table before building a whole site around computers at 1%.
- **Relying on one program.** Programs change rates and terms.
- **Emailing affiliate links.** Many programs ban it, and CASL applies.

## What are the limits of affiliate marketing as a side hustle?

Commissions per sale are small: 1% to 10% on Amazon.ca, and about 1% to 3% in the Bargain Moose example. Income depends on traffic you don't control, from search updates to platform changes, and programs can cut rates or close accounts. The worked math above is illustrative, and the Bargain Moose and Traveling Mitch examples are long-running businesses, not typical results. Expect months of publishing before meaningful income, and recheck each program's live rate table and terms.
