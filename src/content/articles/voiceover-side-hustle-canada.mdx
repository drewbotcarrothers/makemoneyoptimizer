## Who does voiceover in Canada suit?

Voiceover in Canada suits people with a clear speaking voice, good reading stamina, and the patience to re-record a line many times. It also suits bilingual speakers, since English and French work both exist in Canada. It's a poor fit if you can't find a quiet space, dislike hearing your own voice, or need fast income: auditions often go unanswered, and the first booking can take weeks or months.

## How do voiceover marketplaces work in Canada?

Voiceover marketplaces match clients who post jobs with talent who audition. On Voices, the [membership page](https://www.voices.com/talent/memberships) says a free Guest profile lets you upload demos and be privately invited to jobs, while a Premium membership (listed at $499 a year) is needed to be matched with jobs, reply to them, and sell project packages. The [platform fee FAQ](https://www.voices.com/help/knowledge/faq/What-is-the-Platform-Fee) says a 20% fee is calculated on the talent quote and on additional payments, and is not charged on the membership itself. Check the currency at checkout, and read each marketplace's current terms before paying.

You don't need a marketplace at all. Direct outreach to e-learning companies, video producers, and local agencies avoids platform fees, though it takes longer to build.

## Do you need to join a union for voiceover in Canada?

No, not to start. [ACTRA](https://www.actra.ca/) describes itself as the national union of professional performers working in recorded media in Canada. Many beginner jobs on marketplaces are non-union. If you get union work later, the union's rules and rates apply, so read ACTRA's membership information before accepting non-union work once you join.

## How do you start voiceover in Canada?

Starting voiceover in Canada means setting up a quiet recording space, recording a short demo, and auditioning consistently.

### Step 1: Pick two or three work types

E-learning, explainer videos, and phone systems are common entry points. Choose based on your natural voice and reading style.

### Step 2: Set up a quiet space

Record in the quietest, softest room you have. Test for fridge hum, traffic, and echo before buying gear.

### Step 3: Buy a lean kit

A USB mic, closed-back headphones, and a pop filter are enough to learn on.

### Step 4: Learn basic editing

Practise removing breaths, mouth clicks, and long pauses, and exporting clean files at the formats clients request.

### Step 5: Record a short demo per genre

Keep each demo short and specific. Use scripts you wrote or have permission to use.

### Step 6: Audition every week

Set a weekly audition target on a marketplace, or send direct pitches to producers. Track which genres get callbacks.

### Step 7: Agree usage and revisions in writing

Usage (where and how long the audio will run) affects price. Put scope, revisions, and payment terms in writing before recording.

## What mistakes are common in voiceover in Canada?

Common voiceover mistakes in Canada are buying expensive gear before treating the room, sending a generic demo, and not pricing for usage.

- **Gear before room.** An expensive mic in an echoey room still sounds echoey.
- **One demo for everything.** Clients want to hear the genre they're hiring for.
- **Ignoring usage.** Broadcast ads and internal training videos aren't the same job.
- **Paying for a membership too early.** Test with a free profile and direct outreach first.
- **Unlimited revisions.** Agree a number in writing.

## Which tax rules apply to voiceover in Canada?

Voiceover income in Canada is generally self-employment income. Report it on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), along with expenses such as equipment, software, and marketplace fees. If you're over 18 and net self-employment earnings exceed $3,500, you pay both halves of [CPP contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) outside Quebec. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable sales pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html). See [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/) and [how much tax to set aside](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/).
