Amazon FBA in Canada means you buy or make products, ship them to an Amazon.ca fulfilment centre, and Amazon stores, packs and ships each order to the customer. You pay a selling plan fee ($1.49 per item or $29.99 a month), a referral fee on each sale (15% in many categories) and a per-unit fulfilment fee that starts at $5.92 for a small standard-size item. In Jungle Scout's 2024 seller survey, 57% of small sellers had profit margins above 10%, and 13% weren't profitable yet, so the job is mostly picking products where the arithmetic works before you buy.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Selling plans (Amazon.ca, checked October 9, 2026):** Individual at $1.49 per item sold, or Professional at $29.99 a month, each plus selling fees. Amazon suggests Professional once you sell more than 30 units a month.
- **Per-sale fees:** referral fees run 8% to 17% in most categories (15% with a $0.40 minimum in many), and FBA fulfilment starts at $4.46 for an envelope-size item and $5.92 for a standard-size item under 100 g. Amazon said there would be no increase to Canada referral or FBA fees for 2026.
- **Holiday surcharge:** peak FBA fees apply from October 15, 2026 to January 14, 2027, averaging $0.39 more per unit.
- **Margins (Jungle Scout, 1,064 sellers surveyed Dec 2023–Jan 2024):** 57% of small sellers above 10%, 28% above 20%, average 21%; 13% not yet profitable.
- **Tax:** report profit on T2125, pay both CPP shares, and register for GST/HST once taxable sales pass $30,000; Amazon collects GST/HST for vendors who aren't registered on qualifying goods.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does Amazon FBA suit?

FBA suits people who like spreadsheets, can leave a few hundred to a few thousand dollars tied up in stock for two or three months, and will follow a platform's rules to the letter. It's a poor fit if you need cash next month or if losing the money spent on a product that doesn't sell would hurt.

It suits:

- **Bargain hunters** who already shop clearance aisles and can scan products for resale value (retail arbitrage).
- **People with a supplier relationship**, such as a family business, a local maker or a distributor willing to sell wholesale.
- **Makers with a repeatable product** who want Prime shipping without packing every order; handmade one-offs usually fit better on an [Etsy shop](/side-hustles/etsy-shop-side-hustle-canada/).
- **Patient brand builders** who can spend months on product research, a trademark and listing photos before a first sale.

FBA trades control for convenience. Amazon handles storage, shipping, returns and much of customer service, but its fees, its account-health rules and its decisions about which brands you may sell set the limits of your business.

## How does Amazon FBA pay?

Amazon deposits your sale proceeds, minus its fees, into your bank account every two weeks. For each unit, what lands in your account is the sale price minus the referral fee, the FBA fulfilment fee, any storage and other fees, and then you subtract what you paid for the product and to ship it in.

The best public data on Amazon seller profits comes from Jungle Scout's yearly survey. Its 2024 edition (surveyed December 1, 2023 to January 3, 2024; 1,064 sellers with at least one live listing, 916 of them businesses under US$2 million in yearly sales) reported these profit margins for small and mid-size sellers:

| Profit margin | Share of sellers |
|---|---|
| 1–5% | 12% |
| 6–10% | 16% |
| 11–15% | 13% |
| 16–20% | 15% |
| 21–25% | 15% |
| 26–50% | 13% |
| 51–100% | 1% |
| Not yet profitable | 13% |

Jungle Scout put the average margin at 21%. Its [2025 report](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/reports/amazon-seller-report-2025/) (nearly 1,500 respondents, January 10–27, 2025, English only) found rising costs a top concern, with nearly 40% of enterprise brands worried about profitability as shipping, product and advertising costs climb. The survey covers sellers worldwide, not only Canadians.

Canadian-specific figures are older. Amazon reported that in 2021 its Canadian selling partners averaged more than US$85,000 in sales, more than 4,000 Canadian sellers passed US$100,000, and more than 25,000 sellers on Amazon.ca used FBA in Canada. Those are sales, not profits, and averages pulled up by large sellers.

What moves your result most:

- **Buy price.** Most profit is made when you buy. A product bought too high can't be fixed with a better listing.
- **Size and weight.** A 90 g item and a 950 g item at the same price differ by $2.57 in fulfilment fees ($5.92 versus $8.49).
- **Sell-through speed.** Stock that sits through October to December pays the higher $77/m³ storage rate.
- **Brand approvals.** Many brands and categories are gated; getting approved can take months, which keeps competitors out once you're in.
- **Advertising.** Private-label products usually need Amazon Ads to get seen; arbitrage and wholesale sellers often don't.

## What does the math look like on one product?

**Worked example 1, a small private-label item (illustrative).** Assume a 90 g kitchen gadget, standard size, sold for $24.99 in a 15% category. The sale price, landed cost, inbound shipping and ad spend are assumptions; the fees are Amazon.ca's published rates.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sale price | $24.99 |
| Referral fee, 15% | −$3.75 |
| FBA fulfilment, standard first 100 g | −$5.92 |
| Storage, 0.003 m³ box × $40 for one month | −$0.12 |
| Landed cost from supplier (assumed) | −$8.00 |
| Inbound shipping to Amazon (assumed) | −$0.50 |
| Advertising per unit sold (assumed) | −$2.00 |
| **Profit per unit before tax** | **$4.70** (18.8% margin) |

Sell 100 a month and that's $470, minus the $29.99 Professional plan, or $440.01 a month before tax. During the holiday peak the FBA fee rises by about $0.39 a unit on average, which trims about $39 off the same 100 units.

**Worked example 2, a retail arbitrage buy (illustrative).** Assume you find a toy on clearance for $12.00 that sells on Amazon.ca for $34.99, weighs 350 g and sits in a 15% category.

- Referral: $34.99 × 0.15 = $5.25
- FBA fee at 300–400 g: $6.73 (rate card as published by VonClaro)
- Profit: $34.99 − $5.25 − $6.73 − $12.00 = $11.01 per unit, before inbound shipping, prep supplies and storage
- Return on the $12 spent: $11.01 ÷ $12.00 = 92%

That return is why arbitrage sellers scan hundreds of products to find a few. The catch is that you can only buy what's on the shelf, and many brands won't let new sellers list them.

**Worked example 3, cash tied up (illustrative).** If you start with $1,000 of inventory that sells over 60 days and Amazon pays every 14 days, you'll wait roughly 10 to 11 weeks from purchase to the last deposit. Plan to fund a second order from savings, not from the first order's proceeds.

## Which sourcing model should you start with?

There are four common ways to get products into FBA. They differ in money needed, rules and how fast you learn.

| Model | What it is | Money to start | Main risk | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retail arbitrage | Buying discounted products in stores to resell | Lowest; a few hundred dollars | Brand gating and Amazon asking for invoices that a store receipt doesn't satisfy | Learning fast with small buys |
| Online arbitrage | Same, but from online sales | Low | Same gating risk; shipping costs eat margin | People who can't drive to stores |
| Wholesale | Buying from a brand or authorised distributor with invoices | Medium; minimum orders | Thin margins on popular brands | Repeat, steady income |
| Private label | Your own brand, usually made by a manufacturer | Highest; a production run plus a trademark | Product that doesn't sell; copycats | Building an asset over years |

The unnamed Canadian seller profiled below used the first two models for six months to learn which brands sold, then moved to wholesale. That's a common path because arbitrage teaches you sell-through speed with small amounts of money.

## Product ideas that suit Canadian sellers

These are directions to research, each with why it can work on Amazon.ca. Check the referral category, size tier and whether the brand is gated before you buy any of them.

1. **Bilingual-labelled household goods.** Canada requires English and French on prepackaged products, so many U.S. goods can't be sold here as-is.
2. **Winter car accessories** (ice scrapers, seat covers, emergency kits). Strong seasonal demand from October; ship in by early fall.
3. **Hockey and skating accessories** such as tape, skate guards and bags. Year-round in Canada, light and small.
4. **Canadian-made food and snacks** for the Canada Showcase store, if you can get grocery approval and handle expiry dates.
5. **Maple products** in sturdy, small packaging. Popular gift item; check food rules and expiry.
6. **Natural personal care** from Canadian makers, via wholesale. Repeat purchases; beauty is gated for many brands.
7. **Pet accessories** such as treat pouches and travel bowls. Light, small and 15% referral in the pet category.
8. **Camping and cottage gear** like dry bags and lantern hooks. Summer demand; keep units small to avoid oversize fees.
9. **Board games and puzzles** on clearance after the holidays. Good retail arbitrage targets; watch dimensions.
10. **School supplies** bought at summer clearance for resale in late August.
11. **Kitchen gadgets under 500 g.** Easy to ship and store; very competitive, so niche down.
12. **Garden tools** sold by Canadian makers, such as the Vancouver-made Seeding Square Amazon featured in 2023.
13. **Baby products** priced over $15 (15% referral) from authorised distributors. Montreal's Maighan Distribution sells baby brands such as BIBS and Haakaa on Amazon.
14. **Books** bought used for resale. Media categories have their own fee rules; textbooks sell in August and January.
15. **Replacement parts and accessories** for popular appliances. Small, light and searched for by exact model number.
16. **Office and desk accessories** at 15% referral. Steady demand from home workers.
17. **Hobby supplies** (yarn tools, model kits, art supplies). Passionate buyers who search for specific items.
18. **Halloween and Christmas décor** bought at post-season clearance. Cheap to buy, but you hold it for 10 months and pay storage.
19. **Products for cold-weather pets** such as dog boots and coats. Canadian demand is higher than many U.S. sellers stock for.
20. **Your own small brand** of a simple accessory, once you've learned the fees through arbitrage.

## Do you need barcodes or a trademark?

You need a barcode (GTIN) only when you create a new product listing. Arbitrage and wholesale sellers usually list on an existing product page, so they don't. Private-label sellers do.

- **GTINs:** [GS1 Canada](https://gs1ca.org/amazon/) says Amazon.ca asks for a GTIN to identify products, every variation (size, flavour) needs its own, and subscriptions start at $26.25 a year for one GTIN and barcode. Amazon also runs a GTIN exemption process for some products, which needs approval.
- **Trademark:** Amazon Brand Registry, which protects your listing and unlocks brand tools, is tied to a registered or pending trademark. CIPO's [2026 fee](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/trademarks/fees-trademarks) to file a trademark application online is $491.06 for the first class of goods and $149.04 for each additional class. It rises 1.7% to $499.41 on January 1, 2027.
- **Labels:** prepackaged non-food products sold in Canada must show the product identity and net quantity in both English and French, per the [Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/labelling/prepackaged-non-food-consumer-products/what-must-be-label). Amazon's 2025 small-business report described a U.S. brand expanding to Canada whose main change was swapping photos to show French-English labels.

## How do you start? A 12-week plan

### Weeks 1–2: Learn the fees and pick a model

- Read the [Amazon.ca pricing page](https://sell.amazon.ca/pricing) end to end and run five products you already own through the Revenue Calculator.
- Choose a model: arbitrage if you want to learn cheaply, wholesale if you have a supplier, private label only if you can afford a production run and a trademark.
- Set a test budget you can lose without stress. Write it down.

### Weeks 3–4: Open the account and set up tools

- Open a seller account on the Individual plan if you expect fewer than 30 sales a month. Have government ID, a credit card, bank account details and your phone ready.
- Open a separate bank account or card for the business; <AffiliateLink program="koho" label="KOHO" /> is one Canadian option.
- Start a free SellerAmp trial (arbitrage) or a Helium 10 trial (private label) and set up your bookkeeping tool.

### Weeks 5–6: Source a test batch

- Arbitrage: scan clearance aisles at three stores, buy only items with a clear profit after fees and that you're eligible to sell. Keep every receipt.
- Wholesale: contact five brands or distributors and ask for an account; ask whether they allow Amazon resale.
- Aim for 10 to 30 units across several products, not 30 of one product.

### Weeks 7–8: Prep and ship in

- Create the shipment in Seller Central and follow Amazon's prep and labelling rules (poly bags, suffocation warnings, FNSKU labels where required).
- Weigh and label each box, and ship to the fulfilment centre Amazon assigns.
- Log the inbound shipping cost against each product.

### Weeks 9–10: Watch the first sales

- Check prices daily; match only if you still profit after fees.
- Note which products sold in the first two weeks and which haven't moved.

### Weeks 11–12: Review and decide

- Calculate profit per product: sale price minus every fee, landed cost and shipping.
- Reorder only what sold profitably. Remove or liquidate items that haven't sold before storage fees build.
- Switch to the Professional plan once you're past 30 sales a month or need restricted categories.

## Real Canadian Amazon sellers

These are reported cases, chosen because their numbers are public. They aren't typical, and they aren't promises.

**Karen Danudjaja, Blume, North Vancouver, B.C.** In a February 19, 2026 article produced by the Globe Content Studio for Amazon (sponsored content), the co-founder of superfood drink company Blume said she started selling to Vancouver cafés while working a full-time job, labelling products in her living room, and began selling on Amazon in 2022. She said Amazon had grown to about 30% of the business and was growing around 200% a year, and that Prime Day and the Amazon.ca Canada Showcase brought in new customers. The team has grown to 20 people across Canada and the U.S. The article doesn't give dollar sales.

**An unnamed Canadian arbitrage-to-wholesale seller.** Case Distillery reported on August 31, 2026 that a Canadian seller started retail and online arbitrage in April 2025 with no cash and a $10,000 credit line, sold only on Amazon.ca in two categories (food and beauty), and crossed $50,000 a month in gross revenue a little over a year later, at a margin he put at 10–15% (about $5,000 to $7,500 a month). He preps and ships FBA from his house with a scanner, a price-history tool and a spreadsheet costing under $100 a month, and spent five months resubmitting the same invoice to get approved for one brand. The article doesn't name him or state the currency.

**Canadian brands Amazon featured.** Amazon Canada's November 2023 roundup named Hunter & Trove (jewellery, Vancouver), All Natural Advice Skin Care (Oakville, Ontario), Maighan Distribution (baby products, Montreal) and Seeding Square (garden tools, Vancouver) as Canadian small businesses selling on Amazon.ca. It quotes the owners but doesn't give their sales.

What the cases share: a narrow focus (two categories, one product line), a long wait for something competitors can't easily copy (brand approvals, a Canadian-made product), and Amazon as one channel rather than the only one for the brands.

## What mistakes do new FBA sellers make?

- **One big first order.** Ten products × three units teaches more than one product × 30 units.
- **Ignoring size tiers.** A box a few centimetres too big can jump to oversize fees that start above $15 per unit.
- **Buying gated brands.** Check eligibility in Seller Central before you pay; a store receipt often isn't accepted as proof.
- **Holding seasonal stock into October.** Storage nearly doubles from $40 to $77 per cubic metre in October through December.
- **Unilingual labels.** Non-food packaged goods need English and French product identity and net quantity.
- **Mixing personal and business money.** You can't see profit, and the T2125 takes twice as long.
- **Paying for a course before a test.** Amazon's fee page, a free tool trial and one small batch teach the basics.

## Which tax rules apply to Amazon FBA in Canada?

Amazon FBA profit is business income reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with your T1 return, and self-employed people pay both the employee and employer [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contributions on net self-employment income. Inventory is deducted as cost of goods sold when it sells, not when you buy it, so keep every invoice and receipt and count unsold stock at year-end. Amazon fees, a share of internet and phone, prep supplies, software and inbound shipping are generally deductible; see [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).

GST/HST registration follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): worldwide taxable supplies of $30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive quarters, counting all your businesses. Under the CRA's [digital-economy rules for qualifying goods](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/sales-good.html), a distribution platform operator such as Amazon must charge and collect GST/HST on qualifying goods it facilitates for vendors who aren't registered; registered vendors charge and collect it themselves, including on platform sales. Gross sales count toward the $30,000, not profit, so a busy FBA account can cross it faster than you'd expect. The [GST/HST registration guide](/side-hustles/gst-hst-registration-side-hustle-canada/) covers voluntary registration, which lets you claim input tax credits on inventory you buy. For platform reporting in general, see [tax on reselling and gig app platform income](/side-hustles/tax-reselling-gig-apps-platform-income-canada/).

This is not tax, legal or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario and Atlantic Canada:** HST at 13% (Ontario), 14% (Nova Scotia) or 15% (New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, P.E.I.), per the CRA's [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html), on sales to buyers in those provinces once you're registered.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP, and QST applies alongside GST. Quebec's French-language rules on packaging and product information go further than the federal bilingual-label rules.
- **British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba:** these provinces charge their own PST; check each province's rules for online marketplace sales before you register.
- **Alberta and the territories:** 5% GST only once registered.
