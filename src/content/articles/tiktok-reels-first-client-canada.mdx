## What offer gets a first TikTok or Reels brand deal in Canada?

The offer most likely to land a first TikTok or Reels brand deal in Canada is one disclosed video on your account, about a product you have actually used, with a fixed fee, a delivery date, and one round of changes. A month-long ambassador package is a later conversation.

Pick brands that fit what you already post. A cooking account pitching a kitchen shop makes sense to both audiences. A cooking account pitching a crypto app does not, and the audience notices.

## Where do Canadians find a first brand partner for short-form video?

Canadians find a first short-form brand partner among small and local businesses they already buy from, brands that already work with creators of a similar size, and creator marketplaces built into the platforms. Large national brands usually work through agencies with minimum audience sizes.

Contact brands through the route they publish for partnerships, such as a creator form or a partnerships email. If you email a business, ISED's [anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies to a commercial message. The [getting consent](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) page explains when consent can be implied and what the message must contain: your identity, contact details, and an unsubscribe mechanism. This is not legal advice.

## What should a first brand pitch say?

A first brand pitch in Canada should name the product, link two of your videos that fit it, give your honest recent numbers, and propose one specific video. Five sentences are enough.

**Example pitch.** This is a generic example. It is not a pitch that produced a deal.

> Hello. I post weekly short recipes for small kitchens on TikTok and Instagram, and I have used your cast-iron pan in two of them [links]. My last ten videos averaged [number] views; my audience insights show most viewers in Canada. I would like to propose one 45-second recipe video featuring the pan, marked as a paid partnership, posted on a date we agree, for a fixed fee. Could I send a one-page outline?

## How do you prove reach without inflated numbers?

You prove reach for a first TikTok or Reels deal in Canada with screenshots from your own analytics, a short list of recent videos with their real views, and the audience location the platform reports. Bought followers and engagement pods produce numbers that do not match sales, and brands check.

The Competition Bureau's [influencer marketing page](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act) says reviews and endorsements must reflect actual experience and that a tag, discount code, or affiliate link alone is unlikely to be enough disclosure. Use the platform's paid-partnership label plus a clear spoken or written disclosure early in the video.

## How should you price the first TikTok or Reels deal in Canada?

Price the first TikTok or Reels deal in Canada as a flat fee for one video with written usage terms: where the brand may reuse it and for how long. This guide does not state a market rate per view or per follower.

| Path | What the brand gets | What you watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product only | Free product for a video | It is still a material connection that needs disclosure |
| Flat fee, your account | One disclosed video | Revisions that turn into a reshoot |
| Flat fee plus usage | The video for their ads too | Usage period and paid ad rights written down |

Statements about a product in a paid video are the brand's and yours. The Bureau's [false or misleading representations](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations) page covers claims made to the public to promote a product. Do not repeat a health or performance claim you cannot support.

Suppose a local shop pays an assumed $250 for one disclosed video, with no paid ad usage. Suppose planning, filming, editing, and one revision take five hours. The sketch is $250 ÷ 5 = $50 an hour before tax set-aside. If the brand asks for 90 days of ad usage and you add an assumed $100, the deal is $350 for the same five hours, or $70 an hour. These figures are assumptions, not a quote, a forecast, or a typical result.

## What does a 14-day plan for a first TikTok or Reels deal look like?

A 14-day plan for a first TikTok or Reels brand deal in Canada should produce a stats sheet, a short list of fitting brands, and three specific pitches. A signed deal inside 14 days is not guaranteed.

### Days 1 and 2: Read the disclosure rules

Read the Competition Bureau's influencer page. Write your disclosure line and decide where it appears in each video.

### Days 3 to 5: Build the stats sheet

Pull your real numbers for the last ten videos and your audience location. Pick two videos that show a product well.

### Days 6 to 9: List and research brands

List ten brands you have used that fit your niche. Find each one's published partnership contact.

### Days 10 to 14: Pitch three

Send three specific pitches using the example shape. Follow up once. Keep a list of replies and declines.

## What mistakes block a first TikTok or Reels deal in Canada?

Mistakes that block a first TikTok or Reels deal in Canada are pitching brands that do not fit, inflating numbers, and skipping disclosure. Each one costs the deal or the brand's trust.

Accepting a deal without written usage terms lets a brand run your face in ads indefinitely. Promising sales results you cannot control sets up a dispute. Mass-emailing scraped addresses skips anti-spam consent.

## Which tax rules apply to a first TikTok or Reels deal in Canada?

Short-form video income in Canada is self-employment income when you are carrying on the activity for profit, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. GST/HST follows the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) first-dollar rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table, and Quebec charges GST and QST.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement, invoices, payments, and a list of products received for posts with their retail prices. Report the activity on Form T2125 with the T1. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Add other side-hustle revenue to the same pot. Under that test, registration is usually optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA's when-to-register page. Making sponsored short-form videos is not ride-sharing, so the first-dollar rule on the taxi page does not apply unless you also carry passengers for a platform.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made, unless a platform rule you have read says the platform collects. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).
