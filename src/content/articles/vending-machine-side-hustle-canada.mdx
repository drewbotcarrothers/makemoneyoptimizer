- **What it is:** you own or lease a machine, you find a location that will host it, and you keep it stocked, clean, and working.
- **The work:** driving product in, counting what sold, fixing card readers and coin jams, and replacing a location that asks you to leave.
- **Permits:** Toronto's business table lists coin-operated vending machines for zoning, a provincial business name if you need one, and HST. Food is a health-unit question on top of that. A sidewalk cart is a different licence.

A vending machine side hustle in Canada means placing a machine that sells snacks, drinks, or other small goods on someone else's property, then restocking it and keeping it running. It suits people who can lift cases, drive a route, and will sign a location agreement they have read. It is a poor fit if you want income without a weekly visit, or if the pitch is a "turnkey route" you pay for before you have seen the locations.

What the machine, the reader, and the first fill can cost is [vending machine startup costs](/side-hustles/vending-machine-startup-costs-canada/). A cash machine on a similar location model is [an ATM side hustle](/side-hustles/atm-machine-side-hustle-canada/).

## Is a vending machine side hustle worth it in Canada?

It can be worth it when you control a location people already walk past, the machine price is a number you got in writing, and you are willing to do the route yourself. It is not worth it when the only locations on offer are ones a seller promises, the commission to the location eats the margin, or you counted gross sales as pay. There is no official Canadian average for vending profit. Anyone who quotes one without showing product cost, commission, and spoilage is selling the machine, not describing your route.

## What work does a vending route actually take?

The work is physical and repetitive. You buy product, you drive it, you fill slots, you remove anything stale, you empty cash or check the card report, and you deal with a machine that is dark on a Saturday. A location that looked busy in the seller's photo can be a closed office on Friday. Winter roads and building access after hours are part of the job in most cities.

A folding hand truck saves your back on cases of drinks. <AffiliateLink program="amazon-ca" label="A folding hand truck" search="folding hand truck" /> is ordinary route gear. It does not find you a location. Count cash and card deposits in a notebook or a spreadsheet the same day, and keep that record with [record keeping](/side-hustles/record-keeping-side-hustle-canada/).

## How do you get a vending location in Canada?

You get a location by asking the person who controls the space and signing something that says you may put the machine there. Offices, warehouses, apartment laundry rooms, and community halls are private property. The owner or manager can say no, can ask for a share of sales or a flat monthly fee, and can ask you to remove the machine. Get the commission or rent in writing before you bolt anything to the floor.

A public sidewalk is not the same ask. Toronto's [food cart page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/permits-licences-bylaws/food-trucks-food-carts-ice-cream-trucks/food-carts/) is about refreshment vehicles and sidewalk vending permits, including a health inspection letter for food vendors. Do not put a snack machine on a city sidewalk because a plaza said yes.

## Which permits apply to vending machines in Canada?

Rules are municipal and, for food, provincial. Search your address on [BizPaL](https://bizpal-perle.ca/).

Toronto's [business regulations table](https://www.toronto.ca/business-economy/new-businesses-startups/business-regulations/) lists "Coin-Operated Vending Machines" with zoning requirements and sign permits in the municipal column, business registration in the provincial column, and HST in the federal column. That row does not show a separate municipal operating licence of the kind the same table lists for food carts. Read the row again when you apply, because tables change. The page also says its HST note treats registration as optional under $30,000 of sales. The CRA test is the one to follow, and it is measured on taxable supplies, not on a city's paraphrase. See [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html).

Food is extra. Ontario's [Food Premises Regulation](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/170493) applies to food premises. A machine that holds milk, sandwiches, or other food that needs temperature control is a conversation with the local public health unit before you plug it in. Prepackaged candy is not the same risk as a fresh-food machine. Do not guess which products are exempt from a health permit.

Other cities licence vending in their own bylaws. Vancouver has licensed vending machines in the past. Confirm the current business-licence bylaw and fee rather than using an old council report as the price.

## What are the risks of a vending side hustle in Canada?

- **The location ends.** A new manager can unplug you. Your machine then needs a home, and you still own it.
- **Product goes stale or gets stolen.** You ate that cost. A glass front does not stop someone who tips the machine.
- **The commission is negotiated, not standard.** Budget the share you signed, not a percentage from a stranger.
- **Card readers fail.** Sales stop until you or a technician attend. Cash-only machines miss people who do not carry coins.
- **Turnkey placement programs.** Paying a company for "guaranteed locations" before you have visited them is a common way people get a machine and no foot traffic. Upfront fees to unlock earnings are a pattern described in [side hustle scams](/side-hustles/side-hustle-scams-canada/). Visit the address. Speak to the site contact yourself.

## How do you judge a vending stop without a fake revenue number?

### Illustrative example

Every figure below is assumed so the method is visible. None of them is a Canadian average.

Suppose a machine sells $400 of product in a month. Suppose the product cost you $200. Suppose the location agreement takes 20 percent of sales, which is $80. Cash left before your driving, the machine payment, and spoilage is $400 − $200 − $80 = $120. Suppose two visits take three hours in total, including the drive. That $120 is $40 for each of those hours before the other costs, and only in a month that actually sells $400. A month that sells $150 does not scale the same way, because the drive still happens. Replace every input with your quote and your count.

## Which tax rules apply to vending in Canada?

Vending sales you make as a business go on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html). You pay both shares of [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) on the net if you are self-employed. Most candy, pop, and prepared snacks are taxable supplies for GST/HST. Some basic groceries are zero-rated. The CRA's [basic groceries memorandum](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/4-3/basic-groceries.html) is the classification page. Carbonated drinks, candy, and snack foods are the taxable examples that memorandum names. Do not assume a chip bag is zero-rated because a grocery store sells food. Taxable sales count toward the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) across all of your businesses. A machine that lasts for years is usually capital cost allowance, not a one-year expense. This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.
