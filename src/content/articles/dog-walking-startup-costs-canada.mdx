## Who should use a dog walking startup budget in Canada?

The dog walking startup budget in Canada fits people who like dogs, can walk in local weather, and will write down medications, the vet’s number, and which door sticks. The lean column fits a walker who stays on one map and refuses dogs that lunge or have no vaccine record. Dog walking on a day-one group budget is a poor fit for someone with an allergy, housing that bans an emergency animal, or a plan to walk several unknown dogs immediately.

Group walks add gear, such as a waist belt and extra leads, and they add liability. They are a later upgrade after the same two dogs have gone well for a month. Winter gear is part of the budget in most of the country. A mild coastal week is not a Prairie January. ## How do ongoing costs and break-even work for dog walking in Canada?

Ongoing dog walking costs in Canada are waste bags, owner-approved treats, travel when there is any, and the insurance premium, and the break-even sketch further down is an illustrative example with assumed fees. Retail ranges in the tables are typical Canadian retail ranges to verify locally, not a study. Dog walking break-even in the sketch is a method, not a city price and not a promise.

**Waste bags.** The regular consumable. A planning range to replace with your own receipts is about $10–$25 a month for a few daily dogs. Buy the plain bulk pack.

**Treats.** Only the ones owners approve. Ask them to supply special diets. The pouch should not become a grocery category.

**Transit or fuel.** Nothing extra if every dog is a walk from home. A drive each way is a different service. Use your real fare or fuel, plus a small wear set-aside if you drive. Include that cost in the price or stop taking that client.

**Phone.** The plan you already have. Photos to owners are part of the job they expect. The phone is not a new gear purchase unless you added data for the work.

**Insurance.** The premium, monthly or annual. A plan to “get a quote later” is not a zero in the budget. Ask whether a home policy covers paid walks. This is not insurance advice.

**Key replacements and locksmiths.** Rare, and expensive when you are the reason. The prevention is the key routine.

**App fees.** If you use Rover or a similar app, read the current terms in the account for any fee. This guide does not quote a platform fee. Direct clients have no platform fee and all of the scheduling. Word of mouth in one neighbourhood avoids unpaid driving across a metro.

### Step 1: Add the one-time gear

One-time gear means the leash, bags, and anything else you bought before the first paid week.

### Step 2: Add the monthly fixed costs

Monthly fixed costs mean insurance, bags, software if any, and phone only if you added data.

### Step 3: List the variable cost of one visit

Variable cost means transit or fuel, treats you paid for, and parking.

### Step 4: Subtract variable cost from the fee

Contribution equals the fee you received minus the variable cost.

### Step 5: Divide the startup amount by the contribution

Visits to earn back the leash and boots equal startup divided by contribution.

A recurring weekday client is the point of dog walking. Five one-off walks do not build the same break-even as one dog, five days a week.

**Illustrative example.** Made-up fees for the division. Not a city price and not a promise.

Leash, bags, pouch, and a light cost $90. You already own boots. Liability insurance in this sketch is $20 a month. Waste bags average $12 a month. Fixed monthly = $32. No software yet.

One neighbourhood walk pays $22. The walk takes 30 minutes plus 10 minutes of walking there and a two-line update. Variable cost is about $0.50 of bags and no travel. Contribution = $21.50.

Gear payback = $90 ÷ $21.50, or about five walks. The monthly fixed cost takes about two walks ($32 ÷ $21.50). One dog, three walks a week, covers this sketch’s fixed costs and then pays you. A $22 walk that needs a 12 km drive does not. At 8 L/100 km and $1.55/L, 12 km is about $1.49 of fuel before wear and time. The hour falls apart if the drive is as long as the walk. Price the suburban visit higher or decline it. This paragraph is not your rate card.

## Where can dog walking startup spending be lower in Canada?

Dog walking startup spending in Canada stays lower when every dog is reachable on foot, when the owner’s leash is a backup rather than your only equipment, and when branded bandanas and a website wait until two clients ask how to refer you. Special gear the dog needs is the owner’s to provide. A vet-clinic board is enough to test demand.

Do not host pets in your home, and do not offer overnight sitting, until the walking insurance conversation and the written rules exist. Overnight is a different budget: more trust, often a higher premium, and a clear list of what the night includes.

Group walks wait. The waist belt is for calm dogs you know.

## Which tax rules apply to a dog walking budget in Canada?

Dog walking fees in Canada are generally self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole Canada Pension Plan contribution on the net, as described under [Contributions to the Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). Waste bags, treats you paid for, and a small leash are ordinary expenses on that form, while lasting equipment is recovered through [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business portion. GST/HST for dog walking uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule, and Nova Scotia’s HST is 14% on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, veterinary, or insurance advice.

Keep receipts whether fees arrive by e-transfer or an app. An app may send a T4A. Your spreadsheet still matters. If you claim a car, keep a kilometre log: date, destination, kilometres, purpose. Claim the business portion only. Confirm capital cost allowance treatment for the year you file. This guide does not explain input tax credits. If you register, confirm those rules with the CRA or a tax professional. If you are not registered, do not add GST/HST to the walk fee.

The small-supplier line is $30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies — you and associates — in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Under that test, registration is optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the CRA’s when-to-register page. Confirm the rate for the province where the walk happens, including Quebec, on the charge-and-collect table and with the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Search the city for a business licence, and read a building’s visitor rules before you promise a lobby full of dogs. This guide does not state a national licence fee. Leash and stoop-and-scoop bylaws are on the city’s own pages. Follow the ones posted for your route.
