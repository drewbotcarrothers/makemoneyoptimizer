After this budget you can start a neighbourhood dog-walking route without buying a six-dog waist belt, price liability before you have regulars, and see how a 15-minute drive changes a 30-minute walk.

How to choose clients, handle keys, and run a first week is the [dog walking and pet sitting guide](/side-hustles/dog-walking-side-hustle-canada). This page is what it costs. You can start with almost no gear if every owner provides a leash. Buy your own anyway. Client equipment breaks, and you will look unprepared on a sidewalk.

## The quick answer

Ranges are typical Canadian retail. They are not a quote. They assume you walk in your own neighbourhood and are not buying a car. Overnight pet sitting adds almost no gear and a much larger trust and insurance question. Price that as a different product after the walks are boringly reliable.

| How you start | One-time cash, about | What you are carrying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean | $40–$100 | A sturdy leash, a backup slip lead, a box of waste bags, and a simple treat pouch you will use only if the owner agrees. |
| Typical | $120–$300 | The lean kit, a high-visibility layer or light, a lock box or a key routine that is not a labelled house key, and a small human first-aid pouch. |
| Doing it properly | $250–$600, plus insurance | Winter boots you trust on ice, a light, waste bags you will not run out of, and a liability policy aimed at pet care. The quote can be larger than the leash. Get it before you have a route of regulars. |

Dog bites, a pulled shoulder, and a broken vase are the claims. Home insurance often excludes business activity. An app such as Rover may describe coverage for bookings on the platform. Read the current exclusions in your account. App coverage is not a substitute for understanding the policy, and it does not automatically cover the neighbour you invoice directly.

## Who this budget fits

**It fits** if you like dogs, can walk in local weather, and will write down medications, the vet’s number, and which door sticks. The lean column fits a walker who stays on one map and refuses dogs that lunge or have no vaccine record.

**It is a poor fit** if you are allergic, if your housing bans an emergency animal, or if you want six unknown dogs on day one. Group walks multiply the gear (a waist belt, extra leads) and the liability. They are a later upgrade after the same two dogs have gone well for a month.

Winter gear is not optional in most of the country. A mild coastal week is not a Prairie January. ## Ongoing costs, and a simple break-even

**Waste bags.** The honest monthly consumable. A walker with a few daily dogs might spend $10–$25 a month. Buy the plain bulk pack. Do not let this become a boutique line.

**Treats.** Only the ones owners approve. Ask them to supply special diets. Your pouch should not become a grocery category.

**Transit or fuel.** $0 if every dog is a walk from home. A 15-minute drive each way is a different service. Use your real fare or fuel, plus a small wear set-aside if you drive. Include it in the price or stop taking that client.

**Phone.** The plan you have. Photos to owners are part of the job they expect. They are not a gear purchase.

**Insurance.** The premium, monthly or annual. This is the fixed cost that matters. A quote of “I’ll see” is not $0.

**Key replacements and locksmiths.** Rare, and expensive when you are the reason. The prevention is the key routine, not a line you hope stays empty.

**App fees.** If you use Rover or a similar app, the fee is whatever the current Canadian terms say. Read them. Direct clients have no platform fee and all of the admin. Word of mouth in one neighbourhood is the cheaper acquisition channel. A vague metro-wide ad is how you buy unpaid driving.

### How to calculate break-even

1. One-time gear.
2. Monthly fixed: insurance, bags, software if any, phone only if you added data.
3. Variable cost of one visit: transit or fuel, treats you paid for, parking.

Contribution = fee you received minus variable cost.

Visits to earn back the leash and boots = startup ÷ contribution.

A recurring weekday client is the point of this hustle. Five one-off walks do not build the same break-even as one dog, five days a week.

### Illustrative example

**Made-up fees for the division. Not a city price and not a promise.**

Leash, bags, pouch, and a light cost $90. You already own boots. Liability insurance in this sketch is $20 a month. Waste bags average $12 a month. Fixed monthly = $32. No software yet.

One neighbourhood walk pays $22. It takes 30 minutes plus 10 minutes of walking there and a two-line update. Variable cost is about $0.50 of bags and no travel. Contribution = $21.50.

Gear payback = $90 ÷ $21.50, or about five walks. The monthly fixed cost takes about two walks ($32 ÷ $21.50). One dog, three walks a week, covers this sketch’s fixed costs and then pays you. A $22 walk that needs a 12 km drive does not. At 8 L/100 km and $1.55/L, 12 km is about $1.49 of fuel before wear and time. The hour falls apart if the drive is as long as the walk. Price the suburban visit higher or decline it. This paragraph is not your rate card.

## Where you can spend less

Start with dogs you can reach on foot. The route is the business model.

Use the owner’s leash as a backup, not as your only equipment. You still do not need a catalogue of harnesses. Special gear the dog requires is the owner’s to provide.

Skip branded bandanas and a website until two clients ask how to refer you. A vet-clinic board is enough to test demand.

Do not host pets in your home, and do not offer overnight sitting, until the walking insurance and the written rules exist. Overnight is a different budget: more trust, often a higher premium, and a clear list of what “overnight” includes.

Group walks wait. The waist belt is a reward for calm dogs you know, not a way to multiply a thin rate.

## Where not to cut corners

Liability insurance, once you are past a single trial for a friend. A bite or a damaged apartment is the loss that ends a casual walker. Home policies often exclude business. Read the app policy if you have one, and do not assume it covers off-app neighbours. This is not insurance advice.

Ice. Boots and a light are cheaper than a fall with someone else’s dog. In a weather warning, your rule should be a message and a reschedule, not heroics. Write that rule before January.

Vaccines and fit. Walking a dog with no records, or a dog that guards or lunges, to avoid “losing the sale” is the wrong saving.

Keys. An address label on a house key is a security failure, not a system.

You are not a veterinarian, a trainer, or a kennel. Do not buy medical tools and invent a service you are not qualified to sell.

## Canada note

This is not tax, legal, veterinary, or insurance advice.

Fees are generally self-employment income, whether they arrive by e-transfer or an app. Keep receipts for supplies. The T2125 is the usual place this income and these expenses go on a personal return. Budget for both shares of CPP on your net. An app may send a T4A. Your spreadsheet still matters.

**CCA and the log.** Waste bags, treats you paid for, and a leash are ordinary business expenses. Boots and a durable leash are small. A car, if you claim one, is not. Keep a kilometre log: date, destination, kilometres, purpose. Claim the business portion only. Lasting equipment is recovered through capital cost allowance (CCA) on that business portion, in the class the CRA assigns, not necessarily all in the first walk. Confirm the treatment for the year you file.

**GST/HST.** Walking and sitting are taxable services for GST/HST purposes in the ordinary case. The small-supplier test decides whether you must register. The CRA’s line is $30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies — you and associates — in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Under that, registration is optional. Over it, you register on the timetable on the [when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) page. This is not the ride-sharing first-dollar rule.

**Input tax credits.** If you register, you may claim an input tax credit for GST/HST paid on costs to the extent they are for commercial activity. You need receipts that show the tax. If you are not registered, you do not add GST/HST to your walk fee and you do not claim the credit.

Rates differ by province, including Quebec’s QST and Nova Scotia’s 14% HST since 1 April 2025. Use the CRA [rate table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html).

Some cities expect a business licence, and some buildings have pet rules that are not yours to waive. Search the city and read the building’s visitor policy before you promise a lobby full of dogs. Municipal pet bylaws about leashes and stoop-and-scoop already apply to you as the person holding the lead.
