Babysitting in Canada is paid, occasional care of another family's children, usually in their home, for date nights, weekend afternoons, school PA days and the gap after school. Babysits, which tracks rates from sitters on its platform, put the 2026 Canadian average at $18.68 an hour, from $17.34 in Edmonton to $20.53 in Vancouver. Startup costs are small: a first-aid course and a kit. What lets you charge more is training, experience with infants, and being the sitter families trust to rebook.

<QuickAnswer>
- **Going rate:** $18.68 an hour on average in Canada in 2026, up from $18.35 in 2025 and $15.85 in 2023; Toronto $20.20, Montreal $18.46, Calgary $18.03, Halifax $19.27 (Babysits data, reported by Babysits and The Globe and Mail).
- **By age and experience:** parents interviewed by The Globe and Mail in December 2025 paid $10 to $15 an hour for younger teens and $20+ for older, experienced sitters; a Toronto agency charged $33.
- **Government benchmark:** Job Bank lists babysitters at $15.00 low, $19.00 median and $28.00 high per hour nationally (2023–2024 data).
- **Training:** the Canadian Red Cross Babysitting course runs 7 to 8 hours for ages 11 to 15, in person or virtual; a compact first-aid kit costs $7.49 to $46.10 on Amazon.ca (October 9, 2026).
- **Rules:** caring for several families' children in your own home is capped by province (Ontario 5 under 13; B.C. two unrelated children or one sibling group; Alberta 6).
- **Tax:** report on T2125; care in your home for children 14 and under for under 24 hours is GST/HST-exempt.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who does babysitting suit?

Babysitting suits patient, dependable people who show up on time, follow the parents' rules rather than their own, and stay calm when a child is hurt, sick or melting down at bedtime. It's one of the few side hustles that works at 12 or 13 with a course certificate, and it scales up for adults with real credentials: early childhood educators, nursing students, nurses (see [side hustles for nurses](/side-hustles/side-hustles-for-nurses-canada/)), teachers and former nannies charge at the top of the range.

It's a poor fit if you need a fixed weekly schedule from day one (most bookings are evenings and weekends), if you're uneasy being alone in charge of children, or if you can't commit to the nights you promise. Reliability is the product: families rebook the sitter whose evening went exactly as planned.

Good fits:

- Teens 12 and up with the Red Cross certificate and a few family references.
- University and college students with flexible evenings.
- ECE, nursing and education students who can charge more for infant care or special needs.
- Bilingual sitters, especially French speakers outside Quebec, where families look for language practice.
- Seasonal workers in resort towns, where visiting families need sitters for ski days (the RevySitters model).

## How does babysitting pay?

Babysitting is paid by the hour, usually by e-transfer or cash at the end of the evening, with the rate set between you and the family. The factors Babysits lists as raising the rate are the number of children, younger ages, extra tasks such as cooking, homework help, pickup and drop-off, pets, late nights or overnights, and your qualifications and experience.

### What experience adds

The Globe and Mail's December 7, 2025 column by Erin Bury reported that parents she spoke with paid $10 to $15 an hour for younger teens and $20 or more for older or more experienced sitters. Her own family in a small Ontario town paid more than $22 an hour for a sitter in her twenties who was a former nanny and trained nurse. The Summerhill Club, a Toronto babysitting agency, charged $33 an hour for a vetted sitter on its roster. One parent said adding another family's child cost about $5 an hour more.

## How much can you make babysitting?

At the 2026 national average of $18.68, a four-hour Saturday evening pays $74.72, and two evenings a week pay about $149. Teens at $10 to $15 earn less; experienced adult sitters at $22 to $25+ earn more.

## What does the math look like?

**Illustrative.** Rates come from Babysits, The Globe and Mail and RevySitters; the hours, weeks and number of children are assumptions.

Formula: (base rate + extra-child add-on × additional children) × hours × bookings.

| Scenario | Rate | Children | Hours/week (assumed) | Weekly pay | 40 weeks (assumed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Younger teen, one family | $12 (Globe teen range) | 1 | 4 | $48.00 | $1,920 |
| Average sitter, two families | $18.68 (Babysits 2026 avg) | 2 (+$4) | 8 | $181.44 | $7,257.60 |
| Experienced adult, resort town | $25 (RevySitters minimum) | 2 (+$4) | 12 | $348.00 | $13,920 |

Check the middle row: ($18.68 + $4) × 8 = $22.68 × 8 = $181.44 a week; × 40 = $7,257.60.

Startup costs come off the top once: a first-aid kit at $19.95 (First Aid Central Everyday, Amazon.ca, October 9, 2026) plus the course fee your local training partner charges. Most sitters cover both in the first one or two evenings.

## Is babysitting regulated in Canada?

Occasional babysitting in a family's own home is generally treated differently from running child care for several families in your home, which each province regulates. Once you look after children from more than one family at your place, provincial limits on unlicensed child care apply.

| Province | Unlicensed limit, from the official page | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario | Up to 5 children under 13; no more than 3 under age 2; your own children under 4 count | Must tell parents in writing that the program is not licensed by the Government of Ontario and keep the notice for 2 years. [Ontario.ca](https://www.ontario.ca/page/home-child-care-and-unlicensed-child-care-how-many-children-are-allowed) |
| British Columbia | Two children or one sibling group not related to you | Licence-not-required providers are not monitored or inspected. [Gov.bc.ca](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/family-social-supports/caring-for-young-children/family-information/licensed-unlicensed-child-care) |
| Alberta | Up to 6 children, not including your own | The province can investigate complaints and issue stop orders above that number. [Alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/understand-albertas-childcare-system-for-parents-and-families) |

Sitting for one family in their home is the simplest version and the focus of this guide. For other provinces and territories, check the provincial child care ministry before you take children from more than one family at once. Many families also ask for a police information check; the process and fee are set by your local police service.

## How do you start? A 30-day plan

### Week 1: Train

- Book the Red Cross Babysitting course (ages 11–15) or Standard First Aid with CPR (adults). Note the certificate's expiry date.
- Buy a compact first-aid kit and pack a sitter bag: kit, phone charger, a pen, activity cards.
- Read your province's unlicensed child care page if you'll ever watch children at your place.

### Week 2: Set your offer

- Decide ages you'll take, maximum number of children, overnights yes or no, and whether you'll drive (most beginners shouldn't).
- Set your rate using the city table above and your experience: below average if you're new and young, at or above if you're certified and experienced.
- Write a one-page sitter sheet (template below) and ask two adults who've seen you with children to be references.

### Week 3: Find your first families

- Tell neighbours, family friends and parents you know; post once in a local parent group.
- Create a profile on a babysitting platform if you're old enough to meet its rules.
- Offer a short meet-and-greet before every first booking.

### Week 4: Sit, review, rebook

- Run the first evenings by the family info sheet.
- Send a two-line summary after each evening.
- Ask happy families to rebook a regular slot and to pass your name to friends.

## Templates and checklists you can copy

### Sitter sheet (your one-pager)

```
[Your name], babysitter in [neighbourhood]
Training: Red Cross Babysitting course (date) / First Aid + CPR (expiry)
Experience: [ages cared for, years]
Available: [evenings / weekends / after school]
Rate: $___/hour for 1 child, +$___ per additional child; after midnight $___
References: [two names and phone numbers]
Contact: [phone or parent's phone if you're under 16]
```

### Family info sheet (fill in at the meet-and-greet)

```
Parents' cell numbers:
Backup contact nearby:
Home address (for 911):
Allergies / medical conditions / medication (written instructions only):
Bedtimes and routine:
Food and screen rules:
Off-limits areas or activities:
Where the first-aid kit and flashlight are:
Pets and their care:
```

### Evening checklist

- [ ] Arrive 10 minutes early; phone charged.
- [ ] Walk through exits, locks, the first-aid kit and the thermostat.
- [ ] Confirm allergies and any medication in writing.
- [ ] Follow bedtime, food and screen rules exactly.
- [ ] Keep doors locked; no visitors without permission.
- [ ] Tidy what you and the children used.
- [ ] Leave a two-line note: what they ate, when they went down, anything unusual.

### Booking confirmation message

```
Hi [parent], confirming Saturday [date], 6 to 10 p.m. for [children's names].
My rate is $___/hour ($___ total for 4 hours), paid by e-transfer at the end
of the night. Is the ride home after 10 p.m. covered? See you at 5:50.
```

## Real Canadian babysitting businesses on the record

**Xanthe Garahy and Amelia Smith, RevySitters, Revelstoke, B.C.** The Similkameen Spotlight (Black Press) reported on May 31, 2025 that the two lifelong babysitters, 27 and 28, launched an online service matching local and visiting families with vetted sitters, all 19 or older, police-checked and first-aid certified. Sitters keep 80% of what families pay and earn a minimum of $25 an hour plus $4 an hour for each additional child; RevySitters keeps 20%. Each regional winner receives $1,000, and she planned to use part of it to help young sitters get certified.

## What mistakes do new babysitters make?

- **Saying yes to too many children.** If care happens at your home, the provincial limit applies; in the family's home, know your own limit.
- **Guessing about allergies or medication.** If it isn't written down, ask. Don't give medication you weren't told in writing to give.
- **Driving children without a plan.** Car seats, insurance and parental consent all need sorting first. Many sitters simply don't drive children.
- **Leaving the price unspoken.** Agree the rate, the extra-child add-on, after-midnight pay and the ride home before the evening.
- **Underpricing forever.** Raise your rate when you add training, infant experience or a second language; the Babysits city averages are a fair reference.
- **Posting photos of the children.** Never post a child's photo without the parents' permission.
- **Ignoring the expiry date.** Renew first aid and CPR before they lapse.

## Which tax rules apply to babysitting in Canada?

Babysitting income is generally self-employment income, reported on [Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html) with your T1 return. Self-employed people pay both halves of the [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution on net self-employment income above the basic exemption; teens under 18 don't contribute to CPP. Course fees, a first-aid kit and supplies you buy for sitting are business expenses. A full walkthrough is in [how to report side hustle income to the CRA](/side-hustles/report-side-hustle-income-cra-canada/), and what you can deduct is in [side hustle expense deductions](/side-hustles/side-hustle-expense-deductions-canada/).

GST/HST follows the CRA's [small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html): $30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, counting all your side-hustle revenue together. Child care has its own treatment: the CRA's page on [collecting GST/HST on your daycare services](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/daycare-your-home/collecting-goods-services-tax-harmonized-sales-tax-on-your-daycare-services.html) says care and supervision you provide in your home to children aged 14 or under, for periods usually less than 24 hours a day, is free of GST/HST, so you can't add the tax. For sitting in the family's home or for older children, confirm the treatment with the CRA before you charge tax.

Parents may claim [child care expenses on line 21400](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/line-21400-child-care-expenses.html) when the care lets them work or study, so give a dated receipt with your name, the dates and the amount when a family asks, and keep a copy.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

### Provincial notes

- **Ontario:** the unlicensed limit is 5 children under 13, counting your own under 4, with a written notice to parents. Job Bank's Ontario babysitter median is $19.00.
- **Quebec:** QPP replaces CPP. Job Bank's Quebec babysitter median is $21.00, the highest provincial median in the table.
- **British Columbia:** licence-not-required care is limited to two unrelated children or one sibling group. Vancouver's 2026 average of $20.53 is the highest city figure in the Babysits data.
- **Alberta:** up to 6 children not counting your own; Edmonton's $17.34 average was the lowest city figure.
- **Other provinces and territories:** check the child care ministry's page before caring for children from more than one family together.
