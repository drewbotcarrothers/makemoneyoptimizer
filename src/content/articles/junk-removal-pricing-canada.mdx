## What does junk removal cost to run in Canada?

Junk removal in Canada has three cost lines per job: labour, disposal fees, and the vehicle. Disposal is a published municipal number, and labour has a public employee benchmark; the vehicle cost is yours to measure.

| Input | Published figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labour benchmark (furniture mover, employees) | $16.55 low, $22.00 median, $30.29 high per hour, Canada | [Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/16660/ca), NOC 75101, 2023–2024, updated November 19, 2025 |
| Calgary landfill, basic sanitary waste (commercial) | $113 per tonne in 2026; $25 minimum for loads under 250 kg | [City of Calgary](https://www.calgary.ca/for-business/operations/landfill-commercial-rates.html) |
| Calgary surcharge for loads with readily recyclable or compostable material | $180 per tonne | [City of Calgary](https://www.calgary.ca/for-business/operations/landfill-commercial-rates.html) |
| Toronto drop-off depot, garbage and renovation waste | $189.86 per tonne; $19 minimum covers the first 100 kg | [City of Toronto](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/recycling-organics-garbage/drop-off-depots/fees-for-drop-off-depots/) |

Calgary's page says no GST is charged on its landfill rates. Toronto lists a separate commercial disposal service, so confirm which rate applies to you as a hauler before you quote. Rates differ by city and change yearly; recheck the page in January.

## Which pricing models work for junk removal in Canada?

Junk removal in Canada is usually priced by volume (a fraction of a load), per item for single pieces, or with a minimum charge, plus surcharges for heavy material and items with special disposal rules.

| Model | How it works | Works well for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| By volume | 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, or full load of your vehicle | Mixed household junk | Heavy loads (concrete, shingles, soil) that hit weight limits first |
| Per item | One price per couch, mattress, appliance | Single pieces | Stairs and tight doorways |
| Minimum charge | Lowest price for any visit | Small pickups | Losing money on a 20-minute trip |
| Heavy-material surcharge | Extra per load or by weight | Renovation debris | Overloading your trailer or vehicle |
| Special items | Separate fee for mattresses, appliances with refrigerant, paint | Items with their own disposal rules | Dumping something that is not accepted |

Weigh your first loads at the scale and write down volume versus weight. That record becomes your quote sheet.

## How do you calculate a junk removal price in Canada?

Calculate a junk removal price in Canada by adding your labour at a billable rate, any helper's pay, the disposal fee for the expected weight, and the vehicle's fuel and wear, then rounding to a load-fraction price.

### Step 1: Set your billable rate

Pick a target hourly pay and divide by the share of your time that is billable once quoting and driving to the depot are counted.

### Step 2: Estimate weight and disposal

Use the scale tickets from your own loads. Multiply the expected tonnes by your city's rate and respect the minimum.

### Step 3: Add the helper

If someone lifts with you, their pay is a job cost. Job Bank's furniture mover median is a reasonable reference point for that wage.

### Step 4: Add vehicle cost

Fuel for the round trip to the depot, plus a per-kilometre amount for tires, brakes, and trailer wear that you set from your own records.

### Step 5: Round and hold back tax and CPP

Self-employed people pay both CPP shares; the [tax set-aside guide](/side-hustles/how-much-tax-set-aside-side-hustle-canada/) shows how to keep that money aside.

### Illustrative example

This is a teaching sketch with assumed numbers, not a quote, average, or survey. Suppose your target pay is $30 an hour and 70% of your time is billable: $30 ÷ 0.70 = $42.86 an hour. Suppose a quarter-trailer of household junk takes you and a helper 1.5 hours including the depot run, and you pay the helper $22 an hour, Job Bank's national median for furniture movers.

Suppose the load weighs 300 kg in Calgary, so it is over the 250 kg minimum: 0.3 tonnes × $113 = $33.90 disposal. Suppose fuel and wear for the trip is $15.

- Your labour: 1.5 × $42.86 = $64.29
- Helper: 1.5 × $22 = $33.00
- Disposal: $33.90
- Vehicle: $15.00
- Total: $146.19, which you might round to $150 before tax

If the same load had mixed in recyclable cardboard and been charged at Calgary's $180 surcharge rate, disposal would be 0.3 × $180 = $54.00 and the total $166.29. Sorting before the scale is part of pricing.

## When should you raise junk removal prices in Canada?

Raise junk removal prices in Canada when your city raises tipping fees (often in January), when fuel rises, or when scale tickets show your loads are heavier than you priced. Update the sheet the week the new rates post.

Tie the disposal portion to the published rate in your notes, so a fee increase flows straight into the load-fraction prices.

## What junk removal pricing mistakes are common in Canada?

Common junk removal pricing mistakes in Canada are quoting by volume on heavy debris, forgetting the surcharge for unsorted recyclables, and skipping a minimum. Each turns a load into a loss at the scale.

Ask for photos. Ask what is in the boxes. Price renovation debris by weight. Refuse items your depot will not accept, such as hazardous waste, and point the client to the municipal program. Never quote "anything goes."

## How do GST/HST and income tax affect junk removal prices in Canada?

Junk removal income in Canada is self-employment income reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and disposal fees and fuel are business expenses. GST/HST registration becomes required after you pass the [$30,000 small-supplier threshold](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, counting all your side hustles.

Once registered, charge your province's rate from the CRA's [charge and collect page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) generally on the whole invoice, including the disposal portion built into your price.
