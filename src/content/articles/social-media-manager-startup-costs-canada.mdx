Social media management in Canada costs $0 with a phone and free tools, $53.86 with a tripod and a mic, $282.10 with a light and Canva Pro, $632.10 with a starting liability policy, or $1,410.40 with a headset, a paid scheduler and invoicing software. ## Who should use a social media manager startup budget in Canada?

A social media manager budget fits people who can write a caption a stranger would read, keep a calendar for someone else's business, and say no to buying followers. The lean kit fits one local client whose posts you shoot on site. The standard kit fits two or three retainers with branded templates. The pro kit fits someone scheduling across several clients and platforms.

It's a poor fit if you want to promise viral results, if you'll ask for a client's password instead of partner access, or if the budget includes ad spend that should be on the client's card.

## Do you need insurance or a licence for social media management in Canada?

There's no licence to manage social accounts. Three rules matter more.

**Ads and claims.** Posts are advertising. The Competition Bureau's rules on false or misleading claims apply to what you publish for a client. Don't invent reviews or results.

**Email and messages.** If the work includes newsletters or direct messages to customers, [Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) applies. Get consent rules right before you press send.

**Insurance.** Zensurance's [freelancer insurance page](https://www.zensurance.com/freelancer-insurance) lists digital marketers among the freelancers it covers and says freelancer insurance generally starts from $350 to $500 a year for professional liability with a $1 million limit. That's an estimate, not a quote, read on October 9, 2026. Compare options in [APOLLO vs Zensurance vs a local broker](/side-hustles/apollo-vs-zensurance-side-hustle-insurance-canada/). This is not insurance or legal advice.

## Which tax rules apply to a social media manager budget in Canada?

Social media management income in Canada is self-employment income, including a retainer, reported on [Form T2125](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2125.html), and self-employed people pay the whole [Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) contribution, both portions, on the net. Canva, Buffer, and a headset used for the work are generally current expenses. A phone or computer that lasts for years is usually [capital cost allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/claiming-capital-cost-allowance.html) on the business-use portion, not the whole household device. GST/HST uses the [$30,000 small-supplier test](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), not the [taxi and ride-sharing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-specific-situations/taxi-ride-sharing-drivers.html) rule. Nova Scotia's HST is 14% as of 1 April 2025 on the CRA [charge and collect the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html) table.

This is not tax, legal, or insurance advice.

Keep the agreement, the content log, and the payments. Ad spend the client paid is not your income. The small-supplier test is worldwide taxable supplies, yours and your associates', of $30,000 or less in a quarter and over four consecutive quarters. Add other side hustles. This work is not ride-sharing.

If you are registered, GST/HST you paid on business purchases can often be recovered as an [input tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html) to the extent of commercial use, if you were a registrant and you have the documents. Canva's note that it may add GST at checkout is a reason to keep that receipt. If you are not registered, do not charge GST/HST and do not claim the credit. Confirm the current rules with the CRA or a tax professional.

Once registered, charge the rate for the province where the supply is made. Some provinces use HST. Others add PST on top of GST. Quebec charges GST and QST. Use the [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) and set the tax aside.
